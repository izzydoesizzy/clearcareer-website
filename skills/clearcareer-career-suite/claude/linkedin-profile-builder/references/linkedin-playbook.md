# LinkedIn Profile Playbook

Recruiters search LinkedIn like a database: by title, skill, location and keyword. A profile built from the Master Career Profile gets found for the right searches and convinces in the first 10 seconds.

Everything here follows `writing-rules.md` (no em dashes, no banned words). Exception: LinkedIn sections are written in **first person** where noted, because that's how people read LinkedIn.

## 1. Headline (220 characters max)

Formula: `[Target Title] | [Years] in [Industry] | [Portfolio, scope or recognizable clients] | [Quantified win]`
Or for skill-heavy roles: `[Target Title] | [Tool] | [Tool] | [Tool] | [Quantified win]`

Rules:
- Start with the target title from the profile (the title they want, which may differ from their current one, as long as they're qualified for it).
- No subjective qualifiers before the title: no "Accomplished", "Results-oriented", "Passionate".
- At least one number.
- Use the words recruiters search: the exact title text and 2 to 3 top keywords.

Write 10 options. Mark the top 3.

Examples:
| # | Headline |
|---|---|
| 1 | Sales Planning Analyst \| 8+ Years in CPG \| $40M+ Retail Portfolio incl. Walmart, Loblaws, Metro \| Saved $600K+ by Streamlining Client Promotions |
| 2 | Senior Accounts Receivable Manager \| $1B+ Portfolio incl. PepsiCo, Costco, ExxonMobil \| Recovered $56M Past Due in Under 9 Months |
| 3 | Program Manager \| SAP \| Oracle \| Power BI \| Saved 60 Hours a Week Through Automation \| Retained a $20M Project |

## 2. About section (2,600 characters max)

Use this exact structure, with the dot dividers and line breaks:

```
[Summary paragraph, first person or implied first person: target title, years of experience, 3 in-demand skills, industries, noteworthy companies or clients, one big number.]

• • • • •

Career Highlights:

[Emoji] [Achievement title with the number up front] - [Company]
[One-sentence quantified achievement]

[Emoji] [Achievement title] - [Company]
[One-sentence quantified achievement]

(5 highlights total, taken from the strongest quantified achievements in the profile)

• • • • •

[What excites me, max 190 characters: the types of roles and companies they want, and why.]

[Call to action, max 130 characters: a specific reason for a recruiter to reach out.]

Connect with me at: → [email]
```

Use a hyphen between the highlight title and the company, never an em dash. Emoji are fine in highlights only, one per highlight, matched to the result (💰 money, ⏱️ time, 📈 growth, 👥 people, 🏆 awards, 🚀 launches, 🛠️ process).

Sample highlight:
> 💰 $850K Cost Savings - Amerant Bank
> Cut operational costs $850K, including a 39% price decrease in bankcard manufacturing and a 57% cut in shipping rates.

Write two tones: **Professional** and **Conversational** (warmer, more personal opening line). The client picks one.

## 3. Experience entries (per role)

Format:
```
[Title] | [Company]
[City, Region] ([Remote / Hybrid] if relevant) | [Mon YYYY] to [Mon YYYY or Present]
[3 to 4 lines, 380 characters max, first person: the team or context, what you built or led, 2 to 3 numbers from the profile.]
```

Sample:
> As part of the Product Innovation team, I worked with designers, engineers and analysts to improve the host experience. I launched the "Local Hosts" initiative, cutting host setup time 50%. Bookings rose 30%, adding $12M in revenue, with a 4.8/5 guest rating.

Then, under the paragraph, add the role's top 3 to 5 resume bullets (LinkedIn allows bullets in the description). Older roles: the paragraph only.

## 4. Skills (up to 50 allowed, the top 5 matter most)

- **Top 5 (pinned):** the skills that appear most often in job postings for the target titles and that the client can prove.
- **Next 15 to 25:** the rest of their real hard skills, tools and certifications, using the exact words postings use ("Salesforce", not "CRM software").
- Link each skill to the role where it was used.
- Never add a skill the profile can't back up.

## 5. Open to Work settings (private to recruiters by default)

Fill in from the profile:
- **Job titles (up to 5):** the target and alternative titles.
- **Locations:** their city plus up to 4 more, and Remote if they want it.
- **Start date:** immediately or flexible.
- **Job types:** full-time, contract, and so on.
- **Visibility:** "Recruiters only" if they're employed (this hides it from people at their current company, though it's not guaranteed), or "All LinkedIn members" (the green frame) if they're openly searching. Explain the trade-off in one line and let them choose.

## 6. Open to Work post (optional, for public searchers)

```
🚀 [Target Title] on the Move 🚀

[2 sentences: what they've been doing, for how long, where, and that they're ready for what's next.]

🔍 Looking For:
[Titles] roles in [City or Remote], focused on [2 to 3 areas].

ℹ️ About Me:
[2 to 3 sentences with years, specialty and one recognition.]

🏆 Career Highlights:
[Emoji] [Quantified achievement]
[Emoji] [Quantified achievement]
[Emoji] [Quantified achievement]

[One line thanking past colleagues.]

3 WAYS TO HELP:
👍 Like or comment to boost visibility.
🔁 Repost to your network.
🤝 Introductions to [type of company or role] are hugely appreciated.

#[Title] #[Industry] #[Skill] #OpenToWork
```

## 7. Banner tagline

Write 10 short banner taglines (under 60 characters) that each sell one specific value, for example: "Turning Customer Data into Retention", "Cutting Costs Without Cutting Corners". The client picks one to put on a simple banner (Canva has free LinkedIn banner templates).

## 9. Where things go (click path, menus change)

- Headline, location, Open to Work: click the pencil on the profile intro card.
- About: Add profile section > Core > About (or the pencil on the About card).
- Experience: the + or pencil on the Experience card, then each role.
- Skills: Add profile section > Core > Skills, then pin the top 5 under "Manage" or the reorder option.
- Custom URL: Edit public profile & URL, on the right side of the profile.
