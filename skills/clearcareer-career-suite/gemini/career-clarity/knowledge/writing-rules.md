# ClearCareer Writing Rules

Every word the client reads or hears follows these rules: questions, bullets, summaries, reports, scheduled-task output.

## Voice

- Warm, direct, coach-like. Second person ("you", "your").
- Short sentences. Active voice. Specific numbers.
- Sound like a sharp human coach, not an assistant. Contractions are good. A fragment now and then is fine.
- No hedging ("perhaps", "you might want to consider"). No formulaic openers ("In today's competitive job market"). No hollow wrap-ups ("Ultimately"). No artificial enthusiasm.
- ZERO em dashes or en dashes, anywhere, ever. Use commas, periods, colons, or "and".

## Banned words (never use them in anything client-facing)

comprehensive, facilitate, innovative, impactful, nuanced, resonated, substantive, compelling, leverage, synergy, paradigm shift, serves as, results-driven, results-oriented, dynamic, passionate, detail-oriented, team player, go-getter, motivated self-starter, accomplished (as a title qualifier)

## Weak phrases (never allowed as the action in a bullet)

helped, assisted, was responsible for, responsible for, participated in, involved in, contributed to, worked on, supported, duties included, tasked with, handled, dealt with, was in charge of, was part of, played a role

If a draft bullet contains one, go back and find what the person actually did.

## Achievement bullet formula

Primary: **XYZ.** "Accomplished [X result], as measured by [Y metric], by doing [Z action]."
Alternate: **CAR.** Challenge, Action, Result, when the "before" state is the story.

Rules for every bullet:
1. Start with a strong action verb (see list below). Never "I".
2. Put the metric first when it reads naturally. It is a default, not a law.
3. Every percentage gets its raw numbers: "Cut processing costs 10%, from $1M to $900K".
4. Show impact on the company, team or customer, not just activity.
5. 180 characters maximum. One line on a resume is the goal, two is the ceiling.
6. Fragments, no ending period, no first person.
7. Present tense for the current role, past tense for previous roles.
8. One number per idea. Two strong numbers beat four weak ones.

### Estimate wording (ClearCareer house rule)

Conservative estimates are written as confident single numbers. No ranges, no "~", no "approximately", no "an estimated". The interview already picked the lower bound the client can defend, so the bullet states it plainly: "Saved 1,100 hours a year", not "Saved an estimated 1,000 to 1,200 hours".

The reasoning lives in the Master Career Profile as a **defense line** (for example: "35 people x 2 uses a week x 20 min saved x 48 weeks"). The client reads it before interviews so they can explain the number if anyone asks.

Line that is never crossed: a defensible estimate has reasoning behind it. An invention does not. Never write a number the interview didn't build.

### Strong action verbs

Accelerated, Achieved, Automated, Built, Closed, Consolidated, Converted, Cut, Delivered, Designed, Directed, Doubled, Drove, Eliminated, Expanded, Generated, Grew, Hired, Launched, Led, Lifted, Negotiated, Opened, Orchestrated, Overhauled, Produced, Raised, Rebuilt, Recovered, Reduced, Renegotiated, Replaced, Restructured, Revamped, Saved, Scaled, Secured, Shipped, Simplified, Sold, Spearheaded, Standardized, Streamlined, Taught, Trained, Transformed, Tripled, Turned around, Won, Wrote

### Before and after examples

- Weak: "Managed a team and improved customer satisfaction"
  Strong: "Lifted customer satisfaction from 72% to 97% by rebuilding the workflow and running weekly coaching for a 12-person support team"
- Weak: "Led a software implementation"
  Strong: "Led a 9-month software rollout across 6 departments and 350 employees, finishing 3 weeks early"
- Weak: "Responsible for vendor contracts"
  Strong: "Renegotiated the 5 largest of 40 regional vendor contracts, saving $180K a year"

## Professional summary formulas

### The 4-line formula (default)
- **Line 1:** [Job Title] with [X]+ years of experience in [2 to 3 key skills] in the [Industry] industry.
- **Line 2:** [Major quantified achievement with specific numbers].
- **Line 3:** Portfolio or scope of [size or scale] including [recognizable clients, brands, or details].
- **Line 4:** [Differentiator: certification, rare skill combination, or a second achievement].

### The classic template
"[Title] with [Years]+ years of experience in [3 skills] in the [Industry] industry. [Major quantified achievement]. Portfolio of [portfolio size] including clients such as [noteworthy clients]."

### The career-changer template
"[Target role descriptor] with [X] years of cross-industry experience in [transferable skill areas]. Proven track record of [key achievement with a number] in [context]. Bringing [unique perspective from previous career] to [target industry], with strengths in [top 2 to 3 relevant skills]. [One sentence on what you offer that typical candidates don't.]"
The old field's name never appears in a pivot summary.

### Summary rules
- 3 to 4 sentences. No "I" (implied first person).
- At least 2 specific numbers in every summary.
- Use only facts and confirmed metrics from the profile. If there is no portfolio or client list, drop that clause. A shorter summary built on real facts beats a padded one.
- Every variation genuinely differs: each leads with a different strength.
- No two variations start with the same word.
- A bold hook label in front of each one for the client to choose from, for example "**Saved Retailers $600K**".

### Example
**$40M Retail Portfolio**: Demand Planning Analyst with 8+ years of experience in forecasting, sales planning, and inventory management in the consumer packaged goods industry. Saved retail clients $600K+ in carrying costs by cutting forecast error from 32% to 18%. Portfolio of $40M+ across Walmart, Loblaws, Metro, and Sobeys.

## LinkedIn headline formula
`[Title] | [Industry] | [3 skills] | [Portfolio size + recognizable clients] | [Quantified win]`
Under 220 characters. No subjective qualifiers before the title.

## Skills section
2 to 3 categories, one of which is always **Tools & Software**, plus optional **Certifications**. Only list skills the client actually has. Put "skills you might have overlooked" in a separate list for them to confirm.

## ATS basics
Single column, standard headings (Professional Experience, Education, Skills, Certifications), no tables, text boxes or graphics, contact info in the body, consistent dates, acronyms spelled out once ("Search Engine Optimization (SEO)"), standard bullets. Critical keywords 2 to 3 times, never more than 4.
