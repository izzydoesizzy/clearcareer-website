# Hiring Manager Cold Emails

**15 short emails to the person who would be your boss. Pick one, fill in the brackets, and send.**

A hiring manager with an open role has a problem: a gap on their team that's slowing everyone down. If your email shows you understand that gap and you've handled something like it before, you're not a stranger anymore. You're a possible answer.

That's the idea behind every template here. Each one opens with them, proves your value in a line or two, and asks for one small thing.

Fill in every [bracket] with something real. A template you send without changing it reads like a mass email, and it gets treated like one.

**Time:** 10 to 15 minutes per email. **What you'll need:** The job post (if there is one), your resume, the person's name and title, and one specific thing about them or their team.

---

## The rules for every email in this guide

1. **Open with them, not you.** Their posting, their team's news, their post. Something specific and true.
2. **Use one or two wins.** With a number if you have one. Never make one up.
3. **Ask for one small thing.** Usually a 15-minute call.
4. **Keep the body under 120 words.** Short, specific emails get more replies than long ones.
5. **Never claim a connection you don't have.** No pretend mutual friends. No "Re:" on a first email.
6. **Apply through the careers page too** if there's a posting, and say so.
7. **No attachments on a first email.** Offer your resume instead.

Here's what "open with them" looks like in practice:

- **Before:** "I am writing to express my interest in the position."
- **After:** "Your posting says the first 90 days are about fixing the onboarding process. That's the project I led last year."

Same person. Same experience. The second one gets read.

## When there's a posted job

### Template 1: The Priority Match

**Subject:** [Job title]: how I'd help with [top priority]

~~~text
Hi [First name],

I'm reaching out about the [Job title] role. Reading the posting, it sounds like you need someone who can [Priority 1] and [Priority 2].

Here's how I've done both:

1. [Priority 1]: [Win with a number]
2. [Priority 2]: [Win with a number]

I've applied through the careers page. Would you be open to a 20-minute call on [day] to talk about what you need most in this hire?

Thanks,
[Your name]
[Phone] | [LinkedIn URL]
~~~

**When to use it:** The posting is clear about what matters most, and you have a real win for each of the top two priorities.

**Make it yours:** Pull the priorities from the first few bullets of the posting and use their words. If you have a strong match for a third priority, add it as line 3, but stay under 120 words. Name a specific day. Specific asks are easier to say yes to.

### Template 2: The One-Liner

**Subject:** [Job title] at [Company]

~~~text
Hi [First name],

I'll keep this short. I'm a [Your title] who [one-line win with a number], and your [Job title] role looks like a close match.

I've applied online, and I wanted you to have my name too. Open to a 15-minute call?

Thanks,
[Your name]
[LinkedIn URL]
~~~

**When to use it:** You're a strong match, and the person seems to like brief (short LinkedIn posts, a short "About" section, a fast-moving company).

**Make it yours:** Your one-line win carries the whole email. Read it out loud. If it sounds like a resume buzzword ("results-driven leader"), replace it with something you actually did.

### Template 3: The Three Wins

**Subject:** [Your title] interested in [Company]

~~~text
Hi [First name],

I saw [Company] is hiring a [Job title], and I'd love to be considered. A quick snapshot of what I bring:

- [Win 1, with a number]
- [Win 2, with a number]
- [Win 3, with a number]

Would it make sense to talk for 15 minutes? I'm happy to send my resume first if that's easier.

Thanks,
[Your name]
[Phone] | [LinkedIn URL]
~~~

**When to use it:** Your results speak louder than your story. This works well for sales, operations, finance, engineering and other roles with measurable outcomes.

**Make it yours:** Put the win that best matches the posting first. Keep each bullet to one line. If a bullet needs a sentence of explanation, it's the wrong bullet.

### Template 4: The Applied-Already Note

**Subject:** Applied for [Job title], wanted to say hello

~~~text
Hi [First name],

I just applied for the [Job title] role on your team and wanted to introduce myself directly.

I've spent [number] years in [field], most recently [one line on your current or last role]. The line in the posting that jumped out at me was [specific line], because [why it fits you].

If it's helpful, I'd love 15 minutes to learn what you're looking for in this hire.

Thanks,
[Your name]
[LinkedIn URL]
~~~

**When to use it:** Right after you apply, so a real person knows your application exists.

**Make it yours:** Quote one short line from the posting, word for word. It shows you read it, and it tells them exactly which role you mean.

### Template 5: The Company Fan

**Subject:** Long-time [Company] fan, first-time email

~~~text
Hi [First name],

I've followed [Company] since [specific moment: a launch, an article, using the product], and I've always liked how you [specific thing they do well].

When the [Job title] role went up, I had to reach out. I bring [number] years in [field], including [one win with a number].

Would you be open to a quick call about the team and what success looks like in this role?

Thanks,
[Your name]
[LinkedIn URL]
~~~

**When to use it:** You really do know and like the company, its product or its work.

**Make it yours:** Only send this if the fandom is real. One concrete detail ("I've used your app to plan every road trip since 2021") beats three general compliments.

## When there's no posted job

### Template 6: The On-Your-Radar Email

**Subject:** [Your specialty] for [Company]'s [team]

~~~text
Hi [First name],

I know [Company] may not be hiring for [type of role] right now, but I wanted to be on your radar.

I've spent [number] years in [field], focused on [specialty]. Most recently, I [win with a number]. I've been following [Company] because [real reason].

If the timing lines up down the road, I'd love to talk. Happy to send my resume in the meantime.

Thanks,
[Your name]
[LinkedIn URL]
~~~

**When to use it:** A company you'd love to work for, with no matching posting.

**Make it yours:** Put a reminder in your tracker to check their careers page every couple of weeks. If a role opens up, you can reply to this same thread: "The role I mentioned just went up."

### Template 7: The News Hook

**Subject:** Congrats on [launch, expansion or milestone]

~~~text
Hi [First name],

I saw [Company] just [launched X / opened in Y / announced Z]. Congrats to you and the team.

In my experience, that kind of growth usually means [a likely need: busier onboarding, more reporting, a bigger pipeline]. At [Current or last company], I [win that addresses that need].

Is that something your team is thinking about? I'd love 15 minutes to compare notes.

Thanks,
[Your name]
[LinkedIn URL]
~~~

**When to use it:** The company had real news in the last month or two: a launch, funding, a new office, a big hire, a new market.

**Make it yours:** Find the news on the company's newsroom page, LinkedIn page or a quick news search. Keep your guess about their need humble ("usually means"). You don't know their situation yet. You're starting a conversation.

### Template 8: The One Idea

**Subject:** An idea for [Company]'s [project or product]

~~~text
Hi [First name],

I've been looking at [Company]'s [product, project or process], and one idea kept coming back to me: [one-sentence idea].

I tried something similar at [Current or last company], and it [result, with a number if you have one]. Happy to share how it worked.

I'm exploring my next role, and [Company] is near the top of my list. Would you be up for a 15-minute call?

Thanks,
[Your name]
[LinkedIn URL]
~~~

**When to use it:** You've done your homework and have one small, useful idea you've seen work before.

**Make it yours:** Keep the idea modest. You don't know their budget, history or constraints, so offer it as a thought, not a fix. One idea is plenty. Three ideas starts to sound like a lecture.

### Template 9: The Work Sample

**Subject:** [Type of work] I made with [Company] in mind

~~~text
Hi [First name],

I put together [a short case study / a mock-up / a one-page analysis] on [topic tied to the company's work]. Here's the link: [Link]

It shows how I'd approach [problem or project]. I'm looking for a role where I can do more of this, and [Company] is where I'd love to do it.

If it sparks anything, I'd welcome a quick chat.

Thanks,
[Your name]
[LinkedIn URL]
~~~

**When to use it:** Design, writing, marketing, analytics, product and other roles where showing beats telling.

**Make it yours:** Keep the sample short: one page or a two-minute look. Use public information only, and don't dress it up with their logo so it looks official. Share a link, not an attachment.

## Special situations

### Template 10: The Name Drop (only if it's true)

**Subject:** [Mutual contact's name] suggested I reach out

~~~text
Hi [First name],

[Mutual contact's full name] suggested I get in touch. [He/She/They] mentioned you lead [team] and might be open to a quick chat about [topic or role].

I'm a [Your title] with [number] years in [field]. Most recently, I [win with a number].

Would you have 15 minutes in the next week or two? I'd value your perspective.

Thanks,
[Your name]
[LinkedIn URL]
~~~

**When to use it:** Someone really did tell you to reach out, and they're okay with you using their name.

**Make it yours:** Ask your contact first: "Is it okay if I mention you?" Never use a name you haven't cleared. People check, and a fake name drop ends the conversation.

### Template 11: The Career Changer

**Subject:** From [current field] to [target field]: a quick question

~~~text
Hi [First name],

I'm moving from [current field] into [target field], and your team at [Company] does the kind of work I want to do next.

The overlap is bigger than it looks. In [current role], I [win that maps to the new role]. That's close to [specific part of the target role].

Would you be open to a 15-minute call? I'd love your honest take on what your team looks for, and where my background fits.

Thanks,
[Your name]
[LinkedIn URL]
~~~

**When to use it:** You're changing fields and want a hiring manager to see the bridge between your old work and their needs.

**Make it yours:** Translate your win into their language. A teacher who "planned curriculum for [number] students" becomes someone who "designed and delivered training for [number] learners". Same work, new words.

### Template 12: The Relocation Note

**Subject:** Moving to [City] in [month]: [Your title]

~~~text
Hi [First name],

I'm relocating to [City] in [month], and [Company] is one of the first places I wanted to reach out to.

I'm a [Your title] with [number] years in [field]. At [Current or last company], I [win with a number].

I'd love to learn about your team before I arrive. Would a 15-minute video call work in the next few weeks?

Thanks,
[Your name]
[LinkedIn URL]
~~~

**When to use it:** You're moving and want conversations lined up before you land.

**Make it yours:** Give a real month, so they know you're serious. If you'll need a work permit or visa, be upfront once the conversation gets real. The rules are different in Canada and the US, so check what applies to you.

### Template 13: The Second Shot

**Subject:** [Job title]: trying again

~~~text
Hi [First name],

We spoke in [month] about the [earlier role], and while it didn't work out, I really enjoyed learning about your team.

I saw the new [Job title] posting and wanted to reach out. Since we last talked, I've [new win, skill or project], which lines up with [part of the new role].

Would you be open to a quick catch-up?

Thanks,
[Your name]
~~~

**When to use it:** You interviewed with this person before (or applied and spoke with them), and a new role has opened up.

**Make it yours:** Show what's changed since last time. One new win, course or project is enough. Keep the tone warm and light. No need to revisit why it didn't work out.

### Template 14: The Recruiter Note

**Subject:** [Your title], [number] years in [field]: [Job title]

~~~text
Hi [First name],

I saw you recruit for [team or function] at [Company], so I wanted to reach out about the [Job title] role [posting ID, if there is one].

I'm a [Your title] with [number] years in [field]. My most relevant win: [win with a number].

I've applied online. Would you be open to a 10-minute call to see if it's a fit? Happy to send my resume.

Thanks,
[Your name]
[Phone] | [LinkedIn URL]
~~~

**When to use it:** You can't find the hiring manager, or it's a large company where recruiters screen first. It's also a good second email alongside the one to the hiring manager.

**Make it yours:** Recruiters scan fast, so put the role and your title in the subject line. Keep salary out of this email. That conversation comes later.

### Template 15: The Back-to-Work Note

**Subject:** [Your title] ready for [type of role]

~~~text
Hi [First name],

After [a layoff / a career break for family / time off for health / a contract ending], I'm back in the market and focused on [type of role]. [Company]'s work on [specific thing] is a big reason I'm writing.

Before the break, I [win with a number] at [company]. Along the way, I've [kept skills current: a course, volunteer work, a project].

Would you be open to a 15-minute call about your team?

Thanks,
[Your name]
[LinkedIn URL]
~~~

**When to use it:** You're coming back after a gap, a layoff or a break, and you'd rather name it than hide it.

**Make it yours:** One sentence about the gap is enough. You don't owe anyone the details. Spend the rest of your words on what you've done and what you'll do for them.
