# Networking Without the Awkward

**18 easy ways to add value, 13 messages you can send today, icebreakers for events, and the follow-ups that turn a nice chat into a real connection.**

If the word "networking" makes you cringe, you're picturing the wrong thing. You're picturing name tags, forced small talk and someone handing you a business card while looking over your shoulder for someone more important.

Here's what I mean by networking: being useful to people in your field, and staying in touch. That's it.

Think farming, not hunting. You plant a lot of small seeds (a helpful link, a congrats, an introduction) and you keep watering them. Some grow into conversations, referrals and job offers months later. Some don't. Results vary. But if you do this steadily, your next job search won't start from zero.

**Time:** 10 minutes a day is plenty. **What you'll need:** Your LinkedIn feed, your email, and a short list of people you'd like to stay connected with.

---

## 18 ways to add value

You don't need to be senior or well connected to be useful. Here are 18 ways to give before you ask:

1. **Volunteer.** Offer to help with their event, project or community.
2. **Share industry news** that fits their work.
3. **Give thoughtful feedback** on something they wrote, built or presented.
4. **Celebrate their wins:** a new role, a work anniversary, a launch.
5. **Recommend a book** you think they'd enjoy.
6. **Pass along an event:** a webinar, workshop or meetup in their field.
7. **Offer to collaborate** on an article, a podcast episode or a side project.
8. **Make an introduction** to someone useful (ask both people first).
9. **Share what you learned** from a course, talk or conference.
10. **Compliment a specific skill** you've watched them use.
11. **Share your job search finds** (events, resources, openings) with others who are looking.
12. **Ask their take on a trend** in your field.
13. **Suggest a tool** that solves a problem they've mentioned.
14. **Share their post** with your own comment added.
15. **Point them to a mentor** in an area they want to grow in.
16. **Share a challenge you faced** and what helped you through it.
17. **Check in** when they're going through a hard time.
18. **Ask for their advice** on something they're great at. People like being asked.

## Value-first messages

### Template 1: Share Something Useful

**Subject:** Thought you'd like this: [topic]

~~~text
Hi [First name],

I just read [article or report] about [topic] and thought of you, given your work on [their area] at [Company].

[Link]

The part about [specific point] seemed especially relevant. No need to reply. I figured you'd find it useful.

[Your name]
~~~

**When to use it:** Any time you come across something that fits someone's work. It's the easiest seed to plant.

**Make it yours:** Say which part is relevant and why. That one line shows you thought about them, not just about sending something.

### Template 2: Celebrate Their Win

~~~text
Hi [First name],

Congrats on [new role, promotion, launch or anniversary]! [One specific line: what you've noticed about their work, or why it's well deserved.]

Wishing you a great start to the next chapter.

[Your name]
~~~

**When to use it:** LinkedIn tells you when people change jobs or hit work anniversaries. Use those moments.

**Make it yours:** Skip the generic "Congrats!" comment everyone else leaves. A short private message with one specific line stands out.

### Template 3: Pass Along an Event

~~~text
Hi [First name],

There's a [webinar, meetup or workshop] on [topic] on [date]: [Link]

Given your work on [their area], I thought you might like it. I'm planning to go, so if you do too, let's say hello.

[Your name]
~~~

**When to use it:** You've found an event that fits someone's interests.

**Make it yours:** If you're both going, suggest meeting up before or after. It turns an online connection into a real one.

### Template 4: Offer an Introduction

~~~text
Hi [First name],

You mentioned you're looking for [a designer / a mentor in X / advice on Y]. I know [Name], who [one line on why they're a good fit].

Would an introduction be helpful? If so, I'll check with [Name] first and then connect you both by email.

[Your name]
~~~

**When to use it:** Someone mentioned a need, and you know a person who could help.

**Make it yours:** Always ask both people before you introduce them. Nobody likes a surprise introduction they didn't agree to.

### Template 5: Thoughtful Feedback

~~~text
Hi [First name],

I read your [article, post or talk] on [topic] and loved the part about [specific section]. It changed how I think about [related thing].

One thought, in case it's useful: [one modest suggestion or question]. Either way, thanks for putting it out there.

[Your name]
~~~

**When to use it:** Someone shared their work publicly and you have a real, kind, useful reaction.

**Make it yours:** Lead with what you liked. Keep the suggestion small and offered lightly. If you don't have a suggestion, a question works just as well.

### Template 6: Ask for Their Expertise

~~~text
Hi [First name],

You're the person I think of when it comes to [their expertise], so I wanted to ask: [one specific question]?

Even a quick answer would help. Thank you!

[Your name]
~~~

**When to use it:** You have a real question that this person is well placed to answer.

**Make it yours:** One question, answerable in a few lines. "What's the best way to learn [tool] fast?" works. "Can you teach me [tool]?" doesn't.

### Template 7: Support After a Layoff

~~~text
Hi [First name],

I saw the news about the layoffs at [Company] and wanted to check in. No need to reply if you're not up for it.

If it would help to talk things through, brainstorm next steps or get an intro to anyone in my network, I'm here.

[Your name]
~~~

**When to use it:** Someone you know has been affected by layoffs or a tough change.

**Make it yours:** Skip "everything happens for a reason". Just offer something concrete. If they take you up on it, follow through fast.

## Reconnecting

### Template 8: The Honest Reconnection

**Subject:** Reconnecting after too long

~~~text
Hi [First name],

I'll be honest: I should have stayed in touch better. Life got busy, and I let a few good connections go quiet. You're one of them.

I'm in the middle of a career move, and it's reminded me whose perspective I value most. No ask here. I'd love to hear how you're doing, and grab a coffee or a call if you're up for it.

[Your name]
~~~

**When to use it:** Someone you used to know well, who you haven't spoken to in a year or more.

**Make it yours:** Owning the silence is disarming. Most people feel the same way about the connections they've let slide, so they'll be glad you went first.

### Template 9: The Value-First Reconnection

**Subject:** Thought of you: [topic]

~~~text
Hi [First name],

I came across [article, news or resource] about [topic] and immediately thought of you and your work on [their area]: [Link]

I'm exploring my next role and reconnecting with people I respect in [industry]. Would love to catch up if you have a few minutes sometime.

Either way, hope the link is useful.

[Your name]
~~~

**When to use it:** You want to reconnect, and you'd rather open with something useful than with an ask.

**Make it yours:** The link is your reason for writing. Make sure it really fits them.

### Template 10: The Keep-Warm Check-In

**Subject:** Quick update, and a link for you

~~~text
Hi [First name],

A quick update since we talked: [one sentence on your progress, ideally tied to their advice].

I also saw [article, event or news] and thought of our chat about [topic]: [Link]

Hope things are going well on your end. Let me know if I can help with anything.

[Your name]
~~~

**When to use it:** Every 4 to 8 weeks with people who've helped you or who you'd like to stay close to.

**Make it yours:** Add a "keep warm" date to your tracker for each person. When the date comes up, send one useful thing and one line of news.

## At events

### Icebreakers that don't feel forced

Pick two or three that suit you and keep them in your back pocket.

**Getting started**

- "What brought you here today?"
- "What are you hoping to take away from this?"
- "Is this your first time at this event? Any tips on what not to miss?"
- "Hi, I'm [Name]. I'm here because I'm really into [topic]. What about you?"

**About the event**

- "What did you think of the keynote? The point about [topic] got me thinking."
- "Have you been to any good sessions so far? I'm trying to pick my next one."
- "I just came from [session], and it was great. Did you catch it?"
- "I liked your question in [session]. What made you ask it?"
- "Has anything surprised you so far today?"

**About their work**

- "What are you working on these days that you're excited about?"
- "What's a trend in [field] you're paying attention to?"
- "How did you get into [their field]?"

**Easy openers**

- "Did you travel far to get here?"
- "Do you know any good places to eat around here?"
- "What's been the highlight of your week?"

A few more tips:

- **Ask a follow-up question** before you talk about yourself. Curiosity is the whole trick.
- **Write one note** on your phone right after each conversation: their name, what you talked about, anything you promised.
- **Exit kindly:** "I don't want to keep you all night. Can we connect on LinkedIn?"

### Template 11: The After-Event Email

**Subject:** Great meeting you at [event]

~~~text
Hi [First name],

It was great meeting you at [event] [yesterday / on day]. I enjoyed our chat about [specific topic], especially your point about [what they said].

I'd love to keep the conversation going. Would you be open to a 15-minute call in the next couple of weeks?

[Your name]
[LinkedIn URL]
~~~

**When to use it:** Within 24 to 48 hours of meeting someone at an event.

**Make it yours:** Use the note you wrote on your phone. Then send a connection request too (the Event Request in the LinkedIn Requests and Messages guide).

### Template 12: The Promise Keeper

**Subject:** The [resource] I mentioned at [event]

~~~text
Hi [First name],

At [event], I mentioned [the article, tool, contact or resource] when we were talking about [topic]. Here it is: [Link or details]

Hope it helps with [their project or problem]. It was great meeting you.

[Your name]
~~~

**When to use it:** You told someone you'd send them something. Send it within a day.

**Make it yours:** Keeping a small promise fast is one of the best first impressions you can make. It tells people you do what you say.

## Telling your network you're looking

### Template 13: The Soft "I'm Looking" Post

~~~text
After [number] years in [field], I'm looking for my next role in [target area].

I'm most interested in [specific focus: type of company, team or problem]. The work I'm proudest of: [one win with a number].

If you know someone I should talk to, I'd be grateful for an introduction. And if I can ever help you, just ask.
~~~

**When to use it:** A LinkedIn post, or a post in an alumni or professional group once you've been active there for a couple of weeks.

**Make it yours:** Be specific. "Anything in marketing" gives people nothing to work with. "Content lead roles at B2B software companies, remote or in Calgary" gives them something to act on. If you're still employed, check your comfort level before posting publicly.
