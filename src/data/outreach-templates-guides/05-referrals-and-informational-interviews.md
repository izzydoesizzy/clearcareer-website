# Referrals and Informational Interviews

**13 templates for asking for advice, running the conversation, and turning it into introductions and referrals.**

Here's the softest, most human way into a company: ask someone for advice.

An informational interview is a short call where you learn about someone's work, their team and how they got there. You're not asking for a job. You're asking for 15 minutes of perspective. That's a much easier yes.

And here's the funny part. These conversations often turn into introductions and referrals anyway, because people help people they've met and liked. I've seen it happen a lot. Results vary, but it beats sending your resume into the void.

**Time:** 10 minutes to write each email. 15 to 20 minutes per call. **What you'll need:** Your target company list, your LinkedIn, and two or three wins you can mention in a sentence each.

---

## The rules

1. **Give before you ask.** A congrats, a useful link, a kind word about their work.
2. **Make the ask specific.** "Can you help me?" is stressful to answer. "Could I ask you three questions about moving from agency work to in-house?" is easy.
3. **Respect the time.** If you asked for 15 minutes, wrap up at 15. They can choose to keep going. You don't.
4. **Don't ask for a job.** Ask for insight, advice or a name. If there's a job, they'll mention it.
5. **Thank them within 24 hours.** (See the Thank-You Emails guide.)
6. **Earn the referral.** Ask for one after a real conversation, not in a first message to a stranger.

---

## Asking for an informational interview

### Template 1: The Career Path Ask

**Subject:** 15 minutes on your move into [field]?

~~~text
Hi [First name],

I'm a [Your title] exploring a move into [field], and your path from [their previous role] to [their current role] at [Company] is exactly the kind I'm trying to learn from.

I have two or three focused questions about how you made the jump. Would you have 15 minutes for a call in the next couple of weeks?

Happy to work around your schedule. Thanks either way.

[Your name]
[LinkedIn URL]
~~~

**When to use it:** Someone has made the exact move you want to make.

**Make it yours:** Look at their LinkedIn experience section. Name the specific move ("from teaching into instructional design") so they know you looked.

### Template 2: The Alumni Directory Email

**Subject:** Fellow [School] alum: a quick question about [Company]

~~~text
Hi [First name],

I'm a fellow [School] grad ([Program], [Year]) and found you through the alumni directory.

I'm exploring [type of role], and your work as [their title] at [Company] caught my attention. I'd love to hear what the work is really like, especially [specific interest].

Would you have 15 minutes for a call or a virtual coffee? I know alumni requests pile up, so thank you for considering it.

[Your name]
[LinkedIn URL]
~~~

**When to use it:** You found the person through your school's alumni directory or alumni network.

**Make it yours:** Most schools have an online alumni directory or a career services office that can help. A shared program, club or professor makes the opener even warmer.

### Template 3: The Coffee Invite (someone you know)

~~~text
Hi [First name],

It's been too long! I'm in the middle of a career move and would love your take on [specific topic]. You've always had a sharp eye for [their strength].

Could I buy you a coffee [this week or next]? I'll come to your neighbourhood. Thirty minutes, max.

[Your name]
~~~

**When to use it:** A former colleague, old friend or someone you know well enough to invite for coffee.

**Make it yours:** Offer to go to them, and pick up the bill. Small gestures make it easy to say yes.

### Template 4: The Coffee Invite (someone you've met once)

~~~text
Hi [First name],

We met at [event, or through Mutual contact], and I really enjoyed our chat about [topic].

I'm exploring [type of role], and your perspective on [their area] would help a lot. Could I buy you a coffee (or set up a quick video call) in the next couple of weeks?

Totally understand if the timing doesn't work.

[Your name]
~~~

**When to use it:** You've met once, at an event or through a friend, and want to turn it into a real conversation.

**Make it yours:** Send it within a week or two of meeting, while you're still fresh in their memory. Mention one detail from your chat.

## During the conversation

These are spoken scripts. You don't need to say them word for word. Use them as a guide so you're never stuck on what to say next.

### Template 5: The Opening

~~~text
"Thanks so much for making the time. I'll keep us to 15 minutes.

I have three questions:
1. What does a typical week look like in your role?
2. What skills or experiences helped you most in getting here?
3. If you were in my shoes, [one line on your situation], what would you focus on?

And if anyone else comes to mind who I should talk to, I'd love to hear."
~~~

**When to use it:** The first minute of an informational interview or coffee chat.

**Make it yours:** Swap in better questions if you have them. Never ask anything you could find on their website. Then listen. Aim to listen far more than you talk.

### Template 6: The Close

~~~text
"This has been so helpful. Thank you.

Two quick things before I let you go:
1. Is there anything I can help you with? I'd love to return the favour.
2. Is there anyone else you'd suggest I talk to as I look at [field or company]?

I'll send you a short note with [the thing you promised]. Thanks again."
~~~

**When to use it:** When you're close to the time you asked for.

**Make it yours:** If they offer an introduction, say yes and thank them. Then make it easy: send them the blurb from Template 7 the same day.

## Asking for introductions and referrals

### Template 7: The Intro Request (with a blurb they can forward)

**Subject:** Quick favour: an intro to [Target person]?

~~~text
Hi [First name],

I noticed you're connected to [Target person], who leads [team] at [Company]. I'm interested in [type of role] there. Would you be comfortable introducing us?

Here's a short blurb you can forward, to make it easy:

"[Your name] is a [Your title] with [number] years in [field]. Most recently, [he/she/they] [win with a number]. [He/She/They] would love 15 minutes to learn about your team. Email: [Your email]"

No pressure at all if it doesn't feel right. I appreciate you either way.

[Your name]
~~~

**When to use it:** Someone you know is connected to someone you want to meet.

**Make it yours:** The blurb is the most important part. It saves your contact from writing anything, so they're far more likely to send it. Keep it to three sentences.

### Template 8: The Check-First Intro Request

~~~text
Hi [First name],

Would you be open to introducing me to [Target person]? I'm interested in [specific reason], and I think a short conversation would help me a lot.

If you'd rather check with [him/her/them] first before passing my details along, that's perfect. I want to respect your relationship and their time.

Thanks for considering it.

[Your name]
~~~

**When to use it:** Your contact knows the person well and might want to ask them before making an introduction (people call this a "double opt-in").

**Make it yours:** Offering the check-first option shows respect for both people. It's especially good with senior contacts.

### Template 9: The Direct Referral Ask

**Subject:** Would you be comfortable referring me?

~~~text
Hi [First name],

I saw [Company] has a [Job title] opening ([link]), and I think it's a strong fit for my work in [area].

Would you be comfortable referring me? If your company has a referral link, I'll wait and apply through that instead.

To make it easy, here's a one-line summary you can paste: "[Your name] is a [Your title] who [win with a number]."

No pressure. I appreciate you either way.

[Your name]
~~~

**When to use it:** A friend, former colleague or close contact works at the company.

**Make it yours:** Many companies have an internal referral system, and some want you to apply through the employee's link rather than the careers page. Ask first so you don't apply twice.

### Template 10: The Soft Referral Ask

~~~text
Hi [First name],

Hope you're doing well. I'm exploring [type of role] roles, and I remembered you know a lot of people in [industry or company].

Is there anyone you think I should talk to? Even one name would help, and I won't mention you unless you're comfortable with it.

Thanks so much,
[Your name]
~~~

**When to use it:** An acquaintance who knows people in your target field, but whom you don't know well enough to ask for a formal referral.

**Make it yours:** Asking for "a name" is much easier to say yes to than asking for "a referral". If they give you one, use the Name Drop template in the Hiring Manager Cold Emails guide.

### Template 11: The Advice-to-Referral Follow-Up

**Subject:** Thank you, and a question about [Job title]

~~~text
Hi [First name],

Thanks again for our chat last [day or week]. What you said about [specific thing] has stuck with me, and I'm even more excited about [Company].

I just saw the [Job title] opening on your careers page. Based on what you shared, it looks like a strong fit. Would you be comfortable referring me through your internal system? Happy to send anything that helps.

Thanks for all your help so far.

[Your name]
~~~

**When to use it:** You've already had an informational interview with someone at the company, and a matching role has opened.

**Make it yours:** Tie it back to something they told you. It shows you listened, and it reminds them why they liked talking to you.

### Template 12: The Former Colleague Ask

**Subject:** Long overdue hello, and a quick ask

~~~text
Hi [First name],

It's been a while! I saw [their recent update] and wanted to say congrats.

I'm looking for my next [type of role] role. Since we worked together on [project] at [Former company], I wondered if you'd be willing to be a reference, and whether you know anyone hiring for [type of role] right now.

No pressure at all. I'd love to catch up either way.

[Your name]
~~~

**When to use it:** Someone you worked with who can speak to your work.

**Make it yours:** Remind them of a specific project you did together. It helps them remember your work, and it gives them something concrete to say if they're asked.

### Template 13: The Report-Back

**Subject:** Update: my chat with [Name]

~~~text
Hi [First name],

Quick update: I talked with [Name] on [day], and it was great. [One line on what came of it: next steps, advice, an interview].

Thank you again for making the connection. I'll let you know how it turns out, and if I can ever return the favour, just say the word.

[Your name]
~~~

**When to use it:** A week or two after someone introduced or referred you, once something has happened.

**Make it yours:** People love knowing their help mattered. Send a second update when there's real news: an interview, an offer, or even a "no" with good advice attached.
