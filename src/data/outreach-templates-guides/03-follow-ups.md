# Follow-Ups

**When to follow up, what to say, and when to stop. Plus 13 templates for every stage, from a cold email to a final round.**

Most people send one email, hear nothing, and decide outreach doesn't work.

Here's the thing: silence usually means busy, not no. Your email landed between a budget meeting and a fire drill, and it slid down the inbox. A short, friendly follow-up brings it back to the top.

The trick is timing, tone and knowing when to stop. Follow up too fast and you look pushy. Never follow up and your first email was mostly wasted. This guide gives you the cadence I recommend and a template for each moment.

**Time:** 5 minutes per follow-up. **What you'll need:** Your tracker (with the date you sent each email) and your calendar.

---

## The cadence

For a cold email to a hiring manager or contact:

1. **Day 0:** Send your first email.
2. **4 to 6 business days later:** Follow-up 1. A short bump in the same thread.
3. **About a week after that:** Follow-up 2. Add something useful, offer an easier yes, or close the loop.
4. **Then stop.** Three touches in total. If you still want in, try a different person at the same company.

For everything else:

| Situation | When to follow up | How many |
|---|---|---|
| Cold email, no reply | 4 to 6 business days, then about a week later | 2, then stop |
| Applied online, no reply | 7 to 10 business days | 1 |
| After an interview | Thank-you within 24 hours (see the Thank-You Emails guide) | 1 thank-you |
| They gave you a date and it passed | 2 business days after their date | 1, then 1 more a week later |
| After a recruiter call | Same day if they asked for something; 5 business days if it goes quiet | 1 or 2 |
| A networking contact | Every 4 to 8 weeks, with something useful | Ongoing |

---

## Follow-up rules

1. **Reply in the same thread.** They see your original email right below, with no need to search for it.
2. **Make each one shorter than the last.** A follow-up is a nudge, not a repeat.
3. **Add something new when you can.** News, an article, a progress update, a smaller ask.
4. **Skip the guilt.** No "I haven't heard back", no "just checking in", no "per my last email".
5. **Respect their timeline.** If they said "by Friday", wait until Tuesday.
6. **Stop after the third touch.** And stop right away if anyone asks you to.
7. **Log every follow-up** in your tracker, with the next date.

## After a cold email

The simplest first follow-up is the Quick Bump (Template 3 in Land an Interview in 1 Week). These five give you more options.

### Template 1: The Value-Add Follow-Up

**Subject:** Reply in the same thread

~~~text
Hi [First name],

I came across [article, report or news] about [topic] and thought of your team, especially [one line on why it's relevant to them].

[Link]

I'm still keen to chat for 15 minutes about [original topic] if the timing works. Either way, I hope it's useful.

Thanks,
[Your name]
~~~

**When to use it:** As your second follow-up, or as your first if you've found something good to share.

**Make it yours:** Pick something from the last few weeks that ties to their actual work: an industry report, a competitor's move, a talk from a conference in their field. Read it before you send it.

### Template 2: The Progress Update

**Subject:** Reply in the same thread

~~~text
Hi [First name],

A quick update since my last note: I [finished a certification / shipped a project / wrote a short piece] on [topic], which ties into [part of their team's work].

I'd still love 15 minutes to learn more about [team or role]. Does [day] or [day] work?

Thanks,
[Your name]
~~~

**When to use it:** You've done something new and relevant since your first email.

**Make it yours:** The update has to be real and recent. If you don't have one, use the Value-Add Follow-Up instead.

### Template 3: The Easier Yes

**Subject:** Reply in the same thread

~~~text
Hi [First name],

I know a call is a big ask in a busy week. Would it be easier if I sent two quick questions by email instead?

1. [Question one]
2. [Question two]

A few lines back would help a lot. Thanks either way.

[Your name]
~~~

**When to use it:** The person is clearly senior or swamped, and a call might be too much to ask.

**Make it yours:** Ask questions they can answer in a sentence each, like "What's the biggest skill gap on your team right now?" Not "Tell me about your career."

### Template 4: The Graceful Close

**Subject:** Reply in the same thread

~~~text
Hi [First name],

I'll keep this brief. If now isn't the right time, I completely understand, and this is my last note on it.

If things change down the road, I'd love to reconnect. Wishing you and the team a great [month or quarter].

Thanks,
[Your name]
[LinkedIn URL]
~~~

**When to use it:** Your third and final touch. It closes the loop politely and leaves the door open.

**Make it yours:** Mean it. After this one, stop. Some people reply to this email because it takes the pressure off. If they don't, update your tracker to "Closed" and move on.

### Template 5: The LinkedIn Nudge

~~~text
Hi [First name], I sent you a short email last week about [topic or role] and wanted to make sure it didn't get lost. Happy to chat here if that's easier. Thanks!
~~~

**When to use it:** Your email has gone quiet and you're connected on LinkedIn (or you can send a message there).

**Make it yours:** Don't paste the whole email again. Point to it and offer LinkedIn as the easier channel. Some people live in their inbox and some live on LinkedIn.

## After you apply

### Template 6: The Application Check-In

**Subject:** [Job title] application: [Your name]

~~~text
Hi [First name],

I applied for the [Job title] role on [date] and wanted to follow up. I'm especially interested because [one specific reason tied to the team or role].

In my last role, I [win with a number that maps to the posting].

Is there anything else I can send to help with your review?

Thanks,
[Your name]
[LinkedIn URL]
~~~

**When to use it:** 7 to 10 business days after you apply, once you've found the hiring manager or recruiter.

**Make it yours:** Add one detail that wasn't in your resume or cover letter, so the email gives them something new.

### Template 7: The Referred Application Check-In

**Subject:** [Job title] application (referred by [Referrer's name])

~~~text
Hi [First name],

I applied for the [Job title] role on [date] after [Referrer's name] encouraged me to. I wanted to follow up and say hello directly.

[Referrer's first name] and I worked together on [project or team], and [he/she/they] can speak to my work on [relevant area].

Would you have 15 minutes to talk about the role?

Thanks,
[Your name]
[LinkedIn URL]
~~~

**When to use it:** Someone inside the company referred you, and a few days have passed since you applied.

**Make it yours:** Don't copy your referrer on this email. It puts them on the spot. Tell them separately that you followed up, and thank them again.

## After interviews and recruiter calls

### Template 8: After a Recruiter Call

**Subject:** Following up: [Job title] call

~~~text
Hi [First name],

Thanks for the call on [day]. The [Job title] role sounds like a strong fit for my work in [area].

As promised, I've attached [updated resume / references / portfolio link].

What are the next steps from here? I'm flexible on timing.

Thanks,
[Your name]
~~~

**When to use it:** The same day as a recruiter screen, especially if they asked you for something.

**Make it yours:** This is the one follow-up where an attachment belongs, because they asked for it. Name the file clearly: "Firstname-Lastname-Resume.pdf".

### Template 9: The Recruiter Went Quiet

**Subject:** Quick check-in: [Job title]

~~~text
Hi [First name],

Checking in on the [Job title] role we talked about on [date]. I know things get busy.

I'm still very interested. If the timeline has shifted or the role is on hold, a quick update would help me plan.

Thanks,
[Your name]
~~~

**When to use it:** About 5 business days after a recruiter call with no word on next steps.

**Make it yours:** "A quick update would help me plan" is a polite way of asking for a yes or no. Keep it friendly. Recruiters are often waiting on someone else too.

### Template 10: Their Date Passed

**Subject:** Checking in: [Job title]

~~~text
Hi [First name],

You mentioned I'd hear back by [date], so I wanted to check in. I know hiring timelines move.

I'm still excited about the [Job title] role, and I'm happy to send references or anything else that helps.

Thanks,
[Your name]
~~~

**When to use it:** Two business days after the date they gave you.

**Make it yours:** Use their exact words if you can ("you said end of next week"). It's a reminder, not an accusation.

### Template 11: After the Final Round

**Subject:** Following up: [Job title] final round

~~~text
Hi [First name],

I wanted to follow up on our final-round conversation on [date]. I've kept thinking about [specific topic from the interview], and I'm even more excited about the role.

Is there anything else I can share to help with your decision? I'm happy to connect with anyone else on the team.

Thanks,
[Your name]
~~~

**When to use it:** About a week after your final interview, if you haven't heard anything (and after you've sent your thank-you).

**Make it yours:** Pick a topic from the interview that you've had a new thought about. If you have one, add a single line: "One more idea on [topic]: [idea]."

### Template 12: The Competing Offer Nudge (only if it's true)

**Subject:** Timing question: [Job title]

~~~text
Hi [First name],

I have an offer from another company with a deadline of [date]. [Company] is still my first choice, so I wanted to let you know before I decide.

Is there any chance of an update on your timeline before then? I understand if not.

Thanks,
[Your name]
~~~

**When to use it:** You have a real offer in hand, with a real deadline, and you'd rather work at this company.

**Make it yours:** Never bluff. If you don't have an offer, don't send this. You don't need to name the other company. Keep it calm and factual, and only call them your first choice if they are.

## Closing the loop

### Template 13: The Last Note to a Recruiter

**Subject:** One last check-in: [Job title]

~~~text
Hi [First name],

Reaching out one final time about the [Job title] role. If it's been filled or paused, no hard feelings at all.

If something else comes up that fits my background in [area], I'd love to hear about it. Thanks for your time through all of this.

All the best,
[Your name]
~~~

**When to use it:** You've followed up and heard nothing. This ends things on a good note.

**Make it yours:** Recruiters fill lots of roles. A gracious last note makes you someone they're happy to call next time.

---

## The one you never send

Don't write the angry follow-up. Don't tell them their process is unprofessional, demand an explanation or post about it on LinkedIn. I know it's tempting. I've felt it too.

Instead, mark it "Closed" in your tracker, take a breath, and send your next email to a new company. That energy is better spent on the next opportunity.
