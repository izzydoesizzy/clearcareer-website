# LinkedIn Requests and Messages

**14 short notes for connecting with hiring managers, alumni, recruiters and peers, and what to say once they accept.**

LinkedIn is where you find the people. Email is usually where you reach them. Results vary, but I like using both together: email the hiring manager, then send a connection request a day or two later. Your name shows up in two places, and you haven't been pushy in either.

LinkedIn also works on its own, especially with alumni, people who post a lot, and anyone whose email you can't find.

**Time:** 2 to 5 minutes per note. **What you'll need:** A LinkedIn profile with a clear photo and headline, and the person's profile open in another tab.

---

## A note on LinkedIn's limits

LinkedIn caps how long a connection note can be (200 characters on free accounts as I write this, a bit more on Premium), and free accounts only get a handful of personalized notes each month. LinkedIn changes these limits from time to time.

So:

- **Keep every connection note under 200 characters.** All the requests below fit if you keep your brackets short.
- **Save your notes for the people who matter most:** hiring managers, people at your target companies, people you met.
- **Never pitch in a connection note.** The note gets you connected. The message after gets you the conversation.

The formula for a good note: **something you share** + **why them** + **a simple ask** (usually just "would love to connect").

## Connection requests

### Template 1: The Hiring Manager Request

~~~text
Hi [First name], I applied for the [Job title] role on your team and sent you a short email. Would love to connect here too. Thanks!
~~~

**When to use it:** A day or two after you email a hiring manager.

**Make it yours:** Only mention the email if you actually sent one. If you didn't, swap in: "I'm interested in the [Job title] role on your team."

### Template 2: The Post Request

~~~text
Hi [First name], your post on [topic] stuck with me, especially [one short point]. I work in [field] and would love to follow your thinking. Thanks!
~~~

**When to use it:** The person posts on LinkedIn, and you read something of theirs you liked.

**Make it yours:** Name the point in a few words. "Your post on onboarding" is fine. "Your point that onboarding starts before day one" is better.

### Template 3: The Event Request

~~~text
Hi [First name], great meeting you at [event]. I enjoyed our chat about [topic]. Would love to stay in touch here.
~~~

**When to use it:** Within a day or two of meeting someone in person or at an online event.

**Make it yours:** If you only watched them speak, say so: "I enjoyed your talk at [event] on [topic]." Don't imply you met if you didn't.

### Template 4: The School Alum

~~~text
Hi [First name], fellow [School] grad here! I'm exploring [field] roles and your path to [Company] caught my eye. Would love to connect.
~~~

**When to use it:** You went to the same college or university (or the same program, bootcamp or certificate).

**Make it yours:** Find alumni by going to your school's LinkedIn page, clicking **Alumni**, and filtering by company or job title. If you shared a program, mention it. That's an even closer link.

### Template 5: The Former-Employer Alum

~~~text
Hi [First name], I see we both worked at [Former company]. I'm looking at roles in [field] and would love to connect with a fellow alum.
~~~

**When to use it:** You both worked at the same company, even at different times.

**Make it yours:** If you overlapped, add the team or year: "I was on the [team] team in [year]."

### Template 6: The Shared Connection (only if it's true)

~~~text
Hi [First name], [Mutual contact] suggested I reach out. I'm a [Your title] interested in [team or field] at [Company]. Would love to connect.
~~~

**When to use it:** A real mutual contact told you to reach out to this person.

**Make it yours:** Only use this if your contact actually suggested it and is okay with you saying so. Having someone in common on LinkedIn isn't the same as a suggestion.

### Template 7: The Recruiter Request

~~~text
Hi [First name], I see you recruit for [function] roles. I'm a [Your title] with [number] years in [field], open to new roles. Would love to be on your radar.
~~~

**When to use it:** A recruiter (internal or agency) who fills the kinds of roles you want.

**Make it yours:** Check their profile or recent posts to see which roles they actually fill. Mention that area.

### Template 8: The Peer Request

~~~text
Hi [First name], I'm a [Your title] looking at roles like yours at [Company]. I'd love to connect and learn from your experience.
~~~

**When to use it:** Someone already doing the job you want, at a company you're targeting.

**Make it yours:** Peers are often the best source of honest information about a team. Connect first. Ask your questions in a message after they accept (see Template 11).

## Messages after they accept

Send these within a few days of the person accepting your request, while your name is still fresh.

### Template 9: Thanks for Connecting

~~~text
Hi [First name],

Thanks for connecting! I'm reaching out because [specific reason tied to them or their team].

Quick background: I [win with a number] at [Current or last company].

I'm curious about [one specific question about their team or work]. Would you be open to a 15-minute call?

Thanks,
[Your name]
~~~

**When to use it:** The all-purpose first message to almost anyone you've just connected with.

**Make it yours:** Keep it to 3 to 5 short sentences. Make the question something only they could answer, not something you could look up.

### Template 10: The Hiring Manager Message

~~~text
Hi [First name],

Thanks for connecting. I applied for the [Job title] role on your team and wanted to say hello here too.

I bring [number] years in [field]. Most recently, I [win with a number], which looks close to what the posting describes.

If you're open to it, I'd love 15 minutes to hear what you need most in this hire.

Thanks,
[Your name]
~~~

**When to use it:** A hiring manager has accepted your request and you haven't been able to reach them by email.

**Make it yours:** If you already emailed them, don't repeat the email. Send a shorter note instead: "Thanks for connecting! I sent a quick email on [day] about the [Job title] role. Happy to chat here if that's easier."

### Template 11: The Inside Look

~~~text
Hi [First name],

Thanks for connecting! I saw [Company] has an opening for [Job title], and since you're on [team], I'd love your take.

What's the team like, and what makes someone do well there? Even a few lines would help me decide how to approach my application.

Thanks,
[Your name]
~~~

**When to use it:** A peer or teammate at a company where you're applying.

**Make it yours:** Don't ask for a referral in this first message. Let the conversation happen first. If it goes well, the Referrals and Informational Interviews guide has the next step.

### Template 12: The Light Touch

~~~text
Hi [First name],

Thanks for connecting. I've enjoyed your posts on [topic], especially the one about [specific post].

No ask today. I'm exploring roles in [field] and connecting with people whose work I respect. Looking forward to following along.

[Your name]
~~~

**When to use it:** You don't have a specific ask yet, but you want to start a relationship on a warm note.

**Make it yours:** Follow it up by commenting on their next post (Template 14). In a few weeks, you can send a real ask, and it won't feel cold.

### Template 13: The Recruiter Message

~~~text
Hi [First name],

Thanks for connecting! I'm a [Your title] with [number] years in [field], looking for [type of role] in [location or remote].

A quick snapshot: [win with a number].

If anything comes across your desk that fits, I'd love to hear about it. Happy to send my resume.

Thanks,
[Your name]
~~~

**When to use it:** A recruiter has accepted your connection request.

**Make it yours:** Make it easy for them to match you: title, years, location or remote, and one win. That's what they search for.

## Before you connect

### Template 14: The Warm-Up Comment

~~~text
[Specific point from their post] is something I've seen too. At [Current or last company], we [what you did or learned]. Have you found the same with [related question]?
~~~

**When to use it:** A week or two before you message someone who posts regularly. Comment on two or three of their posts first.

**Make it yours:** Never "Great post!" or a clapping emoji. Add a real point or a real question. It's the difference between a name they scroll past and a name they recognize when your message arrives.

---

## A simple LinkedIn cadence

1. **Day 1:** Send the connection request with a note.
2. **Within a few days of them accepting:** Send a message (Templates 9 to 13).
3. **About a week later, if there's no reply:** One short follow-up. "Hi [First name], just floating this back up. Happy to chat whenever works."
4. **Then stop on LinkedIn.** Try email instead, or move on.
