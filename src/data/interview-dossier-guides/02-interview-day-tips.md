# Interview Day Tips

**My practical checklist for before, during and after the interview, including video calls and body language.**

> **Results vary.** These tips help you show up calmer and clearer. They can't guarantee an outcome.

**Time:** 10 minutes to read. Open it again the day before and the morning of.

---

Your dossier gets your content ready. This guide gets YOU ready.

The best answer in the world won't land if your mic cuts out, the link is buried in your inbox, or you spend the first five minutes rushing to catch your breath. None of this is complicated. It's just easy to skip when you're nervous. So let's not skip it.

---

## Before the Interview

### Find out the format
Ask the recruiter: "Can you tell me a bit about the format? Is it a panel, a one-on-one, behavioural, or case-based?" The answer tells you which stories to prepare and how much time you'll have. Recruiters expect this question.

### Confirm the logistics
- The date, the time and the time zone (especially if the company is in another province, state or country)
- The platform (Zoom, Teams or Google Meet) or the address
- Who you're meeting, with names and job titles

Save the link or address somewhere you can find it in five seconds, not five minutes.

### Set up your space
Pick a quiet, well-lit spot. Light in front of you, not behind you. Test your camera, microphone and internet the day before, on the same platform you'll use. Run any updates the night before, not five minutes before.

### Dress one level up
Match the company's dress code or go slightly above it. If you're not sure, business casual works for most interviews. Solid colours look better on camera than busy patterns.

### Keep the job post handy
Have it printed or open in a tab you can find. When you can point to a specific requirement ("You mentioned you need someone who can..."), you sound like someone who did the homework. Because you did.

## During the Interview

### Pause before you answer
Take 2 to 3 seconds to collect your thoughts. It feels like forever to you. To them, it sounds thoughtful. Silence isn't awkward. Rambling is.

### Slow down
Most of us speed up when we're nervous. If you hear yourself rushing, stop, take a breath and start the next sentence slower.

### Use STAR for behavioural questions
When you hear "Tell me about a time when...", go Situation, Task, Action, Result. Your story cards in Section 7 are already in that shape. It keeps you on track and stops the rambling.

### Aim for 60 to 90 seconds per answer
If you pass 2 minutes, wrap it up. You can always offer more: "Would you like me to go deeper on any part of that?" Let them pull. Don't push.

### Match their energy
If they're relaxed and chatty, you can relax too. If they're formal, stay professional. Mirroring their pace makes the conversation feel easier for both of you.

### Show, don't tell
"I'm a great communicator" is a claim. A story about the time you explained a messy problem to a room of people who didn't want to hear it is proof. Proof wins every time.

### It's okay not to know
If you don't know, say so. Then show how you think: "I haven't used that tool, but here's how I'd get up to speed." That's a strong answer. A made-up answer isn't.

## Video Interviews

- **Camera at eye level.** Stack your laptop on books if you need to. Looking down into the camera reads as distracted.
- **Look at the lens when you talk.** That's what eye contact looks like on their screen. When they're talking, you can look at their face.
- **Close everything else.** Shut other tabs and apps, turn off notifications, and put your phone on silent, face down. One ping mid-answer can knock you off your story.
- **Keep the background simple.** A clean wall, a bookshelf or a plain virtual background.
- **Keep water nearby.** A dry throat mid-answer is no fun.
- **Wear headphones.** They cut echo and make you easier to hear. Small wired earbuds look tidier on camera than big headphones.

---

## Phone Interviews

- **Sit up straight or stand.** You'll sound more awake.
- **Keep your dossier open, but don't read from it.** Reading sounds like reading.
- **Smile when you talk.** It changes your tone.
- **Leave a beat before you answer.** They can't see you thinking, so if you need longer, say "Good question, let me think about that for a second."

---

## In-Person Interviews

- **Plan your route.** Aim to arrive 10 to 15 minutes early, and walk in about 5 minutes before your time.
- **Bring the basics.** 2 to 3 printed copies of your resume, a notebook and a pen.
- **Be friendly to everyone,** from the front desk on.
- **Greet each interviewer by name,** and offer a handshake if it feels natural.

---

## Body Language

- **Sit up, but stay relaxed.** Lean in a little to show you're engaged. Don't slouch or lean way back.
- **Use your hands.** Natural gestures (visible on camera) help you look confident and more like yourself.
- **Smile when it fits.** Especially at hello and when the conversation is light. A real smile builds rapport faster than any talking point.
- **Nod while you listen.** Small nods show you're following and thinking about what they said.

---

## After the Interview

### Send a thank-you email within 24 hours
Mention something specific from the conversation, so it couldn't have been sent to anyone else. Keep it to 3 to 5 sentences. Send one to each person you met. Don't skip this one.

### Debrief while it's fresh
Within a few hours, write down the questions they asked, how you answered and what you wish you'd said. Then ask the dossier skill to update your dossier for the next round. Guide 01 shows you how.

### Follow up once
If the date they gave you passes, send one short, polite check-in email. Just one. Then put your energy back into the rest of your search.

---

## The 10-Minute Checklist

Ten minutes before you start, run through this:

- [ ] Link or address open, and you know exactly where you're going
- [ ] Camera, mic and headphones tested (video), or phone charged with a strong signal (phone)
- [ ] Phone on silent, notifications off, other tabs closed
- [ ] Water within reach
- [ ] Job post open or printed
- [ ] Your three salary numbers on a sticky note, out of sight of the camera
- [ ] Your top 3 story titles and your 3 to 4 questions to ask on one page
- [ ] "Tell me about yourself" said out loud once
- [ ] Bathroom break done
- [ ] Three slow breaths. In for 4, hold for 4, out for 4.
