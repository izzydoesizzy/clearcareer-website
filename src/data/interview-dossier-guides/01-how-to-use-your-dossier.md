# How to Use Your Dossier

**How to run your Interview Prep Dossier, a 3-day plan to prepare with it, how to rehearse, and how to keep it fresh between rounds.**

> **Results vary.** A dossier helps you walk in prepared and calmer. It can't guarantee an offer. Hiring depends on a lot you can't control.

**Time:** 15 minutes to read. Plan for about an hour to build and check your first dossier.
**What you'll need:** The job post, your resume, the interview stage and date, and the names of the people you're meeting (if you know them).

---

When someone I coach lands an interview for a job they really want, I build them a prep pack for that one job. The company. The people they're meeting. The doubts the hiring manager might have about them, and their answers. Their stories. Their questions. It usually runs 15 to 20 pages.

This is that pack, turned into a Claude skill you run yourself. Same sections, same order, same rule I follow: nothing made up. Every fact about you comes from your resume or from you. Every fact about the company comes with a source you can click.

Here's how to get the most out of it.

It starts with a cover page and a one-page "How to use this dossier", and it ends with every source in one list.

## How to Run It

### Step 1: Gather five things

1. **The job post.** A link works, but pasted text is safer. LinkedIn and some job boards need a login, so Claude can't always open the link.
2. **Your resume.**
3. **The company name,** if the post doesn't say (agency posts often hide it).
4. **The interview stage and date.** Recruiter screen, hiring manager, panel, final.
5. **Who you're meeting,** if you know. Check the calendar invite or the recruiter's email.

Bonus if you have them: your salary numbers, what matters most to you in your next job, and your story bank.

### Step 2: Ask for it

With the skill installed, type something like:

> "Build my interview dossier. Here's the job post and my resume. It's a hiring manager interview on Thursday at 2 p.m. with [Name], [Their job title]."

Claude asks for anything missing in one message. Then it builds your dossier in four parts:

- **Part 1:** the cover, How to Use, and Sections 1 to 3 (the research)
- **Part 2:** Sections 4 to 6 (your edge, the objections, your playbook)
- **Part 3:** Sections 7 to 10 (stories, questions to ask, processes, strategy)
- **Part 4:** Sections 11 and 12, plus all the sources

Reply "next" to move from one part to the next.

### Step 3: Check the research before you go on

This is the step people skip. Please don't. After Part 1:

- **Click the links.** Does the page say what the dossier says?
- **Check the dates.** Anything marked "(older than 12 months)" might have changed.
- **Check the people.** Right person, right company, right job? If not, tell Claude and it will fix it. It only lists people you name, or people the company publicly ties to the role. It never guesses.
- **Take reviews with a grain of salt.** Review sites only hear from people who chose to post.

If Claude can't search the web where you're using it, it tells you exactly what to look up. Paste what you find and it builds from there.

### Step 4: Fill in your blanks

Every part ends with "Details to confirm": numbers marked "(estimate, confirm)", gaps in your stories marked "Add:", and anything Claude couldn't find. Fill them in. If a number is in your dossier, you need to be able to explain it when they ask a follow-up.

### Step 5: Save it as one document

At the end, Claude offers to turn your dossier into one downloadable file, if it can create files where you're using it. If it can't, copy each part into one Google Doc or Word document, in order. Give it a name you'll find fast, like "Dossier, Northwind Bank, Senior PM".

### No Claude?

Every section has a copy-paste prompt on this page. Start with "Read the job post", then run the sections in order in any AI tool. Paste each finished section into one document, and check every fact before you rely on it.

## The 3-Day Prep Plan

Here's the plan I'd give you if your interview is 3 days away. More time? Stretch it out. Less time? Jump to "Less than 3 days?" at the end of this section.

### 3 days before: Build and read

- Build your dossier, and check the research as you go.
- Read Sections 1 to 3. Don't memorize. Read until the company feels familiar.
- Fill in every "Details to confirm" item.
- Read Section 5 once. Notice which objection makes your stomach drop. That's the one we practise most.

### 2 days before: Rehearse out loud

- Say your "Tell me about yourself" out loud 3 to 5 times. Time it. Change anything that doesn't sound like you.
- Pick 3 to 4 stories from Section 7. Say each one out loud once.
- Practise your top 3 objection talking points until they sound calm, not defensive.
- Run a quiz with the skill: "Quiz me from my dossier." 15 to 20 minutes is plenty.

### 1 day before: Sharpen and set up

- Read Section 11 and pick 1 or 2 things to mention.
- Choose your top 3 to 4 questions from Section 8 for the person you're meeting.
- Read Section 10. Write your three salary numbers on a sticky note.
- Work through "Before the interview" in Guide 02: the format, the logistics, the tech test, the outfit.
- One last short quiz. Then stop. Sleep beats one more read-through.

### The morning of

- Reread Sections 4 and 10. That's your confidence and your plan.
- Say your "Tell me about yourself" out loud once.
- Close the dossier. You know this.

### Less than 3 days?

- **2 days:** do "3 days before" and "2 days before" today, then "1 day before" tomorrow.
- **Tomorrow:** ask for the short version: "My interview is tomorrow. Give me the 24-hour version." It fits on a few pages.
- **Today:** read Section 6, your top 3 objections and your 3 questions to ask. Then breathe.

## How to Rehearse With It

Reading your dossier isn't rehearsing. Saying it out loud is.

- **Out loud, every time.** An answer that looks great on paper can trip you up the first time you say it. Better to find that out in your kitchen than in the interview.
- **Time yourself.** Use your phone's timer. Aim for 60 to 90 seconds for most answers, and about the same for "Tell me about yourself".
- **Record one run.** Just for you. Play it back once. You'll hear the filler words and the rushed endings right away.
- **Let the skill quiz you.** Type: "Quiz me from my dossier. Mix in the objections." It asks one question at a time and gives you one fix per answer, plus a stronger version built only from your facts.
- **Give the hard ones the most reps.** The objection that scares you most gets the most practice. By the third or fourth run, it starts to feel boring. That's the goal.
- **Hand it to a friend.** Give them Sections 5 and 6 and ask them to play the interviewer. Ask them to interrupt you once. Real interviewers do.
- **Know your stories, don't script them.** Learn the story titles, the order of what happened and the numbers. Don't memorize every word. You want to sound like you, telling a story you lived.

## How to Update It Between Rounds

Every round teaches you something. Put it in your dossier while it's fresh.

### Within a few hours of the interview, write down:

- Who you met (names and job titles)
- Every question they asked, as close to word for word as you can
- How you answered, and what you wish you'd said
- What they seemed to care about most
- Any doubts they raised
- What you learned about the role, the team, the pay or the timeline

### Then tell the skill:

> "Round 1 is done. Here are my notes. Update my dossier for the final round with [Name], [Their job title]."

It will:

- Add the next round's people to Section 3 (only the ones you name)
- Add or re-rank objections in Section 5, based on what came up
- Update Section 6 with questions for the next stage and better versions of any weak answers
- Flag the stories you've already told, so you don't repeat them to the same people
- Write new questions to ask that go deeper than last time
- Update your strategy, including whether pay is likely to come up next

Keep the old version. Label the new one "Version 2" with the round name. And if an offer comes, Sections 1 and 10 are the start of your negotiation prep.

## Good to Know

- **Results vary.** Your dossier helps you prepare well. Nobody can promise an offer.
- **Keep your facts true.** Every story, number and title in your dossier should be one you can explain if they ask a follow-up.
- **Check the research.** AI research can be wrong or out of date. Click the links and check names before you rely on them.
- **Salary numbers:** This is general guidance, not legal or financial advice. Several provinces and US states require pay ranges in job posts, and some limit questions about pay history. Rules differ, so check where you live.
- **Need help?** Reply to your purchase email and it comes straight to me.

Build it. Check it. Say it out loud. You've got this.
