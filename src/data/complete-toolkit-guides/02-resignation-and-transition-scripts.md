# Resignation and Transition Scripts

**10 word-for-word scripts for resigning professionally, handling a counter-offer, and announcing your move.**

> **This is general guidance, not legal advice. Results vary.** These scripts are starting points you can adapt. Before you resign, review your employment contract, any non-compete or non-solicit clauses, and your notice period. If a clause worries you, talk to an employment lawyer in your province or state.

You don't need all of these. Pick the ones that match your situation.

**Time:** 15 minutes to read, then use as needed.
**What you'll need:** Your signed offer letter from the new company (don't resign without it), your current employment contract, and your calendar.

---

## Resignation Rules

1. **Don't resign until you have a signed offer letter.** Verbal offers fall through. Handshake deals collapse. Get it in writing first, and make sure any conditions (background check, references) are cleared.
2. **Review your contract.** Check your notice period, non-compete and non-solicit clauses, and any repayment terms (signing bonus, relocation, tuition).
3. **Tell your manager before anyone else.** Not your work friend. Not your team. Your manager hears it first, in person or on a call. Not by email.
4. **Keep it professional.** No matter how much you'd love to tell your boss exactly what you think. Your industry is smaller than you think.
5. **Give the right amount of notice.** Two weeks is common in Canada and the US. Senior roles often give more. Check your contract and the rules where you live.

## Resignation Conversations

### Script 1: The standard resignation (in person or video)

~~~text
Hi [Manager name], thank you for making the time to talk.

I want to let you know that I've accepted a position at another company, and I'll be resigning. My last day would be [Date, based on your notice period].

This wasn't an easy decision. I've valued my time here and learned a lot working with you and the team. I want to make the transition as smooth as possible.

I'm happy to help with [knowledge transfer / training a replacement / documenting processes / wrapping up current projects] during my remaining time.

Thank you for everything.
~~~

### Script 2: When you had a rough experience

Even if the job was hard, leave gracefully. Your reputation goes with you.

~~~text
Hi [Manager name], I wanted to let you know that I've accepted a new opportunity and will be moving on. My last day would be [Date].

I appreciate the experience I've had here and what I've learned. I'm committed to making the transition smooth over the next [notice period].

Please let me know how I can best support the handoff.
~~~

Notice what's not in this script: complaints, criticism, or reasons for leaving. Save the candour for an exit interview (if you choose to give one) or for a trusted friend.

### Script 3: When you love the job but got a better offer

~~~text
Hi [Manager name], this is a hard conversation, because I've genuinely enjoyed working here. But I've been offered an opportunity I can't turn down, and I've decided to accept it.

My last day would be [Date]. I want you to know this decision has nothing to do with the team or the work. It's about [career growth / compensation / location / personal reasons], and the new role offers something I need at this stage.

I'd love to stay connected, and I'm committed to a strong finish during my notice period.
~~~

## The Resignation Letter

After the conversation, send a formal letter. Email is fine.

### Script 4: The formal resignation letter

~~~text
Subject: Resignation: [Your full name]

Dear [Manager name],

Please accept this as formal notice of my resignation from the position of [Your job title] at [Company], effective [Last day].

I'm grateful for the opportunities I've had here, especially [one specific positive: working on a project, learning from the team, growing in my role].

During my remaining [number of weeks], I'm committed to a smooth transition. Please let me know how I can best support the handoff of my responsibilities.

Thank you for everything.

Sincerely,
[Your full name]
[Your personal email]
[Your phone number]
~~~

## Counter-Offer Responses

Your employer may counter-offer when you resign, especially if you're hard to replace. Before you answer, ask yourself one question: does this fix the reasons I wanted to leave, or only the pay? A raise on its own can't fix growth, culture or direction.

### Script 5: Declining a counter-offer politely

~~~text
I really appreciate the counter-offer, and it means a lot that you value my work enough to make it. I've thought about it carefully.

My decision to leave isn't mainly about compensation. It's about [career direction / growth / personal reasons], and I don't think that can be solved here, even with the adjustment.

I've already committed to the new role, and I want to honour that commitment. I hope you understand.
~~~

### Script 6: Buying time on a counter-offer

~~~text
Thank you. I didn't expect this, and I want to give it serious thought. Could I have [48 hours / the weekend] to think it through?

I want to make sure whatever I decide is the right call for everyone.
~~~

**While you think:** Write down why you started looking. Then check each reason against the counter-offer. If most of them are still there, you have your answer.

### Script 7: Accepting a counter-offer (if you genuinely want to stay)

~~~text
I've thought about this carefully, and I'd like to accept the counter-offer. Thank you for making this possible.

To be open with you, the things that led me to look elsewhere were [specific issues]. The counter-offer addresses [which ones it addresses]. I'd like to talk about how we can also address [remaining issues], so this works for the long term.

Can we put the new terms in writing?
~~~

**Always get counter-offer terms in writing** before you withdraw from the new offer. Promises made in an emotional resignation conversation are easy to forget.

## Telling Your Team

### Script 8: Email to your team (after your manager knows)

~~~text
Subject: Moving on, and thank you

Hi team,

I wanted to share that I'll be leaving [Company] on [Last day]. I've accepted a new role that I'm excited about.

Working with this team has been [genuine positive statement]. I've learned [specific thing] from you, and I'm proud of what we accomplished together, especially [specific project or achievement].

During my remaining [number of weeks], I'll be focused on [documenting processes / handing off projects / training]. Please reach out if there's anything specific you need from me.

I'd love to stay connected. You can find me at [personal email] and on LinkedIn at [LinkedIn URL].

Thank you for everything.

Best,
[Your name]
~~~

## LinkedIn Announcements

### Script 9: The new role announcement

~~~text
I'm excited to share that I'm joining [New company] as [New title].

After [number of years] at [Previous company], I'm grateful for [specific: the team, the projects, what I learned]. Leaving wasn't easy, but [New company]'s work on [specific thing] is something I've been watching for a long time, and the chance to [specific contribution you'll make] was one I couldn't pass up.

Thank you to everyone who supported me through this move. Special thanks to [two or three names: former managers, mentors, people who helped].

If you work in [industry or field], let's connect. I'd love to hear what you're building.
~~~

**Tag people only with their okay.** A quick "Mind if I thank you in my post?" goes a long way.

### Script 10: The departure announcement (before you've landed)

~~~text
After [number of years] at [Company], I've decided to move on.

I'm proud of what I accomplished there, especially [one or two specific achievements]. And I'm grateful to [two or three people or teams] for making it a place where I grew a lot as a [your role].

I'm now exploring [type of opportunities] in [field or industry]. If you know of anything that might be a fit, or if you'd just like to connect, I'd welcome the conversation.

Here's what I bring:
- [Key strength or experience one]
- [Key strength or experience two]
- [Key strength or experience three]

Onward.
~~~

## What Not to Do When Leaving

- **Don't badmouth your employer on LinkedIn.** Not in the announcement. Not in the comments. Not in DMs. It reflects on you, not them.
- **Don't coast through your notice period.** How you leave is how you're remembered. Finish strong.
- **Don't take company property or data.** No client lists, no internal documents, no code. This can lead to legal trouble.
- **Don't burn bridges with individuals.** You may work with these people again.
- **Don't announce on social media before telling your manager.** The order matters: manager first, then HR, then your team, then everyone else.
- **Don't forget your departure details.** Ask what happens to unused vacation, any bonus you've partly earned, when your benefits end, and whether your manager will be a reference. In Canada, earned vacation pay is generally owed to you when you leave. In the US, it depends on your state and your employer's policy.

## Departure Checklist

- [ ] Signed offer letter in hand (before any resignation conversation), with conditions cleared
- [ ] Reviewed my contract for notice period, non-compete, non-solicit and repayment clauses
- [ ] Told my manager in person or on a call (before anyone else)
- [ ] Sent a formal resignation letter by email
- [ ] Asked about unused vacation, any partly earned bonus and a reference
- [ ] Created handoff notes for whoever takes over my work
- [ ] Backed up personal files only (never company data)
- [ ] Updated my LinkedIn profile
- [ ] Sent a farewell message to my team
- [ ] Connected with colleagues on personal channels (personal email, LinkedIn)
- [ ] Returned all company equipment on or before my last day
- [ ] Confirmed when my benefits end and my options to keep coverage (in the US, ask about COBRA. In Canada, ask whether your group plan lets you convert to an individual plan)
