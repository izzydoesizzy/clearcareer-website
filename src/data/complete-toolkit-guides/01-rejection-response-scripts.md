# Rejection Response Scripts

**10 word-for-word scripts for responding to a rejection with grace, asking for feedback, and turning a "no" into a future "yes".**

> **Results vary.** These scripts are starting points you can adapt. Not every employer will reply to a feedback request or stay in touch. These give you the best shot, but no specific outcome is guaranteed.

You don't need to respond to every rejection. Use these when the company or the relationship is worth keeping warm.

**Time:** 10 minutes to read, then use as needed.
**What you'll need:** The rejection email (or your notes from the call), and 24 hours of distance before you send anything.

---

## The 24-Hour Rule

Don't respond to a rejection in the first 24 hours. Your emotional brain will write a message your professional brain would never send.

Wait a day. Feel the feelings. Rejection stings, and that's normal. Then open this guide and pick the right script.

## Graceful Responses

### Script 1: The standard graceful response

~~~text
Subject: Re: [Job title] role, thank you

Hi [First name],

Thank you for letting me know. I'm disappointed, but I really appreciated the chance to learn about [Company] and the team.

If anything changes with this role, or if a similar position opens up, I'd welcome the chance to be considered again.

Wishing you and the team all the best.

Best,
[Your name]
~~~

### Script 2: After multiple rounds

~~~text
Subject: Re: [Job title] role, thank you for the experience

Hi [First name],

Thank you for the update. I won't pretend I'm not disappointed. I was genuinely excited about this opportunity and the team.

I want you to know the interview process was one of the best I've experienced. [Specific positive: the questions were thoughtful / the team was welcoming / you were open about the role.]

I'd love to stay in touch. If something opens up that fits my background, I hope you'll keep me in mind.

Thanks again for your time throughout the process.

Best,
[Your name]
~~~

### Script 3: The very brief response

~~~text
Hi [First name],

Thanks for letting me know. I appreciate the opportunity and wish you well in filling the role.

Best,
[Your name]
~~~

Use this when you don't have the energy for more, or when the role wasn't a strong fit anyway. Short and professional is always fine.

## Feedback Requests

Many employers won't share specific feedback, often for legal reasons. Some will, especially after later-stage interviews. It costs nothing to ask.

### Script 4: Asking for feedback after an interview

~~~text
Subject: Re: [Job title] role, feedback request

Hi [First name],

Thank you for the update. I respect the decision.

If you're able to share any feedback on my interviews or application, I'd really appreciate it. I'm working on getting stronger as a candidate, and specific insight would help a lot.

I understand if company policy doesn't allow this. Either way, thank you for the opportunity.

Best,
[Your name]
~~~

### Script 5: Asking a recruiter for feedback

~~~text
Hi [First name],

Thank you for letting me know about the [Job title] decision.

I'd value any feedback you can share about where I fell short, or what the successful candidate brought that set them apart. I want to be a stronger candidate next time.

If there are other roles in your pipeline that might be a better fit for my background, I'd love to stay on your radar.

Thanks,
[Your name]
~~~

### Script 6: When the feedback is vague

Sometimes you'll hear: "We went with someone who was a closer fit."

~~~text
Hi [First name],

Thank you for that context. To help me improve for future opportunities, could you share anything more specific? For example:

- Was there a skill or experience gap?
- Was it something about the interview itself?
- Was the other candidate internal, or did they have specific experience I was missing?

Anything you can share would help me focus my search. I understand if you can't go into detail.

Thanks again,
[Your name]
~~~

## Turning a No Into a Future Yes

### Script 7: Staying on their radar

~~~text
Subject: Re: [Job title] role, staying connected

Hi [First name],

Thank you for being open with me throughout this process. This one didn't work out, but I'm still very interested in [Company].

I'd like to stay connected. Would it be all right if I checked in every [two or three months] in case something new opens up? I don't want to be a pest, just persistent.

In the meantime, I'll keep following [Company]'s work on [specific project or initiative]. I'm excited to see where it goes.

Best,
[Your name]
~~~

### Script 8: The "keep me in mind" close

~~~text
Hi [First name],

I completely understand the decision. For future reference, here's a quick summary of what I bring, in case another role opens up:

- [Number] years in [field]
- Deep experience in [skill one] and [skill two]
- A track record of [key accomplishment]

If anything comes up that matches this profile, I'd love to be one of the first people you reach out to.

Thanks for the opportunity.

Best,
[Your name]
~~~

### Script 9: Following up two or three months later

~~~text
Subject: Checking in from [Your name]

Hi [First name],

I interviewed for the [Job title] role back in [Month], and you kindly said to stay in touch.

Since then, I've [brief update: finished a certification, taken on a new project, gained experience in a relevant area]. I'm still very interested in [Company] and wanted to check whether any roles have opened up that might be a fit.

Happy to send an updated resume if that's helpful.

Thanks for keeping me in mind.

Best,
[Your name]
~~~

### Script 10: When the same role is posted again

~~~text
Subject: Re-expressing interest in the [Job title] role

Hi [First name],

I noticed the [Job title] role has been posted again. I interviewed for it in [Month] and made it to [stage reached].

Since then, I've [specific improvement or new experience that addresses the earlier gap]. I believe I'm an even stronger candidate now.

I'd love the chance to be considered again. I've submitted a fresh application through the careers page. Would a conversation be possible?

Thanks,
[Your name]
~~~

## What Not to Do

- **Don't respond emotionally.** Even if the rejection was handled poorly. Your reply is a professional message, and it may get forwarded.
- **Don't argue the decision.** They've made their choice. Pushing back won't change it, and it burns the bridge.
- **Don't vent about it on LinkedIn.** Vague posts about "a company that didn't value me" are easy to see through, and other hiring managers read them too.
- **Don't ghost them.** A brief reply keeps the door open for future roles.
- **Don't demand an explanation.** Ask politely for feedback. Accept whatever they give (or don't give).
- **Don't take it personally.** Hiring is messy and subjective, and it's shaped by things you'll never see: internal candidates, budget changes, timing.

---

## The Rejection Reframe

Here's the thing: every rejection moves you further through the process. Not in a motivational-poster way. In a math way. Applications turn into interviews, and interviews turn into offers. Each "no" is part of that pipeline, not the end of it.

And handling a "no" with grace makes you memorable. People remember the candidate who replied kindly when it didn't go their way. When the next role opens, that's who comes to mind.
