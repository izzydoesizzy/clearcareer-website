# The 4-Part About

**My About section format: a sharp summary, 5 career highlights with emoji, the roles you want, and a call to action.**

Most About sections read like a cover letter. Long paragraphs, a lot of "passionate", and no proof. A recruiter skims it, finds nothing to hold on to, and moves on.

The 4-part About fixes that. It's built to be skimmed. The top says who you are. The middle proves it with 5 real wins. The bottom says what you want and how to reach you.

This is the exact structure I use when I write About sections with the people I coach. Let's build yours.

**Time:** About 45 minutes. **What you'll need:** Your resume, 5 wins (with numbers where you have them), and the email address you want recruiters to use.

---

## The structure

~~~text
PART 1: Professional summary
Job title, years of experience, 3 skills, industries, noteworthy companies.

• • • • •

PART 2: 5 career highlights, each one like this:

[Emoji] [Achievement title] | [Company]

[One sentence with the result and how you got it]

• • • • •

PART 3: What excites you (190 characters or fewer)
The roles and companies you want, and why.

PART 4: Call to action (130 characters or fewer)
A specific invite for recruiters.

Connect with me at: → [Email address]
~~~

LinkedIn allows up to 2,600 characters in the About section. Sam's full example below comes in at about 1,400, so you have plenty of room. (LinkedIn changes its limits from time to time, so check the counter when you paste.)

---

## Part 1: The summary

Only the first few lines show before someone clicks "see more". So this part does the heavy lifting. Put in:

- Your job title (the one you want next, or the closest one you can honestly claim)
- Your years of experience
- 3 specific skills people hire for
- The industries you've worked in
- Noteworthy companies or clients, but only ones you can share

~~~text
[Job title] with [Number]+ years in [Skill 1], [Skill 2] and [Skill 3] across [Industry 1] and [Industry 2]. I've [what you did] for [noteworthy companies, or a description like "two national retailers"].
~~~

---

## The divider

Five dots with a space between each one:

~~~text
• • • • •
~~~

Copy it from here. It goes between Part 1 and Part 2, and again between Part 2 and Part 3. It gives the reader's eyes a break, on a phone or a laptop.

---

## Part 2: 5 career highlights

This is the heart of it. Each highlight has two lines with a blank line in between:

1. **The title line:** an emoji, a short achievement title that leads with the number, a pipe, and the company.
2. **The detail line:** one sentence on what you did, how you did it, and the result.

Don't skip the blank line. Without it, the highlights run together into one big block, and the whole point is that a recruiter can scan them.

**Pick an emoji that matches the win:**

- 💰 Money saved, earned or managed
- ⏱️ Time saved, faster turnaround
- 📈 Growth, accuracy, scores going up
- 📦 Inventory, operations, production
- 🚚 Logistics, shipping, delivery
- 🤝 Customers, partners, relationships
- 🎓 Training, coaching, onboarding
- 🛠️ Something you built or fixed
- ⭐ Awards and recognition

**Write the title so the number comes first.** "$1.2M Inventory Reduction" beats "Inventory Project". "22% Lower Freight Costs" beats "Freight Improvements".

**Confidential company?** Describe it instead: "National Retailer", "Regional Credit Union", "Global Software Company".

Highlight 1: ________________ | ________________

Detail: ________________

Highlight 2: ________________ | ________________

Detail: ________________

Highlight 3: ________________ | ________________

Detail: ________________

Highlight 4: ________________ | ________________

Detail: ________________

Highlight 5: ________________ | ________________

Detail: ________________

**Stuck at 3?** That's really common, and it doesn't mean you haven't done anything. Look at:

- Things you trained other people to do
- A process you fixed or a tool you set up
- Times someone thanked you, promoted you or gave you more responsibility
- Volunteer roles and side projects that match the job

No number? Ask yourself: How many? How often? How big? From what to what? If you still can't find one, write the win without a number. Never guess a number you'd have to defend in an interview. If you do estimate, say "about" and know how you got there.

---

## Part 3: What excites you (190 characters or fewer)

One or two sentences on the roles and companies you want, and why. Be specific enough that the right recruiter thinks, "That's my job."

~~~text
I'm excited by [type of role] at [type of company] that [what they care about].
~~~

My Part 3: ________________ (____ characters)

---

## Part 4: The call to action (130 characters or fewer)

A short, specific invite. Then your email on its own line.

~~~text
Need someone to [the problem you solve]? Let's talk.

Connect with me at: → [Email address]
~~~

My Part 4: ________________ (____ characters)

Use an email address you check every day. Add the same email to the Contact info section of your profile too.

## Sam's full About (fictional example)

Sam is a fictional supply chain manager. Copy the shape and the spacing. Never the facts.

~~~text
Supply Chain Manager with 12+ years in demand planning, vendor negotiation and warehouse operations across food manufacturing and grocery distribution. I've run planning for a national grocery distributor and two regional food producers.

• • • • •

📦 $1.2M Inventory Reduction | National Grocery Distributor

Cut on-hand inventory by $1.2M in 10 months by moving 300+ slow-selling products to a weekly reorder cycle.

🚚 22% Lower Freight Costs | National Grocery Distributor

Lowered freight spend 22% by consolidating 9 carriers down to 4 and renegotiating rates on the busiest routes.

⏱️ Order-to-Ship Time Cut from 7 Days to 4 | Regional Food Producer

Shortened order-to-ship time by 3 days by redesigning the pick-and-pack layout with the warehouse team.

📈 Forecast Accuracy Up from 71% to 86% | Regional Food Producer

Raised forecast accuracy 15 points in one year by adding promotion calendars and customer input to the monthly plan.

🤝 Zero Stockouts Through Holiday Peak | Regional Food Producer

Kept our top 40 products in stock from November to December by setting up a shared weekly forecast with our 3 largest retail customers.

• • • • •

I'm excited by supply chain leadership roles at food and consumer goods companies that want calm, data-driven planning and teams who talk to each other.

Need someone to keep your shelves full and your costs down? Let's talk.

Connect with me at: → sam@example.com
~~~

Part 3 is 152 characters. Part 4 is 71. The whole thing is about 1,400.

---

## Pasting it into LinkedIn

1. Write it in a plain notes app first, not a word processor, so no odd formatting sneaks in.
2. On your profile, go to the About section and click the pencil icon. No About section yet? Look for "Add profile section" near the top of your profile.
3. Paste it, save, then check it on your phone. Make sure the blank lines and dividers came through.

A clear About section makes it easier for the right people to say yes to a conversation. It can't guarantee they'll reach out. Results vary. But it means that when they do look, they find proof.
