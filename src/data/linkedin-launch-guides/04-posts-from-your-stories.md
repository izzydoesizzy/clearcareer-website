# Posts From Your Stories

**Four LinkedIn posts every job seeker can write, all mined from things that really happened in your work.**

"But I have nothing to post about."

I hear this a lot. And it's almost never true. You have the material already. It's in the wins you wrote down for your headline, the mistake you still think about, and the thing you explain to every new person on your team.

This guide turns those stories into 4 posts: an Industry Insight, a Career Lesson, a Value-Add Tip and an #OpenToWork announcement. One a week gets you a month of showing up on LinkedIn, with nothing made up.

**Time:** 20 minutes to find your stories. 30 to 45 minutes per post. **What you'll need:** Your 3 to 5 wins from Guide 01, a notes app, and a little honesty.

---

## The format rules

These apply to all 4 posts:

- **One sentence per line, with a blank line between lines.** It looks odd in a document. It reads well on a phone.
- **0 to 2 emoji per post.** Zero is fine.
- **No links in the post.** If you want to share one, put it in the first comment.
- **End with an engagement question.** Ask something people can answer from their own experience.
- **3 to 5 hashtags at the very end.** Pick ones people in your field use.
- **1,200 to 1,500 characters.** Long enough to tell a real story, short enough to read in a minute.

And the most important rule: **only real stories.** If it didn't happen, it doesn't get posted. A true small story beats an invented big one, every time.

---

## Mine your stories first

Spend 20 minutes on these questions before you write a single post. Write rough notes. Don't polish.

**For the Industry Insight:**
What's changed in your field in the last year or two? A new tool, a new rule, a shift in what customers want, something everyone's talking about. What does it mean for people who do your job?

My note: ________________

**For the Career Lesson:**
What's a surprising moment, a mistake or a hard challenge from your career? What did you do about it? What would you tell someone facing the same thing?

My note: ________________

**For the Value-Add Tip:**
What could you teach someone in 10 minutes? A shortcut, a checklist, a way of doing something that saves time or avoids a mess. What's a real example of it working?

My note: ________________

**For the #OpenToWork post:**
What job titles do you want? Which industries? Where (city, remote, hybrid)? What are your 3 to 4 best achievements?

My note: ________________

## Post 1: Industry Insight

**The Story Prompt:** A trend or change you've seen in your field, and 3 things it means for people who do the work. It shows you pay attention to where your field is going, not only to your own desk.

**The shape:**

~~~text
[Hook line 1: the change you've noticed, in plain words]

[Hook line 2: why it matters, or what most people are missing]

Here's what I think it means for [people in your role]:

1. [Implication 1]

2. [Implication 2]

3. [Implication 3]

[One line on what you're doing about it, from your own work]

[Engagement question: What are you seeing in your corner of [field]?]

#[Hashtag] #[Hashtag] #[Hashtag]
~~~

Keep it to what you've seen yourself. "Here's what I've noticed on my team" is stronger than a sweeping claim about the whole industry.

## Post 2: Career Lesson

**The Story Prompt:** A surprising moment or challenge, what you did, what you learned, and a takeaway anyone could use. It shows how you think when things go sideways, which is exactly what hiring managers want to know.

**The shape:**

~~~text
[Hook: the lesson or the moment, in one line]

[Set the scene: when, where, what you were responsible for]

[What went wrong, or what surprised you]

[What you did about it, step by step]

[The result, with a number if there is one]

[The lesson, in one line]

[Engagement question]

#[Hashtag] #[Hashtag] #[Hashtag]
~~~

**A full example.** Sam is a fictional supply chain manager. This post is 1,287 characters.

~~~text
My worst forecast taught me more than my best one.

A few years ago, I planned holiday inventory for a regional food producer.

I trusted last year's numbers.

I didn't call our three biggest retail customers.

Big mistake.

One of them had moved its holiday promotion up by two weeks.

Nobody told planning, and I never asked.

We ran short on our top sellers right when shoppers wanted them most.

The warehouse team worked two weekends in a row to catch up. I still feel bad about that one.

All of it came down to a phone call I could have made in five minutes.

So the next year, I changed three things:

1. From September on, I set up a 20-minute weekly call with each of our 3 largest customers.

2. We kept one shared forecast sheet that both sides could edit.

3. Any promotion change got flagged within 24 hours, not at month end.

The result: zero stockouts on our top 40 products through the next holiday peak.

Here's what I took from it.

A forecast is only as good as the conversations behind it.

Last year's data told me what happened.

Only the people could tell me what was about to happen.

I still love a good spreadsheet. 📊

But now I pick up the phone first.

What's a mistake that changed the way you work?

#SupplyChain #DemandPlanning #Forecasting #FoodIndustry
~~~

Notice what Sam didn't do: name the customer, blame anyone, or pretend it was all fine. Owning a mistake and showing the fix is what makes this kind of post land.

## Post 3: Value-Add Tip

**The Story Prompt:** A framework or practical tip from your work that someone could use in 10 minutes, with a real example. It shows you're the kind of person who makes the people around you better.

**The shape:**

~~~text
[Hook: the problem this tip solves, in one line]

[Why it's a problem, in one or two lines]

Here's the 10-minute fix I use:

1. [Step 1]

2. [Step 2]

3. [Step 3]

[A real example of it working: when, what happened, the result]

[Engagement question: What's your go-to fix for this?]

#[Hashtag] #[Hashtag] #[Hashtag]
~~~

The example is what makes this post yours. Without it, it's a tip anyone could have written.

## Post 4: The #OpenToWork announcement

**The Story Prompt:** A clear, confident post that says exactly what you're looking for, backs it with 3 to 4 achievements, and tells people how to help. It turns your network into extra eyes and ears.

**The shape:**

~~~text
[Hook: I'm looking for my next role as a [Job title], and I'd love your help.]

[One or two lines on what you've been doing, and what you want next]

What I'm looking for:
→ [Job titles]
→ [Industries]
→ [Location, remote or hybrid]

What I bring:
→ [Achievement 1, with a number]
→ [Achievement 2, with a number]
→ [Achievement 3, with a number]

How you can help:
[One clear ask: an introduction to someone at a company like X, a heads-up about a role, or a share]

[Engagement question: Who should I be talking to?]

#OpenToWork #[Hashtag] #[Hashtag]
~~~

**Before you post this one:** make sure you're comfortable telling your whole network you're looking. If you're still employed, you can turn on LinkedIn's Open to Work setting for recruiters only instead of posting. LinkedIn says it can't fully guarantee who sees that setting, so think it through.

---

## A simple rhythm

- **Week 1:** Career Lesson (it's usually the easiest to write)
- **Week 2:** Value-Add Tip
- **Week 3:** Industry Insight
- **Week 4:** #OpenToWork, or save it for when you're ready

After you post, stick around for the first hour or so and reply to every comment. That's where the conversations start.

Some posts get a handful of views. Some take off. You can't control which. Results vary. What you can control is showing up with true stories, week after week.
