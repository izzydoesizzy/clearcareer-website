# Messages for 5 Situations

**Connection requests and follow-up messages for recruiters, hiring managers, mutual connections, event contacts and former colleagues, plus search links to find them.**

Here's the thing about LinkedIn outreach: the connection note isn't the pitch. Its only job is to get a yes. The real conversation starts in the message you send after they accept.

So for each of the 5 situations below, you get 3 short connection notes (pick the one that sounds most like you) and 1 follow-up message for after they accept. Every note shows its character count.

**Time:** 2 to 5 minutes per person. **What you'll need:** Your target job title, one win you can say in one sentence, and the person's profile open in another tab.

---

## The rules I follow

- **Keep notes short.** LinkedIn allows up to 300 characters in a connection note as I write this, and some free accounts get less, or only a limited number of personal notes per month. Aim for 200 or fewer. LinkedIn changes these limits, so trust the counter in the note box.
- **Make it personal.** Mention something specific about them or their company: a post, a project, a talk, a shared school or employer.
- **Lead with common ground, not an ask.** "Can you get me a job?" is a hard no. "Your post on onboarding stuck with me" is an easy yes.
- **Follow up within 24 to 48 hours after they accept.** While your name is still fresh.
- **Never send the same message to several people at the same company.** They talk to each other.
- **Keep it conversational.** Write it the way you'd say it out loud.

The counts below include the [brackets] as written. Your count changes once you fill them in, so check the counter before you send.

## Find the people first

Before you message anyone, you need the right people. These LinkedIn search links do the hunting for you. Swap in your own job titles and keywords.

**People search link format:**

~~~text
https://www.linkedin.com/search/results/people/?keywords=YOUR_SEARCH&origin=GLOBAL_SEARCH_HEADER
~~~

**Job search link format:**

~~~text
https://www.linkedin.com/jobs/search/?keywords=YOUR_SEARCH&location=YOUR_LOCATION
~~~

In the link, a space becomes %20, a quote mark becomes %22 and a comma becomes %2C. Here's what that looks like for Sam, a fictional supply chain manager in Toronto:

- **Recruiters:** "supply chain" recruiter. [Search people](https://www.linkedin.com/search/results/people/?keywords=%22supply%20chain%22%20recruiter&origin=GLOBAL_SEARCH_HEADER)
- **More recruiters:** "talent acquisition" "supply chain". [Search people](https://www.linkedin.com/search/results/people/?keywords=%22talent%20acquisition%22%20%22supply%20chain%22&origin=GLOBAL_SEARCH_HEADER)
- **Hiring managers:** "director of supply chain" OR "head of supply chain" OR "VP supply chain". [Search people](https://www.linkedin.com/search/results/people/?keywords=%22director%20of%20supply%20chain%22%20OR%20%22head%20of%20supply%20chain%22%20OR%20%22VP%20supply%20chain%22&origin=GLOBAL_SEARCH_HEADER)
- **Industry peers:** "supply chain manager" food. [Search people](https://www.linkedin.com/search/results/people/?keywords=%22supply%20chain%20manager%22%20food&origin=GLOBAL_SEARCH_HEADER)
- **Jobs:** "Supply Chain Manager" OR "Supply Chain Lead" in Toronto. [Search jobs](https://www.linkedin.com/jobs/search/?keywords=%22Supply%20Chain%20Manager%22%20OR%20%22Supply%20Chain%20Lead%22&location=Toronto%2C%20Ontario%2C%20Canada)

Two tips:

1. **One job per search.** Either list alternatives with OR ("head of supply chain" OR "director of supply chain"), or combine two ideas with a space ("supply chain" recruiter). Don't mix both in one search. Without brackets, LinkedIn may read it differently than you mean.
2. **Filter on the results page.** Open the link, then use LinkedIn's filters for location and current company.

LinkedIn sometimes changes how these links work. If one stops working, paste the search words into the LinkedIn search bar instead. The cc-linkedin-launch skill can build a full set of these links for your titles in one go.

## Situation 1: A recruiter at a company you're targeting

**Connection notes:**

~~~text
Hi [First name], I see you recruit for [Job title] roles at [Company]. I'm a [Your job title] with [Number] years in [Industry] and would love to be on your radar. Thanks!
~~~
(171 characters)

~~~text
Hi [First name], I'm interested in [Company] and noticed you hire for the [Team] team. I'd love to connect and stay on your radar for roles that fit.
~~~
(149 characters)

~~~text
Hi [First name], your post about [Specific detail] caught my eye. I'm a [Your job title] who'd love to work at [Company], and I'd be glad to connect.
~~~
(149 characters)

**Follow-up message:**

~~~text
Thanks for connecting, [First name].

I'll keep this short. I'm a [Your job title] with [Number] years in [Industry], and [Company] is on my short list because [Specific reason, like a product, a value or a recent announcement].

A quick snapshot of what I bring:
→ [Win 1, one line, with a number]
→ [Win 2, one line, with a number]

I saw the [Job title] posting. If my background fits what your team is hiring for, I'd love 15 minutes to hear what you look for in a strong candidate. If the timing isn't right, no worries at all. I'm happy to stay in touch.

My resume is ready to send if it helps.

Thanks,
[Your name]
~~~

No posting? Swap the "I saw the posting" line for: "I know there may not be an opening right now, and that's okay."

## Situation 2: A hiring manager

This is the person you'd report to. Usually one or two levels above the role.

**Connection notes:**

~~~text
Hi [First name], I've been following [Company]'s work on [Specific detail]. I'm a [Your job title] and would love to connect and learn how your team works.
~~~
(155 characters)

~~~text
Hi [First name], I applied for the [Job title] role on your team and wanted to say hello here too. I'd be glad to connect.
~~~
(122 characters)

~~~text
Hi [First name], I saw your team at [Company] is growing. I've spent [Number] years doing [Type of work] and would love to connect.
~~~
(131 characters)

Only use the second note if you really applied.

**Follow-up message:**

~~~text
Thanks for connecting, [First name].

I've been reading about [Specific detail about the team or company], and it lines up with the work I've been doing as a [Your job title].

One example: [Your win, in one or two sentences, with the number].

I'm curious how your team is thinking about [A challenge they likely face, as a question]. If you're open to it, I'd value 15 minutes to hear what's on your plate this year and where someone like me might help.

Either way, thanks for the connection. I'll keep following what your team is building, and I'm happy to pass along anything useful I come across.

[Your name]
~~~

## Situation 3: A mutual connection who could introduce you

This one has an extra step. First, ask your mutual connection for the introduction, and give them a blurb they can forward. Then, if they'd rather you reach out yourself, use their name in the note (only once they've said yes).

**Your message to the mutual connection, with a forwardable blurb:**

~~~text
Hi [Mutual name], hope you're doing well!

I'm looking at [Your job title] roles, and [Company] is near the top of my list. I noticed you're connected to [First name], who works on the [Team] team there. Would you be comfortable introducing us? No pressure at all if not.

To make it easy, here's a short blurb you can copy and send:

"[Your first name] is a [Your job title] with [Number] years in [Industry]. Most recently, [Your first name] [Your win, in one sentence, with the number]. [Your first name] is exploring roles at [Company] and would value 15 minutes to hear about your team. Would you be open to a quick chat?"

Thanks so much. I owe you a coffee.
[Your name]
~~~

**Connection notes to the person you want to meet:**

~~~text
Hi [First name], [Mutual name] suggested I reach out. I'm a [Your job title] exploring roles at [Company] and would love to connect.
~~~
(132 characters)

~~~text
Hi [First name], we're both connected to [Mutual name], who spoke highly of your work on [Specific detail]. I'd be glad to connect.
~~~
(131 characters)

~~~text
Hi [First name], [Mutual name] mentioned you know the [Team] side of [Company] well. I'm a [Your job title] and would value connecting.
~~~
(135 characters)

**Follow-up message:**

~~~text
Thanks for connecting, [First name], and thanks to [Mutual name] for pointing me your way.

I'm a [Your job title] with [Number] years in [Industry]. Recently, I [Your win, in one sentence, with the number].

I'm exploring roles at [Company], and I'd love to hear how you see the [Team] side of things: what's going well, what's hard, and what the strongest people on the team do differently.

Would you be open to a 15-minute call in the next couple of weeks? I'm happy to work around your schedule, and no pressure at all if now isn't a good time.

Thanks again,
[Your name]
~~~

## Situation 4: Someone from an event or webinar

**Connection notes:**

~~~text
Hi [First name], great meeting you at [Event]. I enjoyed our chat about [Specific detail] and would love to stay in touch here.
~~~
(127 characters)

~~~text
Hi [First name], I was at [Event] and your point about [Specific detail] stuck with me. I'd be glad to connect.
~~~
(111 characters)

~~~text
Hi [First name], we were both at [Event] this week. I'm a [Your job title] and would love to keep the conversation going here.
~~~
(126 characters)

Only say you met if you did. If you watched them speak, the second note is the honest one.

**Follow-up message:**

~~~text
Thanks for connecting, [First name]!

I keep thinking about what you said at [Event] about [Specific detail]. It reminded me of [A short, true example from your own work].

I'm a [Your job title], and I'm exploring roles in [Industry]. If you're open to it, I'd love to hear more about how [Company] handles [Topic]. Fifteen minutes on a video call or over coffee would be plenty.

And if there's anything I can send your way, like [an article, a template or a contact that came up in our chat], just say the word.

Either way, it was great to cross paths.

[Your name]
~~~

## Situation 5: A former colleague

**Connection notes:**

~~~text
Hi [First name], it's been a while since our days at [Company]! I'd love to reconnect and hear what you're working on now.
~~~
(122 characters)

~~~text
Hi [First name], I was thinking about [Shared project] at [Company] the other day. Hope you're well. I'd love to reconnect here.
~~~
(128 characters)

~~~text
Hi [First name], I saw your new role at [New company]. Congrats! I'd love to reconnect and catch up.
~~~
(100 characters)

**Follow-up message:**

~~~text
Great to be connected again, [First name]!

How are things at [New company]? [One specific question about their role or a recent post.]

A quick update from my side: I'm [Your current situation, in one plain sentence], and I'm looking at [Your job title] roles in [Industry or city]. Most recently, I [Your win, in one sentence].

If you hear of anything that fits, or know someone I should talk to, I'd be grateful. And if there's anything I can help with on your end, I'm happy to.

Would you be up for a quick call or a coffee sometime soon?

[Your name]
~~~
