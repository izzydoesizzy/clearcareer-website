# Headlines

**My pipe-style headline format, 10 angles to try, and how to test 3 of them.**

Your headline follows your name everywhere on LinkedIn: search results, comments, connection requests, the little preview when someone hovers over your name. Most people leave it as their job title, or let LinkedIn fill it in for them.

That's a missed chance. Your headline is a billboard. It's the first thing a recruiter reads, and sometimes the only thing they read before they decide whether to click.

This is the format I use when I write headlines with the people I coach. Three parts, separated by a pipe ( | ). That's it.

**Time:** 30 minutes to write. A few minutes a week for 6 weeks to test. **What you'll need:** Your resume or LinkedIn profile, the job title you want next, and 3 to 5 wins.

---

## The formula

~~~text
Job Title | Key Experience or Skills | Major Achievement
~~~

**Job Title.** The title you want next, or the closest title you can honestly claim. It goes first because recruiters search by title, and LinkedIn often cuts headlines short in search results and comments. If the start is all they see, make it count.

**Key Experience or Skills.** Years, industries, specialties or tools. Use the words that show up in job posts for your target role.

**Major Achievement.** One real result, with a number if you have one. This is the part that makes someone stop scrolling.

You get up to 220 characters. That's room for a second achievement or a second skills part if you want one. You don't have to use all of it. (LinkedIn changes its limits from time to time, so trust the counter on your profile.)

Can't find the pipe key? On most keyboards it's Shift plus the backslash key, above Enter. On a phone, look in the symbols keyboard.

---

## Start with your wins

Every good headline is built on a real win. Before you write, jot down 3 to 5 things you did that made a difference. Rough notes are fine.

Ask yourself:

- Did I save or make money? How much?
- Did I make something faster? From what to what?
- How big was my work? People, accounts, products, budget, locations.
- What did I build, fix or start that wasn't there before?

Win 1: ________________

Win 2: ________________

Win 3: ________________

Win 4: ________________

Win 5: ________________

No number for a win? Write it down anyway. A clear result without a number beats a number you can't explain in an interview. Guide 02 has more help digging for numbers.

## One person, 10 angles

Here are 10 headlines for Sam, a fictional supply chain manager. Same person, same facts, 10 different angles. Notice that every one starts with a title.

1. Supply Chain Manager | 12+ Years in Food Manufacturing and Grocery Distribution | Cut $1.2M in Inventory in 10 Months (117 characters)
2. Supply Chain Manager | Demand Planning and Vendor Negotiation | Lowered Freight Costs 22% by Consolidating 9 Carriers to 4 (122 characters)
3. Supply Chain and Demand Planning Leader | Food and Consumer Goods | Raised Forecast Accuracy from 71% to 86% in One Year (120 characters)
4. Supply Chain Manager | SAP and Excel Power User | Shortened Order-to-Ship Time from 7 Days to 4 (95 characters)
5. Supply Chain Manager | Grocery and Food Production | Zero Stockouts on Our Top 40 Products Through Holiday Peak (111 characters)
6. Supply Chain Manager | Planning for National Grocery Accounts | $1.2M Inventory Reduction | 22% Lower Freight Spend (115 characters)
7. Supply Chain Manager | Warehouse Layout, Carrier Strategy and Forecasting | Turned a 7-Day Order Cycle into 4 Days (114 characters)
8. Supply Chain Manager | Food Manufacturing | Built a Shared Weekly Forecast with Our 3 Largest Retail Customers (110 characters)
9. Supply Chain Manager for Food Brands | Calm Planning in Busy Seasons | $1.2M Less Inventory, 22% Lower Freight, Zero Peak Stockouts (131 characters)
10. Supply Chain Manager | Food and Grocery | Vendor Negotiation | Inventory Control | Forecasting | Open to Roles in Toronto (121 characters)

Pick the angles that fit the jobs you want. A money result suits roles that are judged on cost. A scale angle suits leadership roles. A tools angle helps when the job posts you're seeing list specific software.

## Test 3, keep 1

You don't have to guess which headline is best. Test them.

Pick 3 from your list, one of each type:

1. **Safe/Direct:** clear and keyword-rich. Your title plus the skills a recruiter would type into search.
2. **Result-Focused:** leads with your strongest result.
3. **Distinctive/Niche:** built on the most specific or unusual thing about you.

Here's what Sam picked:

1. **Safe/Direct:** Supply Chain Manager | Demand Planning, Vendor Negotiation and Warehouse Operations | Food Manufacturing and Grocery Distribution (129 characters)
2. **Result-Focused:** Supply Chain Manager | Cut $1.2M in Inventory in 10 Months | Lowered Freight Costs 22% by Consolidating Carriers (112 characters)
3. **Distinctive/Niche:** Supply Chain Manager for Food Brands | I Keep Shelves Full Through Holiday Peak | Zero Stockouts on Our Top 40 Products (119 characters)

Then run the test:

- Use each headline for 2 weeks, one at a time.
- Every Friday, write down what LinkedIn shows you: profile views, search appearances (if your account shows them) and any recruiter messages.
- After 6 weeks, keep the winner. If it's close, keep the one you're proudest of.

A small test like this gives you rough signals, not proof. Lots of things change how many people see your profile, including how active you are on LinkedIn that week. Results vary. But it beats leaving the same headline up for three years and hoping.

## Skip these

- **Buzzwords.** "Results-driven." "Passionate." "Guru." Nobody types those into a search bar.
- **"Seeking new opportunities" on its own.** Say what you're looking for instead. "Open to Supply Chain Manager Roles in Toronto" fits nicely in one pipe section.
- **Numbers you can't explain.** If a recruiter asks "How did you get to 22%?", you need an answer.
- **Client names you can't share.** Describe them instead: "National Grocery Chains", "Fortune 500 Retailers".
- **An emoji parade.** One emoji is fine if it fits you. Zero is fine too.

To change it on LinkedIn: open your profile, click the pencil icon near your name, and paste it into the Headline box. LinkedIn moves buttons around now and then, so if you don't see the pencil, look for "Edit profile".
