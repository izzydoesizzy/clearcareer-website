# Search Tools

**Boolean search strings, Google Alerts, a daily action plan and a way to find events, so the right jobs and the right people start coming to you.**

Most job searches run on doom scrolling. Open LinkedIn, scroll, sigh, close LinkedIn. Repeat tomorrow.

I want you to swap that for a few tools that do the searching for you. A handful of saved search strings that pull up exactly the jobs and people you want. Google Alerts that drop company news and new postings into your inbox. A simple daily plan so you know what to do when you sit down. And a short list of events and associations where the people who hire you actually hang out.

Set this up once. It keeps paying you back for the rest of your search.

**Time:** About an hour to set everything up. **What you'll need:** Your target job title (plus 2 or 3 close titles), your industry, your city, your company list (from Guide 2) and a Google account.

## Part 1: Boolean search strings

"Boolean" sounds technical. It isn't. It's five building blocks that tell a search box exactly what you mean.

### The five building blocks

| Block | What it does | Example |
|---|---|---|
| AND | Both words must appear | "project manager" AND healthcare |
| OR | Either word can appear | (coordinator OR specialist) |
| NOT | Leaves a word out | NOT intern |
| "Quotes" | Finds the exact phrase | "project manager" |
| (Parentheses) | Groups words together | ("project manager" OR "program manager") |

**On LinkedIn:** type AND, OR and NOT in capital letters. LinkedIn doesn't support the * wildcard. Boolean works in the keyword box for both Jobs and People searches.

**On Google:** a space already means AND. Type OR in capitals. Use a minus sign to leave a word out (-intern). And site: limits the search to one website (site:linkedin.com/in searches LinkedIn profiles).

Every string below has [brackets] for your details. I've filled in the first one for Sam, a fictional project manager in health care, so you can see how it looks.

### Job search strings

Paste these into the LinkedIn Jobs search box. Then set **Date posted** to **Past week**, add your location (or the Remote filter), and switch on the job alert so new matches come to you.

1. **Your title and its close cousins.**

~~~text
("[Target title]" OR "[Close title 1]" OR "[Close title 2]") AND ([Industry word] OR "[Industry phrase]") NOT (intern OR junior)
~~~

Sam's version:

~~~text
("project manager" OR "program manager" OR "delivery manager") AND (healthcare OR "health care" OR hospital) NOT (intern OR junior OR coordinator)
~~~

2. **The senior version.**

~~~text
("senior [Target title]" OR "[Target title] lead") AND ("[Key certification]" OR "[Key skill]")
~~~

3. **Remote or hybrid.** (Use LinkedIn's Remote filter too. This catches postings that only mention it in the text.)

~~~text
("[Target title]" OR "[Close title 1]") AND (remote OR hybrid OR "work from home")
~~~

4. **Skills first.** Good for finding titles you didn't know to search for.

~~~text
("[Key skill]" OR "[Key tool]") AND ("[Target title]" OR "[Close title 1]" OR "[Close title 2]")
~~~

5. **The career changer.** Your transferable skills, the new field, and no senior roles you can't reach yet.

~~~text
("[Transferable skill 1]" OR "[Transferable skill 2]") AND ("[New field title]") NOT (senior OR director OR principal)
~~~

### Recruiter strings

1. **Recruiters who fill roles like yours** (LinkedIn People search, plus a location filter):

~~~text
(recruiter OR "talent acquisition" OR "search consultant") AND ("[Your field]" OR "[Your function]")
~~~

2. **In-house recruiters at a target company** (LinkedIn People search, then set the Current company filter to [Company]):

~~~text
("talent acquisition" OR recruiter OR "talent partner" OR "people partner")
~~~

3. **The Google version** (paste into Google):

~~~text
site:linkedin.com/in ("recruiter" OR "talent acquisition") "[Your field]" "[City]" -jobs
~~~

### Hiring manager strings

1. **Leaders of your function at a target company** (LinkedIn People search, Current company filter set to [Company]):

~~~text
("director" OR "head of" OR "VP" OR "manager") AND ("[Function]") NOT (recruiter OR "talent acquisition" OR assistant OR intern)
~~~

2. **The Google version:**

~~~text
site:linkedin.com/in "[Company]" ("director" OR "head of") "[Function]" -recruiter
~~~

3. **Leaders who speak in public** (this one finds your email hooks too):

~~~text
"[Company]" "[Function]" (speaker OR panel OR podcast OR keynote)
~~~

### Networking strings

1. **People like you, with something in common** (or use LinkedIn's School and Past company filters):

~~~text
("[Your title]" OR "[Close title 1]") AND ("[Your school]" OR "[Past employer]")
~~~

2. **People who already made the move you're making:**

~~~text
("former [Old role]" OR "ex-[Old role]") AND ("[New title]" OR "[Close new title]")
~~~

Sam's version of that one, if Sam were moving into health tech: ("former nurse" OR "ex-nurse") AND ("product manager" OR "product owner").

**Save your best 3.** LinkedIn lets you save job searches with alerts. For People searches, keep the strings in a note so you can paste them in again.

## Part 2: Google Alerts

Google Alerts sends you an email when something new matching your search shows up online. New postings, company news, funding, new offices, a leader's latest podcast. It's free, and it turns "I should check on that company" into something that happens on its own.

### How to set one up

1. Go to google.com/alerts and sign in with your Google account.
2. Type or paste your alert into the box at the top. You'll see a preview of the kind of results you'd get.
3. Click **Show options**.
4. **How often:** "As-it-happens" for company hiring alerts, "At most once a day" for everything else.
5. **Sources:** "Automatic" to start. Switch to "News" if it's too noisy.
6. **Language and Region:** your language, and your country (or "Any region" for remote searches).
7. **How many:** "Only the best results" to start. "All results" if it's too quiet.
8. **Deliver to:** your email.
9. Click **Create Alert**. Repeat for each one.

Tip: set up an email filter so every alert lands in its own folder. Check it once a day, not every hour.

### Your 20 alerts, in 5 categories

Pick 15 to 20 that fit you. Swap in your details. I've filled a few in with fictional companies so you can see the shape.

**Category 1: Company hiring alerts.** For your top companies from your scorecard.

~~~text
"[Company]" (hiring OR careers OR "job opening")
"[Company]" ("new office" OR expansion OR expanding)
"[Company]" "[Your function]" (hiring OR "joins the team")
"[Company]" (funding OR raises OR acquisition)
~~~

Example: "Northwind Bank" (hiring OR careers OR "job opening")

**Category 2: Target role alerts.**

~~~text
"[Target title]" "[City]" (hiring OR "now hiring")
"[Target title]" remote "[Country]" hiring
"[Close title]" "[City]" hiring
"we're hiring" "[Target title]"
~~~

**Category 3: Industry trend alerts.** So you can talk about what's happening in your field, in emails and in interviews.

~~~text
"[Industry]" (trends OR growth OR funding OR expansion) "[Province, state or country]"
"[Industry]" (layoffs OR "hiring freeze")
"[Industry]" report "[Year]"
"[Key skill or tool]" "[Industry]"
~~~

**Category 4: Competitor and peer company alerts.** The companies next to your targets are often hiring for the same skills.

~~~text
"[Competitor 1]" OR "[Competitor 2]" (hiring OR expansion)
"[Peer company]" (appoints OR "names new" OR "new CEO")
"[Peer company]" (launch OR "new product" OR "new service")
"[Industry]" startup "[City]" (funding OR raises)
~~~

Example: "Lumen Health" OR "Bluepeak Software" (hiring OR expansion)

A leadership change can mean a new team gets built, so the "appoints" alert is worth having.

**Category 5: Industry leader alerts.** The people whose names come up in your field, and the leaders at your target companies.

~~~text
"[Leader's name]" "[Company]"
"[Leader's name]" (podcast OR interview OR panel OR keynote)
"[Conference name]" speakers "[Year]"
"[Association name]" "[City]" event
~~~

### Keep them useful

- **Too noisy?** Add quotes around phrases, add a minus word (-stock -sale), switch Sources to "News", or keep "Only the best results".
- **Too quiet?** Drop a quoted phrase, drop the city, or switch to "All results".
- **Once a month:** delete any alert that hasn't given you anything useful, and add alerts for new companies on your scorecard.
- **Save the good stuff.** When an alert hands you something about a person at a target company, copy the link into your outreach tracker. That's your email hook.

## Part 3: Your daily job search action plan

A job search without a plan turns into a day of tab-hopping. So here's the plan. Sit down, look at the day, do the next thing.

### The week at a glance

| Day | Morning (1 to 2 hours) | Afternoon (1 to 2 hours) | Evening (30 minutes) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Monday | Plan the week. Scan fresh postings and score them out of 12. | Apply to your APPLY roles. | Skim your alerts folder and save any hooks. |
| Tuesday | Find one person at each of 3 to 5 target companies. | Write and send 2 or 3 outreach emails. | Comment on 2 or 3 posts from people at your target companies. |
| Wednesday | Apply to your next APPLY roles. | Send any follow-ups that are due. | Skill building. |
| Thursday | Write and send 2 or 3 outreach emails. | Calls and coffee chats, or prep for them. | Update your tracker. |
| Friday | Score 5 new companies. | An event, an association meeting or an online community. | Review the week and plan the next one. |

Weekends: rest. Seriously. A tired job seeker writes tired emails.

### The four daily blocks

**Networking (30 to 45 minutes a day)**
- Send 2 or 3 personal outreach emails or connection requests
- Leave a thoughtful comment on 2 or 3 posts by people at your target companies
- Reply to anyone who wrote back, the same day if you can

**Applications (45 to 60 minutes a day)**
- 1 to 3 quality applications to roles that scored APPLY
- Match your resume to the top 3 requirements in each posting
- Apply on the company's own site, then email the person who leads the team

**Follow-up (15 to 30 minutes a day)**
- Check your tracker for follow-ups that are due (5 to 7 business days after you sent)
- Send thank-you notes within 24 hours of any call
- Update every row you touched today

**Skill building (30 minutes a day)**
- Work on the gaps from your Fit Score pattern note
- One module of a course, one practice session, or one good article in your field

### If you're working full time

Pick three days a week and give each one a focused hour. Same order every time: score, apply, reach out, follow up. Batch your outreach so one evening covers the week's emails, and send them during business hours.

### If you're between roles

Treat it like a part-time job, not a 12-hour shift. Three or four focused hours a day is plenty. Then stop, move your body and do something that isn't your job search. Your energy is part of the plan.

### Weekly milestones

Count what you control. Replies and interviews aren't fully in your hands, and results vary. These are.

- **Week 1:** 10 or more postings scored. 10 to 20 companies on a scorecard. Google Alerts set up. Your first 5 outreach emails sent.
- **Week 2:** 10 more outreach emails sent. Every follow-up sent on time. An application in for every APPLY role.
- **Week 4:** A second round of companies scored. Your pattern note reviewed, and your resume and search titles updated to match. At least one event or association meeting attended.

## Part 4: Finding events and associations

The people who hire you go to things. Industry conferences, association chapter meetings, local meetups, online communities. Showing up (even virtually) puts you in the room before the job gets posted.

### Where to look

- **Professional associations:** search "[Your field] association [Province or state]" and "[Your field] association [Country]". Most have chapters, events, job boards and volunteer roles.
- **Conferences:** search "[Industry] conference [Year] [City]", and check the event calendars of the associations you find.
- **Local meetups:** Meetup, Eventbrite, LinkedIn Events, your local board of trade or chamber of commerce, and your public library's events page.
- **Online communities:** Slack or Discord groups for your field, LinkedIn groups and industry forums.
- **Alumni groups:** your college or university alumni association, and any former-employee groups.

Before you put anything in your calendar, check it's real and current on the official page: the date, the location and the cost.

Volunteering at an event is a great move. It's often free, and it gives you a reason to talk to everyone there.

### How to work an event as a job seeker

**Before:** look at the speaker or attendee list. Pick 3 people you'd like to meet. Prepare a two-sentence intro: who you are and what you're curious about. That's it. No elevator pitch speech.

**During:** ask questions instead of pitching. Two or three real conversations beat twenty business cards.

**After:** follow up within 48 hours, while they still remember you.

### Conversation starters

- "What brought you here today?"
- "What's keeping your team busy right now?"
- "I liked [speaker]'s point about [topic]. What did you think?"
- "How did you get into [field]?"
- "What's one thing you wish more people knew about your role?"

### The follow-up after you meet someone

~~~text
Subject: Great to meet you at [Event]

Hi [First name],

Great to meet you at [Event] on [Day]. I enjoyed our chat about [topic], especially [one detail from the conversation].

[One line: the thing you said you'd send, or a question you'd like to keep talking about.]

Would you be open to a 15-minute call in the next few weeks? No pressure at all if the timing doesn't work.

[Your name]
[LinkedIn URL]
~~~
