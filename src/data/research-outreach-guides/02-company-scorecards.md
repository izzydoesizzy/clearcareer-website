# Company Scorecards

**Score your target companies on five things that matter, weighted to what matters to you, so you know exactly where to aim first.**

A list of 20 companies you'd love to work at is a wish list. It's a great start. But a wish list doesn't tell you where to spend Monday morning.

A scorecard does. You look at each company the same way, give it a score on five areas, weight those scores to your own priorities, and rank the list. The top three get your energy this week. The rest wait their turn.

This is the same method I use to research and rank target companies. You can do it by hand with the templates below, or let the cc-company-scorecards Claude skill do the research for you.

**Time:** About 2 hours for 10 companies, done in a couple of sittings. **What you'll need:** A list of companies (or a target role and city, and we'll build one), your priorities, and a spreadsheet or a printout of the templates.

---

## The five areas

Every company gets a score from 1 to 10 on each of these:

1. **Culture fit:** what employees say, the office policy (remote, hybrid, on-site), and whether their values match yours.
2. **Growth:** is the company growing, steady or shrinking? Funding, revenue news, new offices and products, hiring, layoffs.
3. **Open roles:** are they hiring for your kind of role right now?
4. **Pay:** salary ranges for your target role, and benefits.
5. **Industry position:** are they a leader, a challenger or a niche player, in an industry you want?

---

## Step 1: Pick your companies

Aim for 10 to 20. Fewer than 10 and you'll run out of options fast. More than 20 and the research never ends.

No list yet? Build one in three tiers:

- **Tier 1, Strong fit:** companies where your skills and experience line up best
- **Tier 2, Good fit:** solid options worth pursuing
- **Tier 3, Worth watching:** interesting, but not right now

Where to find companies:

- Competitors of your current and past employers
- Companies posting your target role right now
- Companies in your target industry, near you
- Remote-friendly companies in your field, if you're open to remote work
- Suppliers, partners and customers of companies you've worked for
- Local "best employers" lists, business news and funding news

Mix the sizes: some big employers, some mid-size companies, some growing startups. And only keep a company once you've found its careers page. If you can't find one, it's probably not hiring in a way you can reach.

"Why it fits me" is about you, not about them. "They're a great company" doesn't count. "They're scaling a payroll team and I've run payroll for 300 people" does.

## Step 2: Set your weights

Not everything matters equally to you. Maybe remote work is non-negotiable and the industry doesn't matter much. The weights let your scorecard reflect that.

Give each of the five areas a weight:

- **3:** a top priority
- **2:** important
- **1:** nice to have

Every area gets a weight. Then write down your dealbreakers: the things that rule a company out no matter what. "Must be remote." "No commission-only pay." "No more than a 45-minute commute."

---

## Step 3: Research each company

Give each company a short, focused look. Write down where you found each fact and the date. If you can't find something, write "Not found". Don't guess.

| Area | What to look for | Where to look |
|---|---|---|
| Culture fit | Overall review rating and how many reviews it's based on. Common themes in the last 12 months. The office policy. Stated values. | Employee review sites (Glassdoor and similar), the careers page, recent job posts, news |
| Growth | Funding, revenue news, new offices, products or markets. How many roles are open. Any layoffs or hiring freezes in the last 12 months. | The company newsroom, business news, funding news, the careers page |
| Open roles | Roles that match your target title, with links and dates. Similar roles posted recently. | The careers page, LinkedIn Jobs |
| Pay | Ranges in their own job posts first. Then salary sites for this role at this company. Benefits. | Their job posts, salary sites, the careers page |
| Industry position | Leader, challenger or niche. Two or three main competitors. What they're known for. | Business news, industry publications, the About page |

A quick note on pay: some provinces and US states now require pay ranges in job postings, so always check the posting first.

---

## Step 4: Score each area from 1 to 10

Use this table so every company gets scored the same way. If you found nothing at all for an area, give it a 5 (neutral) and write "Not found".

| Score | Culture fit | Growth | Open roles | Pay | Industry position |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 9 to 10 | Strong recent reviews, policy meets your must-haves, values line up | Clear growth in the last 12 months | A matching role is open now | Meets or beats your target, good benefits | Leader or rising challenger in an industry you want |
| 7 to 8 | Mostly positive, one small mismatch | Stable, some growth signs | Close roles open, or your role gets posted often | Close to your target | Solid player in your industry or a close one |
| 5 to 6 | Mixed, or the policy isn't clear | Flat, no clear signs | The team exists, no opening right now | 5 = not found, or no target set | Niche, or a related industry |
| 3 to 4 | Several concerns on things you care about | Mixed signs (a freeze, small layoffs) | Few roles like yours | Below your target | Outside your industry, skills transfer |
| 1 to 2 | Clear mismatch or serious red flags | Shrinking (big layoffs, closures) | They don't hire for your kind of role | Well below (more than 15% under) | An industry you want to avoid |

Then rate how much you trust your own research for that company:

- **Strong:** recent sources for 4 or 5 areas
- **Some:** sources for 3 areas, or older sources
- **Thin:** sources for 2 or fewer areas

---

## Step 5: Do the maths

For each company:

1. Multiply each score by its weight.
2. Add those five numbers up.
3. Divide by the total of your weights.

That's your **weighted score out of 10**. Round it to one decimal place. Also add up the five plain scores for a total out of 50, so you can see what your weights changed.

Here's a worked example. Priya is a marketing manager in Vancouver (she's fictional, and so are these companies). Remote work and growth matter most to her, so her weights are: Culture 3, Growth 3, Roles 2, Pay 2, Industry 1. Her weights add up to 11.

| Company | Culture | Growth | Roles | Pay | Industry | Total /50 | Weighted /10 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Bluepeak Software | 9 | 8 | 9 | 7 | 8 | 41 | 8.3 |
| Lumen Health | 8 | 9 | 7 | 6 | 6 | 36 | 7.5 |
| Northwind Bank | 6 | 5 | 9 | 9 | 8 | 37 | 7.0 |

The maths for Lumen Health: (8 × 3) + (9 × 3) + (7 × 2) + (6 × 2) + (6 × 1) = 24 + 27 + 14 + 12 + 6 = 83. Then 83 ÷ 11 = 7.5.

Look at what happened. Northwind Bank has the higher plain total (37 against 36), but Lumen Health ranks higher for Priya because culture and growth matter most to her. That's the whole point of weighting. The ranking is yours, not some generic "best companies" list.

Dealbreakers sit on top of the maths. If Northwind Bank required five office days a week and Priya needs remote work, she'd flag it as a DEALBREAKER no matter what it scored.

---

## Step 6: Rank, tier and pick your top 3

Sort your companies by weighted score. Then sort them into tiers:

- **Strong fit:** 8.0 and up. Go after these first.
- **Good fit:** 6.0 to 7.9. Pursue these alongside your first tier.
- **Worth watching:** under 6.0. Set a Google Alert and check back.

Now pick your top 3. For each one, write the first move:

- There's an open role? Apply through the careers page, then email the person who leads the team.
- No open role? Email the person who leads the team anyway, with a short, specific note. (Guide 3 shows you how.)

## A few honest notes

- **Scores are judgment calls.** They're based on what you found, and what you found might be incomplete. That's why the evidence rating matters. A 7.8 with Thin evidence is a guess with a decimal point.
- **Company facts go stale fast.** An open role closes. A layoff gets announced. Check anything important again right before you act on it.
- **Stick to public, work-related information.** You're researching the company, not the people who work there.
- **A high score isn't a promise.** It's a smart place to aim. Results vary.
