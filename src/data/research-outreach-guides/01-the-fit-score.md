# The Fit Score

**Score any job out of 12 in a few minutes, so your time goes to the roles that actually fit.**

Here's a pattern I see all the time. Someone applies to everything that looks close. Forty applications, fifty, sixty. Each one gets a rushed resume and no follow-up, because there's no time left for follow-up. And then nothing comes back, and they decide the problem is them.

The problem usually isn't you. It's the spread. When you apply to everything, nothing gets your best work.

So let's flip it. Before you spend an hour on an application, give the posting a quick, honest score. Six parts, 0 to 2 points each, a total out of 12. High score: go all in (apply, then email the hiring manager). Low score: let it go without guilt.

I use this exact rubric when I score job postings. Now you can do it by hand, with the worksheet at the bottom of this guide, or let the cc-job-scan Claude skill do it for you.

**Time:** 5 to 10 minutes per posting. **What you'll need:** Your resume, the job posting, your target salary, and a pen or a spreadsheet.

---

## Before you score: is it fresh, and is it still open?

Two quick checks first. They save you from scoring a job that's already gone.

### Check 1: Was it posted in the last 7 days?

The newer the posting, the more likely the team is still reading applications. I focus on the last 7 days.

How to read the date:

- "Just posted", "Today" or "5 hours ago": fresh
- "3 days ago": fresh
- "1 week ago": right on the edge. Still worth a look.
- "2 weeks ago" or "30+ days ago": older. Not a hard no, but don't let it jump the queue.
- "Reposted": the role may have been open for a while. Ask about it if you get a call.
- No date at all: check the company's own careers page. It often shows the date the listing site doesn't.

### Check 2: Does the link still work?

Click it. Then look for any of these:

- An error page or "Job not found"
- You land on the general careers page or a search page instead of the posting
- "No longer accepting applications", "This position has been filled", "This posting has expired" or "This job has been closed"

Any of those means it's closed. Move on.

One more tip: apply on the company's own site when you can, not a copy on a job board. It's where your application actually lands, and it's less likely to be stale.

## The six parts

Each part scores 0, 1 or 2. No half points. If you're stuck between two numbers, pick the lower one. Be honest here. An inflated score only fools your future self.

### 1. Skills Match

Does your experience cover what they're asking for?

- **2:** 80% or more of the required skills match your skills and experience
- **1:** 50% to 79%
- **0:** under 50%

How to do it: list the **required** skills from the posting. Skip the "nice to haves" and "bonus points". Tick each one you can back up with something real from your work. Then divide.

Example: the posting asks for 6 things, and you can show 4. That's 4 ÷ 6 = 67%. Skills Match: 1.

If you have a skill but it's not on your resume, you still get the point. Then add it to your resume before you apply.

### 2. Experience Level

Is this the right rung of the ladder for you?

- **2:** the seniority matches (the years and the scope line up)
- **1:** one level up or down (for example, you're a senior specialist and this is a team lead role)
- **0:** two or more levels off

Look at three things: the years they ask for, the title level, and the scope (team size, budget, who you'd report to).

### 3. Industry Fit

Have you worked in this world before?

- **2:** you have direct experience in this industry
- **1:** a close industry, where your knowledge transfers (hospital operations to a health tech company, for example)
- **0:** no overlap

A 0 here isn't a dealbreaker if the rest is strong. Career changers score 0 on this part all the time and still land the role. It just means your email and resume need to explain why you're making the move.

### 4. Location / Remote

Does the work model fit your life?

- **2:** matches what you want exactly
- **1:** a partial match (hybrid when you want remote, more office days than you'd like, or a nearby city)
- **0:** you'd have to move, or it contradicts what you want

Watch for remote roles that only hire in one country. "Remote (US only)" is a 0 for someone in Canada who can't work in the US, and the reverse is true too.

### 5. Growth Potential

Does this move you toward the role you want next?

- **2:** a clear path to the next-level role you want
- **1:** a sideways move that builds new, useful skills
- **0:** a dead end, a step down, or no upside

Be honest with yourself on this one. A step down can be the right call (after a layoff, or when you're changing fields), but score it as what it is.

### 6. Compensation

Does the pay meet your number?

- **2:** meets or beats your target salary
- **1:** within 15% below your target, **or** pay isn't listed
- **0:** more than 15% below your target

How to do it: multiply your target by 0.85. That's your "15% below" line.

Example: your target is $90,000. $90,000 × 0.85 = $76,500.
- Range of $85,000 to $95,000: your target sits inside it. Score 2.
- Range of $75,000 to $80,000: the top is above $76,500. Score 1.
- Range of $65,000 to $72,000: the top is below $76,500. Score 0.

No pay listed? Score 1 and move on. You'll find out in the first call. (Some provinces and US states now require pay ranges in job postings, so check the posting carefully before you assume it's missing.)

This is general guidance, not legal or financial advice.

## Add it up and make the call

Add your six scores. Then:

| Score | The call | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| 9 to 12 | **APPLY** | A strong match. Apply this week, then find the hiring manager and send a short email. |
| 6 to 8 | **CONSIDER** | Worth applying, with gaps. Look at where you lost points (more on that below). |
| 0 to 5 | **SKIP** | A weak match. Spend the hour on a better one. |

If two jobs tie, go after the one posted most recently first. Still tied? Go with the one where your Skills Match is higher.

### What to do with a CONSIDER

Look at which parts lost points.

- **Skills or Experience:** can you close the gap with one strong example in your email or cover note? Then apply.
- **Industry:** can you explain the move in one sentence? Then apply.
- **Location or Compensation:** would you actually take the job like this? If the answer is no, let it go now. It's kinder to you than finding out at the offer stage.

## A worked example (fictional)

Sam is a project manager in Toronto with 7 years running projects at a hospital network. Sam has a PMP certification, wants a Senior Project Manager role (hybrid with up to 2 office days a week, or remote), and has a target salary of $105,000 CAD.

The posting: **Senior Project Manager at Lumen Health** (a fictional company) in Mississauga, Ontario. Hybrid, 4 days a week in the office. $96,000 to $110,000 CAD. Posted 2 days ago.

Required: 5+ years managing projects, PMP, budget management, vendor management, electronic health record (EHR) implementation, Agile delivery.

| Part | Score | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Skills Match | 1 | 4 of 6 required skills (67%). No EHR implementation or Agile delivery. |
| Experience Level | 2 | 7 years against 5+ asked, at the same project scope. |
| Industry Fit | 2 | Direct health care experience. |
| Location / Remote | 1 | Mississauga is close, but 4 office days is more than the 2 Sam wants. |
| Growth Potential | 2 | The senior title is exactly the next step. |
| Compensation | 2 | The $105,000 target sits inside the range. |
| **Total** | **10/12** | **APPLY** |

Sam's next move: apply on Lumen Health's careers page, then email the Director of the project office with one example of a hospital system rollout. And on the first call, ask about flexibility on office days, early, before anyone is attached.

## Spot the patterns

After 10 postings, you're sitting on real market research. Here's how to use it.

Go back through the postings and tally every requirement that shows up more than twice.

| Requirement | How many of my 10 postings ask for it | On my resume? | Do I have it? | What I'll do |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| [Skill, tool or certification] | __ of 10 | Yes / No | Yes / No | Add it / Learn it / Ignore it |
| | __ of 10 | | | |
| | __ of 10 | | | |

Three things to look for:

1. **Hidden wins.** A requirement that keeps showing up, that you have, but that isn't on your resume. Add it today. That's the easiest fix in your whole search.
2. **Real gaps.** A requirement in most of your postings that you don't have yet. That's your next thing to learn, or the thing your emails need to address head on.
3. **Other titles.** Note any other job titles you saw for the same work. Add them to your next search.

Also note how many postings listed pay, and how many were remote, hybrid and on-site. Count only what you actually saw. Ten postings tell you about those ten postings, not the whole market.

---

## A few honest notes

- **The score is a guide, not a verdict.** It helps you decide where your time goes. It doesn't predict who gets hired. Results vary.
- **Don't count the nice-to-haves.** Plenty of people get hired without every "bonus" skill. Score the required list only.
- **A dream company with a low score?** Look at why. If it's the role, keep an eye out for a better one there. If it's pay or location, decide now whether you'd really accept it.
- **Keep your facts true.** If you give yourself credit for a skill, make sure you can talk about it in an interview.
