# Tricky Moments

**Scripts 33 to 52: exploding offers, lowballs, competing offers, "this is our best offer", raises and promotions, and the fear that asking will cost you the job.**

> **This is general guidance, not legal or financial advice. Results vary.** If an offer is pulled after you've accepted it, or a contract clause worries you, talk to an employment lawyer in your province or state.

**Time:** 20 minutes to read. Jump to the moment you're in.
**What you'll need:** Your three numbers, the written offer, and a calm moment. Most of these replies work better after a deep breath than in the heat of the call.

## Exploding Offers

An exploding offer has a very short deadline: "We need an answer by tomorrow." Sometimes it's a real business need. Sometimes it's pressure.

Either way, asking for more time is reasonable. And how they respond tells you something about how they'll treat you once you work there.

### Script 33: Asking for more time

~~~text
Thank you, I'm really excited about this offer. I want to give it the serious thought it deserves, and [24 hours] is a little tight for a decision this big. Could we extend the deadline to [Day]?
~~~

If you're waiting on another process (only if true):

~~~text
I'm wrapping up one other process, and I want to make a clean decision without keeping anyone hanging. Could we extend the deadline to [Day]? I'll give you a firm answer by then either way.
~~~

**If they say no:** Decide based on your numbers and must-haves, not the clock. If the offer clears your walk-away and you'd be happy there, it may still be a yes. If not, you've learned something useful.

### Script 34: "Can you accept today?" (on the call)

~~~text
I'm very interested, and I don't want to keep you waiting. I make my best decisions with the details in front of me. If you can send the offer in writing today, I'll get back to you by [Day].
~~~

## Lowball Offers

A lowball is an offer well below the range you discussed, or well below the market data you found. Stay calm. It's a starting point, not an insult.

### Script 35: Well below the range you discussed

~~~text
Thank you for the offer. I want to be upfront: $[Their number] is well below the $[Low] to $[High] range we talked about in our first conversation, and below what I'm seeing for this role in [City or region]. Can you help me understand how you landed on that number? I'd really like to find a way to make this work.
~~~

**Why ask "how did you land on that?":** Sometimes it's a mistake, a different level than you expected, or a band you didn't know about. Their answer tells you which counter to use next (Guide 3).

### Script 36: Below your walk-away

~~~text
I'm excited about the role, so I want to be honest with you. At $[Their number], I can't say yes. To accept, I'd need the base closer to $[Target]. Is there any way to get there, through base or another part of the package?
~~~

**Notice what's missing:** your walk-away number. You don't share it. You say your target and let them tell you what's possible.

## Competing Offers

The rule here is simple: **only mention another offer if it's real.** Never invent one, never inflate the number. Employers talk, and a bluff that gets called can cost you both offers.

### Script 37: Telling your first choice you have another offer

~~~text
I want to be transparent with you. I've received another offer at $[Amount]. [Company] is my first choice because [genuine reason], so I wanted to come to you before I decide. Is there room to get closer to that number?
~~~

**Good to know:**
- You don't have to name the other company.
- Make it a question, not an ultimatum. "Match it or I'm gone" puts them in a corner.
- Compare total compensation, not just base. A higher base with weaker benefits might not be the better offer. (Guide 6 helps.)

### Script 38: Asking a company to speed up

You have an offer from Company B, but you'd rather work at Company A, which is still interviewing.

~~~text
I wanted to give you a heads-up. I've received an offer from another company, and I need to respond by [Date]. [Company] is the role I'm most excited about. Is there any way to speed up the next steps so I can make a decision with both options in front of me?
~~~

**Why it works:** It's honest, it's respectful of their process, and it gives them a real reason to move.

## "This Is Our Best Offer" and Other Pushback

### Script 39: "This is our best offer"

Pick one of three replies.

**The probe:**

~~~text
I understand there may be limits on base salary. Are there other parts of the package we could look at?
~~~

**The reframe:**

~~~text
I appreciate you telling me. If the base is fixed, could we talk about [a signing bonus / a six-month review in writing / a start date of [Date]]?
~~~

**The pause:**

~~~text
I respect the limits you're working with. I'd like to sleep on it. Can I get back to you by [Day]?
~~~

Then decide based on your walk-away number. If it clears it and your must-haves are met, a "best offer" can still be a good offer.

### Script 40: "This is our standard for this level"

~~~text
I appreciate the context. My experience goes beyond the typical profile for this level, specifically [experience or results that go beyond it]. I think that's worth an adjustment above the standard. Is there any room for that?
~~~

### Script 41: "We need to be fair to the rest of the team"

~~~text
I completely respect that, and I'd want the same if I were on the team. I'm not asking for anything outside the market range. Based on my research, $[Counter number] is competitive for this role, and I'm confident in the value I'll bring.
~~~

### Script 42: "We'll review your salary after the first year"

~~~text
I appreciate that. Could we make it a formal review at six months instead, with a target of $[Target] if I hit [specific goals]? I'd be comfortable with the current number if we put that in the offer letter.
~~~

### Script 43: "Take it or leave it"

~~~text
I understand. Could I have until [Day] to make my final decision?
~~~

Then go back to your sticky note. Above your walk-away and must-haves met? It may still be a yes. Below? Script 52.

## Raises and Promotions at Your Current Job

Asking your own manager feels different from negotiating with a stranger. The same rules apply: be specific, bring evidence, ask for one clear number, get it in writing.

**Before you ask, gather:**
- Three to five results from the past year, with numbers where you have them.
- Anything you've taken on that wasn't in your original job description.
- Market data for your role in your city (posted ranges are great for this).
- Timing: ask before budgets are set for next year, not after.

### Script 44: Asking for the meeting (email)

~~~text
Subject: Time to talk about my role

Hi [Manager name],

Could we set up 30 minutes in the next couple of weeks to talk about my role and compensation? I'd like to walk you through what I've taken on this year and talk about what's next.

Thanks,
[Your name]
~~~

### Script 45: The raise conversation

~~~text
Thanks for making the time. Over the past [time period], my role has grown. I've [result one], [result two], and taken on [new responsibility]. Based on that, and on market data for similar roles in [City], I'd like to ask for an increase to $[Number].

[Pause. Let them respond.]

What would it take to make that happen?
~~~

### Script 46: A promotion with a small raise

~~~text
Thank you, I'm excited about the promotion and I'm ready for it. I'd like to talk about the pay that comes with it. The new role includes [bigger scope, like managing the team or owning the budget]. For [New title] roles, the market range I've found is $[Low] to $[High], and the increase puts me at $[Their number]. Could we get to $[Number]?

If the budget is tight right now, could we set a date to revisit it, and put that in writing?
~~~

### Script 47: When the answer is "not now"

~~~text
I understand. Could you help me understand what would need to be true for this to happen? If we agree on specific goals and a date to revisit, I'll make sure I hit them. Could we put that in writing?
~~~

**Then follow through.** Send a short email after the meeting that recaps the goals and the date. Put a reminder in your calendar.

## When Your Current Employer Counter-Offers

You resign, and your manager comes back with more money to stay. It's flattering. Slow down before you decide.

**A quick check before you answer:**
- Does the counter fix the reasons you started looking, or only the pay?
- Did it take a resignation to get it? What does that tell you about the next raise?
- Is it in writing, with dates?
- How would you feel telling the new company no?

### Script 48: Buying time

~~~text
Thank you. I didn't expect this, and I want to give it real thought. Could I have until [Day] to think it through?
~~~

### Script 49: Declining the counter-offer

~~~text
I really appreciate the counter-offer, and it means a lot that you'd want me to stay. I've thought about it carefully. My decision isn't mainly about money. It's about [growth / direction / the kind of work I want to do next], and I've committed to the new role. I want to make the next [notice period] as smooth as possible for the team.
~~~

If you decide to stay instead, get every change in writing before you withdraw from the other offer.

## "Will Negotiating Get My Offer Pulled?"

This fear stops a lot of people from asking at all, so let's talk about it honestly.

It can happen. Offers do get withdrawn sometimes. But a polite, specific ask is a normal part of hiring. The risk goes up when:

- The ask is aggressive, or framed as an ultimatum.
- There's a bluff (a fake offer, a fake deadline).
- The asks keep coming, round after round.
- The company's budget or plans change (which has nothing to do with you).

You control the first three. Be kind, be specific, keep it to one round (two at most), and you've done what you can.

### Script 50: The reassurance line

Use this mid-negotiation if you're worried the ask is landing badly.

~~~text
I want to be clear: I'm excited about this role and I want to make it work. I'm asking because I want us to start on the right foot.
~~~

### Script 51: If they say the offer is withdrawn

Only use this if you'd still take the original offer.

~~~text
I'm sorry to hear that, and I'm a little surprised, since I was asking in good faith. I'm still very interested in the role. If the original offer of $[Their number] is still possible, I'd be glad to accept it.
~~~

**If an offer is pulled after you've accepted** (especially if you've already resigned), talk to an employment lawyer in your province or state. Your options depend on where you live and what you signed.

## Walking Away

### Script 52: Declining gracefully

~~~text
Thank you for working with me on this. I've given it serious thought, and the total package is below what I need to accept. This wasn't an easy decision, because I'm genuinely impressed by [Company] and the team.

If the budget changes or the role grows, I'd welcome a future conversation. I wish you the best in finding the right person.
~~~

**Walk away when:**
- The final offer is below your walk-away number.
- They won't move on anything (salary, benefits, flexibility).
- Your must-haves aren't met.
- Your gut says no. Listen to it.

**Think twice when:**
- You're walking away out of pride, not math.
- The total package (not just base) actually meets your needs.
- You have no other options and the offer is very close to your walk-away.
