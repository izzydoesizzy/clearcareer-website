# The Recruiter Call and the Salary Question

**Scripts 1 to 11: exactly what to say when someone asks "What are your salary expectations?"**

> **This is general guidance, not legal or financial advice. Results vary.** These scripts are starting points. Adapt them to your role, your market and your own way of talking.

**Time:** 15 minutes to read, then 5 minutes of practice before each call.
**What you'll need:** Your three numbers (anchor, target, walk-away) and the job posting. Check it for a posted pay range.

---

The salary question usually comes up in the first or second conversation. Often before you know much about the role.

That's not a trick. The recruiter wants to make sure you're both in the same ballpark before everyone spends hours on interviews. Fair enough.

Your goal on this call is simple:

1. Learn their range.
2. Stay in the running.
3. Don't lock yourself into a number too early.

## Two-Minute Prep Before Any Recruiter Call

- Your three numbers on a sticky note in front of you.
- The posted range (if there is one) written next to them.
- One sentence about the value you bring. For example: "I've spent five years running [type of project] for [type of team]."
- A decision: will you talk about base salary or total compensation? Saying "total compensation" gives you room later.

---

## When They Ask Your Expectations

### Script 1: Ask for their range first

~~~text
That's a fair question. I'm still learning about the scope of the role, so I'd rather not put a number out too early. Could you share the range you've budgeted for this position?
~~~

If you want a version that shows you've done your homework:

~~~text
I've done some research and I have a good sense of the market for this kind of role. But I'd love to hear what you've budgeted first, so I can tell you whether we're in the same ballpark.
~~~

**Why it works:** You're polite, you're not dodging, and you hand the first number back to the person who already knows the budget.

### Script 2: When the posting shows a range

~~~text
I saw the posted range of $[Low] to $[High]. Based on what I've heard about the scope so far, I'd expect to be in the upper part of that range. Is that range still current for this role?
~~~

**Use it when:** Your experience genuinely fits the upper part of the range. If you're newer to this kind of role, say "within that range" instead, and save the upper-end case for when you counter.

### Script 3: When they insist you go first

~~~text
Sure. Based on my research for [Job title] roles at this level in [City or region], I'm seeing about $[Target] to $[Anchor]. I'd expect something in that range, depending on the full package. Does that line up with your budget?
~~~

**Key move:** Start your range at your **target**, not your walk-away. The bottom of any range you say is the number they'll hear.

### Script 4: Anchor with total compensation

~~~text
Based on my [Number] years in [Specialty] and the market data I've looked at for [Job title] roles in [City or region], I'm targeting around $[Anchor] in total compensation. Is that within your range?
~~~

**Use it when:** They reached out to you, your skills are hard to find, or you're in other hiring processes right now. Only mention other processes if they're real.

**Why "total compensation":** It leaves room to split the number across base, bonus, equity and other parts of the package later.

### Script 5: When they ask what you make now

~~~text
I'd rather focus on what this role is worth than on my current pay, since the scope is different. Based on my research, roles like this are paying about $[Target] to $[Anchor]. What range do you have in mind?
~~~

If they push:

~~~text
I keep my current compensation private, but I'm happy to share what I'm looking for. For this role, I'm targeting $[Target] to $[Anchor].
~~~

**Good to know:** Some provinces and states limit or ban employers from asking about your pay history. Either way, you don't have to answer.

### Script 6: The online application form

If the salary field accepts text, type:

~~~text
Negotiable, based on the full package
~~~

Or a range:

~~~text
$[Target] to $[Anchor]
~~~

If the field only accepts a number, enter your target or your anchor. Never your walk-away. Avoid joke values like 0 or 1: they look careless, and some systems may screen them out.

## When You Hear Their Range

### Script 7: Their range is below your walk-away

~~~text
Thank you for being upfront about that. It's below what I'm targeting for this level of role. Before we both put more time in, is there any flexibility in that range, or room to look at this at a more senior level?
~~~

**Why it matters:** It's much better to find this out now than after four rounds of interviews. If there's no flexibility, you can decide calmly whether to keep going.

### Script 8: Their range works for you

~~~text
Thanks, that's helpful. It sounds like we're in the same ballpark. I'll want to see the full package before we land on a final number, but I'm happy to keep the conversation going.
~~~

**Watch out:** Don't say "Perfect, that's great!" Even if it is. Over-the-moon tells them the bottom of their range works for you.

### Script 9: Ask about the full package and the process

~~~text
Could you walk me through the rest of the package? I'm curious about the bonus structure, benefits, vacation, and how flexible the team is on remote work. And what does the timeline look like from here?
~~~

**Why it works:** You learn what's negotiable later, and you find out how fast you need to move.

## Later in the Process

### Script 10: The hiring manager asks in a late interview

If you already gave the recruiter a range:

~~~text
I'm really excited about this role, and I'm confident we'll land on something fair. I shared a range of $[Target] to $[Anchor] with [Recruiter name], and after today that still feels right. Once I see the full offer, I'm happy to talk through the details.
~~~

If you haven't given a number yet:

~~~text
Based on what I've learned about the scope today, I'd expect total compensation in the range of $[Target] to $[Anchor]. I'm flexible on how we put the package together.
~~~

### Script 11: "If we offered $X, would you accept?"

~~~text
That's in the range I'd seriously consider. I'd want to see the full offer in writing, including the bonus, benefits and vacation, before I commit. But I'm very interested, and I'd look forward to seeing it.
~~~

**Why it works:** You stay warm without saying yes to a number before you've seen the whole package. If $X is below your target, swap the first line for: "That's a little below what I was expecting. I'd want to see the full package before I can say yes."

## Practise Before the Call

Say your chosen scripts out loud three times. Then have someone ask you the question in a few different ways:

- "What are you looking for, salary-wise?"
- "What's your number?"
- "We need a figure before I can move you forward."
- "What are you making now?"

If you have the Claude skill installed, type: **"Play a recruiter and ask me the salary question three different ways. Give me feedback after each answer."**

## Cheat Sheet

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| Ask for their range first | Name your walk-away number |
| Start any range at your target | Share your current salary because you feel you have to |
| Say "total compensation" | Say "I'm flexible" without a number |
| Ask about the full package | Celebrate their range out loud |
| Find out early if the range is too low | Wait until the offer to learn you're far apart |
