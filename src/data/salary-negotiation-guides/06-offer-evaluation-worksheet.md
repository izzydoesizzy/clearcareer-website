# Offer Evaluation Worksheet

**Compare up to three options side by side: total compensation, must-haves, deal-breakers and the things a salary number doesn't show.**

> **This is general guidance, not legal or financial advice. Results vary.** For tax questions (especially equity) talk to an accountant. For contract clauses, talk to an employment lawyer.

**Time:** 30 to 45 minutes per offer.
**What you'll need:** Each written offer, your three numbers, your current pay stub and benefits summary, and a calculator. Print this page or copy it into a doc.

---

Here's the thing: two offers with the same base salary can be very different jobs. Bonus, benefits, vacation and flexibility all add up. This worksheet makes the full picture visible so you can decide with your head, not just your nerves.

Use the third column for your current job if you're weighing an offer against staying (for example, after a counter-offer).

## Part 2: Collect the Details

Before you can compare, you need the facts. For each offer, tick off what you know. Anything missing becomes a question for the recruiter (Guide 3, Script 14).

- [ ] Base salary
- [ ] Bonus target, and how it has actually paid out
- [ ] Signing bonus, and any clawback terms
- [ ] Equity: type, amount, vesting schedule, latest value
- [ ] Retirement: RRSP match or pension (Canada), 401(k) match (US)
- [ ] Health and dental: what's covered, and what you'd pay
- [ ] Vacation days, and whether they increase over time
- [ ] Remote or hybrid arrangement, in writing
- [ ] Learning budget or other stipends
- [ ] Start date and when benefits start
- [ ] Probation period
- [ ] Title and who you'd report to
- [ ] Decision deadline

| Learning budget and stipends | $_____ | $_____ | $_____ | |
| Commute and remote savings | $_____ | $_____ | $_____ | |
| **Total, year one** | **$_____** | **$_____** | **$_____** | |
| **Total, year two onward** | **$_____** | **$_____** | **$_____** | Leave out one-time money |
| Vacation (days) | _____ | _____ | _____ | |

### How to fill in each line

- **Base salary:** The number in the offer letter.
- **Annual bonus:** Target percentage times base. Then adjust if you've learned it usually pays out more or less than target. If you don't know, write the target and mark it "(estimate, confirm)".
- **Signing bonus:** Count it in year one only. If it has to be repaid when you leave early, note that.
- **Equity:** The value that vests each year, using today's value, not hoped-for growth. For a private company, write "uncertain" next to it. Example: a grant worth $40,000 today that vests over four years counts as about $10,000 a year.
- **Retirement:** The dollar amount the employer adds. Example: a 4% match on a $90,000 base is $3,600 a year. (Example numbers only.)
- **Health and dental:** In the US, this can be a big difference between offers, so compare premiums and deductibles. In Canada, compare extended health, dental and drug coverage, and what you'd pay out of pocket.
- **Commute and remote savings:** Transit, gas, parking, lunches. A rough yearly number is fine. Mark it as your estimate.
- **Vacation:** Keep it in days. If you want a dollar figure, divide base by about 260 working days to get a day's pay, then multiply.

### Quick check

- Is Offer A's year-two total at or above my walk-away? Yes / No
- Is Offer B's year-two total at or above my walk-away? Yes / No
- Which offer's base is closest to my target? _____

**Why year two matters:** Signing bonuses and one-time payments make year one look bigger. Year two shows what the job pays once that's gone.

## Part 8: Before You Sign

Read the final offer letter line by line. Tick each one.

- [ ] Title matches what we agreed
- [ ] Base salary matches
- [ ] Bonus terms are written down (target, timing, conditions)
- [ ] Signing bonus amount and any clawback terms are clear
- [ ] Equity: amount, vesting schedule and any cliff are written down
- [ ] Vacation days match
- [ ] Remote or hybrid arrangement is in writing
- [ ] Start date is right
- [ ] I know when benefits start
- [ ] I know the length of any probation period
- [ ] I've read any non-compete, non-solicit and intellectual property clauses (and asked an employment lawyer if something worries me)
- [ ] I know any conditions (background check, references) and when they'll be cleared
- [ ] Any relocation repayment terms are clear
- [ ] Every promise from our conversations (like a six-month review) is in the letter

**Don't resign from your current job until the signed offer is back and every condition is cleared.**
