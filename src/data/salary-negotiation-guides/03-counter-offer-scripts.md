# Counter-Offer Scripts (Email and Phone)

**Scripts 12 to 21: from "We'd like to make you an offer" to "I'm happy to accept."**

> **This is general guidance, not legal or financial advice. Results vary.** No script guarantees a higher offer. These give you clear, polite words so you're not improvising under pressure.

**Time:** 20 minutes to read, then 15 minutes to fill in your version.
**What you'll need:** The written offer, your three numbers, your completed worksheet from Guide 6, and one or two real results from your work you can point to.

---

## The Shape of Every Counter

Every counter in this guide follows the same four beats:

1. **Thanks.** Grateful and specific. Name something real you're excited about.
2. **Reasons.** The scope of the role, your results, and the market data you found. Not your bills.
3. **The ask.** One clear number for each thing you want.
4. **Together.** A warm close that makes it a shared problem to solve.

Then stop. Let them answer.

**Which number do you ask for?** Ask above your target, so there's room to meet in the middle and still land at your target or better. Your anchor is a good counter number if you can back it up.

**Keep your alternatives in your back pocket.** If your first message says "or a signing bonus would also work," you've handed them an easy way to pick the cheaper option. Ask for what you want first. Bring out the alternatives (Guide 4) if base is firm.

## When the Offer Arrives

### Script 12: The verbal offer (phone)

Your only goals on this call: say thank you, get it in writing, and set a date.

~~~text
Thank you so much. I'm really excited about this, especially [specific thing about the role or team]. Could you send me the full offer in writing so I can review everything properly? I'd like to get back to you by [Day]. Does that work on your end?
~~~

A shorter version, if you're caught off guard:

~~~text
Thank you, that's great news. Could you send the details in writing? I'll review everything and get back to you by [Day].
~~~

**Don't** say yes or no on this call, even if the number is better than you hoped. 24 to 72 hours is a normal ask.

### Script 13: Email confirming you got it

~~~text
Subject: Thank you: [Job title] offer

Hi [First name],

Thank you for the offer for the [Job title] role. I'm excited about the chance to [specific thing you'd work on].

I'm reviewing the details now and will get back to you by [Day and date]. If anything changes with timing on your end, please let me know.

Thanks again,
[Your name]
~~~

### Script 14: Questions before you counter (email)

You can't evaluate what you can't see. Ask before you negotiate.

~~~text
Subject: Re: [Job title] offer

Hi [First name],

Thanks again for sending the offer. Before I respond, I have a few quick questions so I understand the full package:

1. What's the target bonus, and how has it paid out in recent years?
2. When do health and dental benefits start?
3. [For equity: What type of equity is it, how many units, what's the vesting schedule, and what's the latest valuation or share price?]
4. What's the vacation policy, and does it increase with years of service?
5. Is the [hybrid or remote] arrangement written into the offer letter?

Thanks so much,
[Your name]
~~~

**Tip:** Delete any question that doesn't apply. Add any that do (relocation, probation period, retirement match).

## The Counter

### Script 15: Counter by email (base salary)

~~~text
Subject: Re: [Job title] offer

Hi [First name],

Thank you again for the offer. I'm excited about joining [Company] and working on [specific project or goal you discussed].

I've reviewed the full package, and the one area I'd like to discuss is base salary. Based on the scope we talked about, including [specific responsibility], and my research on [Job title] roles in [City or region], I was hoping for a base salary of $[Counter number].

In my current role, I [specific result with a number, if you have one], and that's the kind of work I'm ready to bring to your team.

If we can get there, I'm ready to accept. Would you be open to a quick call this week to talk it through?

Best,
[Your name]
~~~

**Optional data line** (only with real data you found): "Postings for similar roles in [City] list ranges of $[Low] to $[High]."

**Only keep "If we can get there, I'm ready to accept" if it's true.** It's a strong line because it tells them exactly what closes the deal. Don't use it if you'd still want to negotiate other things.

### Script 16: Counter by phone (base salary)

~~~text
You: Thanks so much for sending the offer over. I've reviewed everything and I'm really excited about the role, especially [specific thing]. There's one thing I'd like to talk through.

Them: Sure, go ahead.

You: The base salary of $[Their number] is a bit below what I was expecting. Based on the scope we discussed and my research for this role in [City or region], I was hoping we could get to $[Counter number]. Is there flexibility there?

[Stop talking. Let them answer, even if the silence feels long.]

If they say yes:
You: That's great. Thank you for working with me on this. Could you send the updated offer in writing?

If they need to check:
You: Of course. Thank you for taking it back to the team. When do you think you'll have an answer?

If base is firm:
You: I understand. Would you be open to looking at other parts of the package to close the gap? [Go to Guide 4 for the specific asks.]
~~~

**Before the call:** Write your counter number at the top of a page in big letters. Look at it when you say it.

### Script 17: Multi-item counter by email

Use this when the offer is short in more than one place. Two or three asks, ranked, all in one message.

~~~text
Subject: Re: [Job title] offer

Hi [First name],

Thank you for the offer. I'm genuinely excited about the role and the team, and I want to make this work.

After reviewing the full package, there are two areas I'd like to discuss:

Base salary: Based on the scope of the role and my research for [Job title] roles in [City or region], I was hoping for $[Counter number].

[Second item, for example vacation]: I currently have [Number] weeks of vacation, and I'd like to keep something comparable. Would [Number] weeks be possible?

[Optional third item, for example start date]: I'd also like to start on [Date] so I can hand off my current work properly.

What matters most to me is that the full package reflects the role. Would you be open to a call to talk these through?

Best,
[Your name]
~~~

### Script 18: Multi-item counter by phone

~~~text
You: Thank you again for the offer. I'm excited, and I want to make this work. After going through the details, there are two things I'd like to talk about.

First, base salary. Based on the scope and my research, I was hoping for $[Counter number].

Second, [vacation / start date / remote days]. [One sentence on what you'd like and why.]

[Stop talking. Let them respond.]

If they can do one but not the other:
You: Thank you, I appreciate that. Help me understand what's possible on [the other item]. Is there anything we could do there?
~~~

**Lead with the ask that matters most.** If they only say yes to one thing, you want it to be that one.

## After You Counter

### Script 19: When you haven't heard back

Wait about three business days, then:

~~~text
Hi [First name],

Just checking in on my note from [Day]. I'm still very excited about the role and want to keep things moving. Is there anything you need from me?

Thanks,
[Your name]
~~~

One check-in is enough. They may be waiting on an approval.

### Script 20: When they come back partway

They offer more, but not all of it. You can accept, or make one final, small nudge.

To accept:

~~~text
Thank you for going back to the team on this. I really appreciate it. That works for me. Could you send the updated offer letter?
~~~

To nudge once (only if the gap still matters to you):

~~~text
Thank you for going back to the team. I really appreciate the movement. If we can get to $[Number between their new number and your counter], I'm ready to sign today.
~~~

**One nudge, then decide.** Going back and forth over and over costs goodwill you'll want on your first day.

### Script 21: Accepting and confirming the terms (email)

Use this to accept, or as a recap right after you agree on a call.

~~~text
Subject: Re: [Job title] offer: happy to accept

Hi [First name],

I'm thrilled to accept the offer for the [Job title] role. Thank you for working with me on the details.

To confirm what we agreed:
Base salary: $[Amount]
[Signing bonus: $[Amount]]
[Vacation: [Number] weeks]
[Remote or hybrid: [Arrangement]]
Start date: [Date]

Could you send the updated offer letter with these terms? I'll sign as soon as it arrives. And please let me know the next steps for onboarding.

Looking forward to getting started,
[Your name]
~~~

**Don't resign from your current job yet.** Wait until the signed offer is back and any conditions (background check, references) are cleared.

## Cheat Sheet

| Situation | Script |
|---|---|
| You just got a verbal offer | 12, then 13 |
| You need details before you can decide | 14 |
| One thing to fix: base | 15 (email) or 16 (phone) |
| Two or three things to fix | 17 (email) or 18 (phone) |
| No reply after three business days | 19 |
| They met you partway | 20 |
| You're ready to say yes | 21 |
| Base is firm | Guide 4 |
| They push back hard | Guide 5 |

*This is general guidance, not legal or financial advice. Results vary.*
