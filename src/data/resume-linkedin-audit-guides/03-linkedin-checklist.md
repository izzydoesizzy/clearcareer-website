# LinkedIn Checklist

**The 24 checks behind every LinkedIn audit, so you can score your own profile in 20 minutes.**

This is the exact rubric the LinkedIn skill uses. Five areas (photo, banner, headline, About and Experience) and the rules that turn your ticks into a verdict. No AI needed.

Your LinkedIn profile is a billboard. People decide in a glance whether to keep reading, so the top of your profile does most of the work. That's why we start there.

Same rule as the resume checklist: tick a box only when you can point at the proof. "I think my headline has keywords" doesn't count. Find them.

**Time:** 20 to 30 minutes. **What you'll need:** Your LinkedIn profile open on a computer, your phone, a pen, and two or three job posts for the role you want.

---

## How to score yourself

1. Open your profile and click **see more** on your About section and your 3 most recent roles.
2. Work through one area at a time. Tick a box only when the check is fully met.
3. **REQUIRED** checks matter most. If you miss one, that area can't get the top verdict.
4. Use the verdict rules under each area to pick your verdict.
5. Fill in the scorecard at the end, then fix in this order: Needs Attention, then Mixed Signals.

---

## Four quick tests first

- **The search test.** Search your target job title in LinkedIn's search bar and click **People**. Look at how the headlines appear in the results. Now picture yours in that list. Would you click on it?
- **The phone test.** Open your profile on your phone. Is any banner text hidden behind your photo? Does your headline get cut off before the important part?
- **The "see more" test.** Read only the lines of your About section that show before "see more". Would a stranger click to read the rest?
- **The blank-role test.** Scroll through your Experience. Count the recent roles with nothing written under them.

---

## Area 1: Profile photo

- [ ] **REQUIRED: Photo exists.** There's a real photo, not LinkedIn's default grey silhouette.
- [ ] **REQUIRED: Solo headshot.** Just you. Not a group shot, not cropped out of a group, not a photo with your dog or from your vacation.
- [ ] **Face clearly visible.** Your face is the clear subject and fills most of the frame, not a small figure in a wide shot.
- [ ] **Suits your field.** Your clothing fits your industry and target role. Both extremes (very formal for a casual field, beachwear for anything) work against you.
- [ ] **Good light, clean background.** Even light on your face and a plain or non-distracting background. No harsh shadows, clutter or party settings.

You don't need a professional photographer. A phone camera, a window in front of you and a plain wall behind you will do the job.

**Your verdict:**
- **Needs Attention:** no photo, or not a solo headshot, or a photo that would undercut your credibility at first glance.
- **Mixed Signals:** a solo photo, but with big quality issues: very poor lighting, very casual for your target role, or a distracting background.
- **Good Foundation:** a professional, recognizable headshot with small issues (a slightly off crop) that don't hurt.
- **Strong:** confident, approachable and professional. Good light, clean background, clothing that fits your field.

---

## Area 2: Banner

The banner is the wide image behind your photo at the top of your profile.

- [ ] **REQUIRED: Custom banner.** You've replaced LinkedIn's plain default background with your own image.
- [ ] **Looks intentional.** It looks deliberate and well made, not like a random photo or a rushed image.
- [ ] **Fits your work.** It reflects your field, profession or brand. Not a holiday photo with no link to your work.
- [ ] **Says something.** It has text such as your name, your headline, a tagline, a credential or a key result.

Your photo covers part of the banner's left side, so check on a computer and on your phone that none of your text is hidden behind it. Free design tools have LinkedIn banner templates sized for you.

**Your verdict:**
- **Needs Attention:** the default banner is still there.
- **Mixed Signals:** custom, but a generic stock photo, a personal photo with no link to your work, or low quality.
- **Good Foundation:** custom, professional and relevant to your field, even without text.
- **Strong:** custom, well made, and it tells a visitor something about who you are and what you do.

---

## Area 3: Headline

The headline is the line right under your name.

- [ ] **REQUIRED: More than title and company.** It goes beyond "Marketing Manager at Acme Corp", which is what LinkedIn fills in by default.
- [ ] **REQUIRED: Value or difference.** It says what you do and for whom, or the result you deliver. Not just a label.
- [ ] **Search keywords.** It includes the words recruiters search for in your target role. Check your job posts: which titles and skills come up again and again?
- [ ] **Not too long.** Under about 200 characters (LinkedIn allows 220), with the most important words first, because long headlines get cut off in search results and on phones.
- [ ] **Distinctive.** It would stand out in a list of 20 people with your title, instead of sounding like all of them.

**What a fix looks like** (a fictional example: Priya, a payroll specialist):

> Before: Payroll Specialist at Acme Foods
>
> After: Payroll Specialist | Multi-Province Payroll for 600+ Employees | Year-End Reporting and Payroll Audits

The second one tells a recruiter her scope and her specialties, and it's full of words they'd search for.

**Your verdict:**
- **Needs Attention:** it's the default "Title at Company" with nothing added, or it says nothing about your value.
- **Mixed Signals:** it has more than a title, but it's generic, stuffed with keywords that don't say anything, or sounds like everyone else.
- **Good Foundation:** clear value and relevant keywords. Could be more distinctive.
- **Strong:** says right away who you are, what you do and why you're worth a click. Keywords and a distinctive angle.

---

## Area 4: About section

- [ ] **REQUIRED: Not empty.** You've written something. An empty About section is a missed chance to show up in search and tell your story.
- [ ] **REQUIRED: A hook up top.** Your first line or two make a reader want to click "see more". Only the first few lines show before it.
- [ ] **First person, a real story.** It reads like you wrote it about yourself. Not a buzzword list, not a third-person bio, not your resume summary pasted in.
- [ ] **Keywords.** The words from your target role are woven in naturally, so you show up in LinkedIn search.
- [ ] **Clear call to action.** It ends by inviting people to connect, send a message or reach out about something specific. If you're comfortable, add a professional email address.

LinkedIn gives you up to 2,600 characters here. You don't need all of them. Three to five short paragraphs is plenty.

**Your verdict:**
- **Needs Attention:** empty, or an opening so generic there's no reason to read on.
- **Mixed Signals:** there's a hook, but the rest is a resume dump or a list of buzzwords with no sense of the person.
- **Good Foundation:** a readable story with a hook and some keywords. May be missing a call to action.
- **Strong:** a first-person story with a strong hook, natural keywords and a clear call to action. It sounds like a person.

---

## Area 5: Experience

- [ ] **REQUIRED: Roles have descriptions.** At least your 2 or 3 most recent roles have something written under them.
- [ ] **Achievement-focused.** More than half your bullets describe outcomes, not just duties.
- [ ] **Numbers.** At least some descriptions include numbers, percentages, dollar amounts or other measurable results.
- [ ] **Strong action verbs.** Bullets open with strong verbs (Led, Built, Grew, Delivered). No "Responsible for".
- [ ] **Results structure.** Bullets follow Challenge, Action, Result, not a flat list of tasks.

Your resume is the fastest source here. Your best resume bullets can go straight into your Experience section. Just make sure the job titles and dates match your resume exactly.

**Your verdict:**
- **Needs Attention:** one or more recent roles are blank, or every description is duties only.
- **Mixed Signals:** descriptions exist, but they read like a job posting: duties, no numbers, no results.
- **Good Foundation:** a mix of duties and achievements, with some numbers.
- **Strong:** achievement-rich, with numbers and strong verbs. A recruiter could understand your impact from your profile alone.

## What to fix first

Start at the top of your profile and work down. The top is what everyone sees.

- **Quick wins (under 30 minutes each):** add a photo, swap the default banner, rewrite your headline.
- **Bigger jobs (an hour or two):** your About section, then your 3 most recent roles.
- **Leave Good Foundation and Strong alone** until everything else is done.

When you're done, score yourself again with a fresh copy, or run the LinkedIn skill and ask for a re-audit with new screenshots.

Results vary. A profile that scores well can't guarantee recruiter messages or interviews. But it makes sure that when someone does look you up, they find a reason to reach out.
