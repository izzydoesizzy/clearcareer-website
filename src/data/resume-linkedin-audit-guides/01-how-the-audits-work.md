# How the Audits Work

**The same checklist I use to review resumes and LinkedIn profiles, in three depths you pick.**

When I open someone's resume, I'm not hunting for typos first. I'm asking one question: if a hiring manager skimmed this for a few seconds, would they know who you are and why you're worth a call?

Then I go area by area with a checklist. Each area gets a verdict. Each verdict comes with evidence from your actual resume or profile, never generic advice. That's the method inside this kit. I turned it into two Claude skills (one for your resume, one for your LinkedIn) and two checklists you can use on your own.

This guide walks you through the three depths, what to upload, how to grab screenshots and the LinkedIn PDF, what to do with the results, and how to re-check your work after you edit.

**Time:** 5 minutes to set up. 10 to 30 minutes to read and act on an audit. **What you'll need:** Your resume (a PDF is best), your LinkedIn profile, and a target role or a job post.

---

## The four verdicts

Every area of your resume or profile gets one of these:

| Verdict | What it means | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| **Needs Attention** | Something basic is missing or broken. | Fix these first. |
| **Mixed Signals** | The basics are there, but something sends the wrong message. | Fix these next. |
| **Good Foundation** | It works. A few small fixes would make it sharper. | Polish once the bigger stuff is done. |
| **Strong** | It's doing its job. | Leave it alone. Seriously. |

The LinkedIn audit has one more status: **Not checked**. Your photo and banner can only be judged from a screenshot. If you don't send one, the audit says so instead of guessing.

Each verdict comes from a checklist with a few REQUIRED checks. Miss a REQUIRED check and that area can't get the top verdict, no matter how good the rest looks. Guides 02 and 03 show you every check.

## The three depths

### Snapshot

**What you get:** a one-paragraph overall impression, a table with a verdict and one line for each area, and the single fix to make first.

**Use it when:** you want a quick read before you spend time on anything. It's also a nice way to check a friend's resume.

### Full audit (the default)

**What you get:** the overall impression, then every area with its verdict, a short headline (6 to 10 words) and 3 bullet points of evidence. It ends with your top 3 fixes, in order.

**Use it when:** you're ready to work and want to know exactly what's holding you back. It tells you what to change and where. It doesn't write the new words for you.

### Rewrite Guide

**What you get:** your actual lines, quoted. For each one: what's not working, the rewrite and why it's better. For your resume, that's your summary, your weakest bullets, your numbers, your keywords and your positioning. For LinkedIn, it's 3 headline options with my pick, a full About section, and 3 rewritten bullets for each of your 3 most recent roles.

**Use it when:** you're ready to make the changes.

The Rewrite Guide only uses facts that are already on your resume or profile (or that you tell it). When a number would make a line stronger and it isn't there, you'll see **[add a number]**. At the end there's a short list of questions to help you find each one.

| Depth | What you get | Rewrites? | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Snapshot | Verdict table and your first fix | No | A quick read |
| Full audit | Verdict, headline and evidence for every area, plus top 3 fixes | No | Knowing exactly what to fix |
| Rewrite Guide | Your lines quoted and rewritten | Yes | Making the changes |

If you're not sure, start with the Full audit. That's what the skills do if you don't pick.

## What to upload

### For the resume audit

- **Your resume.** Upload the PDF if you can, because layout is part of the audit. A Word file works too. Pasted text works, but then the audit can't check your layout and will tell you so.
- **Your target role or a job post.** A job title is enough to start. A real job post is better, because the keyword check compares your resume to the words in that post. Paste the full text of the post. A link alone often won't open for an AI tool.

You can remove your phone number and street address before you upload. The audit doesn't need them.

### For the LinkedIn audit

You have three options. Screenshots are best.

1. **Screenshots** of your profile. This is the only way to get your photo and banner checked.
2. **The PDF export** of your profile. Great for your headline, About and Experience text. It doesn't show your banner, or your photo the way visitors see it, so add one screenshot of the top of your profile.
3. **Pasted text.** Copy your headline, About section and Experience descriptions into the chat.

Also send your **target role**, and your **resume** if you have it. The resume gives the Rewrite Guide real achievements to work with, and lets the audit check that your job titles and dates match.

## How to take screenshots of your profile

Do this on a computer if you can. The text is easier to read and you can fit more in.

1. Go to LinkedIn, click **Me** at the top, then **View Profile**.
2. Click **see more** in your About section, and in each of your 3 most recent roles, so the full text shows.
3. Take your screenshots:
   - **Windows:** press Windows key + Shift + S, then drag over the area you want.
   - **Mac:** press Command + Shift + 4, then drag over the area you want.
   - **iPhone:** press the side button and volume up together.
   - **Android:** on most phones, press power and volume down together.
4. Grab 3 to 5 shots:
   - The top of your profile: banner, photo, name, headline
   - Your About section, fully open
   - Your Experience section, 1 or 2 shots to cover your 3 most recent roles
   - Optional: Featured and Skills

**Tip:** zoom your browser out a little (Ctrl and minus on Windows, Command and minus on a Mac) to fit more in each shot. Keep the text readable.

If a screenshot shows your email or phone number, crop it out or leave it. The audit won't repeat it back.

## How to save your profile as a PDF

1. On a computer, go to your LinkedIn profile.
2. Click the **More** button under your name and headline.
3. Click **Save to PDF**. The file downloads to your computer.
4. Upload that PDF to Claude, along with one screenshot of the top of your profile.

The mobile app may not have this option, so use a web browser. LinkedIn moves its buttons around from time to time. If you can't find **More**, look for **Save to PDF** in the menus under your name.

## How to run an audit

**In Claude, with the skills installed,** just ask:

- "Here's my resume. I'm going for operations manager roles. Do a full audit."
- "Snapshot my resume against this job post." (then paste the posting)
- "Here are screenshots of my LinkedIn. Give me the Rewrite Guide. I'm going for UX research roles."

The skill asks for anything missing in one message, then gets to work.

**In any other AI tool,** open the prompt cards on this page:

1. Copy the **rubric** prompt (resume or LinkedIn) and paste it into a new chat.
2. Copy the prompt for the depth you want, fill in the [square brackets], and paste it in with your resume, PDF or screenshots.
3. Check every fact in the answer before you use it.

## How to use the results

Here's what I want you to do with your audit:

1. **Read the bold lines first.** The bold sentence at the top, the verdicts and the bold labels are the skim layer. Read only those and you'll have the full picture in a minute or two.
2. **Fix in verdict order.** Every Needs Attention area first, starting with failed REQUIRED checks. Then Mixed Signals. Good Foundation can wait.
3. **Work the "Fix these first" list one item at a time.** Three fixes done today beat fifteen fixes planned for "this weekend".
4. **Don't paste rewrites blindly.** Read each one out loud. Does it sound like you? Could you explain every word in an interview? Change anything that doesn't feel true.
5. **Fill every [add a number].** Check old reports, emails, performance reviews, dashboards and your sent folder. If you can only estimate, make it an honest estimate you can explain. Never send a resume with the brackets still in it.
6. **Make your resume and LinkedIn match.** Same job titles, same employers, same dates. The wording can differ. The facts can't.
7. **Save your audit.** Copy it into a doc. You'll want it for the re-audit.

## The re-audit routine

An audit is a snapshot of one version. Once you've edited, check your work.

1. **Save the new version** with a new name (for example "Resume_v2").
2. **Run the re-audit.** In Claude: "I updated my resume. Re-audit it and show me what changed." In another tool, use the Re-audit prompt card and paste your last verdicts.
3. **Read the Before and Now table.** Every area shows its old verdict, its new one, and what made the difference.
4. **Check "New issues".** Edits break things: a tense slip, a date that changed, a bullet that pushed onto a third page.
5. **Repeat** until every area is Good Foundation or better.

You don't need Strong everywhere. Good Foundation or better in every area means your resume and profile are doing their job. At that point, your time is better spent on applications and conversations with real people.

**When to re-audit:**

- After every round of edits
- Before you apply for a role that's different from your usual target (run it with that job post)
- When you add a new job, a big project or a new certification
- Every couple of months while you're searching, so things don't drift

---

## Good to know

- **Results vary.** The audits use the same checklist I use, but a strong resume and profile can't guarantee interviews or offers. Hiring depends on a lot you can't control.
- **The verdicts are a guide.** They come from a fixed rubric, so they're consistent. They're still one read of your documents. You know your field. If something doesn't fit, trust that.
- **Keep your facts true.** Every number, title and date should be something you can explain if someone asks. The skills never invent facts, and you shouldn't either.
