# Resume Checklist

**The 30 checks behind every resume audit, so you can score your own resume with a pen and 30 minutes.**

This is the exact rubric the resume skill uses. Six areas, five checks each, and the rules that turn your ticks into a verdict. No AI needed. Print it, open your resume next to it, and be honest with yourself.

Here's the thing about self-checks: we're all too kind to our own resume, or way too harsh. The fix is to look for evidence. Don't tick a box because you *think* you've done it. Tick it because you can point at the line that proves it.

**Time:** 30 to 45 minutes. **What you'll need:** Your resume (printed or as a PDF), a highlighter or a highlight tool, a pen, and one job post for the kind of role you want.

---

## How to score yourself

1. Work through one area at a time.
2. Tick a box only when the check is fully met. If it's partly met, leave it blank and write "partly" next to it.
3. **REQUIRED** checks matter most. If you miss one, that area can't get the top verdict.
4. Use the verdict rules under each area to pick your verdict.
5. Fill in the scorecard at the end, then fix in this order: Needs Attention, then Mixed Signals.

---

## Four quick tests first

These take 5 minutes and tell you a lot.

- **The glance test.** Look at the top third of your resume for a few seconds, then look away. Can you say your name, your most recent job title and the company? Better yet, hand it to a friend and ask them.
- **The highlighter test.** Highlight every number on the page: amounts, percentages, counts, timeframes. Then count how many bullets have at least one. Fewer than half? Your achievements need work.
- **The first-word test.** Read only the first word of every bullet. Circle every "Responsible", "Helped", "Assisted", "Worked" and "Involved". Each circle is a bullet to fix.
- **The stranger test.** Read your summary out loud. Would someone who has never met you know what job you want and why you're good at it?

## Area 1: First impressions and layout

- [ ] **REQUIRED: Clear hierarchy.** Someone skimming for a few seconds can find your name, your current or most recent job title, and the company.
- [ ] **Clean, readable layout.** Easy to skim. No crowded columns, boxes or sidebars that make the eye jump around.
- [ ] **Consistent formatting.** Fonts, bullet styles, date formats and spacing match from top to bottom.
- [ ] **Right length.** Rule of thumb: 1 page if you have under 10 years of experience, up to 2 pages if you have 10 or more. Not padded, not crammed.
- [ ] **No distracting design.** No photo, heavy borders, graphics, charts, skill bars or decoration. These waste space and can confuse applicant tracking systems (the software many employers use to collect and search applications).

In Canada and the US, also leave off your date of birth, marital status and full home address. City and province or state is enough.

**Your verdict:**
- **Needs Attention:** the REQUIRED check fails, or 2 or more other checks clearly fail.
- **Mixed Signals:** the REQUIRED check is met, but 1 or 2 other checks clearly fail.
- **Good Foundation:** everything is mostly met. Small inconsistencies that won't distract a reader.
- **Strong:** everything is fully met. The layout helps the reader find what they need.

---

## Area 2: Story and positioning

- [ ] **REQUIRED: Has a summary.** There's a summary, profile or positioning statement near the top.
- [ ] **REQUIRED: Summary names a target.** It names a specific role type, industry or level. "Senior operations leader in financial services", not "results-driven professional".
- [ ] **About value, not wants.** It says what you offer, not what you want from a job. No "seeking a role where I can grow".
- [ ] **Clear career thread.** Your titles and moves tell a story a stranger could follow from early career to today.
- [ ] **Pivots are explained.** If you switched industries or functions, your summary bridges the change.

**Your verdict:**
- **Needs Attention:** either REQUIRED check fails. No summary, or a summary with no target.
- **Mixed Signals:** both REQUIRED checks are met, but the summary is generic or full of clichés, or there's a gap or pivot nothing explains.
- **Good Foundation:** clear positioning and a clear thread. Small clarity issues a quick rewrite would fix.
- **Strong:** your summary says right away who you are and who you're for, and your career story backs it up.

---

## Area 3: Achievements vs. responsibilities

- [ ] **REQUIRED: Numbers.** Your bullets include numbers, percentages, dollar amounts, volumes or timeframes. Approximate figures count.
- [ ] **REQUIRED: Strong action verbs.** Bullets open with specific verbs (Led, Grew, Cut, Launched, Negotiated), not "Responsible for", "Helped", "Assisted with" or "Worked on".
- [ ] **Mostly achievements.** More than half your bullets describe outcomes, not just duties.
- [ ] **CAR structure.** Bullets show the Challenge, the Action you took and the Result, not the action alone.
- [ ] **Impact is clear on its own.** A hiring manager can see the business impact from the bullet, without asking you a follow-up question.

**Your verdict:**
- **Needs Attention:** both REQUIRED checks fail, or almost every bullet is a duty with no impact.
- **Mixed Signals:** one REQUIRED check fails, or only 1 or 2 bullets have numbers while the rest are duties.
- **Good Foundation:** both REQUIRED checks are met. Most bullets show impact or numbers.
- **Strong:** every check is met. Your bullets show measurable impact again and again.

**What a fix looks like** (a fictional example: Sam, a logistics coordinator):

> Before: Responsible for tracking shipments and updating customers.
>
> After: Cut late-delivery complaints from 30 to 12 a month by building a daily tracking sheet for 4 regional carriers and sending customers proactive updates.

Same job. Same person. The second one shows a problem, what Sam did, and the result.

## Area 4: Keywords and ATS

Grab your job post for this one. Highlight the skills, tools and phrases it repeats, then look for them on your resume.

- [ ] **REQUIRED: Role keywords.** The words from your target role show up naturally on your resume, in your summary and bullets, not only in a list.
- [ ] **Skills section.** You have a section for skills, tools, competencies or areas of expertise.
- [ ] **Matches how jobs are posted.** Your terms match the words in real job posts for your role ("P&L management" rather than "budget oversight", if that's what the posts say).
- [ ] **ATS-friendly format.** No multi-column layouts, text boxes, tables, key details in headers or footers, or graphics that parsing software can drop or scramble.
- [ ] **No keyword stuffing.** Keywords appear in real sentences, not dumped in a block or hidden in white text.

Only add a keyword if it's true. If the post asks for a tool you've never used, don't put it on your resume. Put it on your learning list instead.

**Your verdict:**
- **Needs Attention:** no role keywords at all, or formatting risky enough that parsing software could scramble or drop key details.
- **Mixed Signals:** some keywords, but a real mismatch in vocabulary, or formatting that's partly risky.
- **Good Foundation:** keywords present and well placed. Small gaps or one formatting risk.
- **Strong:** keywords throughout, language that closely matches your target role, no visible formatting risks.

---

## Area 5: Career story and trajectory

- [ ] **REQUIRED: Leads to the target.** Your path points logically toward the role or level you're going for now.
- [ ] **Growth or deliberate moves.** Your titles, scope and responsibilities grow over time, or your sideways moves clearly build toward a goal.
- [ ] **Gaps are handled.** Rule of thumb: gaps of 6 months or more are either absent or addressed with a short note, a mention in your summary, or dates that make sense.
- [ ] **Reasonable tenure.** Rule of thumb: about 18 months or more per job on average. Stints under 12 months are explained or grouped (contract, consulting, a company closure).
- [ ] **Consistent signals.** Your titles, company sizes and scope tell a consistent story. Anything surprising gets a line of context.

A gap is not a crime. You decide how much to share. A one-line note ("Career break: family care" or "Professional development and contract work") is usually plenty.

**Your verdict:**
- **Needs Attention:** your path clearly doesn't lead to your target, or there are unexplained gaps of 6 months or more.
- **Mixed Signals:** the path is there, but short stints, a pivot or scope that doesn't add up would raise questions straight away.
- **Good Foundation:** a clear path. Small questions that are easy to answer in an interview.
- **Strong:** your path clearly supports your target and reads as a logical route to it.

---

## Area 6: Language and presence

- [ ] **REQUIRED: No filler phrases.** None of these: "responsible for", "assisted with", "helped to", "worked on", "involved in", "participated in", "supported the team".
- [ ] **No tired clichés.** None of these: "detail-oriented", "team player", "results-driven", "passionate about", "dynamic", "go-getter", "motivated self-starter".
- [ ] **Consistent tense.** Present tense for your current job, past tense for past jobs, no mixing within one job.
- [ ] **No first person.** No "I", "my", "me" or "our" in the body text.
- [ ] **Matches your level.** Your words fit your career level. Not junior-sounding if you're senior, not executive-sounding if you're early in your career.

Use your word processor's Find tool (Ctrl + F or Command + F) and search for each filler phrase and cliché. It takes 2 minutes and catches everything.

**Your verdict:**
- **Needs Attention:** filler phrases throughout (in 3 or more jobs, or in your summary), or language that sounds far more junior than your experience.
- **Mixed Signals:** some filler in 1 or 2 jobs, clichés that weaken the impact, or tense or pronoun slips more than once.
- **Good Foundation:** mostly strong. A bit of filler a 30-minute edit would clear up.
- **Strong:** clear, confident language that fits your level. No filler, no clichés.

## What to fix first

- **Any failed REQUIRED check.** These are the biggest wins. A missing summary or zero numbers drags everything else down.
- **Then every Needs Attention area.** Work top to bottom.
- **Then Mixed Signals.** These are usually one focused edit each.
- **Leave Good Foundation and Strong alone** until the rest is done. Polishing a strong section feels productive. It usually isn't.

When you've made your changes, score yourself again with a fresh copy of this checklist. Or run the resume skill and ask for a re-audit, so you get a second set of eyes on the result.

Results vary. A resume that scores well on every area still can't guarantee interviews. But it means your resume isn't the thing holding you back.
