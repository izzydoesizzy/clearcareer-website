# Interview Scripts

**15 word-for-word scripts for the questions that trip people up most.**

> **Before you use these:** These scripts are frameworks. Adapt them to your own experience and your own voice, and rehearse until they sound natural, not memorized. Interviews depend on a lot more than scripts, so results vary.

You don't need to memorize all 15. Pick the ones you're most likely to face in your next interview and practise those out loud.

Your thank-you emails are in Guide 08. Your full prep checklist is in Guide 06.

**Time:** 20 minutes to read, then practise out loud. **What you'll need:** Your resume, your story bank (Guide 01), and a mirror or your phone to record yourself.

---

## The Golden Rule of Interview Answers

Every answer follows the same shape: **Context, Action, Result.** What was the situation? What did you do? What happened because of what you did?

Keep answers under 2 minutes. If the interviewer wants more detail, they'll ask. Short and specific beats long and rambling every time.

## The Big Five Questions

### Script 1: "Tell Me About Yourself"

This is not your life story. It's a 60 to 90 second professional pitch. Guide 03 has three formulas if this one doesn't fit you.

```
"Sure. I'm a [Your role or specialty] with [X years] of experience in [Field or industry]. Most recently, I was at [Company], where I [One key accomplishment with a measurable result].

Before that, I spent [X years] at [Previous company] focused on [Relevant area], where I [Second accomplishment or skill].

What draws me to this role at [Company] is [Specific reason tied to the job post or the company]. My experience in [Relevant skill] applies directly to [Challenge or goal they've mentioned].

I'm looking forward to digging into the details today."
```

**Tip:** Customize this for every interview. The bones stay the same. The last paragraph should match the role in front of you.

### Script 2: "Why Are You Leaving?" or "Why Did You Leave?"

**If you were laid off:**

```
"The company went through a restructuring that affected [Number] roles across [Team or company]. My role was one of them. It wasn't performance-related. In fact, my last review [Positive detail, for example: highlighted my work on the X project].

It gave me the chance to be more intentional about my next move, and that's what led me here."
```

**If you're leaving by choice:**

```
"I've had a great experience at [Company]. I've learned a lot and contributed [Specific thing]. But I've reached a point where I'm looking for [Growth opportunity, new challenge, or specific thing this role offers], and that isn't available in my current role. This position caught my eye because [Specific reason]."
```

**Never:** Badmouth your former employer, even if they deserve it. The interviewer will wonder what you'll say about them one day.

### Script 3: "Why This Company?"

```
"Three reasons.

First, [Specific thing about their product, mission or market]. I've been following [Company]'s work on [Specific initiative], and it lines up with what I care about professionally.

Second, the role itself. The focus on [Key responsibility from the job post] is exactly where my skills are strongest.

Third, the team. From what I've learned about the culture [Reference something specific: a LinkedIn post from someone on the team, something said in an earlier interview, a talk their leader gave], it sounds like a place where I'd do my best work."
```

### Script 4: "What's Your Biggest Weakness?"

Don't say "I'm a perfectionist." Don't say "I work too hard." Give a real weakness, and show what you do about it.

```
"I tend to [Genuine weakness, for example: take on too much, hold on to work I should delegate, get deep into details]. I know this about myself, so I've built [Specific system or habit] to manage it.

For example, at [Company], I noticed I was [Specific example of the weakness showing up]. So I [Specific action you took], and the result was [Positive outcome].

It's still something I watch for, but I have systems in place to catch it early."
```

### Script 5: "Where Do You See Yourself in 5 Years?"

```
"In five years, I want to be [Realistic progression from this role]. Specifically, I'd like to have [Developed a skill, led a team, built a program, expanded into an area] that builds on the work I'd be doing here.

What excites me about [Company] is that I can see a path from this role to [Next level], and I'd like to grow with an organization rather than starting over somewhere new every couple of years."
```

## Behavioural Questions

### Script 6: "Tell Me About a Time You Handled Conflict"

```
"At [Company], I was working on [Project] with [Person or team], and we disagreed on [Specific issue].

I set up a one-on-one to understand their side. I learned their concern was [Their legitimate point]. I shared mine, which was [Your point].

We agreed on [Compromise or solution], and the result was [Positive outcome]. What I took away from that is [Lesson about collaboration or communication]."
```

### Script 7: "Tell Me About a Time You Failed"

```
"At [Company], I [Specific failure: missed a deadline, made the wrong call, underestimated the scope].

The impact was [What happened]. I took responsibility by [Specific action: told the people affected, proposed a fix, adjusted the plan].

What I learned was [Specific lesson]. Since then, I've [Specific change in how you work]. I think that experience made me better at [Relevant skill]."
```

### Script 8: "Tell Me About a Time You Led a Project"

```
"I led [Project name or description] at [Company]. The goal was [Specific objective].

I [Specific actions: pulled the team together, defined the scope, managed the timeline, cleared blockers]. The biggest challenge was [Specific obstacle], and I handled it by [Your solution].

The result was [Measurable outcome: delivered on time, under budget, beat the target by X%]. Afterwards, the project was [Recognized, adopted by other teams, expanded]."
```

## Salary Questions During Interviews

*This is general guidance, not legal or financial advice. To work out the numbers behind these scripts, use the Salary Number Generator.*

### Script 9: The Early Salary Question

Use this in a first call, before you know enough about the role.

```
"I'd like to learn a bit more about the role and the full scope before I put a number on it. Can you share the range you've budgeted for this position?"
```

If they push back, give a researched range instead of saying "I'm flexible." Flexible sounds like "I'll take whatever you offer."

**If the job post lists a pay range** (several provinces and US states now require it), you can work with it directly:

```
"The posting lists [Posted range]. Based on my experience in [Area], I'd expect to be in the upper part of that range. Is there flexibility within it?"
```

### Script 10: The Mid-Process Salary Question

```
"I've done my research and I have a solid sense of the market for this type of role. I'm expecting something in the range of $[Low end of your range] to $[High end of your range], but I'm most interested in the total package and the opportunity itself. What range did you have in mind?"
```

## Closing the Interview

### Script 11: Questions to Ask Them

Always have 2 or 3 questions ready. Guide 06 has a full list, sorted by who you're talking to. A few that work almost anywhere:

```
"What does success look like in this role in the first 90 days?"

"What's the biggest challenge the team is facing right now?"

"How would you describe the team culture? What kind of person does well here?"

"What's your favourite part about working at [Company]?"

"What are the next steps in the process, and what's the timeline?"
```

### Script 12: The Strong Close

```
"Thank you for the conversation. I want you to know I'm very interested in this role. Everything I've heard today tells me my experience in [Specific area] would let me contribute quickly.

Is there anything about my background you'd like me to expand on, or any concerns I can address?"
```

## The Curveballs

### Script 13: When You Don't Have a Perfect Example

Don't freeze, and don't make something up. Bridge to the closest true story.

```
"I haven't faced that exact situation, but I dealt with something close to it at [Company]. [Tell the closest true story in Context, Action, Result.] If I faced the situation you described, I'd [How you'd apply what you learned]."
```

### Script 14: Explaining a Gap in Your Resume

Short, honest, and pointed forward. You don't owe anyone private details.

```
"Between [Month and year] and [Month and year], I [Honest, brief reason: took time to care for a family member, dealt with a health matter that's now resolved, was laid off and took time to choose my next move carefully].

During that time, I [Anything relevant: kept my skills current with X, did freelance or volunteer work, completed a course]. I'm ready to get back to [Type of work], and this role is exactly the kind of work I want to be doing."
```

### Script 15: When They Ask About a Skill You Don't Have Yet

```
"I haven't used [Skill or tool] in a professional setting yet. What I have done is [Closest related skill or tool], which [Why it transfers]. When I needed to learn [Something new] at [Company], I got up to speed in [Timeframe] by [How you learned it]. I'd take the same approach here, and I've already started by [Something you've actually done, if true]."
```

Only include that last line if it's true. If you haven't started, leave it out.

## Key Takeaway

Interviews are performances, and the best performers rehearse. These scripts give you the structure. Your job is to fill in the brackets with your real experience, practise until the words feel natural, and walk in with the confidence that comes from preparation.

You don't need to be perfect. You need to be prepared.
