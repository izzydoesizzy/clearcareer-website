# Salary positioning prompts

These prompts research the pay for your top job title, turn it into a percentile table, and set your anchor, target and walk-away numbers. This is general guidance, not legal or financial advice. Results vary.

## Position my salary for my top title

**What it does:** Finds 3 to 5 real pay sources for one job title where you want to work (or shows you exactly where to look), then turns them into a percentile table and your three numbers.
**When to use it:** Once you've picked your top title, and before your first recruiter call.

### Step 1: Find the pay sources

~~~text
Research the base pay for this role. This is general guidance, not legal or financial advice.

Job title: [Job title]
Level: [For example intermediate, senior, lead, manager]
Years of experience: [Number]
Industry: [Industry]
Where I want to work: [City, province or state, country, or "remote" plus the country I live in]

Do this:
1. Tell me the currency we're working in (CAD or USD).
2. If you can search the web, run 3 to 5 searches and find 3 to 5 sources for this title, level and location. Use a mix of source types:
   - Job posts that list a pay range. Several provinces and US states now require pay ranges in job posts, and the rules change, so don't tell me a rule applies to me unless you're sure.
   - Salary sites: Glassdoor, Payscale, Indeed, LinkedIn, and Levels.fyi for tech roles. Look at the median, not the average.
   - Government data: Job Bank (jobbank.gc.ca) in Canada, or the Bureau of Labor Statistics (bls.gov/oes) in the US.
   - Free annual salary guides from recruiting firms.
3. Only report a figure from a page you actually opened. For each source, give what it shows (low, median, high, or percentiles), whether it's base or total pay, the date of the data, and the link. Never make up a figure or a link.
4. If you can't search the web, or you can't verify a figure, say so plainly. Don't guess. Give me the exact searches to run, and a blank table to fill in:
   Source | Low | Median | High | Base or total pay | Date | Link
   I'll paste back what I find.
~~~

**Replace:** [Job title], [For example intermediate, senior, lead, manager], [Number], [Industry], [City, province or state, country, or "remote" plus the country I live in]

### Step 2: Build my percentile table and three numbers

~~~text
Using only the pay data from Step 1, or the data I paste below, set my salary positioning. This is general guidance, not legal or financial advice.

Job title: [Job title]
Where I want to work: [Location]
Currency: [CAD or USD]
Years of experience: [Number]
My pay data (if I'm pasting my own): [Each source with its name, low, median and high, base or total pay, date and link]
The lowest pay I can live on, if I know it: [Amount, or "not sure"]

Do this:
1. Build a percentile table for base pay with four rows: 25th, 50th (median), 75th and 90th.
   - When a source gives percentiles (the Bureau of Labor Statistics does), use them as they are.
   - When a source only gives a low, median and high, use its median for the 50th. Use its low or high for a percentile only if the source says which percentile it is.
   - When you have to work a percentile out from ranges, show how in one line and label it "(estimate, confirm)".
2. Build a sources table with 3 to 5 rows: Source | Range | Median | Date | Link.
3. Set my three numbers:
   - Anchor (my opening number if I have to name one first): around the 85th to 90th percentile.
   - Target (what I'm aiming to land): the 65th to 75th percentile.
   - Walk-away (my floor): the 25th percentile. If the lowest pay I can live on is higher, use my number instead and say why.
   Sit higher in each band if my experience is above what this role usually asks for, and lower if it's below.
   To work out a percentile between two rows, go in a straight line between them: the 85th is two thirds of the way from the 75th to the 90th, and the 65th is 60% of the way from the 50th to the 75th. Show the maths in one line for each number.
   Round each number to the nearest $1,000 and give one sentence on why.
4. Keep the whole section under 300 words.

Use this layout:

Salary positioning: [Job title], [Location]

A table: Percentile | Base pay ([Currency])

A table: Source | Range | Median | Date | Link

Your three numbers
Anchor: [Amount], [one sentence on why]
Target: [Amount], [one sentence on why]
Walk-away: [Amount], [one sentence on why]

This is general guidance, not legal or financial advice. Results vary.

Rules:
- Use only figures from a page you actually opened or that I gave you. Never add salary data of your own.
- Label every estimate "(estimate, confirm)".
- State the currency on every number, and say it's base pay.
- Number ranges use "to", for example "$80K to $95K". No dashes.
- If the sources disagree a lot, or there are fewer than 3, say so and tell me what to look for next before I rely on these numbers.
~~~

**Replace:** [Job title], [Location], [CAD or USD], [Number], [Each source with its name, low, median and high, base or total pay, date and link], [Amount, or "not sure"]

## Answer the salary question with my numbers

**What it does:** Turns your three numbers into short, word-for-word answers for when a recruiter asks about pay.
**When to use it:** Before a recruiter screen, or right after one where the salary question caught you off guard.

### Step 1: Write my answers

~~~text
Write short answers I can say out loud when a recruiter asks about pay. This is general guidance, not legal or financial advice.

Job title: [Job title]
Location: [Location]
Currency: [CAD or USD]
My anchor: [Amount]
My target: [Amount]
My walk-away: [Amount]

Write three answers, each under 40 words:
1. Redirect: I ask for their budgeted range before I give a number.
2. If they push for a number: I give a range from my target to my anchor, based on my research for this role and location.
3. If their range is below my walk-away: I thank them, say it's below what I'm looking for, and ask if there's flexibility.

Rules:
- Never use my walk-away in an answer. It's private.
- Friendly, plain and confident. No apologies, no over-explaining.
- After each answer, remind me to stop talking and let them respond.
- Never suggest inventing another offer or inflating my current pay.
~~~

**Replace:** [Job title], [Location], [CAD or USD], [Amount]
