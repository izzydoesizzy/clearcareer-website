# The salary question

Prompts for the recruiter call, before there's an offer. This is general guidance, not legal or financial advice. Results vary.

## Answer "What are your salary expectations?"

**What it does:** Writes your answers to the salary question in your own words, built on your three numbers.
**When to use it:** Before a recruiter screen or any early interview where money might come up.

### Step 1: Draft my answers

~~~text
Act as my salary negotiation coach. A recruiter is going to ask me about my salary expectations, and I want to be ready.

The role: [Job title] at [Company]
Location: [City, province or state]
Posted pay range: [Posted range, or "none"]
My three numbers: anchor $[Anchor], target $[Target], walk-away $[Walk-away]
What I bring: [One or two sentences about my experience and results]
Who reached out first: [Me or them]

Write four short answers I can say out loud, each two to four sentences:
1. Ask for their range first, politely, without dodging.
2. If the posting shows a range: say where I expect to fit and ask if it's still current. Only suggest the upper part of the range if my experience supports it.
3. If they insist I go first: give a range that starts at my target and tops out at my anchor, and say it depends on the full package.
4. If I'm in a strong position (they reached out, rare skills, real other processes): name my anchor as total compensation.

Rules: never use my walk-away number in any answer. No apologizing. Plain, warm, confident language. Don't add any market data I haven't given you. After the four answers, give me one line on which to use first and why.
~~~

**Replace:** [Job title], [Company], [City, province or state], [Posted range], [Anchor], [Target], [Walk-away], [One or two sentences about my experience and results], [Me or them]

### Step 2: The follow-up questions

~~~text
Now give me short replies for what usually comes next:
1. Their range is below my walk-away. I want to ask about flexibility or a more senior level before we both invest more time.
2. Their range works. I want to keep going without sounding over the moon.
3. I want to ask about the rest of the package (bonus, benefits, vacation, remote) and the hiring timeline.
4. They ask: "If we offered $[Their number], would you accept?" I want to stay warm without committing before I see the full offer in writing.

Keep each reply to two or three sentences.
~~~

**Replace:** [Their number]

## Answer "What do you make now?"

**What it does:** Gives you a polite way to keep your current pay private and steer back to what the role is worth.
**When to use it:** When a recruiter or application asks for your current or past salary.

### Step 1: Draft my reply

~~~text
Act as my salary negotiation coach. A recruiter asked what I make now, and I'd rather not share it.

My location: [City, province or state]
My target and anchor: $[Target] and $[Anchor]
Why my current pay isn't a good guide (optional): [Reason, for example a bigger scope or a career change]

Write:
1. A first reply that steers to what this role is worth and asks for their range.
2. A firmer second reply if they push, that keeps my pay private and shares my target range instead.
3. One line on whether employers in my province or state may be limited in asking about pay history, worded as something I should check, not as legal advice.

Keep each reply to two or three sentences, polite and confident.
~~~

**Replace:** [City, province or state], [Target], [Anchor], [Reason, for example a bigger scope or a career change]

## Fill in the salary field on an application

**What it does:** Tells you what to type when an online form asks for a salary number.
**When to use it:** When an application has a required salary or expectations field.

### Step 1: Decide what to enter

~~~text
An online application for [Job title] at [Company] asks for my salary expectations.

The field: [Text box or numbers only]
Posted pay range: [Posted range, or "none"]
My target and anchor: $[Target] and $[Anchor]

Tell me exactly what to type. If the field accepts text, give me a short line such as "Negotiable, based on the full package" or a range that starts at my target. If it only accepts a number, tell me whether to enter my target or my anchor and why. Never suggest my walk-away number, and never suggest a joke value like 0 or 1.
~~~

**Replace:** [Job title], [Company], [Text box or numbers only], [Posted range], [Target], [Anchor]
