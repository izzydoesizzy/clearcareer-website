# Counter scripts

Prompts for responding to an offer, drafting your counter in email and phone versions, asking for more beyond base pay, and closing the deal in writing. This is general guidance, not legal or financial advice. Results vary.

## Respond to a verbal offer

**What it does:** Gives you a short, warm reply that buys time and gets the offer in writing.
**When to use it:** The moment you get an offer by phone or video, or right after.

### Step 1: The phone reply and the follow-up email

~~~text
Act as my salary negotiation coach. I just got a verbal offer (or I'm about to).

Company: [Company]
Job title: [Job title]
Who called: [Recruiter or hiring manager name]
Something specific I'm excited about: [Specific thing about the role or team]
When I want to respond by: [Day]

Write:
1. A phone reply of three or four sentences: thank them, name the specific thing I'm excited about, ask for the full offer in writing, and agree on when I'll get back to them. Don't accept or decline.
2. A shorter version in case I'm caught off guard.
3. A follow-up email under 80 words that thanks them, confirms I'm reviewing it, and names the date I'll respond by.

Plain, warm language. No apologizing.
~~~

**Replace:** [Company], [Job title], [Recruiter or hiring manager name], [Specific thing about the role or team], [Day]

## Draft my counter-offer

**What it does:** Writes your counter as an email and as a phone script, using one clear number for each ask.
**When to use it:** After you've reviewed the written offer and decided what to ask for.

### Step 1: Draft the email and the phone script

~~~text
Act as my salary negotiation coach. Help me counter a job offer. I need an email version and a phone version.

THE OFFER
Company: [Company]
Job title: [Job title]
Who I'm talking to: [Name and role]
The offer: [Base salary and the rest of the package]

MY SIDE
My three numbers: anchor $[Anchor], target $[Target], walk-away $[Walk-away]
What I'm asking for, in priority order: [Ask 1 with a number], [Ask 2 with a number, optional], [Ask 3 with a number, optional]
Market data I found (only real data, with sources): [Market data]
One or two real results from my work: [Results]
Something specific from the interviews I'm excited about: [Specific thing]
Would I accept if they meet my asks? [Yes or no]

HOW TO WRITE IT
Use four beats:
1. Thanks: grateful and specific.
2. Reasons: the scope of the role, my results and the market data. Not my personal bills.
3. The ask: one clear number for each ask, in my priority order.
4. Together: a warm close that suggests a quick call.

If my counter number is below my anchor, check with me that I'm asking high enough to leave room to land at my target or better. Don't offer alternatives (like a signing bonus instead of base) in this first message. If I said yes to "Would I accept", add the line "If we can get there, I'm ready to accept." If I said no, leave it out.

EMAIL: under 200 words, with a subject line "Re: [Job title] offer".

PHONE SCRIPT: written as "You:" lines, with a stage direction in square brackets after my number that tells me to stop talking and wait. Then three short branches: if they say yes, if they need to check with someone, and if base is firm.

Never invent numbers, results or market data. Never suggest bluffing about other offers or deadlines. Never mention my walk-away number.
~~~

**Replace:** [Company], [Job title], [Name and role], [Base salary and the rest of the package], [Anchor], [Target], [Walk-away], [Ask 1 with a number], [Ask 2 with a number, optional], [Ask 3 with a number, optional], [Market data], [Results], [Specific thing], [Yes or no]

### Step 2: Tighten it

~~~text
Now review both versions like a tough editor:
1. Remove any apology, hedge or filler ("sorry", "I hate to ask", "I was just wondering", "if that's okay").
2. Make sure every ask has exactly one number, not a range.
3. Make sure the reasons are about the role, my results and the market, not my personal finances.
4. Cut the email to the fewest words that still sound warm.
5. Read the phone script out loud in your head. Fix anything that sounds written rather than spoken.

Show me the final versions, then list the three changes that mattered most.
~~~

**Replace:** nothing in this step

## Ask for more beyond base pay

**What it does:** Builds a short list of beyond-base asks and the words for each.
**When to use it:** When the employer says base is firm, or when something other than base matters more to you.

### Step 1: Pick my asks and write the lines

~~~text
Act as my salary negotiation coach. The employer says base salary is firm at $[Their number]. My target was $[Target].

What matters to me, ranked: [Ranked list, for example signing bonus, start date, remote days, vacation, title, learning budget, equity, early review, bonus, relocation, benefits from day one]
What I'd give up by leaving my current job: [Current vacation, bonus, benefits, setup]
Location: [City, province or state]

Do this:
1. Pick my top one or two asks from my list. Explain in one line each why they fit my situation.
2. For a signing bonus, suggest the gap between their base and my target as a logical amount, and remind me to check for a clawback.
3. For an early review, include "in the offer letter" and specific goals.
4. For equity, list what I should ask to understand (type, amount, vesting, latest value) and remind me to talk to an accountant about tax.
5. Write one combined message (two or three sentences) that asks for my top two together, for both email and phone.

Use only my numbers. If you suggest any amount, label it "(estimate, confirm)" and explain how you got it.
~~~

**Replace:** [Their number], [Target], [Ranked list], [Current vacation, bonus, benefits, setup], [City, province or state]

## Follow up and close

**What it does:** Covers what to send after your counter: a check-in, a final nudge, and an acceptance that confirms every term in writing.
**When to use it:** After you've sent your counter.

### Step 1: If I haven't heard back

~~~text
I sent my counter to [Name] at [Company] on [Day] and haven't heard back. Write a check-in email under 50 words: friendly, still excited, no pressure, and asks if they need anything from me.
~~~

**Replace:** [Name], [Company], [Day]

### Step 2: If they came back partway

~~~text
I countered at $[My counter]. They came back with $[Their new number]. My target is $[Target] and my walk-away is $[Walk-away].

Tell me plainly whether to accept or make one final nudge, and why. Then write both: a short acceptance (two sentences) and a one-time nudge (two sentences) that names a number between their new number and my counter and says I'm ready to sign if we get there. Remind me that one nudge is the limit.
~~~

**Replace:** [My counter], [Their new number], [Target], [Walk-away]

### Step 3: Accept and confirm the terms

~~~text
I'm ready to accept. Write an acceptance email to [Name] at [Company] that:
1. Says I'm thrilled to accept the [Job title] role and thanks them for working with me.
2. Lists every agreed term on its own line: [Agreed terms, for example base, signing bonus, vacation, remote setup, title, start date, early review].
3. Asks for an updated offer letter with these terms, and says I'll sign when it arrives.
4. Asks about next steps for onboarding.

Under 150 words. After the email, remind me in one line not to resign from my current job until the signed offer is back and any conditions (background check, references) are cleared.
~~~

**Replace:** [Name], [Company], [Job title], [Agreed terms]
