# Role-play

Prompts for rehearsing negotiation conversations out loud, one turn at a time, with feedback after every reply. This is general guidance, not legal or financial advice. Results vary.

## Rehearse the negotiation call

**What it does:** The AI plays the recruiter, pushes back like a real one, and coaches you after every line.
**When to use it:** The day before (or the hour before) a salary conversation, after you've drafted your counter.

### Step 1: Set up the role-play

~~~text
Act as a recruiter so I can practise a salary negotiation. We'll go one turn at a time.

THE SCENARIO
Company: [Company]
Job title: [Job title]
Stage: [The recruiter screen / The verbal offer / My counter call / Their reply to my counter]
The offer so far: [Offer details, or "no offer yet"]
My three numbers: anchor $[Anchor], target $[Target], walk-away $[Walk-away]
What I plan to ask for: [My asks]
Difficulty: [Easy, Medium or Hard]

HOW TO PLAY
1. Before we start, set a private budget ceiling for base salary and keep it hidden. Base it only on my numbers, never on market data:
   Easy: up to my anchor. Friendly, with some room on extras.
   Medium: up to my target. Moves once on base, then holds. Open to one or two extras if I ask well.
   Hard: a little below my target. Says "this is our best offer", pushes a short deadline, and only moves on one extra if I ask clearly.
2. Play [Recruiter name], a recruiter at [Company]. Sound like a real recruiter: friendly, busy, and protective of the budget.
3. One turn at a time. Say one thing as the recruiter, then stop and wait for my reply. Never write my lines for me.
4. Over the conversation, use realistic pushback such as: "What are you making now?", "Our range is fixed for this level", "We need to be fair to the rest of the team", "Would you accept (a number below my target) today?", "We'll need an answer by Friday".
5. After each of my replies, give me feedback first, in this exact format, then continue in character on a new line:
   Coach: What worked: (one line). Tighten: (one line). Try saying: "(a stronger version of my line)"
   [Recruiter name]: (your next line)
6. In your feedback, call out any of these when I do them: apologizing, over-explaining, filling a silence, giving a range that starts below my target, mentioning my walk-away, saying "I'm flexible" with no number, accepting on the spot, or bluffing.
7. If I type "pause", step out of character and answer my question as my coach. If I type "resume", carry on.
8. End the role-play when we reach an agreement, when I walk away, or when I type "end". The ceiling is a practice number only, not real market data.

Start now with your first line as the recruiter.
~~~

**Replace:** [Company], [Job title], [The recruiter screen / The verbal offer / My counter call / Their reply to my counter], [Offer details], [Anchor], [Target], [Walk-away], [My asks], [Easy, Medium or Hard], [Recruiter name]

### Step 2: The debrief

~~~text
End the role-play and give me a debrief in this format:

SCORECARD (Yes, Partly or No for each)
Opened with thanks and something specific: ___
Gave one clear number for each ask: ___
Stayed quiet after saying my number: ___
Didn't apologize or over-explain: ___
Kept my walk-away private: ___
Asked for the final terms in writing: ___
Stayed calm and kind under pushback: ___

RESULT
Your hidden ceiling was: $___
Where I landed: $___ base, plus ___
Compared to my target: above, at or below

THREE LINES TO PRACTISE
1. (the line I should say differently next time, rewritten)
2. (another)
3. (another)

Then ask whether I want to run it again at a harder level, try a different stage, or switch to email.
~~~

**Replace:** nothing in this step

## Practise the salary question

**What it does:** Drills the "What are your salary expectations?" moment until your answer feels natural.
**When to use it:** Before a recruiter screen.

### Step 1: Run the drill

~~~text
Act as a recruiter doing a first phone screen for the [Job title] role at [Company]. I want to practise answering the salary question.

My three numbers: anchor $[Anchor], target $[Target], walk-away $[Walk-away]
Posted range: [Posted range, or "none"]

Ask me the salary question five different ways, one at a time, and wait for my answer each time. Mix it up: "What are you looking for?", "What's your number?", "We need a figure to move you forward", "What are you making now?", and "If we offered (a number near my target), would you take it?"

After each answer, give me feedback in this format, then ask the next version:
Coach: What worked: (one line). Tighten: (one line). Try saying: "(a stronger version)"

Flag it right away if I name my walk-away, start a range below my target, apologize, or share my current pay without meaning to. After the fifth question, give me my best answer of the five, cleaned up, to keep on a sticky note.
~~~

**Replace:** [Job title], [Company], [Anchor], [Target], [Walk-away], [Posted range]

## Practise asking for a raise

**What it does:** The AI plays your manager so you can rehearse a raise or promotion conversation.
**When to use it:** Before a raise meeting or performance review.

### Step 1: Set up the role-play

~~~text
Act as my manager so I can practise asking for a raise. We'll go one turn at a time.

My role: [Job title]
My current pay: $[Current pay]
The number I'm asking for: $[Number]
My main results: [Results]
My manager's style: [Supportive, busy, cautious or tough]

HOW TO PLAY
1. Stay in character as my manager. Be realistic: interested, but careful with budget.
2. One turn at a time. Never write my lines.
3. Over the conversation, use at least two of these: "The budget is set for this year", "Let's talk about it at your review", "Everyone on the team is at a similar level", "What would you say is your biggest contribution?", "I can't promise anything".
4. After each of my replies, give feedback first, then continue in character:
   Coach: What worked: (one line). Tighten: (one line). Try saying: "(a stronger version)"
   Manager: (your next line)
5. Call it out if I apologize, undersell my results, drop my number, or leave without agreeing on a next step and a date.
6. When I type "end", give me a short scorecard: clear number, specific results, handled pushback, agreed next step in writing. Then three lines to practise.

Start now with your first line as my manager.
~~~

**Replace:** [Job title], [Current pay], [Number], [Results], [Supportive, busy, cautious or tough]
