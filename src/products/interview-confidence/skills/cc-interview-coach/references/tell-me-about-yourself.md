# "Tell me about yourself" prompts

These prompts write and tighten the opening answer of almost every interview, using only your real experience.

## Write my "Tell me about yourself"

**What it does:** Writes a 60 to 90 second answer, a 30-second version and a cue card from your resume and the job post.
**When to use it:** As soon as you have an interview booked, and again for each new role.

### Step 1: Give it your material

~~~text
Act as my interview coach. Write my answer to "Tell me about yourself" for this interview.

My resume:
[Paste your resume]

The job post:
[Paste the job post]

The achievement I'm proudest of that fits this role: [Your top achievement, with the number if you have one]
Anything about me that's unusual or a bit different: [Optional: a career change, a mix of skills, a unique background]
The stage of this interview: [Recruiter screen, hiring manager, panel or final round]

Use this 5-part structure:
1. Introduction (about 10 seconds): my job title, my years of experience and my field, in one sentence.
2. Track record (20 to 30 seconds): my most recent role and ONE strong, relevant achievement with a number.
3. Bridge (15 to 20 seconds): 2 or 3 specific requirements from the job post, and how my experience matches them.
4. Differentiator (10 to 15 seconds): the thing I bring that most candidates won't, backed by evidence.
5. Close (about 10 seconds): one sentence on why this specific role excites me. End with energy, not "So, yeah."

If I'm changing careers, organize it around the theme that connects my experience instead of a timeline: the thread, two examples that prove it, and why it fits this role.

Rules:
- Use only facts from my resume or from what I've told you. Never invent numbers, employers, titles or dates.
- If you think an estimate would help, ask me first, and label it "(estimate, confirm)".
- Include at least one real number.
- Mention the role or company specifically, so it's clearly not a generic answer.
- No life story, no hobbies, no "I'm a hard worker", no buzzwords.
- Write it the way a person talks: contractions, short sentences, easy to say out loud.

Give me:
1. The full answer (about 150 to 250 words, 60 to 90 seconds out loud).
2. A 30-second version for phone screens (introduction, one achievement, why this role).
3. A cue card: 5 short bullets I can practise from, so I don't memorize it word for word.
4. Anything you needed but didn't have, as a question for me.
~~~

**Replace:** [Paste your resume], [Paste the job post], [Your top achievement, with the number if you have one], [Optional: a career change, a mix of skills, a unique background], [Recruiter screen, hiring manager, panel or final round]

### Step 2: Tighten it

~~~text
Now read my answer as if you were the interviewer.

1. Time it at a normal speaking pace. If it runs over 90 seconds, cut from the Bridge or the Differentiator first.
2. Flag any sentence that sounds like a script or a LinkedIn profile, and rewrite it so it sounds like me talking.
3. Flag anything an interviewer might ask a follow-up about, and give me a one-line answer ready for it.
4. Check that the last sentence connects to this specific role.

Show me the tightened version and a list of what you changed.
~~~

**Replace:** Nothing. Paste this after Step 1.

## Adapt my answer for a different interview

**What it does:** Reshapes your existing answer for a phone screen, a panel, an executive, or a close cousin of the question.
**When to use it:** When the format or the question changes but your story doesn't.

### Step 1: Pick the version you need

~~~text
Here is my current answer to "Tell me about yourself":

[Paste your current answer]

Adapt it for: [Phone screen, panel interview, executive interview, "Walk me through your resume", "Why should we hire you?", or a 30-second elevator pitch at a networking event]

Guidelines for each:
- Phone screen: 30 to 40 seconds. Introduction, one achievement, why this role. Save the rest.
- Panel: address the broadest need in the Bridge, because each person cares about something different (results, culture, strategy).
- Executive: talk about scale and business impact (budgets, teams, results for the business), not tasks.
- "Walk me through your resume": same content, but in order from earliest relevant role to now, one highlight per role.
- "Why should we hire you?": skip the introduction. Go straight to what I deliver and why it matters for this role.
- Elevator pitch: who I am, one highlight, what I'm looking for. Under 30 seconds.

Use only my facts. Never add numbers, employers or titles I haven't given you.
~~~

**Replace:** [Paste your current answer], [Phone screen, panel interview, executive interview, "Walk me through your resume", "Why should we hire you?", or a 30-second elevator pitch at a networking event]
