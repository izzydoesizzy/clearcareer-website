# Likely questions prompts

These prompts read a job post and tell you what you're likely to be asked, what might worry the employer, and what to ask them back.

## Predict my interview questions from a job post

**What it does:** Pulls the employer's priorities out of a job post and lists the questions you're most likely to hear, mapped to your stories.
**When to use it:** As soon as an interview is booked, before you start practising.

### Step 1: Paste the job and your background

~~~text
Act as an experienced hiring manager for this role and help me prepare.

The job post:
[Paste the job post]

Company: [Company]
Interview stage: [Recruiter screen, hiring manager, panel, technical or final round]
Who I'm meeting (if known): [Name and job title]

My resume:
[Paste your resume]

My story bank (if I have one):
[Paste your story titles with a one-line summary each, or write "none yet"]

Do this:
1. List the employer's top 3 to 5 priorities for this role, based on what the post repeats, lists first, or describes as a problem. Quote the words from the post that tell you this.
2. List 12 to 15 questions I'm likely to be asked at this stage, grouped as:
   - Openers (for example "Tell me about yourself", "Why this role?")
   - Behavioural questions tied to each priority
   - Role-specific or technical questions
   - Motivation questions (why this company, why now, why leaving)
   - Questions about possible gaps between my background and the post
3. For each question, give me: why they're asking it, what a strong answer needs to show, and which of my stories fits best. If none fits, write "no story yet" and ask me one question to help me find one.
4. Don't write full answers with details I haven't given you. Give me answer outlines that use only my real facts.

Show the questions as a table I can practise from.
~~~

**Replace:** [Paste the job post], [Company], [Recruiter screen, hiring manager, panel, technical or final round], [Name and job title], [Paste your resume], [Paste your story titles with a one-line summary each, or write "none yet"]

### Step 2: Find their concerns about me

~~~text
Now compare my resume with the job post, the way a careful hiring manager would.

Make a table with three columns:
1. A concern they might have about me (for example: no direct industry experience, a gap in my resume, a missing tool or certification, short time in recent roles, more senior or more junior than the role).
2. Why they might think it (point to the job post or my resume).
3. How I can honestly address it, using evidence from my real experience. If I don't have evidence, say so and suggest how to handle it truthfully.

Then pick the top 2 concerns and write a short, calm answer for each that I could use if they come up, or include in my thank-you email.

Never suggest I claim experience I don't have.
~~~

**Replace:** Nothing. Paste this after Step 1.

## Write questions to ask the interviewer

**What it does:** Gives you 5 to 7 sharp questions tailored to the company, the role and the person you're meeting.
**When to use it:** The day before any interview.

### Step 1: Describe the conversation

~~~text
Help me prepare questions to ask at the end of my interview.

Company: [Company]
Role: [Job title]
Who I'm meeting: [Name, job title, and anything I know about them]
Interview stage: [Recruiter screen, hiring manager, panel or final round]
What I already know about the team or company: [Notes from the job post, the company website, news or earlier interviews]
Things I really need to know before I'd accept: [Optional: for example remote work, travel, growth path, team size]

Give me 5 to 7 questions that:
- Fit this person's role (a recruiter, a future manager, a peer and an executive each need different questions)
- Can't be answered by reading the company website
- Help me learn what success looks like, what the challenges are, and how the team works
- Save pay and benefits for the recruiter or a later stage, unless I've said they're a deal-breaker

End with this closing question, worded naturally: "Based on our conversation, is there anything about my background that gives you pause?" Then tell me how to respond if they name a concern: thank them, answer with evidence, and check that it helped.
~~~

**Replace:** [Company], [Job title], [Name, job title, and anything I know about them], [Recruiter screen, hiring manager, panel or final round], [Notes from the job post, the company website, news or earlier interviews], [Optional: for example remote work, travel, growth path, team size]
