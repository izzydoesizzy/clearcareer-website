# Debrief prompts

These prompts help you get the most out of an interview after it's over: what went well, what to fix, and what to send next.

## Debrief my interview

**What it does:** Rebuilds your interview with you, one question at a time, and turns it into a debrief and a plan for the next round.
**When to use it:** Within 24 hours of any interview, while the details are fresh.

### Step 1: Rebuild the interview

~~~text
Act as my interview coach. I just finished an interview and I want to debrief it properly.

Company: [Company]
Role: [Job title]
Interview stage: [Recruiter screen, hiring manager, panel or final round]
Who I met: [Names and job titles]
Format: [Phone, video or in person], about [Number] minutes

Ask me questions ONE at a time to rebuild what happened. Cover:
- Each question they asked, in order, and roughly how I answered
- Follow-up questions they asked, and which topics they kept coming back to
- What they said about the team's challenges and priorities
- Anything that felt off, or any answer I'm unhappy with
- What they said about next steps and timing
- What I liked and didn't like about the role, the people and the company

Keep each question short. When you have enough, tell me and move to the debrief.
~~~

**Replace:** [Company], [Job title], [Recruiter screen, hiring manager, panel or final round], [Names and job titles], [Phone, video or in person], [Number]

### Step 2: Write the debrief

~~~text
Now write my debrief. Use only what I told you.

1. Questions they asked: a numbered list.
2. What went well: 2 or 3 answers that landed, and why.
3. What to fix: for my 2 or 3 weakest answers, what was missing and a stronger version built only from my facts. Put anything you'd need from me in [square brackets].
4. Their top 3 priorities, in their words, as best I remember them.
5. Possible concerns they might have about me, and how I could address each one honestly.
6. Prep for the next round: the stories to sharpen, new stories I may need, and questions to ask next time.
7. My own read: based on what I said I liked and didn't like, the questions I should get answered before I'd accept an offer.
8. Follow-up dates: thank-you email today, a check-in if I haven't heard back by [Date they gave, or about 10 days from now], and one final follow-up a week or so after that.

Don't predict whether I'll get the job. Interview signals are not guarantees. Focus on what I can control.

Finish by asking whether I want to write the thank-you email now.
~~~

**Replace:** [Date they gave, or about 10 days from now]

## Score my interview from notes or a transcript

**What it does:** Gives you a strict, question-by-question review when you have detailed notes or a transcript.
**When to use it:** When you have written notes or a transcript of a practice or real interview. Only record an interview if everyone has agreed to it.

### Step 1: Paste your notes

~~~text
Act as a strict but fair interview reviewer. Review the interview below.

Role: [Job title] at [Company]
The job post:
[Paste the job post]

My notes or transcript:
[Paste your notes or transcript]

For each question the interviewer asked:
1. Write the question.
2. Summarize my answer in 2 to 4 bullets.
3. Score it (0 = missing, 1 = okay, 2 = strong) on:
   - Structure: was it organized and easy to follow?
   - Specific example: did I give a real, work-related story? (Score 0 if not.)
   - Impact: did I show a result, with a number where one exists?
   - Relevance: did it connect to what this role needs?
4. Say what was good, what was missing, and give one specific fix.

Then give me:
- A table of all questions with their scores
- My top 3 strengths and top 3 fixes
- The 2 answers to rework first, with a stronger version of each using only my facts

Score honestly. Never invent anything I didn't say. Don't predict whether I'll move forward.
~~~

**Replace:** [Job title], [Company], [Paste the job post], [Paste your notes or transcript]
