# STAR story bank prompts

These prompts build a bank of 10 to 12 true interview stories by interviewing you one question at a time and digging for the numbers.

## Build my STAR story bank

**What it does:** Interviews you one question at a time and turns your answers into 10 to 12 STAR stories with real numbers.
**When to use it:** A week or more before your next interview, or any time you want a story bank you can reuse.

### Step 1: Start the story interview

~~~text
Act as my interview coach. I want to build a bank of 10 to 12 STAR stories (Situation, Task, Action, Result) from my real work experience.

My background:
[Paste your resume, or list your last 3 roles with company, job title and years]

The role I'm targeting: [Target job title]

How to run this:
1. Ask me ONE question at a time. Wait for my answer before you ask the next one.
2. Work through these themes in order, one story per theme to start:
   - A problem I solved that nobody asked me to solve
   - A time I dealt with a difficult person
   - A time I failed or made a mistake
   - A time I led something (with or without the title)
   - A time I hit a number or beat a target
   - A time I worked under real pressure
   - A time I convinced someone to change their mind
   - A time I learned something quickly
3. For each story, ask follow-up questions until you have:
   - Situation: one or two sentences of context
   - Task: what was on ME specifically
   - Action: three to five specific things I did, in order
   - Result: what changed, with a number if one exists
4. Dig for numbers on every story. Ask me: How many? How much? How fast? Compared with what? Who noticed?
5. If I don't know a number, help me find it (old reports, emails, performance reviews, project files). If I still can't find it, work out an honest estimate with me, show how you got it, and mark it "(estimate, confirm)".
6. Never make up a number, employer, job title, date, quote or outcome. If something is missing, ask me.
7. If I say "we", ask me what my part was. Don't let me take credit for the team's work, and don't let me hide my own.
8. After every 3 stories, show me a short table of the bank so far and the themes still missing.
9. Keep your messages short and encouraging. If my first answer is vague, that's normal. Ask a better question.

Start with your first question.
~~~

**Replace:** [Paste your resume, or list your last 3 roles with company, job title and years], [Target job title]

### Step 2: Write up the bank

~~~text
Now write up my story bank. Use only what I told you. Keep my words where you can.

For each story, use this format:

STORY [Number]: [Short memorable title]
Themes: [Theme 1], [Theme 2], [Theme 3]
Role: [Job title] at [Company], [Year]
Situation: (1 or 2 sentences)
Task: (1 sentence)
Action: (3 to 5 sentences, first person, specific)
Result: (1 or 2 sentences, with the number)
Questions this answers: (3 to 5 common interview questions)

Each story should be about 150 to 250 words, which is 60 to 90 seconds out loud.

Then add:
1. A coverage table: story number, title, and a tick for each theme it covers (leadership, problem-solving, collaboration, conflict, failure, pressure, influence, learning, results, customer focus).
2. A cheat sheet: one line per story that I can read 15 minutes before an interview.
3. Any theme with no story yet, plus one question to help me find one.
4. Any number marked "(estimate, confirm)", listed in one place so I can check them.
~~~

**Replace:** Nothing. Paste this after Step 1 is finished.

## Turn one resume bullet into a STAR story

**What it does:** Takes a single line from your resume and asks you the questions needed to turn it into a full interview story.
**When to use it:** When you have a strong resume bullet but freeze when someone says "Tell me more about that."

### Step 1: Pick the bullet

~~~text
Act as my interview coach. Here is one line from my resume:

"[Paste one resume bullet]"

It's from my time as [Job title] at [Company], around [Year].

Ask me questions ONE at a time to turn this into a STAR story (Situation, Task, Action, Result). Find out what was happening before, what was on me specifically, the exact steps I took, and what changed afterwards. Dig for a number: how many, how much, how fast, compared with what.

Never invent details. If I don't know a number, help me estimate it honestly and label it "(estimate, confirm)".

When you have enough, write the story in 150 to 250 words, then list 3 interview questions it could answer.
~~~

**Replace:** [Paste one resume bullet], [Job title], [Company], [Year]

## Map interview questions to my stories

**What it does:** Matches the most common behavioural questions to the stories in your bank, so you never search your memory under pressure.
**When to use it:** Once your story bank is written, and again before each interview.

### Step 1: Paste your bank

~~~text
Here is my STAR story bank:

[Paste your story bank, or a list of story titles with a one-line summary each]

For each of these questions, tell me which story fits best and which part of the story to emphasize (Situation, Action or Result):

1. Tell me about a time you led a team through a challenge.
2. Describe a decision you made without all the information.
3. Tell me about a time you worked with a difficult colleague.
4. Tell me about a time you disagreed with your manager.
5. Describe a time you received critical feedback.
6. Tell me about a time you improved a process.
7. Tell me about a time you failed.
8. Tell me about a time you had to manage competing priorities.
9. Describe a time you explained something complex to a non-expert.
10. Tell me about a time you went out of your way for a customer.
11. Describe a time you had to learn something quickly.
12. Tell me about a time you persuaded people to change direction.

Show it as a table: question, best story, backup story, what to emphasize.

Then tell me:
- Which questions have no good story yet
- Which story I'm leaning on too much (used for more than 4 questions)
- Whether I have at least 2 failure or feedback stories
~~~

**Replace:** [Paste your story bank, or a list of story titles with a one-line summary each]
