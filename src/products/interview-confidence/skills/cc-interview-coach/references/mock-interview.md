# Mock interview prompts

These prompts turn the AI into a realistic interviewer who asks one question at a time, waits for you, and gives honest feedback after each answer.

## Run a mock interview

**What it does:** Plays the interviewer for a full practice round, with feedback and a stronger version of each answer.
**When to use it:** Two or three days before an interview, and again the day before for your weakest questions.

### Step 1: Set the stage

~~~text
Act as the interviewer for a practice interview. Make it feel real.

The role: [Job title] at [Company]
The job post:
[Paste the job post]

My resume:
[Paste your resume]

My story bank (optional):
[Paste your story titles with a one-line summary each, or write "none"]

Interview stage: [Recruiter screen, hiring manager, panel or final round]
Who you're playing: [For example: the hiring manager, a director of operations who is friendly but busy]
Difficulty: [Friendly, realistic or tough]
Number of questions: [Number, for example 6]
Anything to focus on: [Optional: for example the failure question, salary, explaining my layoff]

How to run it:
1. Ask ONE question at a time, in character. Then stop and wait for my answer. Never answer for me.
2. Mix the question types the way a real interviewer would at this stage: an opener, behavioural questions tied to the job's priorities, a motivation question, and one question about a possible gap in my background.
3. Sometimes ask a natural follow-up before giving feedback, like a real interviewer would: "What was your part specifically?", "What would you do differently?", "What was the result?"
4. After each answer (and any follow-up), step out of character and give feedback in exactly this format:

   What worked: (one or two specific things)
   Fix this first: (the single most important change)
   Score (0 = missing, 1 = okay, 2 = strong):
   - Structure (clear point or clear STAR): _/2
   - Specific example (score 0 if there was no real example): _/2
   - Impact (a result or a number): _/2
   - Relevance to this role: _/2
   - Delivery (under 2 minutes, "I" not just "we", no rambling): _/2
   A stronger version: (rewrite my answer using ONLY the facts I gave. Put anything missing in [square brackets] so I can fill it in. Never invent numbers, employers, titles or outcomes.)

5. Then ask me: "Want to try that one again, or move to the next question?"
6. If I give a very short answer, don't score it yet. Ask me one question to draw out more, like a good interviewer would.
7. Be honest but kind. I want to find my weak spots here, not in the real interview.

Start in character with your first question.
~~~

**Replace:** [Job title], [Company], [Paste the job post], [Paste your resume], [Paste your story titles with a one-line summary each, or write "none"], [Recruiter screen, hiring manager, panel or final round], [For example: the hiring manager, a director of operations who is friendly but busy], [Friendly, realistic or tough], [Number, for example 6], [Optional: for example the failure question, salary, explaining my layoff]

### Step 2: Get the summary

~~~text
That's the end of the practice interview. Step out of character and give me a summary:

1. A table of every question, its topic and my score out of 10.
2. My top 3 strengths, with an example of each from my answers.
3. My top 3 fixes, in order of importance.
4. The one question I should practise most before the real interview, and why.
5. Any story I should add to my story bank, based on gaps you noticed.

Don't predict whether I'll get the job. Focus on what I can improve.
~~~

**Replace:** Nothing. Paste this when the mock interview is done.

## Practise one hard question

**What it does:** Drills a single tough question until your answer is clear, honest and short.
**When to use it:** For the question you dread most, such as "Why did you leave?", "What's your biggest weakness?", a resume gap, or salary expectations.

### Step 1: Name the question

~~~text
Act as my interview coach. I want to practise one question until I can answer it with confidence:

"[The question you dread]"

Here's my honest situation: [The real facts behind your answer, for example: I was laid off in a restructuring in March, or I left after 8 months because the role changed]

The role I'm interviewing for: [Job title]

How to run this:
1. Ask me the question as an interviewer would. Wait for my answer.
2. Give me feedback: what worked, the one fix that matters most, and a stronger version using only my facts.
3. Ask me again, a little differently each time, for 3 rounds. Add one realistic follow-up question in round 3.
4. At the end, give me a final version I can say in under 60 seconds, plus a one-line answer for the most likely follow-up.

Keep it honest. Never suggest I hide or bend the truth. Help me say the true thing well.
If the question is about salary, add: "This is general guidance, not legal or financial advice."
~~~

**Replace:** [The question you dread], [The real facts behind your answer, for example: I was laid off in a restructuring in March, or I left after 8 months because the role changed], [Job title]
