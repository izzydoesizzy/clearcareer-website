# Thank-you email prompts

These prompts write the email you send after an interview, including the "Silver Bullet" that mirrors the team's top priorities and matches each one with proof.

## Write my Silver Bullet thank-you email

**What it does:** Writes a thank-you email that names the team's top 3 priorities and matches each one with a true achievement of yours.
**When to use it:** After any interview where you learned what the team cares about most, usually the hiring manager or final round.

### Step 1: Fill in the context

~~~text
Write a post-interview thank-you email for the person I just interviewed with.

Job title: [Job title]
Company: [Company]
Interviewer's first name: [First name]
What I liked about the interview, the people or the company: [Something specific]
A memorable moment or something I learned: [Something they said or shared]

The team's top 3 priorities for this role, based on the conversation and the job post:
1. [Priority 1]
2. [Priority 2]
3. [Priority 3]

My relevant achievement for each priority:
1. [Achievement for priority 1, with the number]
2. [Achievement for priority 2, with the number]
3. [Achievement for priority 3, with the number]

Optional (delete if nothing came up):
A concern they seemed to have, or an answer I fumbled: [The concern]
The real experience that eases it: [Your evidence]

Why I'm genuinely excited about this role: [The challenge, opportunity or mission]

Use this structure:

Subject: Thank you, [Job title] conversation

Hi [First name],

Thank you so much for taking the time to speak with me today. [One or two sentences: what I enjoyed and the memorable moment.]

From our conversation, it sounds like the team is looking for someone who can [Priority 1], [Priority 2] and [Priority 3]. Here's a bit more on how I'd approach each one:

1. [Priority 1]
[One or two sentences on my achievement, with the number.]

2. [Priority 2]
[One or two sentences on my achievement, with the number.]

3. [Priority 3]
[One or two sentences on my achievement, with the number.]

[Optional: one calm sentence addressing the concern, with evidence.]

I'm excited about the chance to work with the team at [Company] because [the reason]. Thanks again for your time, and I look forward to hearing from you.

All the best,
[Your name]

Rules:
- Use only the facts I gave you. Never invent numbers, employers, titles or results.
- Keep it under about 250 words.
- Write the priorities in the interviewer's own words where I've given them.
- Warm and confident, not stiff. No buzzwords. No overclaiming like "I'm the perfect fit."
- If I interviewed with more than one person, ask me for one detail from each conversation and write a separate version for each.
~~~

**Replace:** [Job title], [Company], [First name], [Something specific], [Something they said or shared], [Priority 1], [Priority 2], [Priority 3], [Achievement for priority 1, with the number], [Achievement for priority 2, with the number], [Achievement for priority 3, with the number], [The concern], [Your evidence], [The challenge, opportunity or mission], [Your name]

## Write a standard thank-you email

**What it does:** Writes a short, specific thank-you for a recruiter screen or an early-round interview.
**When to use it:** Within 24 hours of a first conversation.

### Step 1: Give it the details

~~~text
Write a short thank-you email (4 to 6 sentences) after my interview.

Job title: [Job title]
Company: [Company]
Interviewer's first name and role: [First name], [Their job title]
One specific thing we discussed: [Topic]
One thing that made me more interested in the role: [Something they said]
My most relevant skill or achievement for what they need: [Skill or achievement]

Structure: thank them, mention the specific topic, say what made me more interested, connect my skill to a goal or challenge they mentioned, and close by offering anything else they need.

Use only my facts. Warm, plain and specific. No buzzwords. Give me a subject line too.
~~~

**Replace:** [Job title], [Company], [First name], [Their job title], [Topic], [Something they said], [Skill or achievement]

## Write a post-final-round thank-you email

**What it does:** Writes a thank-you after the final round that shows you understand the role and you're ready to move forward.
**When to use it:** Within 24 hours of a final interview. Send one to each person you met.

### Step 1: Give it the details

~~~text
Write a thank-you email after my final-round interview.

Job title: [Job title]
Company: [Company]
This email is for: [First name], [Their job title]
Other people I met that day: [Names]
A topic we talked through in detail: [Topic]
The project, challenge or goal they described that excites me most: [Project or challenge]
My experience that carries over directly: [Relevant skill or project]

Structure: thank them for the final-round conversation, mention the people I met and the topic, say I'm more confident it's the right fit and why (the project plus my experience), and close by saying I'm excited about joining and happy to provide anything they need to decide.

Under 150 words. Use only my facts. Give me a subject line too.
~~~

**Replace:** [Job title], [Company], [First name], [Their job title], [Names], [Topic], [Project or challenge], [Relevant skill or project]

## Write a follow-up email

**What it does:** Writes a short check-in, a timing update, a reply to a rejection, or a stay-in-touch note.
**When to use it:** While you wait for an answer, or after you hear back.

### Step 1: Pick the situation

~~~text
Write a short follow-up email for this situation:

[Pick one: a check-in after their deadline passed, a check-in when they gave no deadline, a timing update because I have another offer, a reply to a rejection, or a stay-in-touch note a few months later]

Job title: [Job title]
Company: [Company]
Person: [First name], [Their job title]
Date of my last interview: [Date]
Something useful or relevant I can mention: [Optional: an article, an idea about a problem they raised, or an update on my side]
If I have another offer, my deadline to answer: [Optional: date]

Rules:
- Under 120 words. Friendly, not pushy.
- For a check-in, add the useful item if I gave one, so it's more than "any update?"
- For a timing update, only mention another offer because I've told you I have one. Say this company is my first choice only if I've said so.
- For a rejection, thank them, keep the door open, and offer to hear feedback without pushing for it.
- Never invent facts. Give me a subject line too.
~~~

**Replace:** [Pick one: a check-in after their deadline passed, a check-in when they gave no deadline, a timing update because I have another offer, a reply to a rejection, or a stay-in-touch note a few months later], [Job title], [Company], [First name], [Their job title], [Date], [Optional: an article, an idea about a problem they raised, or an update on my side], [Optional: date]
