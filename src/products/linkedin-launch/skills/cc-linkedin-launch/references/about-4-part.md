# The 4-part About prompts

Izzy's About section format: a professional summary, a dot divider, 5 emoji career highlights, another divider, the roles you want, and a call to action with your email. Use these prompts with the cc-linkedin-launch skill in Claude, or paste them into any AI chat tool. Swap everything in [square brackets] for your own details. The sample inside the prompt is a fictional person, so copy the structure and line breaks, never the facts.

LinkedIn allows up to 2,600 characters in the About section as this is written. LinkedIn changes its limits, so the counter on the profile is the final word.

## Write my 4-part About section

**What it does:** Writes a ready-to-paste About section in 4 parts: a summary, 5 emoji career highlights, the roles you want, and a call to action with your email.
**When to use it:** When your About section is empty, reads like a cover letter, or doesn't show a single number.

### Step 1: Write the About section

~~~text
Act as a LinkedIn coach. Write my LinkedIn About section in the exact 4-part structure below, including the dividers and the blank lines.

The job title I'm targeting: [Target job title]
Industries I'm aiming for: [Target industries]
The email I want recruiters to use: [Email address]

My resume or LinkedIn profile text:
[Paste your resume or profile]

My best wins (aim for 5, with numbers where I have them):
[Your wins]

THE 4-PART STRUCTURE

PART 1: A professional summary in 2 to 3 sentences. Include my job title, my years of experience, 3 specific skills people hire for, the industries I've worked in, and noteworthy companies or clients from my resume (only ones I can share). Only the first lines show before "see more", so put the title and years up front.

• • • • •

PART 2: 5 career highlights. IMPORTANT: put the title line and the detail on SEPARATE LINES, with a blank line between them, like this:

[Emoji] [Achievement title] | [Company]

[One sentence with the quantified achievement: what I did, how I did it, and the number]

Pick an emoji that fits each win: 💰 money, ⏱️ time, 📈 growth, 📦 inventory or operations, 🚚 logistics, 🤝 customers or partners, 🎓 training, 🛠️ something I built or fixed, ⭐ recognition. Put a pipe between the title and the company, not a dash. If a company is confidential, describe it instead, like "National Retailer".

• • • • •

PART 3 (190 characters or fewer): 1 to 2 sentences on the types of roles and companies that excite me, and why.

PART 4 (130 characters or fewer): A specific call to action for recruiters.

Then this line:
Connect with me at: → [Email address]

RULES
- Use only facts from my resume and wins. Never invent numbers, employers, clients or results. If I have fewer than 5 wins, write the ones I have and tell me how many are missing. Don't make one up.
- If you estimate anything, mark it (estimate, confirm).
- Be specific. No "passionate professional", "results-driven" or "proven track record".
- No em dashes or en dashes.
- After the About, show the character count for Part 3, Part 4 and the whole section (up to 2,600).

SAMPLE RESULT (fictional person; copy the structure and line breaks, never the facts)

Supply Chain Manager with 12+ years in demand planning, vendor negotiation and warehouse operations across food manufacturing and grocery distribution. I've run planning for a national grocery distributor and two regional food producers.

• • • • •

📦 $1.2M Inventory Reduction | National Grocery Distributor

Cut on-hand inventory by $1.2M in 10 months by moving 300+ slow-selling products to a weekly reorder cycle.

🚚 22% Lower Freight Costs | National Grocery Distributor

Lowered freight spend 22% by consolidating 9 carriers down to 4 and renegotiating rates on the busiest routes.

⏱️ Order-to-Ship Time Cut from 7 Days to 4 | Regional Food Producer

Shortened order-to-ship time by 3 days by redesigning the pick-and-pack layout with the warehouse team.

📈 Forecast Accuracy Up from 71% to 86% | Regional Food Producer

Raised forecast accuracy 15 points in one year by adding promotion calendars and customer input to the monthly plan.

🤝 Zero Stockouts Through Holiday Peak | Regional Food Producer

Kept our top 40 products in stock from November to December by setting up a shared weekly forecast with our 3 largest retail customers.

• • • • •

I'm excited by supply chain leadership roles at food and consumer goods companies that want calm, data-driven planning and teams who talk to each other.

Need someone to keep your shelves full and your costs down? Let's talk.

Connect with me at: → sam@example.com
~~~

**Replace:** [Target job title], [Target industries], [Email address], [Paste your resume or profile], [Your wins]

### Step 2: Check the facts and the counts

~~~text
Before I paste this into LinkedIn, check it:

1. List every number in my About and where it came from: my resume, my wins, or your estimate. Mark anything that didn't come straight from me (estimate, confirm).
2. Show the character count for Part 3 (190 or fewer), Part 4 (130 or fewer) and the whole section (up to 2,600). Trim anything that's over.
3. Check that each highlight has its title line, then a blank line, then the detail line.
4. Point out any word a recruiter might find vague, and suggest a plainer one.
~~~

**Replace:** Nothing. Paste this after Step 1.

## Find my 5 career highlights

**What it does:** Interviews you one question at a time to find 5 real wins with numbers for your About section, headlines and posts.
**When to use it:** When you have fewer than 5 wins, or your wins don't have numbers yet.

### Step 1: Start the interview

~~~text
Act as my career coach. I need 5 real career highlights for my LinkedIn About section, each with a number if one exists.

My resume or LinkedIn profile text:
[Paste your resume or profile]

The job title I'm targeting: [Target job title]

How to run this:
1. Ask me ONE question at a time and wait for my answer.
2. Start with my most recent role and work back.
3. Ask about money (saved, earned or managed), time (faster, sooner, hours saved), scale (people, accounts, products, locations), quality (fewer errors, complaints or returns) and firsts (something I built, started or fixed).
4. When I give you a win, ask follow-up questions until you know what I did, how I did it, and what changed. Ask: How many? How much? How fast? Compared with what?
5. If I don't know a number, help me find it (old reports, emails, performance reviews) or work out an honest estimate with me. Mark it (estimate, confirm).
6. Never make up a number or a win. If I say "we", ask what my part was.
7. Keep your messages short and encouraging. A vague first answer is normal. Ask a better question.
8. Once you have 5, show them in a table: emoji, highlight title, company, one-sentence detail, and where the number came from.

Start with your first question.
~~~

**Replace:** [Paste your resume or profile], [Target job title]
