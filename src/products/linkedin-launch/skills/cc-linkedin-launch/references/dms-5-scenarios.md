# Connection request and message prompts for 5 situations

Izzy's outreach kit for LinkedIn: for each of 5 situations, 3 connection request notes with character counts and 1 follow-up message for after they accept. Use these prompts with the cc-linkedin-launch skill in Claude, or paste them into any AI chat tool. Swap everything in [square brackets] for your own details. The sample inside the prompt is fictional.

Connection notes can be up to 300 characters as this is written, and some free accounts get less or a limited number of notes per month. LinkedIn changes these limits, so aim for 200 characters or fewer and trust the counter in the note box.

## Write my 5-situation message kit

**What it does:** Writes 3 connection request notes (with character counts) and 1 follow-up message for each of 5 situations: a recruiter, a hiring manager, a mutual connection, an event contact and a former colleague.
**When to use it:** Before you start reaching out on LinkedIn, so you have a message ready for every kind of person.

### Step 1: Write the kit

~~~text
Act as a LinkedIn outreach coach for job seekers. Write connection request notes and follow-up messages I can adapt for each person.

The job title I'm targeting: [Target job title]
Industries I'm aiming for: [Target industries]
My top win, in one sentence: [Your top win]
Other true details I can mention (years of experience, a specialty, a tool): [Other details]

Start with a short best-practices section (5 to 6 bullets) that covers:
- Connection notes can be up to 300 characters, and some free accounts get less or a limited number of notes per month. Aim for 200 or fewer.
- Make it personal: mention something specific about the person or their company.
- Lead with common ground or value, not an ask.
- Follow up within 24 to 48 hours after they accept.
- Never send the same message to several people at the same company.
- Keep messages conversational, not formal.

Then write templates for ALL 5 situations. For EACH situation, give:
- 3 connection request notes, each 200 characters or fewer. After each note, show the character count in parentheses, like (176 characters). Count every character, including spaces and punctuation, with the brackets as written.
- 1 follow-up message to send after they accept (100 to 200 words).

Situation 1: A recruiter at a company I'm targeting
Situation 2: A hiring manager (the person I'd report to)
Situation 3: A mutual connection who could introduce me. Write the 3 notes and the follow-up for the person I want to meet. Also write a short message asking my mutual connection for the introduction, with a "forwardable blurb": 3 to 4 sentences about me, written so they can copy and paste it straight to the other person.
Situation 4: Someone I met, or heard speak, at an event or webinar
Situation 5: A former colleague I want to reconnect with

RULES
- Use these placeholders for anything about the other person: [First name], [Company], [Job title], [Team], [Event], [Specific detail], [Mutual name]. Never invent facts about them.
- Use only facts about me from what I've given you. Never invent numbers or wins.
- Only mention an application, a meeting, a shared connection or an introduction if it really happened. Add a one-line note under any template where that matters.
- No pitch in the connection note. The note gets the connection. The follow-up starts the conversation.
- Conversational and specific, never salesy. No em dashes or en dashes.

EXAMPLE (fictional, for structure only)
Situation 4, note 1:
Hi [First name], great meeting you at [Event]. I enjoyed our chat about [Specific detail] and would love to stay in touch here. (127 characters)
~~~

**Replace:** [Target job title], [Target industries], [Your top win], [Other details]

## Write messages for one person

**What it does:** Writes 3 connection notes and a follow-up for one specific person, using what you know about them.
**When to use it:** When you've found someone and want a note that sounds like you wrote it just for them.

### Step 1: Describe the person

~~~text
Write LinkedIn connection notes and a follow-up message for one person.

Situation (recruiter, hiring manager, mutual connection, event contact or former colleague): [Situation]
Their first name: [First name]
Their company and role: [Company], [Job title]
What I know about them (a post, a talk, a project, something we share): [Specific detail]
What I'd like from this (stay on their radar, a 15-minute chat, an introduction, advice): [Your goal]

About me:
The job title I'm targeting: [Target job title]
My top win, in one sentence: [Your top win]

Write:
1. 3 connection notes, each 200 characters or fewer, with the character count after each one in parentheses. (LinkedIn allows up to 300, and some free accounts get less.)
2. 1 follow-up message for after they accept, 100 to 200 words, ending with one easy question or ask.
3. If the situation is a mutual connection: a short message asking them for the introduction, plus a 3 to 4 sentence forwardable blurb about me that they can copy and paste.

Use only what I've told you about them and about me. Don't invent shared history. No em dashes or en dashes.
~~~

**Replace:** [Situation], [First name], [Company], [Job title], [Specific detail], [Your goal], [Target job title], [Your top win]

## Trim my connection note

**What it does:** Counts the characters in your note and gives you 3 shorter versions that keep the point.
**When to use it:** When LinkedIn says your note is too long, or you want room to spare.

### Step 1: Count and trim

~~~text
Here's my LinkedIn connection note:
[Paste your note]

1. Count the characters, including spaces and punctuation, and tell me the total.
2. Write 3 versions at 200 characters or fewer (make one of them around 150), each with its character count in parentheses.
3. Keep the person's name, the specific detail and my reason for connecting. Cut filler first, like "I hope this finds you well" or "I wanted to reach out".
4. Don't add anything I didn't say. No em dashes or en dashes.

LinkedIn's counter is the final word. If my account shows a lower limit, I'll tell you and you can trim again.
~~~

**Replace:** [Paste your note]
