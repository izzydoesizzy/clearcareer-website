# Headline prompts

Izzy's pipe-style LinkedIn headline: Job Title | Key Experience or Skills | Major Achievement. Use these prompts with the cc-linkedin-launch skill in Claude, or paste them into any AI chat tool. Swap everything in [square brackets] for your own details. The samples inside the prompts are fictional and only show the structure, so never copy their numbers.

LinkedIn allows headlines of up to 220 characters as this is written. LinkedIn changes its limits, so the counter on the profile is the final word.

## Write 10 pipe-style headlines

**What it does:** Writes 10 LinkedIn headlines in the Job Title | Key Experience or Skills | Major Achievement format, each from a different angle, using only your real wins.
**When to use it:** When your headline is only your job title, or you want strong options to test.

### Step 1: Write 10 headlines

~~~text
Act as a LinkedIn coach who writes headlines that recruiters want to click.

The job title I'm targeting: [Target job title]
Industries I'm aiming for: [Target industries]

My resume or LinkedIn profile text:
[Paste your resume or profile]

My 3 to 5 best wins (with numbers if I have them):
[Your wins]

Write 10 high-impact LinkedIn headlines for recruiters hiring a [Target job title].

FORMAT
Use pipe separators ( | ) between the parts:
Job Title | Key Experience or Skills | Major Achievement
You can add a second achievement or a second skills part if it fits.

RULES
1. Start every headline with the target job title, or the closest title I can honestly claim. Recruiters search by title, and LinkedIn often cuts headlines short in search results and comments, so the title goes first.
2. Give each headline a different angle: years and industries, a money result, a time result, a tools or specialty angle, a scale angle (team size, budget, number of accounts), a "who I help" angle, and so on.
3. Up to 220 characters each. After each headline, show the character count in parentheses, like (148 characters). Count spaces and punctuation.
4. Use only facts from my resume and wins. Never invent numbers, employers, tools, clients or awards. If a win has no number, use it without one. If you estimate anything, mark it (estimate, confirm).
5. Only name companies or clients that appear in my resume and that I could share publicly. Otherwise describe them, like "National Grocery Chains".
6. No buzzwords such as results-driven, passionate, detail-oriented or guru. No em dashes or en dashes. Capitalize the main words, like the examples.
7. Output a numbered list, one headline per line, and nothing else.

EXAMPLES (fictional people, for structure only; never copy their numbers)
1. Supply Chain Manager | 12+ Years in Food Manufacturing and Grocery Distribution | Cut $1.2M in Inventory in 10 Months (117 characters)
2. Payroll Specialist | Multi-Province Payroll for 900+ Employees | Cut Monthly Pay Errors from 40 to 6 (100 characters)
3. Customer Success Manager | B2B Software | $3M Book of Business | Kept 95% of Accounts Through a Price Increase (110 characters)
~~~

**Replace:** [Target job title], [Target industries], [Paste your resume or profile], [Your wins]

### Step 2: Pick 3 to test

~~~text
From those 10 headlines, pick 3 to test, one of each type:

1. Safe/Direct: clear and keyword-rich. The title plus the skills a recruiter would type into search.
2. Result-Focused: leads with my strongest result.
3. Distinctive/Niche: built on the most specific or unusual thing about my experience.

You can tighten the wording, but don't add new facts. For each one, show the character count and one line on why it's in the test.

Then recommend which one to run first and why, and give me a simple test plan:
- Run each headline for 2 weeks, one at a time.
- Each Friday, write down what LinkedIn shows me: profile views, search appearances (if my account shows them) and any recruiter messages.
- After 6 weeks, keep the one that did best, or the one I like most if they're close.

Remind me that a small test like this gives rough signals, not proof. Results vary.
~~~

**Replace:** Nothing. Paste this after Step 1.

## Fix my current headline

**What it does:** Checks the headline you have now against the pipe format and gives you 3 stronger versions.
**When to use it:** When you already have a headline and want a quick upgrade, not 10 new options.

### Step 1: Check and rewrite

~~~text
Here is my current LinkedIn headline:
[Paste your current headline]

The job title I'm targeting: [Target job title]
My best 2 to 3 wins: [Your wins]

Check my headline against this format: Job Title | Key Experience or Skills | Major Achievement (pipe separators, up to 220 characters).

Tell me in 3 short bullets:
1. Does it start with the title a recruiter would search for?
2. Does it show what I'm good at, in words people search for?
3. Does it prove it with a real result?

Then write 3 better versions: Safe/Direct, Result-Focused and Distinctive/Niche. Show the character count after each one in parentheses. Use only facts I've given you. Don't invent numbers. No em dashes or en dashes.
~~~

**Replace:** [Paste your current headline], [Target job title], [Your wins]
