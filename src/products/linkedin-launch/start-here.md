# Start here: LinkedIn Launch Pack

Welcome in. This pack is my own LinkedIn formats, the ones I use when I write profiles and outreach with the people I coach: a pipe-style headline, a 4-part About section with 5 career highlights, connection requests and follow-ups for 5 situations, and posts built from your own stories. You get five short guides you can read and fill in, plus a Claude skill that writes all of it with you, from your real resume and your real wins. It works whether you're polishing a profile you've had for years or building your very first one. Start small. A better headline today beats a perfect profile next month.

## Your first 10 minutes

1. Write down 3 wins from your work. Rough notes are fine. Add a number to each one if you have it.
2. Open **Guide 01** and read "The formula".
3. Write one headline: your target title, a pipe, your key skills, a pipe, your best win.
4. Check it's 220 characters or fewer, then paste it into your LinkedIn headline.

That's it. You've just fixed the line that follows your name everywhere on LinkedIn. No profile yet? Open **Guide 05** instead and start with Step 1.

## A 7-day plan

- **Day 1:** Write your 3 to 5 wins, then 10 headlines (Guide 01). Put your favourite on your profile.
- **Day 2:** Write your 4-part About (Guide 02) and paste it in. Check it on your phone.
- **Day 3:** Update your photo and banner, and add 12 to 15 skills (Guide 05, Steps 2, 3 and 7).
- **Day 4:** Build your search links and find 10 people to contact (Guide 03).
- **Day 5:** Send 5 connection notes. Follow up with anyone who accepts.
- **Day 6:** Mine your stories and write your first post, a Career Lesson (Guide 04).
- **Day 7:** Post it, reply to every comment, send 5 more notes, and pick your next headline to test.

Starting from a blank profile? Do Guide 05 on Days 1 to 3, then pick up the plan from Day 4.

## Good to know

- **Results vary.** A stronger profile and better messages help the right people find you and reply, but nobody can promise views, replies, interviews or offers.
- **Keep your facts true.** Every number, title and story should be one you can explain if someone asks. If you estimate, say "about" and know how you got there.
- **Every example is fictional.** Sam, Priya, Marcus and Dana aren't real people. Copy the shape, never their numbers.
- **LinkedIn changes its limits.** As I write this, headlines can be up to 220 characters, the About section up to 2,600, and connection notes up to 300 (some free accounts get less, or a limited number per month, so aim for 200 or fewer). LinkedIn's own counter is the final word.
