# Campaign method: pick, find, reach

Read this before you start a campaign. It covers how to pick the companies, find one person at each, work out their email address and find a real hook. The email itself is in `references/email-rules.md`. The copy-paste prompts are at the end of this file.

The idea behind it: don't just apply and wait. Find the person who hires for the role, send a short and specific email about their work and yours, then follow up.

**1. Pick the companies**

From the user's list, choose the top 10 (or the number they asked for). Prioritize:

- The strongest skill and experience fit for the user
- A mix of company sizes: some large, some mid-size, some growing startups
- Companies where a warm, personal note is likely to land (a team that's growing, a leader who posts or speaks, a recent launch)
- Different angles, so the user isn't sending ten versions of the same email

Give one sentence for each: why this company is a top target for outreach.

**2. Find one person per company**

Look for one person, in this order:

1. The hiring manager or team lead for the kind of role the user wants
2. The head of that department
3. A talent acquisition or recruiting lead
4. Another relevant senior person in that part of the company

Who that is depends on size:

- **Under about 50 people:** the founder, the CEO or the head of the function
- **Mid-size:** the director or manager who leads the team
- **Large:** the manager of the specific team. A recruiter is a second contact, not the first.

Where to look (public sources only):

- **The job post:** some show "Meet the hiring team", and many say "reporting to the [Title]".
- **LinkedIn:** the company page, then People, then a keyword for the function plus a level ("operations director", "head of design").
- **The company website:** Team, About or Leadership pages, and blog author pages.
- **Google:** search LinkedIn profiles with a string like this:

~~~text
site:linkedin.com/in "[Company]" "[Title]"
site:linkedin.com/in "[Company]" ("director" OR "head of" OR "manager") "[Function]"
~~~

- **Press releases, conference speaker pages, podcast guest pages and industry association pages.**

Before you use a name:

- Check the source is recent enough to show they still hold the role. An old press release isn't enough on its own.
- If two people share a name, use the profile link and the company to tell them apart. If you still aren't sure, write "Not found".
- Record: name, title, profile link (the exact URL, or "Not found"), one sentence on why this person is a good contact, and where you found them.

Never guess who someone is. A wrong name in an email is worse than no email.

How to help without web search: suggest the two or three likely titles for the person the role reports to (a Senior Accountant usually reports to an Accounting Manager or a Controller, for example), give the user the search strings above, and ask them to paste back what they find.

**3. Work out the email address**

Work through these in order. Stop at the first solid address.

1. **Published:** the person's profile contact info, their posts, a personal website, a speaker bio, a podcast page, or a press release that lists them as a media contact.
2. **The company pattern:** find any real, published address at the same company domain (a press release, a job post that lists a recruiter's email, a blog author page, a named contact on the website). Apply its format to the person's name. Common patterns:
   - first.last@company.com
   - first@company.com
   - firstlast@company.com
   - flast@company.com (first initial, then last name)
   - firstl@company.com (first name, then last initial)
   - first_last@company.com
3. **A finder or verifier tool:** the user can use one. Many have a free tier with a few searches a month. Never say they need a paid tool.
4. **Free checks:** search the address in quotes. In Gmail, type the address in the To field and hover over it. A name or photo suggests it's real (no pop-up doesn't prove it's wrong).

How to read what the user finds:

- **Valid or verified:** send it.
- **Accept-all or risky:** the server accepts everything, so the tool can't tell. Send to the best guess and try the next pattern if it bounces.
- **Bounced:** try the next pattern, or reach the person on LinkedIn instead.

Label every guessed address "(pattern guess, verify before sending)", and say which published address the pattern came from. Give one backup pattern. One person, one best guess, one email: never suggest sending the same email to several guessed addresses at once. A general inbox (info@, careers@) is a last resort.

**4. Find the hook**

The hook is one specific, real thing the person has done. It proves the user did their homework, and it gives the email a reason to exist.

What counts:

- A post or article they wrote
- A talk, panel or podcast they were on
- A quote in a news story
- A project, launch or initiative they led
- A promotion or a move to a new role
- A milestone for their team

What doesn't count: their job title, a general description of the company, or praise ("your impressive career").

Where to look: their public posts as they show up in search results, the company blog and newsroom, conference agendas, podcast listings, industry news and trade publications.

Rules:

- Prefer something from the last 12 months, and note the date.
- Record the source link. The user must be able to open it and read it before they send.
- If you can't find anything about the person, use a recent company item (a launch, an expansion, a new office) with its link, and say plainly that the hook is about the company.
- Never invent a hook, and never stretch what the source says.

**A finished contact card (fictional)**

~~~text
3. Harbourline Logistics (a fictional company)
Why this company: A mid-size freight company growing its Ontario network, and the user cut delivery delays at a regional distributor.

Contact: Morgan Tremblay, Director of Operations
Profile: [Exact profile link]
Found on: [Company leadership page link]
Email: morgan.tremblay@harbourline.example (pattern guess, verify before sending)
How I got the pattern: a press release lists media contact jamie.ross@harbourline.example, so the pattern is first.last
Backup pattern: mtremblay@harbourline.example (pattern guess, verify before sending)

Hook: Morgan spoke on a panel about cutting empty truck kilometres at a regional supply chain conference in [Month].
Check before you send: [Conference page link], [date]
~~~

## Find the right person

**What it does:** Works out who most likely hires for your role at a company, and gives you the searches to find them.
**When to use it:** You have a target company but no name to write to.

### Step 1: Get the likely titles and searches

~~~text
I want to reach the person who would hire for this role.

Company: [Company]
Company size (if I know it): [Size, or "not sure"]
The role or team I want: [Target role or team]
Job post (if there is one): [Paste the job post, or delete this line]

1. Tell me the two or three most likely titles for the person this role reports to.
2. If you can search the web, find one publicly listed person at this company with one of those titles. Give me their name, title, the exact profile link (or "Not found") and the source where you found them. Only use people listed on the company website, a profile that shows up in search results, a press release, a conference or podcast page, or the job post. Never guess who someone is. If you aren't sure, say "Not found".
3. Give me two Google search strings I can run myself, in this shape: site:linkedin.com/in "[Company]" "[Title]"
4. Remind me to check that the person still works there before I write.
~~~

**Replace:** [Company], [Size, or "not sure"], [Target role or team], [Paste the job post, or delete this line]

## Work out an email address

**What it does:** Lists the most likely work email addresses for a person, based on a real published address at the same company.
**When to use it:** You've found the right person and need to reach them by email.

### Step 1: List the likely addresses

~~~text
Help me work out a work email address.

Person: [First name] [Last name]
Company: [Company]
Company website: [Company website]
A real address I found at the same company (if any): [Example address and where you found it, or "none yet"]

1. If you can search the web, look for an address the person published themselves (a profile, a speaker bio, a press release). If you find one, give me the link.
2. If not, use the real address I gave you (or one you find on a press release, job post or blog author page at the same domain) to work out the company's pattern, and tell me where it came from.
3. Give me my best guess and one backup. Label each "(pattern guess, verify before sending)".
4. Never tell me an address is verified. You can't check it.
~~~

**Replace:** [First name], [Last name], [Company], [Company website], [Example address and where you found it, or "none yet"]

### Step 2: Check before you send

~~~text
Before I send, walk me through checking the address:
1. Search it in quotes on Google.
2. Type it in the To field in Gmail and hover over it to see if a name or photo appears.
3. If I use a free email verifier, tell me what "valid", "accept-all" and "bounced" mean and what to do with each.
Remind me: one person, one best guess, one email. If it bounces, try the backup pattern or reach them on LinkedIn.
~~~

**Replace:** nothing. Use it in the same chat, right after Step 1.

## Find a specific hook

**What it does:** Finds one specific, real thing a person has done that you can mention in the first line of your email, with the link.
**When to use it:** You have a name, and you want your email to read like it could only have been written to them.

### Step 1: Find the hook

~~~text
Find one specific, real thing this person has done professionally that I could mention in a short email.

Person: [Name], [Title] at [Company]
Profile link (if I have it): [Profile link, or delete this line]

Look for: a post or article they wrote, a talk, panel or podcast they were on, a quote in a news story, a project, launch or initiative they led, a promotion, or a milestone for their team. Prefer the last 12 months.

Give me:
1. The thing, in one or two sentences
2. The source link and the date
3. One detail from it I could mention to show I actually read or watched it

Rules: if you can't search the web, tell me. Never invent anything about this person. If you find nothing about them, give me a recent company item (a launch, an expansion) with its link, and say it's about the company. Use public, work-related information only.
~~~

**Replace:** [Name], [Title], [Company], [Profile link, or delete this line]
