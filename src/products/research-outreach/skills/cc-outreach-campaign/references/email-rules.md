# Email rules

Read this before you write any outreach email or follow-up. Follow it exactly. The copy-paste prompts are at the end of this file.

The goal: a short note that could only have been written to this one person at this one company, in the user's own voice. Like writing to someone you'd want to grab a coffee with. Warm, curious and real. Not formal, not transactional, not desperate.

**The shape**

75 to 125 words, not counting the sign-off. Brevity is respect.

1. **Greeting:** "Hi [First name]," and then a blank line.
2. **Opening (1 to 2 sentences):** start with them. Use one of the 8 opener patterns below, and a different one for each email in a batch. Include one specific detail that proves the user actually read, watched or listened. Connect it to the user's own work or curiosity, not to praise.
3. **Bridge (2 to 3 sentences):** connect their work to one win from the user's background that relates to it. State it plainly, with the user's own number if they have one. It should feel like "oh, I've worked on something like that", not a list of achievements.
4. **Transition and ask (2 to 3 sentences):** say why the user wants to talk, using one of the 5 transition patterns below. Then a warm, low-pressure ask for 15 minutes. Then an exit clause.
5. **Sign-off:** the user's name, and their email or LinkedIn link. If they use a booking link and want it in, add one short line before the sign-off.

**The 8 opener patterns**

Pick one per email. Never repeat a pattern within a batch. The examples are fictional.

1. "I loved [their specific thing], especially [one detail]."
   Example: "I loved your panel on cutting empty truck kilometres, especially the point that backhaul planning starts when the quote goes out."
2. "Your [thing] got me thinking about [related idea from the user's own work]."
   Example: "Your post on onboarding checklists got me thinking about the 30-day plan I built for new pharmacy techs."
3. "I've been following [Company]'s work on [topic], and [specific observation]."
   Example: "I've been following Lumen Health's work on virtual care, and the move to evening appointments looks like a smart fit for shift workers."
4. "[Specific detail from their work] really stuck with me because [personal reason]."
   Example: "Your line about fixing the handoff, not the hire, really stuck with me because handoffs were most of my last project."
5. "I was reading about [their project or initiative] and [curious question or observation]."
   Example: "I was reading about Northwind Bank's new small business portal and wondered how your team picked the first features to launch."
6. "Something you said about [topic] in [place] resonated, [brief why]."
   Example: "Something you said about vendor scorecards on a supply chain podcast in March resonated, since I rebuilt ours last year."
7. "Your path from [A] to [B] is the kind of move I find really interesting, [why]."
   Example: "Your path from nursing to health tech product work is the kind of move I find really interesting, since I'm making a similar one from teaching."
8. "I noticed [Company] just [recent thing], [genuine reaction tied to the user's experience]."
   Example: "I noticed Bluepeak Software just opened a Halifax office, and having helped open a regional office before, I know how busy that first year gets."

**The 5 transition patterns**

Pick one per email, and vary them across a batch.

1. "I'd love to hear more about how your team is approaching [specific thing related to their work]."
2. "I have a couple of questions about [specific part of their work or the company's direction]."
3. "I'm curious how [something from their notable work] is playing out now."
4. "I'd love to learn more about [specific initiative, product or direction] and where it's headed."
5. "I keep thinking about [specific detail from their work] and would love to hear your perspective."

**The ask**

One small ask: 15 minutes. For example:
- "Would you be open to a 15-minute call in the next couple of weeks?"
- "Could I borrow 15 minutes of your time next week or the week after?"

**Exit clauses**

End the ask with one, so saying no feels easy:
- "No pressure at all if the timing doesn't work."
- "Totally understand if you're swamped."
- "If now isn't a good time, no worries at all."
- "If you're not the right person for this, I'd be grateful for a pointer."

**Subject lines**

2 to 5 words. Personal and conversational, like a text to a friend, not a business memo. Never "Re:" or "Fwd:" unless it's a real thread. Examples:
- "Your logistics post + a question"
- "Fellow alumni, quick question"
- "[Company] + [target role]?"
- "Your panel on backhauls"
- "Question about the Halifax office"

**Never use these**

- "I'm reaching out" or "I wanted to reach out" to start a sentence
- "I hope this email finds you well", "To whom it may concern"
- "I'm confident I'd be a great fit", or anything else that presumes
- Buzzwords: "synergy", "leverage", "value-add", "results-driven", "detail-oriented"
- Big praise: "genuinely impressive", "incredible", "amazing", "brilliant", "inspiring"
- Scroll clichés: "stopped me mid-scroll", "caught my eye", "jumped out at me", "I stumbled across", "I couldn't help but notice", "I was blown away"
- "Pick your brain"
- "Thanks in advance", "Looking forward to hearing from you"
- Em dashes or en dashes
- Attachments on a first email (offer the resume instead)

Openers show curiosity and shared interest, not admiration or flattery.

**Write in the user's voice**

If the user shared something they've written, match it: sentence length, contractions, how they greet people, how they sign off. Use their words for their wins. If they didn't, write plainly and warmly, with contractions and short sentences. Don't add exclamation marks unless the user uses them. Never put words in their mouth they wouldn't say.

**Two finished emails (fictional people, companies and numbers)**

~~~text
Subject: Your panel on empty kilometres

Hi Morgan,

I loved your panel on cutting empty truck kilometres, especially the point that backhaul planning starts when the quote goes out.

That matches what I saw at a regional distributor. I rebuilt our route planning sheet with the dispatch team, and late deliveries fell from 14% to 6% over two quarters.

I'd love to hear more about how your team is approaching backhauls as the Ontario network grows. Would you be open to a 15-minute call in the next couple of weeks? No pressure at all if the timing doesn't work.

Riley Quinn
riley.quinn@email.example
~~~

**Follow-up rules**

- Send it in the same thread, 5 to 7 business days after the first email, only if there's no reply.
- 2 to 3 sentences. Light and friendly. Add one small new thing if the user has one (a thought, a relevant link, a short update), or simply bring the email back to the top of their inbox.
- No guilt: never "Did you get my email?" or "Just checking in again".
- One more short note about a week later is fine: a graceful close. After two follow-ups with no reply, stop and move on. Suggest a second person at the same company if the user wants to keep going.
- If you know today's date, give real dates. If not, say "business day 5" and so on.

Fictional examples:

~~~text
Follow-up 1 (same thread, 5 to 7 business days later)

Hi Morgan,

Bringing this back to the top of your inbox in case it got buried. I'd still love 15 minutes on how you're planning backhauls in Ontario, whenever it suits you. If not, no worries at all.

Riley
~~~

~~~text
Final note (about a week after that, then stop)

Hi Morgan,

I'll leave it here so I don't crowd your inbox. If your team ever wants to compare notes on route planning, I'd be glad to talk. Thanks again for the panel, it's stayed with me.

Riley
~~~

**Before you show an email: the check**

- Every fact about the person comes from a source you can link, and you've given the link.
- Every win, number and connection comes from the user.
- The opener and transition patterns are different from the other emails in the batch.
- 75 to 125 words, not counting the sign-off. Count them. Under 75: add one more specific detail. Over 125: cut.
- The subject line is 2 to 5 words.
- No banned phrases. No em dashes or en dashes.
- One ask, one exit clause.
- It reads out loud like a person talking, not a press release.

## Write one outreach email

**What it does:** Writes a short, specific email (75 to 125 words) to one person, in your voice, with a 2 to 5 word subject line.
**When to use it:** You've found the right person and one real thing they've done, and you're ready to write.

### Step 1: Write it

~~~text
Write a short networking email from me to [Their name], [Their title] at [Company].

About me: [Paste your resume, or 2 to 3 wins with numbers]
The role or team I'm interested in: [Target role or team]
Why this company: [One sentence]
Something specific they did: [The post, talk, project or news item, with the link]
Any real connection: [Shared school, former employer, mutual contact, or "none"]
How I write (optional): [Paste a short email you've written, or delete this line]
My sign-off: [Your name and email or LinkedIn]

Structure:
1. "Hi [First name]," then a blank line.
2. Opening, 1 to 2 sentences, about them. Use one of these patterns: "I loved [their thing], especially [one detail]" / "Your [thing] got me thinking about [my related work]" / "I've been following [Company]'s work on [topic], and [observation]" / "[Detail from their work] really stuck with me because [reason]" / "I was reading about [their project] and [question or observation]" / "Something you said about [topic] in [place] resonated, [why]" / "Your path from [A] to [B] is the kind of move I find really interesting, [why]" / "I noticed [Company] just [recent thing], [reaction tied to my experience]". Include one specific detail. Curiosity, not praise.
3. Bridge, 2 to 3 sentences: connect their work to one of my wins that relates to it. Plain, not boastful.
4. Transition and ask, 2 to 3 sentences: why I want to talk (for example "I'd love to hear more about how your team is approaching [thing]" or "I'm curious how [thing] is playing out now"), then ask for 15 minutes, then an exit clause like "No pressure at all if the timing doesn't work."
5. My sign-off.

Rules:
- 75 to 125 words, not counting the sign-off. Show the word count.
- Subject line: 2 to 5 words, personal and conversational.
- Use only facts I gave you. Never invent a connection, a number or a detail about them.
- Never use: "I'm reaching out", "I wanted to reach out", "I hope this email finds you well", "I'm confident I'd be a great fit", "pick your brain", "thanks in advance", "looking forward to hearing from you", buzzwords, big praise ("incredible", "amazing", "inspiring"), or clichés like "caught my eye" or "I stumbled across".
- No dashes. No attachments.
- Make it sound like me.

Output: SUBJECT: [subject line], then the email, then the word count.
~~~

**Replace:** [Their name], [Their title], [Company], [Paste your resume, or 2 to 3 wins with numbers], [Target role or team], [One sentence], [The post, talk, project or news item, with the link], [Shared school, former employer, mutual contact, or "none"], [Paste a short email you've written, or delete this line], [Your name and email or LinkedIn]

### Step 2: Check it

~~~text
Now check the email against this list and fix anything that fails. Then show me the final version.
- Every fact about the person matches the source I gave you.
- Every win and number is mine.
- 75 to 125 words, not counting the sign-off.
- Subject line is 2 to 5 words.
- One ask and one exit clause.
- None of the banned phrases, and no dashes.
- It sounds like a person talking, not a template. Read it as if you were the recipient: does it feel like it was written only to them?
~~~

**Replace:** nothing. Use it in the same chat, right after Step 1.

## Write the follow-up

**What it does:** Writes a short, friendly follow-up for an email that hasn't had a reply, and a final note for later.
**When to use it:** 5 to 7 business days have passed since your first email, with no reply.

### Step 1: Write the follow-ups

~~~text
I sent this email [Number] business days ago and haven't heard back:
[Paste the email you sent]

Something small and new I could add (optional): [A thought, a relevant link or a short update, or "nothing"]

Write two notes I can send in the same thread:
1. Follow-up 1, to send now: 2 to 3 sentences, light and friendly. Bring the email back to the top of their inbox and add the new thing if I gave you one.
2. A final note, to send about a week later if there's still no reply: 2 to 3 sentences that close gracefully and leave the door open.

Rules: no guilt ("Did you get my email?"), no dashes, only facts I gave you, and it should sound like me. After the final note, I stop.
~~~

**Replace:** [Number], [Paste the email you sent], [A thought, a relevant link or a short update, or "nothing"]
