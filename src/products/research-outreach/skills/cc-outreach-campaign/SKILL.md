# Outreach Campaign

For each of the user's top companies: find the one person who hires for the role, work out how to reach them, find something real they've done, and write a short, specific email in the user's voice, with a follow-up and a tracker.

## Before you start

Check what the user has already given you, then ask for anything missing in one short message. Don't guess.

- **Their background:** a resume, or 2 to 3 wins with numbers if they have them.
- **Target role:** the job title they want, and the kind of team they'd join.
- **The companies:** a ranked list (for example from the cc-company-scorecards skill), or 5 to 15 company names with a line on why each fits. Default to the top 10.
- **Their sign-off:** the name and email or LinkedIn link they want under each email. Use [Your name] and [Your email] placeholders if they skip it.
- **How they write (optional):** a short email or message they've written, or three words for their tone. The emails should sound like them, not like a template.
- **Any real connections (optional):** a shared school, a former employer, a mutual contact who suggested reaching out. "None" is a fine answer.

A good first message looks like this:

~~~text
Happy to build your outreach campaign. I need:
1. Your resume, or 2 to 3 wins from your work
2. The job title you want
3. Your target companies (or your ranked list)
4. Your name and email for the sign-off
5. Optional: a short email you've written, so these sound like you
6. Optional: any real connections at these companies
~~~

## How to work

1. **Pick the companies.** Read `references/campaign-method.md`. From the user's list, choose the top 10 (or the number they asked for) where a short personal email is most likely to land: the strongest skill and experience fit, a mix of company sizes, and different angles. Give one line on why each made the cut. Offer to swap any.
2. **Check whether you can search the web.** If you can, research each contact yourself. If you can't, say so in one line and switch to guided mode: tell the user which titles to search and where, and ask them to paste back the person's name, title and profile link, plus one thing that person has posted or worked on. Then write the emails from what they give you.
3. **Find one person per company.** Look for the hiring manager or team lead for the role first, then the head of the department, then a talent acquisition lead, then another relevant senior person. Only use people who are publicly listed: the company website, a profile that shows up in search results, a press release, a conference or podcast page, or a job post that names the hiring team. Check the source is recent enough to show they still hold the role. If you aren't sure you have the right person, write "Not found" and give the user the search to run. Never guess who someone is.
4. **Suggest how to find their email.** Look for a published address first. If there isn't one, find a real, published address at the same company domain and apply its pattern to the person's name. Say where you saw the pattern. Label every guess "(pattern guess, verify before sending)" and give one backup pattern. You can't verify an address, so never imply that you did.
5. **Find the hook.** Find one specific, real thing the person has done: a post, a talk, a podcast, a quote in an article, a project, a launch, a promotion or an initiative they led. Record the source link and date. If you can't find anything about the person, use a recent company item (a launch, an expansion) with its link, and say the hook is about the company. Never invent a hook.
6. **Write the email.** Read `references/email-rules.md` and follow it exactly: greeting, an opening about them (one of the 8 opener patterns, a different one for each email), a bridge to one matching win, a transition (one of the 5 transition patterns), a 15-minute ask and an exit clause. 75 to 125 words, not counting the sign-off. A subject line of 2 to 5 words. Check it against the banned phrases and count the words before you show it.
7. **Write the follow-up.** One short follow-up per email, 2 to 3 sentences, to send in the same thread 5 to 7 business days later if there's no reply. Use the follow-up rules in `references/email-rules.md`.
8. **Build the tracker.** Read `references/tracker.md` and give the tracker table with every contact filled in, plus the sending plan.
9. **Work in batches of 5.** Show the first 5 companies in full, then offer the next batch. Don't cut corners on research to fit more in.

Present each company in this shape:

~~~text
[#]. [Company]
Why this company: [One sentence]

Contact: [Name], [Title]
Profile: [Exact link, or "Not found"]
Found on: [Source link]
Email: [Best guess] (pattern guess, verify before sending)
How I got the pattern: [The published address or source it's based on]
Backup pattern: [Second guess] (pattern guess, verify before sending)

Hook: [One or two sentences on the specific thing they did]
Check before you send: [Source link and date]

Subject: [2 to 5 words]

Hi [First name],

[Opening: about them, with the hook]

[Bridge: one win that connects]

[Transition, the ask, and an exit clause]

[Your name]
[Your email or LinkedIn]

Word count: [N]

Follow-up (same thread, 5 to 7 business days later, only if there's no reply):
[2 to 3 sentences]
~~~

## What you can do

1. **A full campaign:** the person, their email pattern, a hook, an email and a follow-up for each company, plus a tracker.
   "Here's my resume and my top 10 companies. Build my outreach campaign."
2. **Find the right person:** the likely hiring manager for a role, and where to look.
   "Who should I email about a data analyst role at [Company]?"
3. **Work out an email address:** the likely pattern, where it comes from, and how to check it.
   "How do I work out [Name]'s email at [Company]?"
4. **Find a hook:** one specific thing the person has done, with a link.
   "Find something specific [Name] has posted or worked on that I can mention."
5. **Write one email:** 75 to 125 words, in your voice, with a short subject line.
   "Write an email to [Name], [Title] at [Company]. Here's my resume and her recent post."
6. **Follow-ups:** short notes for the people who haven't replied.
   "I emailed 5 people last week. Here's what I sent. Write my follow-ups."
7. **A tracker:** a simple table to keep the campaign moving.
   "Give me a tracker for these 10 contacts."

## Rules

- Only use people who are publicly listed. Never guess who someone is, never mix up two people with the same name, and never invent names, titles, profile links or email addresses.
- Label every guessed email "(pattern guess, verify before sending)". Never imply you checked whether an address works.
- Every hook has a source link the user can open. No source, no hook. Tell the user to read it before they send.
- Use only the user's real wins, numbers and connections. Never invent a shared connection, a mutual contact or a fact about the recipient.
- Never invent numbers, employers, titles or dates. Ask before estimating a metric, and label any estimate "(estimate, confirm)".
- Write in the user's voice: their words, their tone, their sign-off. No buzzwords, no flattery, nothing that reads like a template.
- 75 to 125 words, one ask, no attachments on a first email. Offer the resume instead.
- Use public, work-related information only. Never use someone's personal social media, home address or family details.
- One-to-one emails only. Don't help with mass blasts, scraped contact lists or bought lists. These are personal, one-to-one emails: if someone asks the user to stop, they stop. Follow the spirit of Canada's anti-spam law (CASL) and the US CAN-SPAM Act. This is general guidance, not legal advice.
- Never promise a reply, an interview or a job. Results vary.
- Keep the user's facts. Don't upgrade a title, stretch a date range or inflate a result.
- Match the user's spelling: Canadian or US, based on their resume or messages.
- Plain language. No em dashes or en dashes in anything you write for the user.
