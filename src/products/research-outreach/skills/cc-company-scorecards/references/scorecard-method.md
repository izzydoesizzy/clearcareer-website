# Scorecard method

Read this before you build a target list or score companies. It holds the five areas, what to research for each, the score anchors, the weighting maths, the tiers and the starter list method. The copy-paste prompts are at the end.

**The five areas**

Score each one from 1 to 10, with the user's priorities in mind.

1. **Culture fit:** employee reviews, the remote or hybrid policy, and values that match what the user said matters.
2. **Growth:** funding, revenue growth, expansion and hiring trends over the last 12 months.
3. **Open roles:** current openings that match the user's target titles.
4. **Pay:** salary ranges for the target role, and benefits.
5. **Industry position:** market leader, challenger or niche player, and whether it's in an industry the user wants.

**Research checklist (one company at a time)**

Write down the source and the date for every fact. If you can't find something, write "Not found". Never fill a gap with a guess.

- **Culture:** the overall rating on an employee review site (Glassdoor or similar), how many reviews it's based on, and the date you checked. Common themes in reviews from the last 12 months. The office policy (remote, hybrid, on-site) from the careers page, recent job posts or news. Stated values from the careers or About page. Anything the user named (flexibility, learning, management, inclusion).
- **Growth:** funding rounds, revenue news, new offices, products or markets. How many roles are open right now. Layoffs or hiring freezes in the last 12 months. For public companies, the headline from their latest results.
- **Open roles:** roles that match the target titles on the careers page and LinkedIn Jobs, with links and posted dates. Close roles posted recently.
- **Pay:** ranges in the company's own job posts first, then salary sites for this role at this company. Benefits from the careers page. Some provinces and US states now require pay ranges in job postings, so check the posting before anything else.
- **Industry position:** leader, challenger or niche. Two or three main competitors. What the company is known for.

**Score anchors**

Use these to keep scores consistent across companies. If you find no evidence at all for an area, score it 5 (neutral), write "Not found", and let it lower the evidence rating.

Culture fit
- 9 to 10: strong recent reviews, a work policy that meets the user's must-haves, values that line up, no recent red flags
- 7 to 8: mostly positive, with one small mismatch
- 5 to 6: mixed reviews, or the policy isn't clear
- 3 to 4: several concerns on things the user cares about
- 1 to 2: a clear mismatch or serious red flags

Growth
- 9 to 10: clear growth in the last 12 months (new funding, expansion, steady hiring, reported revenue growth)
- 7 to 8: stable, with some growth signs
- 5 to 6: flat, or no clear signs either way
- 3 to 4: mixed signs (a hiring freeze, a small round of layoffs)
- 1 to 2: shrinking (major layoffs, closures, money trouble in the news)

Open roles
- 9 to 10: one or more open roles that match the target title right now
- 7 to 8: close or related roles open, or the target role gets posted often
- 5 to 6: the team exists, but there's no matching opening right now
- 3 to 4: few roles of this kind at the company
- 1 to 2: the company doesn't hire for this kind of role

Pay
- 9 to 10: posted or reported ranges meet or beat the user's target, with good benefits
- 7 to 8: close to the target
- 5: not found, or the user has no target (a neutral score, marked "Not found" or "No target set")
- 3 to 4: below the target
- 1 to 2: well below the target (more than 15% under)

Industry position
- 9 to 10: a leader or fast-rising challenger in an industry the user wants
- 7 to 8: a solid player in the target industry or a close one
- 5 to 6: a niche player, or a related industry
- 3 to 4: outside the target industry, but the user's skills transfer
- 1 to 2: an industry the user wants to avoid

**Evidence rating**

Give each company one rating, so the user knows how much to trust the scores.

- **Strong:** recent sources for 4 or 5 areas
- **Some:** sources for 3 areas, or older sources
- **Thin:** sources for 2 or fewer areas, or mostly "Not found" or general knowledge

**Weights and the maths**

Turn the user's priorities into weights: 3 for a top priority, 2 for important, 1 for nice to have. Every area gets a weight.

Weighted score = (each score multiplied by its weight, added up) divided by (the weights added up). Round to one decimal place. The result is out of 10. Also show the plain total out of 50, so the user can see the effect of their weights.

A worked example (fictional companies, fictional person). Priya is a marketing manager in Vancouver. Remote work and growth matter most to her. Her weights: Culture 3, Growth 3, Roles 2, Pay 2, Industry 1. The weights add up to 11.

~~~text
Company            | Culture | Growth | Roles | Pay | Industry | Total /50 | Weighted /10
Bluepeak Software  | 9       | 8      | 9     | 7   | 8        | 41        | 8.3
Lumen Health       | 8       | 9      | 7     | 6   | 6        | 36        | 7.5
Northwind Bank     | 6       | 5      | 9     | 9   | 8        | 37        | 7.0

Bluepeak Software: (9x3) + (8x3) + (9x2) + (7x2) + (8x1) = 27 + 24 + 18 + 14 + 8 = 91. 91 / 11 = 8.3
Lumen Health:      (8x3) + (9x3) + (7x2) + (6x2) + (6x1) = 24 + 27 + 14 + 12 + 6 = 83. 83 / 11 = 7.5
Northwind Bank:    (6x3) + (5x3) + (9x2) + (9x2) + (8x1) = 18 + 15 + 18 + 18 + 8 = 77. 77 / 11 = 7.0
~~~

Northwind Bank has a higher plain total than Lumen Health, but Lumen Health ranks higher because Priya cares most about culture and growth. That's the point of the weights.

**Dealbreakers**

If a company fails one of the user's dealbreakers (for example, Priya needs remote work and a company requires five office days a week), add a DEALBREAKER flag next to its name, whatever its score. Don't drop it silently. Let the user decide.

**Tiers**

- **Strong fit:** weighted score 8.0 and up. Go after these first.
- **Good fit:** 6.0 to 7.9. Worth pursuing alongside the first tier.
- **Worth watching:** under 6.0. Keep an eye out for news or a new posting.

**Starter list method (when the user has no list)**

Build 20 to 30 real companies in the three tiers. Only include a company if you found it in a search and found its careers page. If you can't confirm a company exists or find its careers page, leave it out.

Where to look for companies:
- Competitors of the user's current and past employers
- Companies posting the user's target role right now
- Companies in the user's target industries, near their location
- Remote-friendly companies in their field, if they're open to remote work
- Suppliers, partners and customers of the user's past employers
- Local "best employers" lists, business news and recent funding news

Mix company sizes: some large employers, some mid-size companies, some growing startups.

For each company, give one table row:

~~~text
| Company | Why it fits | Size | Location | Careers page | Recent news |
~~~

"Why it fits" is about the user: their skills, experience and goals, not a description of the company. "Recent news" is one item from the last 12 months with a link, or "Not found".

## Build a target company list

**What it does:** Builds a list of 20 to 30 real companies in three tiers, each with why it fits you, a careers page link and one recent news item.
**When to use it:** You don't have a target list yet, or yours is short and you want more ideas.

### Step 1: Build the list

~~~text
Build me a target company list. Search the web to check that every company is real and to find its careers page. If you can't search the web, tell me before you start.

My target role: [Target job title]
My background: [Paste your resume, or 3 to 5 lines on your experience]
My location: [City, province or state]
Work model I want: [Remote, hybrid or on-site]
Industries I'm interested in: [Industries]
Companies I already know about (optional): [Companies, or delete this line]

Give me 20 to 30 companies in three tiers:
Tier 1: Strong fit
Tier 2: Good fit
Tier 3: Worth watching

For each tier, use a table: Company | Why it fits me | Size | Location | Careers page link | One recent news item (with link and date)

Rules:
- Only real companies you found in a search. Never make up a company, a link or a news item.
- If you can't find a company's careers page, leave the company out.
- "Why it fits me" is about my skills, experience and goals, not a description of the company.
- Mix large employers, mid-size companies and growing startups.
- Focus on companies near my location, plus remote-friendly companies if I'm open to remote work.
~~~

**Replace:** [Target job title], [Paste your resume, or 3 to 5 lines on your experience], [City, province or state], [Remote, hybrid or on-site], [Industries], [Companies, or delete this line]

## Score and rank my companies

**What it does:** Scores your target companies on five areas, weights the scores to what matters most to you, and ranks them into tiers.
**When to use it:** You have 10 to 20 companies and need to decide which ones get your time first.

### Step 1: Set my weights

~~~text
I'm going to score my target companies on five areas: culture fit, growth, open roles, pay and industry position.

What matters most to me, in order: [Your priorities, for example remote work, growth, pay]
My dealbreakers: [For example "must be remote" or "none"]

Turn my priorities into weights: 3 for a top priority, 2 for important, 1 for nice to have. Every area gets a weight. Show me the five weights in one line and ask me to confirm before you go on.
~~~

**Replace:** [Your priorities, for example remote work, growth, pay], [For example "must be remote" or "none"]

### Step 2: Research and score

~~~text
Now research and score these companies. Work in batches of 5.

Companies: [List your 10 to 20 companies]
My target role: [Target job title]
My location and work model: [City, province or state, and remote, hybrid or on-site]
My target salary: [Amount and currency, or "not set"]
My background: [Paste your resume, or 3 to 5 lines on your experience]

If you can search the web, research each company and link every fact with the date you found it. If you can't, tell me, and score only from what I paste below.

Score each area from 1 to 10:
- Culture fit: employee reviews (rating, number of reviews, date), office policy, values that match mine
- Growth: funding, revenue news, expansion, hiring trends, any layoffs in the last 12 months
- Open roles: current openings that match my target role, with links
- Pay: ranges in their own job posts, then salary sites; benefits. If you can't find pay or I have no target, score 5 and say why.
- Industry position: leader, challenger or niche, and whether it's an industry I want

For each company give: the five scores with a one-line reason and source each, an evidence rating (Strong, Some or Thin), and any dealbreaker. If you find nothing for an area, score it 5 and write "Not found". Never guess, and never invent ratings, salaries, funding, layoffs or open roles.

What I've found so far (optional): [Paste notes, reviews or job posts, or delete this line]
~~~

**Replace:** [List your 10 to 20 companies], [Target job title], [City, province or state, and remote, hybrid or on-site], [Amount and currency, or "not set"], [Paste your resume, or 3 to 5 lines on your experience], [Paste notes, reviews or job posts, or delete this line]

### Step 3: Rank and pick my top 3

~~~text
Now rank them.

For each company, work out the weighted score: multiply each score by its weight, add those up, and divide by the total of my weights. Round to one decimal place. Also show the plain total out of 50.

Give me:
1. A ranked table: Rank | Company | Culture | Growth | Roles | Pay | Industry | Total /50 | Weighted /10 | Evidence | Flag
2. The tiers: Strong fit (8.0 and up), Good fit (6.0 to 7.9), Worth watching (under 6.0)
3. My top 3 companies to go after first, with one or two sentences on why each, and the best way in (an open role to apply for, or a team to reach out to)
4. Anything I should check myself before I act on it
~~~

**Replace:** nothing. Use it in the same chat, right after Step 2.

## Score one company from my notes

**What it does:** Turns notes you've gathered yourself into a scored, weighted scorecard for one company.
**When to use it:** You're using an AI tool without web search, or you've already done the digging and want it organized.

### Step 1: Score it

~~~text
Score this company for me using only the notes I paste below. Don't add facts I didn't give you.

Company: [Company]
My target role: [Target job title]
My weights (3 = top priority, 2 = important, 1 = nice to have): Culture [Weight], Growth [Weight], Roles [Weight], Pay [Weight], Industry [Weight]
My target salary: [Amount and currency, or "not set"]

My notes:
[Paste what you found: review ratings and themes, office policy, news, open roles, pay ranges, competitors]

Score each area from 1 to 10 with a one-line reason. If my notes don't cover an area, score it 5 and write "Not found". Then give me the weighted score out of 10 (each score times its weight, added up, divided by the total of the weights, one decimal place), the plain total out of 50, an evidence rating (Strong, Some or Thin), one key insight on whether this company fits me, and what I should look up next.
~~~

**Replace:** [Company], [Target job title], [Weight], [Amount and currency, or "not set"], [Paste what you found: review ratings and themes, office policy, news, open roles, pay ranges, competitors]
