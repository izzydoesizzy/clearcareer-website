# Interview prompts

Eight prompts to prepare for every stage of an interview, and to learn from it afterward. Use them with the cc-interview skill in Claude, or paste them into any AI chat tool. Swap everything in [square brackets] for your own details. The samples inside the prompts are fictional, so never copy their numbers into your answers.

## Generate "Tell Me About Yourself"

**What it does:** Writes a focused answer to "Tell me about yourself" that connects your background to this exact job.
**When to use it:** Before any interview, since it's almost always the first question.

### Step 1: Write your answer

~~~text
Act as a career coach. Help me write a strong, personal answer to the interview question "Tell me about yourself." Use my resume and the job description to highlight my most relevant skills and experience. Follow the template below.

My resume:
[Paste your resume]

The job description:
[Paste the job description]

My top achievements for this role (optional):
[Your top achievements]

TEMPLATE
1. Introduction
   - My name and professional title
   - My current or most recent role and company
2. Most recent professional background
   - My most recent relevant experience
   - One significant quantified achievement, with the specific actions I took and the results, to show my value
3. Fit with the role
   - The top 2 to 3 requirements from the job description
   - How my skills and experience match them, with specific examples
4. What sets me apart
   - What makes me different, such as unusual skills or perspectives
   - Personal traits that make me a good fit for the team and culture
5. Motivation and close
   - Why I'm interested in this role and company
   - How the role fits my goals and qualifications
   - Real enthusiasm for the opportunity

Keep it to about 60 to 90 seconds spoken (roughly 150 to 220 words). Use only facts from my resume and what I've shared. If something is missing, leave a [bracket] for me to fill in. Write it the way people talk, with contractions and short sentences.
~~~

**When to use it:** When you have an interview booked and want to practise the questions you're most likely to get.

### Step 1: Get your 10 questions

~~~text
Job title: [Target job title]
Industry: [Target industry]
Company: [Target company]
My resume (optional, for better example answers): [Paste your resume]

Act like a professional interview coach.

Write 10 interview questions tailored to the target company, job and industry. For each, give a strategy for how to answer it and an example answer.

If I've shared my resume, build the example answers from my real experience. If not, write sample answers and put [brackets] where my real details and numbers should go. Never invent numbers about me. If you mention facts about the company, only use ones you're sure of, and remind me to double-check them.

Format each question as its own 2-column table:

Q1 |
Question | [Question]
Topic | [Topic]
Strategy | [Strategy]
Example answer | [Answer]

Repeat for all 10 questions.
~~~

## Post-Interview Analysis

**What it does:** Scores every answer from your interview transcript with a strict rubric and tells you exactly how to improve each one.
**When to use it:** Right after an interview, while it's fresh, using a transcript or detailed notes.

### Step 1: Score your interview

~~~text
TASK
Act as an interviewer in a highly competitive hiring process. Analyze the interview transcript below, pull out every question the interviewer asked, and give a detailed, strict but constructive analysis of my answers. Score sparingly. Give points only when they're truly earned.

MY DOCUMENTS
Interview transcript or my notes: [Paste the transcript or your notes]
My resume: [Paste your resume]
Job title: [Job title]
Company profile or job description: [Paste the company profile or job description]

SCORING GUIDE
0: Doesn't meet the requirement (for example, no specific work story)
1: Average
2: Excellent

FOCUS AREAS
- Content: accuracy, relevance and completeness
- Delivery: clarity, confidence and engagement
- Relevance: fit with the job description
- Impact: use of numbers and outcomes to show my impact on the company's goals

PREFACE: LIST OF QUESTIONS
List every question the interviewer asked, ignoring my answers and any side comments from the interviewer.
1. [Question 1]
2. [Question 2]
(continue as needed)

PART 1: QUESTION-BY-QUESTION ANALYSIS
For each question, use this format:

Question [#]: [Question topic] (Total score: X/2)
Question: the exact text from the transcript
Key points from my answer:
- [Point 1]
- [Point 2]
- [Point 3]
- [Point 4]
Scoring rubric, as a table with these columns: Scoring criteria | Score X/2 | What was good | What was missed
Rows:
- Was the answer well organized and logical?
- Was it easy to follow and understand?
- Did I give a specific, relevant work story? (Score 0 if not.)
- Did I use numbers to show impact?
- Did I use stories with numbers and outcomes to show how my work helped the company's bigger goals?
- [Context-specific criterion 1]
- [Context-specific criterion 2]
- [Context-specific criterion 3]
- [Context-specific criterion 4]
- Total score
Summary: overall strengths, areas to improve, and specific actions to make this answer better.

PART 2: OVERALL FEEDBACK
Question scores: a table with Question # | Question topic | Score X/2, plus a total row.
Overall interview score: (sum of question scores / total possible score) x 100%
Likelihood of moving to the next round: Very unlikely, Unlikely, Likely, or Very likely
What went well: specific strengths, such as strong answers, good examples or confident delivery
Areas to improve: specific gaps, such as thin detail, missed points or weak examples
Summary: a short wrap-up of my performance, with final recommendations for my next interview

EXAMPLES
Good answer: complete, covers every part of the question, shows deep understanding, and matches the job requirements.
Needs improvement: thin on detail, misses key points, or doesn't match the job description.

IMPORTANT
- Include and analyze every question from the transcript.
- Ignore the interviewer's own responses and side comments when scoring.
- Score 0 when there's no specific work story. Score harshly when there are no numbers showing impact.
- Give specific, actionable ways to improve each answer.
- Keep the tone professional and respectful.
- Treat the likelihood rating as a rough read, not a prediction. Results vary.

CHECKLIST (confirm each before you finish)
- Reviewed every document (transcript, resume, job title, company profile)
- Listed every interviewer question in the preface
- Used the exact text of each question
- Ignored interviewer side comments when scoring
- Analyzed every question in the set format
- Scored each answer with the scoring guide
- Included key points from each answer
- Filled in the rubric for each question
- Wrote a summary for each question
- Calculated each question's total score
- Calculated the overall score as a percentage
- Rated the likelihood of moving forward
- Summarized what went well and what to improve
- Gave examples of good answers and answers that need work
- Kept a professional, respectful tone
~~~

**Replace:** [Paste the transcript or your notes], [Paste your resume], [Job title], [Paste the company profile or job description]

## Write an Elevator Pitch

**What it does:** Writes 5 short elevator pitches from your resume in two styles: quick and clear, or impact-focused.
**When to use it:** Before networking events, coffee chats, career fairs, or any time someone asks "So, what do you do?"

### Step 1: Write 5 pitches

~~~text
Act as a career coach and communication coach. Based on my resume below, write 5 different elevator pitches using the two formats below.

My resume:
[Paste your resume]

Roles I'm targeting (optional): [Target roles]

FORMAT 1: QUICK AND CLEAR
Structure:
- Name
- Current or most recent role, or professional identity
- Key skills or specialties
- One clear achievement or experience highlight
- The role or opportunity I'm looking for now

FORMAT 2: IMPACT-FOCUSED
Structure:
- Name and role or industry
- One or two strong, quantified achievements
- Area of expertise
- The role or opportunity I'm targeting now

Include a mix of roles I could be qualified for based on my resume (and the target roles above, if I gave any), and tailor each pitch slightly to that goal. Each pitch should take 30 to 45 seconds to say out loud. Use only facts from my resume.

EXAMPLES (fictional people)

1. The "Quick and Clear" pitch
Good for introverts or busy events.
Template: "Hi, I'm [Name]. I'm a [Job title] with experience in [Industry or key skill area], especially strong in [Top skills]. I'm looking for [Target role or company type], ideally where I can [what you love doing or the value you add]."
Example: "Hi, I'm Jordan. I'm a marketing strategist with 5+ years in B2B tech. I specialize in lead generation and campaign performance, and I'm looking for a growth marketing role at a startup that values data and creativity."

2. The "Impact-Focused" pitch
Good when you have strong numbers or want to stand out.
Template: "I'm [Name], a [Job title or field] who recently [quantified achievement or project]. I specialize in [Key skills], and I'm looking for roles in [Industry or role] where I can drive similar results."
Example: "I'm Aisha, a customer success manager who helped cut churn by 20% in 6 months with a new onboarding approach. I love building strong client relationships, and I'm looking for a role at a mission-driven SaaS company."
~~~

## Create a Compelling Career Story

**What it does:** Asks you 12 questions about one project or moment, then turns your answers into a short career story, built on Stan Miller's 4P framework.
**When to use it:** When you need a memorable story for "Tell me about a project you're proud of" or a networking chat.

### Step 1: Answer the questions and get your story

~~~text
Write a powerful career story based on my answers to a series of questions. The story is about: [The project or moment you want to talk about]

First, ask me the 12 questions below, one at a time. Wait for each answer. If an answer is thin, ask one short follow-up before moving on. Then use the sample questions, answers and final story below as a model for structure, tone and flow.

THE 12 QUESTIONS
1. When did the project or moment take place?
2. Where were you working?
3. Who were you helping?
4. What did they want?
5. What was stopping them?
6. What was the risk to them if they didn't reach their goal?
7. Why did they pick you?
8. What was your role or title?
9. Could you relate to the people who had the problem?
10. Name 3 things you did to help.
11. What did they get? How did the story end?
12. How did that make you feel?

SAMPLE ANSWERS (fictional person and company)
1. March 2022
2. Brightline Software
3. The whole company, especially people managers
4. A fair, clear way to recognize good work
5. There was no clear process for recognition. Every manager did it differently.
6. Biased decisions, unfair outcomes, people feeling unappreciated, and promoting the wrong people, which hurts the business
7. I care about fair process, and they trusted me to build one.
8. HR Business Partner
9. Yes. I've felt overlooked at work before.
10. Built a role clarity guide for managers. Created a simple framework for performance conversations. Set clear guidelines for recognition and promotion.
11. One organized framework that managers use for role conversations, priorities and recognizing good work
12. Proud. I built something from scratch that made work fairer for people.

SAMPLE FINAL STORY
"In March 2022, I was the HR Business Partner at Brightline Software, and I was asked to fix how we recognized good work. The company cared about recognition, but there was no clear way to make it happen fairly or consistently. Every manager did it differently. That led to biased decisions, people feeling undervalued, and the risk of promoting the wrong people, which hurts any business. They chose me because I care about fair process, and honestly, I know how it feels to be overlooked at work.

So I built a three-part approach. First, I created a role clarity guide so managers could define what great work looks like in each role. Next, I built a simple framework for performance conversations. Finally, I set clear guidelines for recognizing and promoting people. The result was one organized framework that gave managers the tools to make fair recognition decisions. It was really rewarding to build something from scratch that made work fairer for the people around me."

Using this structure, take my answers and write a similar story that shows my impact, my motivation and the outcome. Make it professional and engaging, capturing what I contributed and how it felt to make a difference. Use only what I tell you.

Keep the final story under 1,300 characters.
~~~

**Replace:** [The project or moment you want to talk about]

## Identify Employer Objections and Concerns

**What it does:** Lists the doubts a hiring manager might have about you for this role, with a plan to answer each one.
**When to use it:** Before an interview or a cover letter, when you want to handle the hard questions before they come up.

### Step 1: Find the objections

~~~text
My resume:
[Paste your resume]

The job description:
[Paste the job description]

Based on the job description and my resume, list the objections a hiring manager might have about my experience for this role.

Create a 2-column table: Objection | Strategy to overcome it

Be honest and specific. Look at things like gaps, short stints, missing skills or credentials, an industry or career change, a seniority mismatch, or location. For each strategy, say where to use it (resume, cover letter or interview) and give me a one-line example of what to say, using only facts from my resume.

Don't raise anything about age, health, family status, religion or other personal traits that employers shouldn't consider.
~~~

## STAR Story Bank

**What it does:** Builds 10 to 12 interview-ready STAR stories from your resume, tagged by skill and matched to the questions they answer.
**When to use it:** When you freeze on "Tell me about a time when..." and want a library of stories ready to go.

### Step 1: Build your story bank

~~~text
Act as a senior behavioural interview coach. You know the STAR method (Situation, Task, Action, Result) and how to turn real work experience into clear, memorable stories that interviewers remember.

MY MATERIALS
My resume: [Paste your resume]
Target role: [Target role]
Extra stories, notes or performance reviews (optional): [Paste any notes]

Use my resume first, then my notes. Use the target role to decide which skills matter most.

PICKING STORIES
Write 10 to 12 stories that give the widest coverage:
1. Role variety: draw from at least 2 to 3 different jobs.
2. Skill coverage: cover the main behavioural skills listed below.
3. Recency: favour the last 5 to 7 years, plus 1 to 2 earlier stories if they show something unique.
4. Impact variety: mix individual wins with team and leadership wins.
5. Problem types: include proactive stories (improvements, new ideas) and reactive ones (a crisis or challenge).

SKILL CATEGORIES
Across all stories, cover at least 8 of these:
Leadership: influencing others, making tough calls, setting direction
Problem-solving: analytical thinking, creative fixes, working with ambiguity
Teamwork and collaboration: cross-functional work, building consensus
Communication: presenting, persuading decision-makers, clear writing
Adaptability and change: shifting priorities, learning fast, handling uncertainty
Results: driving outcomes, meeting deadlines, beating targets
Customer focus: understanding needs, delivering value, managing relationships
Initiative and innovation: improvements nobody asked for, new ideas
Conflict resolution: disagreements, hard conversations, finding middle ground
Prioritization: competing demands, workload, delegation
Technical expertise: specialized knowledge, new tools, technical problems
Mentoring and development: coaching others, sharing knowledge

FORMAT FOR EACH STORY
Story [number]: [Short title]
Skills: [Tag 1], [Tag 2], [Tag 3]
Role: [Job title] at [Company]
When: [Approximate timeframe]

Situation: 2 to 3 sentences setting the scene: the company, team size, business conditions, and what was at stake. Be specific ("our online booking platform", not "the product").

Task: 2 to 3 sentences on the challenge or goal I owned, why it mattered, and what would have happened if nothing changed.

Action: 4 to 6 sentences on the specific steps I took. This is the most important part. Use "I", not "we", even for team work. Include the approach I chose and why, key decisions, tools or methods, how I handled obstacles, and who I worked with or led.

Result: 2 to 3 sentences with numbers wherever I've given them: revenue, savings, time saved, percentage changes, what changed for the team or customers, any recognition, and what I learned.

Questions this story answers: 3 to 5 common interview questions, for example "Tell me about a time when you...", "Describe a situation where you..." or "Give me an example of..."

BUILDING THE STORIES
1. Resumes are compressed. Where a story needs context I haven't given you, write [confirm: what you need to know] instead of guessing.
2. Make actions specific. Turn "Led a cross-functional project" into real steps: who I worked with, what meetings I ran, what tools I used, what I decided.
3. Push for numbers. Use the numbers I've given you. If a result has no number, write [add a number: what to measure] so I can fill it in. Never make up a figure.
4. First person, active voice: "I spotted the problem," "I proposed the fix," "I led the team."
5. Keep each story to 200 to 300 words, so I can tell it in about 2 minutes.
6. Keep my voice. If I've shared stories in my own words, keep my natural phrasing.

AFTER THE STORIES
Coverage matrix: a table with one row per story and one column per skill category, with an X where the story fits.

Quick reference card: a table with story number, title, a one-line summary and the top 2 skills. Something I can review 15 minutes before an interview.

Usage tips (5 to 7):
- How to pick the right story fast during an interview
- How to adapt a story when the question doesn't quite match
- The CAR shortcut (Challenge, Action, Result) for when a full STAR answer is too long
- How to handle "Tell me about a time you failed" (pick a growth story and spend most of it on what you learned)
- Practise out loud. Reading a story isn't the same as telling it.
- How to signal the end of an answer so you don't ramble

AVOID
- Stories or achievements that aren't in my resume or notes
- Vague actions like "worked on", "was responsible for" or "helped with"
- Results with no number and no [add a number] prompt
- Tagging every story "Leadership". Be honest about what each one shows.
- Stories that are too similar
- "We" as the main subject of the Action section
- Any story over 350 words

TONE
Professional but conversational: what a confident, clear person would say in an interview. Not over-rehearsed, not too casual. Specific to my career, not generic.
~~~

**Replace:** [Paste your resume], [Target role], [Paste any notes]

## Pre-Interview Confidence Builder

**What it does:** Builds a personal "proof file" of your real wins, strengths and praise, plus a 5-minute ritual to calm your nerves before the interview.
**When to use it:** The day before or the morning of an interview, especially when imposter syndrome is loud.

### Step 1: Build your confidence document

~~~text
Build me a pre-interview confidence document from my real career facts. Every section must be personal and based on evidence from what I give you. Nothing generic. Include all 7 sections.

MY MATERIALS
My resume: [Paste your resume]
The role I'm interviewing for: [Job title and company]
Feedback, reviews or recommendations (optional): [Paste any feedback]
Weaknesses or concerns I worry about (optional): [Your concerns]

SECTION 1: MY TOP 10 CAREER ACHIEVEMENTS
Open with: "These aren't opinions. These are facts from your career. Read them and let them land."
For each achievement:
[Number]. [Achievement title]
What you did: [a short description of the action]
The impact: [the result with numbers: revenue, percentage, time saved, people affected]
Why it matters: [one sentence on why this is impressive in my field]
In your own words: "[a direct quote from my resume or notes, if there is one]"
How to choose:
- Favour achievements with numbers (revenue, percentages, team sizes, savings)
- Pull from different roles and career stages to show a pattern
- Weight recent wins more, but include early standouts
- Keep the CAR structure (Challenge, Action, Result) if my resume uses it
- Look for scale (size of impact), difficulty (how hard it was) and recognition (awards, promotions, praise)
If I have fewer than 10 real achievements, list the ones I have. Don't pad the list.

SECTION 2: PATTERNS IN HOW OTHERS SEE ME
If I've shared feedback, reviews or recommendations, find 3 to 5 positive themes. For each:
Theme: [a name, for example "Calm under pressure"]
Evidence: [specific moments from my materials]
In their words: "[a quote from the feedback, if there is one]"
What this signals to employers: [how it shows up as value at work]
If I haven't shared any feedback, give me 5 reflection questions to find these themes myself (for example "What do people come to you for help with?").

SECTION 3: WHAT I BRING THAT OTHERS DON'T
Not a generic skills list. The specific mix that makes me valuable.
Technical strengths: a table with Skill | My level (Expert, Advanced or Proficient) | Why it's less common at my level
Experience strengths: 3 experiences most people at my level don't have, for example "You've led a team through a merger."
My "superpower" combination: "You combine [Skill A] + [Skill B] + [Experience C]. Most people with [Skill A] don't also have [Skill B]. That means you can [your unique value]."
Industry knowledge that's hard to teach: each area, with years or depth of exposure.
Base this only on my materials. Don't back up claims about how rare something is with made-up statistics.

SECTION 4: EVIDENCE THAT OTHERS VALUE MY WORK
Open with: "Imposter syndrome says you're fooling everyone. Here's the evidence that you're not."
- Quotes from reviews or recommendations, with the source
- Awards and formal recognition, with context
- Promotions, and what they say about how my employer valued me
- Informal praise: times people sought me out for help or praised my work
Proof points to return to when I feel like I don't belong, for example: "You were selected for [opportunity] over [number] other candidates," "You were promoted to [role] after [time]," or "[Person or team] asked to work with you because [reason]."
Use only real facts I've given you. If I haven't shared any, show the framework and suggest I collect 2 to 3 LinkedIn recommendations or write down praise I remember.

SECTION 5: TURNING "WEAKNESSES" INTO GROWTH STORIES
Pick 3 likely concerns, from my list above or ones you can see in my resume (such as a career gap, short stints, a missing credential, a first management role, or a technical gap). For each:
Growth story: [the concern]
The old story: "I struggle with [weakness]."
The growth story: "Earlier in my career, I noticed [the weakness]. I took action by [specific steps: a course, coaching, practice, asking for feedback]. The result was [a real improvement]. Today, [how it's now a managed strength]."
Example interview answer: [a full answer, 30 to 45 seconds long]
Ways to reframe:
- Career gap: an intentional transition or growth period
- Job-hopping: range of experience and adaptability
- Missing credential: hands-on experience over theory
- Management challenges: leadership growth
- Technical gaps: proof I learn fast
Only use steps and results I've actually told you about. Put [brackets] where I need to add my real details.

SECTION 6: MY 5-MINUTE PRE-INTERVIEW RITUAL
"Do this 30 to 60 minutes before your interview. Not the night before (you'll forget). Not 5 minutes before (you'll be rushed)."
Minute 1, body reset: Stand tall, roll your shoulders back, unclench your jaw. Take 3 slow breaths: in for 4 seconds, hold for 4, out for 6.
Minute 2, evidence review: Re-read your top 3 achievements from Section 1 and say them out loud: "I [achievement 1]. That happened. I did that." "I [achievement 2]. That's my track record." "I [achievement 3]. That's who I am at work."
Minute 3, identity statement: "I'm a [title] with [X years] of experience. I've [top achievement]. I bring [what sets me apart]. I'm not here to beg for a job. I'm here to find out if this is a fit for both of us. They need someone with my skills. I'm that person."
Minute 4, reframe the interview:
- This isn't a test. It's a conversation between two professionals.
- They're hoping you're the right person so they can stop interviewing.
- You have value to offer. Your job is to explain it clearly.
- Worst case, you get practice and information.
Minute 5, practical check:
- Review the interviewer's name and how to say it
- Recall one thing about the company you're genuinely excited about
- Smile
- Say out loud: "I'm ready. Let's go."
Fill in the brackets with my real details.

SECTION 7: EVIDENCE-BASED AFFIRMATIONS
Open with: "These aren't wishful thinking. Each one is backed by a fact from your career."
Write exactly 7, each tied to a real fact:
1. "I deliver measurable results." Evidence: [a specific number from my resume]
2. "I solve complex problems." Evidence: [a challenge I solved]
3. "People trust me with important work." Evidence: [a responsibility I was given]
4. "I grow and adapt." Evidence: [career progress or new skills]
5. "I make teams better." Evidence: [team impact]
6. "I know my field." Evidence: [years and areas of expertise]
7. "I've handled harder things than this interview." Evidence: [a tough moment I got through]
If my materials don't support one, leave it as a placeholder for me to fill in.

TONE
- Warm but direct, like a coach talking to me before a big moment
- Evidence first: never claim something without a fact from my career
- Not cheesy. Skip "You've got this!" energy. Use "Here's proof you're qualified" energy.
- Specific: every point ties back to something real
- Kind about imposter syndrome: it's normal and very common. Then answer it with evidence.
~~~

**Replace:** [Paste your resume], [Job title and company], [Paste any feedback], [Your concerns]
