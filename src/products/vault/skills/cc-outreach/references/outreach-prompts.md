# Outreach prompts

Nine copy-paste prompts for the emails and messages that get you into real conversations. Fill in everything in [square brackets] with your own details. Leave anything in {curly braces} alone: those are slots the AI fills from what you gave it. Then paste the prompt into Claude or any AI chat and edit the draft until it sounds like you.

Some prompts work better with a bit of research on the person or company. If your AI can search the web, ask it to. Otherwise, paste in what you find.

## Informational Interview Request Email

**What it does:** Writes a short, specific email that asks someone whose work you admire for a 20-minute advice call.
**When to use it:** You came across someone through their post, podcast, talk or article and want to learn from them.

### Step 1: Write the email

~~~text
Write an email asking for a short informational interview. This is an advice call, not a job ask.

About me:
- My name: [Your name]
- My job title: [Your job title]
- My field or industry: [Your industry]
- Years of experience: [Years of experience]
- My LinkedIn URL: [LinkedIn URL]

About them:
- Their name: [Contact name]
- Their company: [Target company]
- Their field or industry: [Their field]

How I came across them:
[Where you found them: a blog post, interview, video, podcast, talk, tutorial or resource]

What stood out to me about it:
[Something that genuinely stood out: an idea, opinion, approach or resource]

What I'm hoping to achieve in my career:
[Your career goal or mission in one or two sentences]

My strongest achievement with a number in it:
[One quantified achievement]

The work I want to do more of:
[The type of work or role you're moving toward]

A day and time I could suggest for a call:
[Specific day and time]

Rules:
- Use only the facts above. Don't invent details about me or about them.
- Keep every paragraph under 200 characters.
- Plain text, ready to paste into an email, plus a subject line under 60 characters.

Use this template:

Hi {their first name},

I recently came across your {podcast, post, video, talk or interview} and {the specific thing that stood out}. What you said about {topic} really resonated with me and made me think of {my achievement, mission or approach}.

A bit about me: my name is {my name}, and I'm a {my job title} working in {my industry}.

- {My quantified achievement}
- {My focus and professional mission}
- {The work I'm looking to do more of}

Given your experience in the field and your work at {their company}, I'd love your advice on how to approach breaking into a role in {industry or type of company}.

Would you be open to a short 20-minute call on {day and time}?

It would mean a lot to me.

Looking forward to hearing from you!

All the best,
{my name}
{my LinkedIn URL}
~~~

**Replace:** [Your name], [Your job title], [Your industry], [Years of experience], [LinkedIn URL], [Contact name], [Target company], [Their field], [Where you found them: a blog post, interview, video, podcast, talk, tutorial or resource], [Something that genuinely stood out: an idea, opinion, approach or resource], [Your career goal or mission in one or two sentences], [One quantified achievement], [The type of work or role you're moving toward], [Specific day and time]

## Email to the Hiring Manager

**What it does:** Writes a direct email to the hiring manager that matches your best achievements to the 3 main priorities in the job post.
**When to use it:** You've found a job you want and you know (or can find) the name of the person who'd be your boss.

### Step 1: Share the job post and your resume

~~~text
I'm applying for a job and want to email the hiring manager directly. Read the job post and my resume below.

Then reply with only this:
1. The 3 main priorities for this role, in the employer's own words where you can.
2. For each priority, the achievement from my resume that best proves I can do it. If nothing fits well, say so.

Don't write the email yet. Wait for my next message.

Job post:
[Paste the full job post]

My resume:
[Paste your resume text]
~~~

**Replace:** [Paste the full job post], [Paste your resume text]

### Step 2: Write the email

~~~text
Now write the email to the hiring manager, using those 3 priorities and my matching achievements.

- Hiring manager's name: [Hiring manager name]
- My full name: [Your full name]
- Company: [Company]
- Job title: [Job title]
- Team or department: [Team or department]
- A day and time I can suggest for a call: [Specific day and time]
- My LinkedIn URL: [LinkedIn URL]
- My phone number: [Phone number]

Rules:
- Use only achievements and numbers from my resume. Never invent or round up a number.
- If a priority has no strong match, tell me instead of stretching the truth.
- Keep it under 200 words, plain text, with a subject line under 60 characters.

Use this template:

Hi {hiring manager's first name},

My name is {my full name} and I'm writing to connect about the {job title} role at {company}.

It sounds like the {team or department} team at {company} is looking for someone with {priority 1}, {priority 2} and {priority 3}.

Here's how my experience lines up with that:

1. {Priority 1}: {my top achievement for priority 1}

2. {Priority 2}: {my top achievement for priority 2}

3. {Priority 3}: {my top achievement for priority 3}

I'm confident I'd make a stellar addition to the team and would love to chat more! Would you be open to a short 20-minute call on {day and time} to learn more about the role and the team?

Thanks in advance for your consideration. Looking forward to hearing from you!

All the best,
{my full name}
{my LinkedIn URL}
{my phone number}

3. Leading planning conversations: I managed a $12M product portfolio across four national grocery accounts and ran their monthly planning meetings.

I'm confident I'd make a stellar addition to the Northwind Foods team and would love to chat more! Would you be open to a short 20-minute call on Friday at 10 a.m. to learn more about the role and the team?

Sam Taylor
linkedin.com/in/sam-taylor-example
555.010.0000
~~~

## The "Silver Bullet" Thank-You Email

**What it does:** Writes a post-interview thank-you email that names the role's top 3 priorities and gives fresh proof that you can deliver each one.
**When to use it:** Within 24 hours of any interview, while the conversation is still fresh for everyone.

### Step 1: Write the email

~~~text
Write a post-interview thank-you email to the people who interviewed me.

- Job title: [Job title]
- Industry: [Industry]
- Interviewer name(s): [Interviewer name(s)]
- Company: [Company]

What I liked about the interview, the people or the company:
[Something you genuinely liked: a moment, a person, something you learned]

The top 3 priorities for this role, based on the conversation and what I know about the job:
- Priority 1: [Priority 1]
- Priority 2: [Priority 2]
- Priority 3: [Priority 3]

My best achievement for each priority (new ones I didn't already share in the interview are even better):
- For priority 1: [Achievement for priority 1]
- For priority 2: [Achievement for priority 2]
- For priority 3: [Achievement for priority 3]

Optional (delete these two lines if you don't need them):
A mistake I made, or a concern they seemed to have about me: [Mistake or concern]
Relevant experience (direct or indirect) that answers it: [Experience that answers the concern]

Rules:
- Use only the facts above. Don't invent numbers or details.
- If I deleted the optional part, skip that paragraph.
- If there's more than one interviewer, write one version for each person and change the memorable moment for each.
- Plain text, ready to paste into an email, with a short subject line.
- Warm and confident. Never grovelling.

Use this template:

Hi {first name},

Thank you so much for taking the time to speak with me today. It was such a pleasure meeting you and learning about the company and the team. {A memorable moment from the interview, or something I learned and appreciated.}

Based on our conversation, it sounds like the team at {company} is looking for someone who can {priority 1}, {priority 2} and {priority 3}.

With that in mind, here are a few more things I'm confident will make me the ideal fit for the role:

1. {Priority 1}
{An achievement that shows my skill, approach and results}

2. {Priority 2}
{An achievement that shows my skill, approach and results}

3. {Priority 3}
{An achievement that shows my skill, approach and results}

{Optional: one short paragraph that addresses the concern or mistake, using the experience I gave you.}

I'm excited about the opportunity to work with the team at {company} because of {the challenge, opportunity or value that excites me}. {One sentence restating my fit and what I'd bring.}

Thanks again for the opportunity, and I look forward to hearing from you.

All the best,
{my name}
~~~

**Replace:** [Job title], [Industry], [Interviewer name(s)], [Company], [Something you genuinely liked: a moment, a person, something you learned], [Priority 1], [Priority 2], [Priority 3], [Achievement for priority 1], [Achievement for priority 2], [Achievement for priority 3], [Mistake or concern], [Experience that answers the concern]

## Cold Email Templates (3 Variants)

**What it does:** Builds three cold email templates (recruiter, hiring manager and warm introduction request) with subject lines, a follow-up sequence, a signature and a pre-send checklist.
**When to use it:** You're starting outreach to people at your target companies and want ready templates instead of a blank page.

### Step 1: Build the templates

~~~text
Act as an outreach coach who helps job seekers land interviews through direct email. You know that busy people decide in a few seconds whether to read or delete an email.

Use my details and resume below to build a set of cold email templates. Pre-fill them with my real achievements, and leave {curly brace} slots only for the details that change for each person.

About me:
- Target role: [Target role]
- Target industry: [Target industry]
- Location: [Your city and province or state]
- Years of experience: [Years of experience]
- My strongest achievements with numbers: [Your top 3 achievements]
- Credentials, tools or skills worth mentioning: [Certifications, tools or skills]
- My pronouns, for the intro blurb: [Your pronouns]
- Target companies (optional): [Target companies, or delete this line]

My resume:
[Paste your resume text]

Build the document in this order.

1. Strategy overview (150 to 200 words)
- Most of the work happens in the subject line and the first sentence. If they don't open it and read line one, nothing else matters.
- Set honest expectations: plenty of cold emails get no reply, so plan on volume and polite follow-up. Results vary.
- Best send times: Tuesday to Thursday, 7 to 9 a.m. in the recipient's time zone. Avoid Mondays and Fridays.
- Finding email addresses: tools like Hunter.io, Lusha or RocketReach, the firstname.lastname@company.com pattern, press releases and conference speaker bios.
- Log every email in a simple job search tracker (a spreadsheet is fine).

2. Variant 1: Recruiter outreach (75 to 125 words, not counting subject line and signature)
Use when: I found a recruiter for my target role on LinkedIn, a job post lists a recruiter, or someone referred me to a recruiter. Recruiters scan all day, so make it easy to scan and easy to act on.
Give 3 subject line options, like:
- {Target role}: {X} years in {industry} | Referred by {name} or Found via {source}
- Experienced {title} interested in {company}: {top achievement in 5 words}
- Quick question about {department or role} openings at {company}
Body structure:
- Opening (1 sentence): why I'm reaching out and how I found them. Be specific.
- Value (2 to 3 sentences): who I am, my headline achievement, and why I fit {company}. Lead with my strongest, most relevant number.
- Relevance (1 to 2 sentences): connect my experience to a specific role, team or project. Include the job posting ID if there is one.
- Social proof (1 sentence): a referral, mutual connection or relevant credential. Only one I actually have.
- Call to action (1 sentence): a specific, low-commitment ask, like "Would you be open to a 10-minute call this week to see if my experience fits what you're looking for?"
Avoid: attaching my resume in the first email (offer to share it instead), listing more than 2 achievements, mentioning salary, and "To Whom It May Concern".

3. Variant 2: Hiring manager direct (100 to 150 words)
Use when: I've found the likely hiring manager, the company may not have a posting yet, or I was introduced and need to follow up by email. These are the decision-makers, so show I understand their challenges and can solve a specific problem.
Give 3 subject line options, like:
- {Specific result}: can I do the same for {company}?
- {Mutual connection} suggested I reach out | {title} with {industry} experience
- Idea for {company}'s {department or challenge}, from {my first name}, {title}
Body structure:
- Hook (1 sentence): something specific about the company, team or their work, like a product launch, a news story, a LinkedIn post or a talk. Show I did my homework.
- Bridge (1 to 2 sentences): connect the hook to my experience. "That resonated with me because..." or "I tackled a similar challenge when..."
- Proof (2 to 3 sentences): my top 1 or 2 achievements that match what this team needs, in this shape: "Achieved X, measured by Y, by doing Z."
- The ask (1 to 2 sentences): a conversation, not "Are you hiring?" Something like "I'd love to share a few thoughts on {topic} and learn where your team is heading. Would you have 15 minutes for a quick conversation?"
Position me as a peer, not someone asking for a favour.
Avoid: opening with "I'm looking for a job", a generic email that could go to any company, going over 150 words, asking "Are you hiring?", and copying the recruiter.

4. Variant 3: Warm introduction request (125 to 175 words, not counting the blurb)
Use when: someone I know (a former colleague, mentor, friend or alum) is connected to a person at a target company. A warm intro is the most valuable outreach I can do, so it deserves the most care.
Give 3 subject line options, like:
- Quick favour: introduction to {target person} at {company}?
- Could you connect me with {target person}?
- {Target person} at {company}: would you be comfortable making an intro?
Body structure:
- Warm opening (1 sentence): something real about our relationship, a recent update of theirs, or a shared memory.
- Context (2 to 3 sentences): what I'm working on, positive and forward-looking. "I'm exploring opportunities in {field}" beats "I got laid off and need a job."
- The ask (2 sentences): exactly who I want to meet and why, framed as value for both sides.
- Make it easy: a short forwardable blurb they can copy and paste (below). This is the part that gets the intro made.
- Give an out and offer to help back (1 to 2 sentences): "Totally understand if you're not comfortable making the intro, no pressure at all. And let me know if there's ever anything I can do for you."
Forwardable blurb (use my pronouns):
"Hi {target first name}, I wanted to introduce you to {my full name}. {Pronoun} is a {title} with {X} years of experience in {industry}, most recently at {company}. {Pronoun} is exploring {role type} opportunities and I thought your team at {target company} might be a great fit. I'll let {my first name} take it from here."
Avoid: sending this to someone I haven't spoken to in 5+ years without reconnecting first, vague asks like "anyone at {big company}", skipping the blurb, making it about my problems, and forgetting to thank them whether or not they make the intro.

5. Subject line testing (100 to 150 words)
How to test: send version A to the first 10 people and version B to the next 10, then compare opens and replies. Test name-drop vs. achievement-first vs. question. If 20 sends get zero replies, change the subject line and the first sentence.

6. Follow-up sequence (works for all three variants)
- Follow-up 1, 3 to 4 business days later, reply in the same thread, 2 to 3 sentences: bump it to the top of their inbox and add one new piece of value (a relevant article, congratulations on company news, or a sharper version of my ask).
- Follow-up 2, 7 business days after that, same thread, 2 to 3 sentences: shorter and more direct. "Hi {name}, I know things get busy. I'd still love to connect, even a 10-minute call would be great." Then my strongest one-line value.
- Follow-up 3, 14 business days after that, same thread, 2 sentences: the graceful close. "Hi {name}, I'll keep this brief. If the timing isn't right, I completely understand, and if things change I'd love to reconnect. Wishing you and the team all the best."
Never send more than 3 follow-ups. After 3, move on.

7. Email signature (3 lines, no quotes, logos or graphics)
{My full name}
{Target title} | {Key skill 1} | {Key skill 2}
{Phone} | {Email} | {LinkedIn URL}

8. Pre-send checklist
- Every slot personalized for this person
- Subject line under 60 characters
- Body under 150 words (175 for the warm intro)
- At least one specific achievement with a number
- A clear, low-commitment ask
- Name and company spelled right
- Sending Tuesday to Thursday, 7 to 9 a.m. their time
- Logged in my tracker
- Follow-up reminder set for 3 to 4 business days

Formatting: separate each variant with a line and a numbered header, put the word count target next to each, and keep each email template easy to copy.

Rules for every template:
- Never longer than 175 words. Short is respectful.
- No "Dear Sir/Madam" or "To Whom It May Concern". Find the name.
- No filler like "results-driven professional", "I bring a unique blend of" or "I'm confident I'd be a great fit".
- Ask for a conversation, never for a job.
- No salary expectations.
- No attachments in the first email unless they asked for a resume.
- Plain text only: no colours, custom fonts or HTML.
- Don't send the identical email to several people at one company. They talk to each other.
- No fake "Re:" subject lines, and no "Quick question" when there's no question.
- Use only facts from my resume and details. Never invent a referral, a mutual connection or a number.

Tone: confident, respectful and short. One busy professional writing to another. No desperation, no over-explaining, no fake enthusiasm. I should sound like someone who did the research, knows their value and genuinely wants a conversation.
~~~

**Replace:** [Target role], [Target industry], [Your city and province or state], [Years of experience], [Your top 3 achievements], [Certifications, tools or skills], [Your pronouns], [Target companies, or delete this line], [Paste your resume text]

## Alumni and Former Colleague Outreach Scripts

**What it does:** Writes a full alumni outreach kit: how to find people, connection requests, follow-ups, former colleague messages, a group strategy, conversation starters and emails.
**When to use it:** You want to reach people who share your school, program, past employer or professional community.

### Step 1: Build the kit

~~~text
Build me a complete alumni outreach kit. Use my school, program, past employers and affiliations below, and personalize every script with them.

About me:
- University or college: [University or college]
- Program and graduation year: [Program and graduation year]
- Past employers: [Past employers]
- Other affiliations (bootcamps, associations, clubs, teams, volunteer groups): [Other affiliations]
- What I'm exploring now: [Target role and industry]
- Years of experience: [Years of experience]
- My LinkedIn URL: [LinkedIn URL]
- My phone number, for email signatures: [Phone number]

My resume (optional, for extra details):
[Paste your resume text, or delete this line]

Write every section below. Use {curly brace} slots only for details that change for each person.

1. Why alumni outreach works (short)
Explain it in plain words, with no statistics: a shared school or employer creates an instant "one of us" feeling, many alumni like to pay it forward, "We both went to {school}" is a much warmer opener than "I found your profile on LinkedIn", a shared institution works as a trust shortcut, and people who were once helped by an alum often want to return the favour. Remind me not to be transactional: lead with curiosity, offer value, and let the relationship grow on its own.

2. How to find alumni at my target companies
- LinkedIn alumni tool: go to my school's LinkedIn page, click the Alumni tab, and filter by where they work, what they do, where they live and what they studied. 2nd-degree connections are ideal because we already share a contact. Save the profiles I want to contact.
- LinkedIn searches I can paste, filled in with my details:
  "{University name}" AND "{Target company}"
  "{University name}" AND "{Target job title}" AND "{Target company or industry}"
  "{Former company name}" AND "{Target company}"
- Alumni associations and directories: the school's alumni portal or career centre, "{University} Alumni" groups on LinkedIn (join and take part before reaching out to individuals), program cohort groups, bootcamp Slack groups, and club or team networks.
- Former company networks: official company alumni groups on LinkedIn, where my former colleagues work now (they're warm contacts at new companies), and the industry events they attend.
- A priority table, warmest first: direct former colleague, same school and same program, same school and different program, same professional program or bootcamp, former company but we didn't work together, same school with no other overlap. Columns: connection type, warmth (hot, warm, moderate, cool) and priority (highest, high, medium). No response rate numbers.

3. LinkedIn connection requests (each under 280 characters, never asking for anything yet)
- Template A, same program: "Hi {name}, fellow {University} {program} grad here! I'm exploring {target area} opportunities and noticed your path to {their role} at {company}. Would love to connect and learn from your experience."
- Template B, different program: "Hi {name}, I'm a {University} alum ({year}, {program}) and came across your profile while researching careers in {their field}. Your move from {previous role} to {current role} is really interesting. Would love to connect!"
- Template C, shared connection: "Hi {name}, fellow {University} alum here. I see we're both connected to {mutual connection}. I'm exploring roles in {area} and would value your perspective as someone at {company}. Would love to connect!"
- Template D, their content: "Hi {name}, fellow {University} grad here. Your recent post on {topic} really resonated with me, especially {specific point}. I'm working in {my area} and would love to connect with a fellow alum in the space."
Rules: mention the school in the first sentence, reference something about them (not just me), include my graduation year only if it's close to theirs, and use a school nickname or mascot only if it's real for my school.

4. Follow-up messages after they accept (send within 24 to 48 hours, write all three in full)
- Template A, informational interview request: thank them for connecting, one sentence on where I am and what I'm exploring, a specific thing about their company or team, why their career path interests me, a request for 15 to 20 minutes of virtual coffee, an offer to work around their schedule (even 10 minutes would help), and a sign-off with my school and class year.
- Template B, specific role inquiry: thank them, mention the open {job title} role, my {X} years in {area} and why I'm drawn to {company}, and ask for 15 minutes before I apply to understand the team and what they're really looking for. Say I appreciate connecting either way.
- Template C, lighter touch (when I'm not sure what to ask): thank them, note their time at {company}, say I'm exploring {area} and would love to stay in touch, and offer a no-pressure chat sometime.

5. Former colleague reconnection scripts (write all three in full)
- Template A, close former colleague: subject line "Long overdue reconnection, and a quick question". Congratulate them on {their recent update}, one sentence on what I've been doing since {shared company}, say they're one of the first people I thought of, mention {a specific project or memory}, ask for 20 minutes to catch up and hear their take on {their industry or company}, and offer to help them too.
- Template B, a colleague I wasn't close with: say we overlapped at {shared company} (my team and years), mention {a specific detail like a meeting, project or company event}, what I'm exploring, why their company interests me, a request for a brief chat, and one genuine compliment about their work or reputation.
- Template C, senior leader from a former company: subject line "{Shared company} alum seeking your perspective". Who I am (title, years, who I reported to), that I admired their leadership of {team or initiative}, what I'm exploring, a request for 15 minutes, and that I completely understand if they can't.

6. Alumni group strategy
- Week 1: join my school's alumni group, my program's group, former employer alumni groups, industry alumni groups and local chapters.
- Weeks 1 to 2: engage before asking. Comment thoughtfully on 3 to 5 posts a week, share one useful article, congratulate people on milestones, answer questions where I know the answer, and introduce myself in any new-member thread. People respond better to a name they've seen helping out.
- Week 3 onward: pick 5 to 10 members at target companies and send personalized connection requests that mention the group.
- Alumni events: career panels, reunions, company alumni meetups and online webinars. Meeting in person makes every follow-up message warmer.
- A soft job search post for the group, for after 2 weeks of engaging. Don't say "I'm looking for a job." Say I'm starting a new chapter after {X} years in {field}, I'm exploring {target area}, I'm especially interested in {specific focus}, I'd love insights or connections from the {school or company} community, and I'm happy to return the favour. End with 2 or 3 relevant hashtags.

7. Conversation starters by situation
Write 2 to 3 for each: reaching out cold to a school alum, reaching out to a former colleague, in person at an alumni event, and commenting on their LinkedIn post before I message them. End with this rule: lead with the shared experience, not the ask. Never open with "Can you refer me?" Open with "I'd love to hear about your experience."

8. Email templates for outreach outside LinkedIn (write both in full)
- Template A, cold email from the alumni directory: subject "Fellow {University} alum: quick question about {company or industry}". Who I am (class year and program), how I found them, what I'm exploring, what caught my attention about their role, a request for 15 minutes, thanks for their time, and a signature with my school and class year, LinkedIn and phone.
- Template B, after a warm introduction: subject "{Mutual contact} suggested I reach out: {University} connection". Say who sent me and why, one short paragraph about me, and ask for 15 to 20 minutes with a promise to come with specific questions.

Tone: genuine and strategic. Every script should sound like a real person wrote it. Confident, never entitled: a shared school opens a door, it doesn't guarantee anything. Short, so I can personalize each message in under 2 minutes. Warm but professional.

Rules: use only facts I gave you. Never invent a shared memory, a mutual connection or a detail about the other person. Leave a {slot} wherever I need to add a real detail.
~~~

**Replace:** [University or college], [Program and graduation year], [Past employers], [Other affiliations], [Target role and industry], [Years of experience], [LinkedIn URL], [Phone number], [Paste your resume text, or delete this line]

## Cover Letter Template (3 Variants)

**What it does:** Builds a cover letter system with three versions (traditional, results-driven and story-based), an achievement bank and a slot guide you can customize in minutes.
**When to use it:** You're applying to several roles and want strong cover letters without starting from scratch each time.

### Step 1: Build the system

~~~text
Act as a resume writer who specializes in cover letters that get past applicant tracking systems (ATS, the software that screens applications) and still sound like a real person.

Using my resume and details below, build me a cover letter system I can customize for each application in a few minutes.

About me:
- Full name: [Your full name]
- City and province or state: [Your city and province or state]
- Email and phone: [Your email and phone]
- LinkedIn URL: [LinkedIn URL]
- Target roles: [Target roles]
- Target industry: [Target industry]
- Years of experience: [Years of experience]

My resume:
[Paste your resume text]

Optional, a job post to tailor the first draft to:
[Paste a job post, or delete this line]

Build these sections in order.

1. Quick-customize instructions (5 to 7 bullets)
- How to pick the right version:
  Traditional: conservative industries (finance, law, government), large corporations, formal postings.
  Results-driven: tech, sales, marketing, and any role that values measurable outcomes.
  Story-based: startups, creative roles, culture-focused companies, and referrals.
- How to fill the {slots} in a few minutes.
- Always swap in the most relevant achievement from the achievement bank.
- Length: 250 to 350 words, about three-quarters of a page.

2. Getting past the ATS
- Mirror 3 to 5 keywords straight from the job post. Show me an example of finding them and working them in.
- Formatting: standard fonts, simple paragraphs, and no tables, columns, graphics, special characters, headers or footers in the body when submitting online.
- File type: PDF or Word, whichever the application system prefers.
- File name: FirstName-LastName-Cover-Letter-CompanyName.pdf
- Email subject line, when emailing directly: {Full name}: {Job title} application, {job ID if there is one}

3. Version 1: Traditional (polished and formal)
Header: my name, city, email, phone and LinkedIn, the date, then {hiring manager name or "Hiring Team"}, {company name}, and the address if I have it. Greeting: "Dear {hiring manager name or Hiring Team},"
- Opening (2 to 3 sentences): name the role and company, how I found it (referral, job board, company website), and one sentence connecting my experience to their need.
- Body 1, priority match (3 to 4 sentences): my top achievement against the role's main requirement, in this shape: "In my role as {title} at {company}, I {achievement with numbers}." Leave a {priority from the job post} slot.
- Body 2 (3 to 4 sentences): a second achievement, plus 2 to 3 tools or skills from the job post.
- Body 3, fit and motivation (2 to 3 sentences): something specific about the company (mission, news, product or values) and why it matters to me. Slot: {specific thing about the company}.
~~~

## Follow-Up Cadence

**What it does:** Writes a complete follow-up system: three emails for a cold application, three for a referred application, two after a networking event, plus a timing cheat sheet.
**When to use it:** You've applied or met someone and don't want to guess what to send, or when.

### Step 1: Build the follow-ups

~~~text
Write me a complete follow-up system for my job search. Write every email out in full, ready to personalize and send. Actual emails, not descriptions of emails.

About me:
- My name: [Your name]
- My phone and LinkedIn URL: [Phone and LinkedIn URL]
- My core skill area: [Your core skill area]
- One achievement I can mention: [One achievement with a number]
- A specific application I'm following up on (optional): [Job title and company, or delete this line]

Build these sections.

1. Strategy overview
- Why following up matters: it shows real interest and professionalism.
- The rules:
  1. Every follow-up adds value. Never send "just checking in" with nothing in it.
  2. Space them out. Too often feels pushy, too rarely and they forget you.
  3. Always give them an easy out.
  4. Each email gets shorter than the last.
  5. Mention something specific about the role or company every time.
- Best send times: Tuesday to Thursday, 7 to 8:30 a.m. or 5 to 6 p.m. in their time zone. Avoid Mondays (inbox overload) and Fridays (weekend mode).

2. Standard application sequence (3 emails, for a cold application)
- Email 1, 5 to 7 business days after applying: a gentle check-in. Confirm they received it, say I'm still interested, and briefly reinforce my fit in 4 to 6 sentences. Mention my background in {skill area}, what draws me to {a specific part of the role or company}, and one achievement that matches a priority in the job post. Give 3 subject line options, like "Following up: {job title} application, {my name}".
- Email 2, 12 to 14 business days after applying: a value-add. Share something useful and connect it to the role in 3 to 5 sentences. Ideas: a company announcement or product launch, an industry report, a regulation change, a competitor's move, or a relevant talk. Give 3 subject line options, like "Thought you might find this useful: {topic}".
- Email 3, 19 to 21 business days after applying: the graceful close. 3 to 4 sentences, the shortest of the three. Say I understand how busy hiring gets, I'd still love to connect if the role is open, and if not, I'd welcome staying in touch for future roles. Give 3 subject line options, like "Closing the loop: {job title} at {company}".

3. Referred application sequence (warmer, and it moves faster)
- Email 1, 2 to 3 days after applying: subject "Referred by {referrer name}: {job title} application". Say {referrer} suggested I reach out, how I know them, what they told me about the team, my most relevant achievement, that I applied through {channel}, and ask for 20 minutes this week or next.
- Email 2, 1 week after applying: a quick follow-up with something new I've done since my last note (a certification, an article, a project) and why my {key skill} fits {team need}. Mention that {referrer} speaks highly of the team.
- Email 3, 2 weeks after applying: the gentle close. Mention {referrer} recommended me, say I want to respect their time, and ask to stay on their radar for future roles that fit my background in {core area}.

4. After a networking event
- Email 1, within 24 to 48 hours: subject "Great meeting you at {event}, {my name}". Mention the specific topic we discussed and a point they made, what I'm working on, and ask for 15 to 20 minutes of virtual coffee in the next week or two. Right after sending it, I send a LinkedIn connection request that mentions the event.
- Email 2, 1 week later if there's no reply: a short insight or article tied to our conversation, no pressure, happy to stay in touch on LinkedIn, and I hope we cross paths at the next {event type}.

5. Timing cheat sheet (a plain-text timeline)
Standard: Day 0 apply. Day 5 to 7, email 1 (gentle check-in). Day 12 to 14, email 2 (value-add). Day 19 to 21, email 3 (graceful close).
Referred: Day 0 apply. Day 2 to 3, email 1 (referrer mention). Day 7 to 8, email 2 (progress update). Day 14, email 3 (gentle close).
Networking: Day 0 event. Day 1 to 2, email 1 plus LinkedIn request. Day 7 to 8, email 2 (value-add nudge).

6. Do's and don'ts
Do: personalize every email, reference specific details from the job post, conversation or company news, keep subject lines under 50 characters where you can, proofread twice (a typo in a follow-up stings more than in an application), and track every follow-up.
Don't: send more than 3 follow-ups for one application, follow up within 48 hours of the last email (unless they replied), use guilt ("I haven't heard back..." or "I'm disappointed..."), copy the referrer on follow-ups, send on weekends or holidays, or use "Just checking in" as a subject line.

Rules: use only the facts I gave you. Leave {slots} for anything specific to one company or person.

Tone: professional but human, short, and confident without arrogance. Genuine interest, never desperation.
~~~

## Coffee Chat Prep Package

**What it does:** Builds a one-sitting prep package for a networking chat: a research brief, tailored questions, your talking points, a 20-minute flow, a follow-up plan and a quick-reference card.
**When to use it:** You have a coffee chat or advice call booked and want to walk in prepared, with one clear ask.

### Step 1: Build the package

~~~text
Build me a coffee chat prep package I can read on my phone 10 minutes before the meeting and feel ready.

About the person I'm meeting:
- Name, title and company: [Their name, title and company]
- Their LinkedIn URL: [Their LinkedIn URL]
- How we connected: [Mutual contact, event, LinkedIn or alumni group]
- What I know about them so far: [Paste notes from their profile, posts, talks or articles]

About me:
- My background: [Your title and years of experience]
- My target role and industry: [Target role and industry]
- 3 achievements with numbers: [Your top 3 achievements]
- What I'm hoping to get from this chat: [An introduction, advice, or insight on a team or role]

If you can search the web, look up public professional information about this person and their company, and link your sources. If you can't, work only from what I pasted and tell me what I should go look up.

Build these 6 sections.

1. Contact research brief
Name, current role, location, time in role and LinkedIn. Career path (previous roles, companies, years) and one line on what their moves suggest about their priorities (growth, specializing, changing industries). Recent activity and interests (posts, talks, causes). Shared ground (mutual contacts, school, past employers, communities). Company intel (recent news, team size if known, culture signals). If something isn't known, leave it blank and tell me how to find it, including what to look for in their LinkedIn Activity section and how to use mutual connections.

2. Tailored questions (5 to 7)
Rules: nothing I could Google ("What does your company do?" kills the conversation). Ask about their experience, not facts: "What surprised you most about moving from {previous industry} to {current industry}?" beats "Tell me about your industry." Show I did my homework. Go from broad to specific. End with a question that leads naturally to my ask.
Group them as opening questions (rapport), core questions (insight) and strategic questions (leading to the ask). Under each question, add one line on why it works.

3. My talking points
- A 30-second pitch: "I'm {name}, a {title} with {X} years of experience in {field}. Most recently, I {key achievement in one sentence}. I'm exploring opportunities in {target area} because {genuine reason, not 'I need a job'}. I'm especially interested in {focus}, and that's what made me want to talk to you."
- My 3 achievements in 2 sentences each, with a note on when to use each one. I'll pick 1 or 2 based on the flow and won't force all three.
- My ask (choose one, and be specific, because vague asks get vague results):
  Referral: "Based on our conversation, do you think it makes sense for me to connect with {person or team} at {company}? I'd love an introduction if you're comfortable."
  Advice: "If you were in my position with my background in {X}, what would your next move be?"
  Information: "I'm very interested in the {team or role} at {company}. Is there anything you'd recommend I know before I apply?"
- Topics to avoid: complaining about current or past employers, asking about salary at their company, asking them to submit my resume (let them offer), and taking over the conversation with my story (aim for about 70% listening).

4. Conversation flow (20 minutes)
- Minutes 0 to 2: thank them, mention how we connected, and a short icebreaker from our shared ground.
- Minutes 2 to 5: their story. Ask the career path question, listen for themes I can come back to, and ask one natural follow-up.
- Minutes 5 to 12: the core questions. Share my talking points only when it's natural. Use the boomerang: when they ask about me, answer in about 30 seconds, then turn it back to them with a related question.
- Minutes 12 to 16: strategic questions and my ask. Transition with something like "That's really helpful. I'm curious..." If they seem open, be direct. If they seem hesitant, soften it to an advice ask.
- Minutes 16 to 18: wrap up. "I know we're coming up on time, and I want to respect your schedule." Share one takeaway, confirm any next steps or intros they offered, and ask "Is there anything I can help you with?"
- Minutes 18 to 20: thank them sincerely and say I'll follow up by email.

5. Follow-up plan
- Within 24 hours: a thank-you email, written in full, that names one specific thing they said and what I'll do with it, and follows up on any introduction or resource they offered.
- Within 1 week: a thoughtful comment on their latest LinkedIn post, and endorse a few of their skills if it fits.
- Within 2 to 4 weeks: send an article related to our chat, update them on how their advice worked out, and report back on any introduction they made.
- Ongoing: comment on their posts every few months, send a short holiday or new year note, share wins, and congratulate them on role changes or company news.

6. Quick-reference card (to read 5 minutes before)
Their name, title and company, our shared ground, their recent interest, my one-sentence pitch, my one ask, my top 3 questions, and a reminder: listen more than you talk, phone away, use their name.

Rules: use only facts I gave you or that you found and linked. Never invent a detail about this person.

Tone: practical, warm and specific. Networking is relationship-building, not a transaction.
~~~
