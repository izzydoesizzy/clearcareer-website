# Resume audit formats

The exact output for each depth of the resume audit, plus the re-audit you run after edits. The cc-resume-review skill follows these formats. In any other AI tool, paste the ClearCareer resume rubric first (from the rubric file), then paste the prompt for the depth you want.

Verdicts are always one of: Needs Attention, Mixed Signals, Good Foundation, Strong.

## Resume Snapshot

**What it does:** Gives you an overall impression and a one-line verdict for each of the 6 areas of your resume, plus the one fix to make first.
**When to use it:** When you want a quick read on where your resume stands before you spend time on it.

### Step 1: Run the Snapshot

~~~text
Run a Snapshot audit of my resume, using the ClearCareer resume rubric I gave you. If you don't have the rubric, ask me for it before you start.

My target role: [Target role]
Job post (optional):
[Paste the job post, or write "none"]

My resume:
[Paste your resume]

Score each of the 6 areas against the rubric. Check the REQUIRED items first, then apply the verdict rules. Base every verdict on what's actually in my resume.

Output exactly this, and nothing else. Start straight with the first heading.

## Overall impression

**(One bold sentence: the single most important takeaway about this resume.)**
(One plain sentence of context with specific evidence: a real job title, company or number from my resume, or one that's missing.)

## At a glance

| Area | Verdict | What I see |
|---|---|---|
| First impressions and layout | (verdict) | (one line, 12 words or fewer, pointing to my resume) |
| Story and positioning | (verdict) | (one line) |
| Achievements | (verdict) | (one line) |
| Keywords and ATS | (verdict) | (one line) |
| Career trajectory | (verdict) | (one line) |
| Language and presence | (verdict) | (one line) |

## Your first fix

(One sentence: the single change that would make the biggest difference, and where in the resume to make it.)

Rules:
- Verdicts are only: Needs Attention, Mixed Signals, Good Foundation, Strong.
- Every line points to something real in my resume. No advice that could apply to anyone.
- If you can't check something (for example, layout from pasted text), say so in that row instead of guessing.
- Don't rewrite any of my lines in this Snapshot.
- Write to me as "you". Warm and direct, not harsh, not a cheerleader.
- Never invent facts about me. Write without em dashes.
~~~

## Resume Full audit

**What it does:** Diagnoses each of the 6 areas with a verdict, a short headline and 3 points of evidence, then ranks the 3 fixes that matter most.
**When to use it:** When you're ready to work on your resume and want to know exactly what's holding it back and what to change.

### Step 1: Run the Full audit

~~~text
Run a Full audit of my resume, using the ClearCareer resume rubric I gave you. If you don't have the rubric, ask me for it before you start.

My target role: [Target role]
Job post (optional):
[Paste the job post, or write "none"]

My resume:
[Paste your resume]

Score each of the 6 areas against the rubric. Check the REQUIRED items first, then apply the verdict rules. If I gave you a job post, use its words and requirements for the keyword check, and to judge whether my story and career path point at this role.

Output exactly this structure, in this order, with a line of three hyphens between sections. Start straight with the first heading.

## Overall impression

**(One bold sentence: the single most important takeaway about this resume.)**
(One or two plain sentences of context that mention real job titles, companies or numbers from my resume.)

Example of the style:
**Strong background, but the resume buries the lead at every turn.**
Your project work at two national retailers is impressive, but a hiring manager skimming the page won't see it.

## First impressions and layout

**Verdict:** (verdict)

(One headline of 6 to 10 words. A plain verdict, not a full sentence of explanation. Example: "Busy layout competes with the content for attention.")

- **(Label of 3 to 5 words):** (what you see, with evidence from my resume, and what to change)
- **(Label):** (evidence and what to change)
- **(Label):** (evidence and what to change)

## Story and positioning

**Verdict:** (verdict)

(Headline, 6 to 10 words. Example: "No clear answer to 'what do you do?'")

- **(Label):** (evidence from my resume)
- **(Label):** (evidence)
- **(Label):** (evidence)

## Achievements vs. responsibilities

**Verdict:** (verdict)

(Headline, 6 to 10 words. Example: "Hiring managers see activity, not impact.")

- **(Label):** (evidence: name actual roles and bullets, or the missing numbers)
- **(Label):** (evidence)
- **(Label):** (evidence)

## Keywords and ATS

**Verdict:** (verdict)

(Headline, 6 to 10 words. Example: "Language doesn't match how these roles are posted.")

- **(Label):** (evidence. With a job post, name the actual words from the post that are missing. Without one, name the kinds of keywords that are missing.)
- **(Label):** (evidence)
- **(Label):** (evidence)

## Career story and trajectory

**Verdict:** (verdict)

(Headline, 6 to 10 words. Example: "Career path raises questions a hiring manager will ask.")

- **(Label):** (evidence: the actual timeline, company names, how the roles progress)
- **(Label):** (evidence)
- **(Label):** (evidence)

## Language and presence

**Verdict:** (verdict)

(Headline, 6 to 10 words. Example: "Language signals junior, experience says otherwise.")

- **(Label):** (evidence: the actual verbs and phrases from my resume)
- **(Label):** (evidence)
- **(Label):** (evidence)

## Fix these first

1. (The most important fix: what to change and where.)
2. (The second.)
3. (The third.)

Rules:
- Bold labels are 3 to 5 words. They're the skim layer: reading only the labels should give me the full picture. Put a colon after each label.
- Every point refers to something real in my resume (a role, a company, a number, a phrase, or something missing). Nothing generic.
- Headlines are 6 to 10 words, direct, no hedging. Not "There may be some issues with...", just "No clear positioning statement."
- Say what to change, but don't write the new wording. Rewrites belong in the Rewrite Guide.
- If an area is strong, say so briefly and move on. Don't invent problems.
- If you can't check something, say so instead of guessing.
- Write to me as "you". Warm and direct, like a coach beside me.
- Never invent facts about me. Write without em dashes.
~~~

## Resume Rewrite Guide

**What it does:** Quotes your actual resume lines, says what isn't working, and gives you a ready-to-paste rewrite and why it's better, using only the facts already on your resume.
**When to use it:** After a Full audit (or instead of one), when you're ready to make the changes.

### Step 1: Get the Rewrite Guide

~~~text
Create a Rewrite Guide for my resume, using the ClearCareer resume rubric I gave you. If you don't have the rubric, ask me for it before you start.

My target role: [Target role]
Job post (optional):
[Paste the job post, or write "none"]

My resume:
[Paste your resume]

For every problem you find: quote my actual text, explain what's wrong, and give me the exact replacement.

Output exactly this structure, in this order, with a line of three hyphens between sections. Start straight with the first heading.

## Overall assessment

**(One bold sentence: the single most important thing holding this resume back.)**
(Two or three plain sentences with specific evidence: real job titles, companies, numbers, or their absence. Name the pattern, not just the symptom.)

## Professional summary

**Verdict:** (verdict)

(Headline, 6 to 10 words. Example: "Summary reads like a job description, not a pitch.")

For each main problem (2 or 3 at most):

**What you have now:**
> (my exact text)

**What's not working:**
(1 or 2 sentences)

**The rewrite:**
(complete replacement text, ready to paste)

**Why it's better:**
(1 or 2 sentences)

If I have no summary, write one from the facts in my resume and say it's new.

## Work experience bullets

**Verdict:** (verdict)

(Headline, 6 to 10 words. Example: "Duties are listed where achievements should be.")

Pick the 3 or 4 bullets that most need work. For each:

**Role:** (Job title at Company)

**What you have now:**
> (the exact bullet)

**What's not working:**
(1 or 2 sentences)

**The rewrite:**
(strong action verb, what I did, and the result)

**Why it's better:**
(1 or 2 sentences)

## Achievements and numbers

**Verdict:** (verdict)

(Headline, 6 to 10 words. Example: "Numbers exist but aren't framed as business impact.")

For 2 or 3 achievement problems, use the same four parts. Write each rewrite in CAR order: Challenge, Action, Result.

## Keywords and ATS

**Verdict:** (verdict)

(Headline, 6 to 10 words.)

For 2 or 3 keyword or formatting problems, use the same four parts. Quote a heading, line or bullet that shows the gap. With a job post, name the exact words from the post that are missing. Only work a keyword into a rewrite if my resume shows I have that skill or experience. List the rest after this section as "Keywords to check: the post asks for (keyword). If you have this, add it to (where)."

## Career story and positioning

**Verdict:** (verdict)

(Headline, 6 to 10 words. Example: "Career path raises questions a hiring manager will ask.")

For 1 or 2 positioning problems, use the same four parts. The rewrite can be a headline under my name, a revised title line, a short note for a gap or a pivot, or a new opening sentence for my summary.

## Layout and language quick fixes

3 to 5 bullets for anything left from the layout and language areas of the rubric (format, length, tense, filler phrases, first person):
- **(Label):** (what to change and where)

## Numbers to fill in

(A numbered list of every [add a number] and [add: ...] in this guide, each with one question that helps me find the real figure. Example: "Bullet 2, Operations Coordinator: About how many orders did your team process each week?")

Rules:
- Quote my exact text in blockquotes. Never paraphrase a quote or make one up.
- Use only facts from my resume and from what I tell you in this chat. Never invent numbers, employers, job titles, dates, tools, team sizes or results.
- Where a number would help and my resume doesn't have it, write [add a number]. For any other missing detail, write [add: (what's needed)], for example [add: the tool you used].
- Keep my scope. "Helped with" doesn't become "led". A team of 4 doesn't become "a large team".
- Every rewrite is complete and ready to paste, apart from the [add ...] markers.
- If a section is strong, say so in one line and move on. Don't invent problems.
- Match my spelling (Canadian or US). Resume lines use commas, colons or full stops, not dashes. Write without em dashes.
- Write to me as "you". Warm and direct, like a coach working alongside me.
~~~

**Replace:** [Target role], [Paste the job post, or write "none"], [Paste your resume]

### Step 2: Fill in your numbers

~~~text
Here are my answers for the "Numbers to fill in" list:
[Paste your answers, one per line, with the bullet number]

Put them into the rewrites. Mark any figure I called a guess or "about" as "(estimate, confirm)". If I skipped one, rewrite that line so it still reads well without a number.

Then give me my final lines as one clean list I can paste into my resume: the summary first, then the bullets grouped by job, most recent job first. No commentary, just the lines.
~~~

## Resume re-audit after edits

**What it does:** Re-scores your updated resume and shows what changed since your last audit, what's still left, and anything the edits broke.
**When to use it:** Every time you finish a round of edits, and before you send your resume for a role that's different from your usual target.

### Step 1: Run the re-audit

~~~text
I've updated my resume. Re-audit it with the ClearCareer resume rubric and compare it with my last audit. If you don't have the rubric, ask me for it before you start.

My target role: [Target role]
Job post (optional):
[Paste the job post, or write "none"]

My last verdicts:
[Paste the At a glance table or the verdicts from your last audit, or write "earlier in this chat"]

My updated resume:
[Paste your updated resume]

Output exactly this, and nothing else:

## What changed

| Area | Before | Now | What made the difference |
|---|---|---|---|
(one row for each of the 6 areas, 12 words or fewer in the last column)

## Still to fix

(Up to 3 items, most important first, one line each: what to change and where.)

## New issues

(Anything the edits introduced: a number that changed between versions, a tense slip, inconsistent dates, a line that runs onto a new page. Write "None" if there aren't any.)

Rules:
- Use the same 4 verdicts and the same rubric as before.
- Be honest. If an area didn't improve, keep its verdict. Only raise a verdict when the rubric's rules say so, not because I made an effort.
- Point to real lines in my updated resume.
- Never invent facts about me. Write without em dashes.
~~~

**Replace:** [Target role], [Paste the job post, or write "none"], [Paste the At a glance table or the verdicts from your last audit, or write "earlier in this chat"], [Paste your updated resume]
