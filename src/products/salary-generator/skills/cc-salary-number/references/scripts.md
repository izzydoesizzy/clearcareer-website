# Salary script prompts

These prompts fill in word-for-word scripts with your three numbers, then let you practise them out loud. This is general guidance, not legal or financial advice.

## Write my salary scripts

**What it does:** Fills in the word-for-word scripts for "What are your salary expectations?" and the follow-ups, using your own numbers.
**When to use it:** Once you have your anchor, target and walk-away, and before any call where pay might come up.

### Step 1: Give it your numbers

~~~text
Write my salary scripts using my numbers. This is general guidance, not legal or financial advice.

Role: [Job title] at the [Level] level in [City or region]
Currency: [CAD or USD]
My anchor: [Amount]
My target: [Amount]
My walk-away (private, never say this out loud): [Amount]
Years of experience: [Number] in [Specialty]
Benefits or conditions that matter most to me: [For example bonus, equity, remote work, vacation]
Where I am in the process: [First recruiter call, mid-interviews, or offer in hand]
Does the job post list a pay range? [The posted range, or "no"]

Fill in every script below with my details. Keep my walk-away out of every script. Keep each one short enough to say in about 20 seconds.

SCRIPT 1: REDIRECT (early calls, before I know the full role)
"I'd like to learn more about the role and the full scope before putting a number out there. Can you share the range the team has budgeted for this position?"

IF THEY PUSH:
"I'm open within the market range for [Job title] at the [Level] level in [City or region]. Based on my research, I'd expect that to be in the [Anchor minus 10%] to [Anchor] range. Does that line up with what you're working with?"

SCRIPT 2: ANCHOR HIGH (when I have bargaining power, such as rare skills or they approached me)
"Based on my [Number] years of experience in [Specialty], and the market data I've reviewed for [Job title] roles in [City or region], I'm targeting [Anchor] in total compensation. Is that within your range?"
(Only mention other conversations if I've told you I'm having them.)

SCRIPT 3: RANGE WITH CONDITIONS (works in most situations)
"Based on my research and experience, I'm looking at [Target] to [Anchor] for this type of role. The exact number would depend on the full package, including [Benefits that matter most to me]. Where does that land relative to your budget?"

SCRIPT 4: WHEN THE POST LISTS A RANGE
"The posting lists [Posted range]. With my experience in [Specialty], I'd expect to be in the upper part of that range. Is there flexibility within it?"

SCRIPT 5: "WHAT ARE YOU MAKING NOW?"
"I'd rather focus on the value of this role and the market for it. Based on my research, roles like this pay [Target] to [Anchor], and that's the range I'm looking at."

SCRIPT 6: WHEN THE OFFER ARRIVES (buy time, never accept on the spot)
"Thank you, I'm really excited about this. Could you send the full offer in writing? I'd like to take 24 to 48 hours to review the details, and I'll get back to you by [Day]."

SCRIPT 7: "THAT'S ABOVE OUR BUDGET"
"I appreciate you being upfront. What range do you have for the role? If the base is fixed, I'd be glad to look at other parts of the package, like [For example a signing bonus, extra vacation, or an earlier salary review]."

THE SILENCE (after any number)
After you say your number, stop talking. Count to five in your head. Don't explain, apologize or lower it. Let them answer first.

After the scripts:
1. Tell me which 2 scripts I'm most likely to need at my stage, and why.
2. Add one line on local rules: some provinces and US states limit or ban questions about pay history, and rules vary, so I should check where I live.
3. Remind me to say each script out loud 3 times before my call.
~~~

**Replace:** [Job title], [Level], [City or region], [CAD or USD], [Amount], [Number], [Specialty], [For example bonus, equity, remote work, vacation], [First recruiter call, mid-interviews, or offer in hand], [The posted range, or "no"]

## Practise the salary conversation

**What it does:** Plays a recruiter or hiring manager so you can rehearse your salary scripts out loud, with feedback.
**When to use it:** The day before any call where pay might come up.

### Step 1: Start the role-play

~~~text
Play a recruiter for the [Job title] role at [Company or "a company in my industry"] so I can practise talking about salary. This is general guidance, not legal or financial advice.

My anchor: [Amount]
My target: [Amount]
My walk-away (private, so you know when I'm about to go too low): [Amount]
Currency: [CAD or USD]
How tough to be: [Friendly, realistic or tough]

How to run it:
1. Ask me about salary expectations the way a real recruiter would. Then stop and wait for my answer.
2. Push back once or twice in realistic ways: "Just give me a number", "What are you making now?", "That's above our budget."
3. After each of my answers, step out of character and tell me: what worked, the one fix that matters most, and a stronger version of what I said using my numbers.
4. Flag it right away if I say a number below my target, mention my walk-away, apologize for my number, or fill the silence after stating it.
5. Run 3 rounds, then give me a short summary: my strongest line, my biggest habit to fix, and the one script to practise most.

Never invent salary data. Use only the numbers I gave you.
~~~

**Replace:** [Job title], [Company or "a company in my industry"], [Amount], [CAD or USD], [Friendly, realistic or tough]
