# Three numbers prompts

These prompts work out your walk-away number, the full value of a pay package, and your anchor, target and walk-away together. This is general guidance, not legal or financial advice.

## Find my walk-away number

**What it does:** Turns your monthly costs and must-haves into the lowest salary you can accept.
**When to use it:** First, before you look at any market data, so the market doesn't talk you out of what you need.

### Step 1: List your costs

~~~text
Help me work out my walk-away salary: the lowest yearly pay I can accept. This is general guidance, not legal or financial advice.

My location: [City, province or state]
Currency: [CAD or USD]

My monthly costs:
- Housing (rent or mortgage, property tax, home insurance): [Amount]
- Utilities (power, heat, water, internet, phone): [Amount]
- Transportation (car payment, insurance, gas, transit): [Amount]
- Food (groceries and eating out, honestly): [Amount]
- Insurance (health, dental, life, disability) that I pay myself: [Amount]
- Debt payments (student loans, credit cards, other): [Amount]
- Childcare or dependants: [Amount]
- Savings (at least an emergency fund contribution): [Amount]
- Everything else (subscriptions, clothing, personal): [Amount]

My must-haves in a new role: [For example health and dental coverage, remote work, at least 3 weeks of vacation, no weekend shifts]

Do this:
1. Add up my monthly total and multiply by 12.
2. Add a 15% buffer (multiply by 1.15). Show the maths.
3. Explain that these are after-tax costs, but salaries are quoted before tax. Tell me to use a take-home pay calculator for my province or state to find the before-tax salary that covers this amount. If I ask you to estimate the before-tax figure, show your working and label it "(estimate, confirm)".
4. List any must-have that would cost me money if a job doesn't include it (for example paying for my own health coverage), and ask me what it would cost so we can add it.
5. Give me my walk-away number in a single line, with the currency.

Use only the numbers I give you. If a line is blank, ask me about it rather than guessing.
~~~

**Replace:** [City, province or state], [CAD or USD], [Amount], [For example health and dental coverage, remote work, at least 3 weeks of vacation, no weekend shifts]

## Calculate my total compensation

**What it does:** Adds up everything a job pays you, not just the base salary, so you can compare offers fairly.
**When to use it:** When you have an offer, or when you're comparing two roles.

### Step 1: Enter the package

~~~text
Help me calculate the total yearly value of this pay package. This is general guidance, not legal or financial advice.

Currency: [CAD or USD]
Base salary: [Amount]
Annual bonus: [Target %, and how often people actually hit it, if I know]
Signing bonus: [Amount or none]
Equity, stock or RSUs: [Value per year at today's price, and the vesting schedule, or none]
Commission: [Realistic yearly amount, or none]
Retirement matching (RRSP, pension or 401(k)): [Match % and limit, or none]
Health and dental (employer's share): [Amount if known, or what's covered]
Vacation days: [Number]
Remote or hybrid: [Days at home per week]
Learning or professional development budget: [Amount or none]
Other (transit, phone, wellness, gym): [Amount or none]

Do this:
1. Make a table: Component | Yearly value | Notes.
2. Show year 1 separately from a normal year, because signing bonuses and equity vesting can make year 1 look bigger than it is.
3. For vacation, show the value as days multiplied by my daily rate (base divided by about 260 working days). Show the maths.
4. For remote work, ask me what I'd save on commuting, parking and lunches rather than guessing.
5. Use only the values I give you. If you need to estimate something, ask me first and label it "(estimate, confirm)".
6. Give me the total, and point out the 2 or 3 components worth asking about before I accept (for example how often the bonus actually pays out).
~~~

**Replace:** [CAD or USD], [Amount], [Target %, and how often people actually hit it, if I know], [Amount or none], [Value per year at today's price, and the vesting schedule, or none], [Realistic yearly amount, or none], [Match % and limit, or none], [Amount if known, or what's covered], [Number], [Days at home per week]

## Set my three numbers

**What it does:** Combines your walk-away and your market research into your anchor, target and walk-away, with a reality check.
**When to use it:** After you've found your walk-away and gathered at least 5 pay ranges.

### Step 1: Bring it together

~~~text
Set my three salary numbers. This is general guidance, not legal or financial advice.

Role: [Job title], [Level], in [Location]
Currency: [CAD or USD]
My walk-away (from my costs): [Amount]
My market consensus range: [Low] to [High]
Percentile data, if I have it (25th, median, 75th): [Amounts, or "none"]
What gives me bargaining power, if anything: [For example rare skills, more experience than the post asks for, they approached me, another offer in hand]
Benefits or conditions that matter most to me: [List]

Calculate:
1. Walk-away: my number from above. Below this, I decline or keep looking.
2. Target: the midpoint of my market consensus range. Show the maths.
3. Anchor: the 75th percentile if I gave you percentile data. Otherwise, 10 to 15% above my target, staying within what my research supports. Use the higher end only if I listed real bargaining power. Show the maths.

Then do a reality check:
- If my walk-away is above my market range, tell me plainly that this role level may not meet my needs, and list my options (aim for a higher level, look in a different market, or adjust my costs).
- If my target is below my walk-away, tell me negotiation alone won't fix it.
- If my anchor is above anything my research shows, bring it down to what I can back up.

Show the result as a table: Number | Amount | How we got it | Sources behind it.

Use only the figures I gave you. Never add salary data of your own. Label any estimate "(estimate, confirm)".
~~~

**Replace:** [Job title], [Level], [Location], [CAD or USD], [Amount], [Low], [High], [Amounts, or "none"], [For example rare skills, more experience than the post asks for, they approached me, another offer in hand], [List]
