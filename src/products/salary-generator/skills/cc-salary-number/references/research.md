# Salary research prompts

These prompts show you where to find real pay data for your role and location, and turn what you find into one market range. This is general guidance, not legal or financial advice.

## Research the pay range for my role

**What it does:** Gives you a step-by-step research plan for your exact role and location, and searches for posted ranges if your AI tool can browse.
**When to use it:** Before your first recruiter call, or as soon as salary comes up.

### Step 1: Set up the research

~~~text
Help me research the market pay range for this role. This is general guidance, not legal or financial advice.

Job title: [Job title]
Level: [For example junior, intermediate, senior, manager, director]
Industry: [Industry]
Location: [City, province or state, country, or "remote, living in" plus where you live]
Years of experience: [Number]
Company size (if I have a specific company in mind): [Approximate number of employees, or "not sure"]

Do this:
1. Tell me the currency we're working in (CAD or USD).
2. Give me a research plan using these four sources, with exact steps for each:
   a. Job posts that list a pay range. Tell me whether my province or state requires pay ranges in job posts, but only if you're sure. If you're not sure, tell me to check the current rules where I live. Several provinces and US states now require them, and the rules change.
   b. Salary sites: Glassdoor, Payscale, Indeed, LinkedIn job posts, and Levels.fyi if this is a tech role. Remind me to look at the median and at total pay, not just base salary.
   c. Government data: Job Bank (jobbank.gc.ca) in Canada, or the Bureau of Labor Statistics (bls.gov/oes) in the US. Free annual salary guides from recruiting firms are worth a look too.
   d. People: recruiters in my field, and people in similar roles (asking about ranges, not their personal pay).
3. If you can browse the web, search for job posts with pay ranges for this title, level and location. Only report a range you can link to a page you actually opened. Include the source, the link and the date posted. Never estimate or invent a range and present it as data.
4. If you can't browse, or can't verify a figure, say so plainly. Don't guess. Tell me exactly what to search for and I'll paste back what I find.
5. Give me a blank table to fill in: Source | Low | High | Notes (base or total pay, date, link).
~~~

**Replace:** [Job title], [For example junior, intermediate, senior, manager, director], [Industry], [City, province or state, country, or "remote, living in" plus where you live], [Number], [Approximate number of employees, or "not sure"]

### Step 2: Turn my ranges into one market range

~~~text
Here are the pay ranges I found:

[Paste each range with its source, the low and high figures, whether it is base or total pay, and the date]

Do this:
1. Put them in a table: Source | Low | High | Base or total pay | Date.
2. Flag anything that looks out of place: very old data, a different level or title, a different city, total pay mixed in with base pay.
3. Group them into four source types: job posts, salary sites, government data, and people or recruiters.
4. Find the market consensus range: where at least 3 of the 4 source types overlap. Show me how you worked it out.
5. If the sources disagree a lot, or I have fewer than 5 ranges, say so and tell me what to look for next before I rely on the range.

Use only the numbers I gave you. If you calculate something (an overlap or a midpoint), show the maths. Don't add any figures of your own. If you suggest an estimate, ask me first and label it "(estimate, confirm)".
~~~

**Replace:** [Paste each range with its source, the low and high figures, whether it is base or total pay, and the date]

## Ask a recruiter or contact about pay

**What it does:** Writes a short, comfortable message asking a recruiter or someone in your field what they're seeing for pay.
**When to use it:** When online data is thin, or you want a reality check from someone close to the market.

### Step 1: Describe who you're asking

~~~text
Write a short message (under 100 words) asking about pay for my target role.

Who I'm asking: [A recruiter at the company, an agency recruiter in my field, or someone who works in a similar role]
How I know them: [For example we've never met, we met at an event, a former colleague]
Where I'll send it: [Email or LinkedIn]
Target role: [Job title], [Level], in [City or region]

Rules:
- Ask about the range for the role, not about their personal pay.
- For a company recruiter, ask what range is budgeted for the position.
- For an agency recruiter, ask what they're seeing for this kind of role in this market right now.
- For a peer, make it easy to say no, and offer to return the favour.
- Friendly and plain. No pressure.

Then give me a one-line way to ask the same question out loud on a call.
~~~

**Replace:** [A recruiter at the company, an agency recruiter in my field, or someone who works in a similar role], [For example we've never met, we met at an event, a former colleague], [Email or LinkedIn], [Job title], [Level], [City or region]
