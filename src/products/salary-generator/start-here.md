# Start here: The Salary Number Generator

Welcome in. Here's a moment I want to save you from: a recruiter asks "What are your salary expectations?", your mind goes blank, and you blurt out a number you made up on the spot. This kit fixes that. You'll work out three numbers (your anchor, your target and your walk-away), back them up with real market data, and practise the exact words to say. There's a guide you can fill in on this page, and a Claude skill that walks you through it step by step and writes your scripts with your numbers in them. It takes about an hour, and you'll use those numbers in every salary conversation that follows.

## What's inside

- **Guide: The Salary Number Generator.** The full method: your walk-away worksheet, four free sources for market research, a total compensation worksheet, how to set your three numbers, and three word-for-word scripts.
- **Claude skill: cc-salary-number.** Asks for your role and location, shows you exactly how to research pay, works out your three numbers with you, and fills in your scripts.
- **Its prompt cards:** the same method as copy-paste prompts (research, walk-away, total compensation, three numbers, scripts and a practice role-play), so you can use them in any AI tool.

## Your first 10 minutes

1. Open the guide and fill in the walk-away worksheet in Step 1 with rough monthly numbers. Rough is fine for now.
2. Multiply your yearly total by 1.15 to add a buffer. Then plug it into a take-home pay calculator for your province or state to find the before-tax salary that covers it. That's your walk-away.
3. Search a job board for your exact title in your city, and open 3 posts that list a pay range. Write each range down.
4. Read Script 1 (Redirect) in Step 5 of the guide, and say it out loud once.

You now have a floor you won't go below, the start of your market research, and an answer ready if a recruiter calls tomorrow.

## A 7-day plan

- **Day 1:** Fill in your walk-away worksheet and turn it into a before-tax number.
- **Day 2:** Collect 5 to 10 job posts with pay ranges for your title, level and location.
- **Day 3:** Check two salary sites and the government data for your country (Job Bank in Canada, the Bureau of Labor Statistics in the US).
- **Day 4:** Find your market consensus range, where at least 3 of your 4 sources overlap.
- **Day 5:** Fill in the total compensation worksheet for your current job or an offer.
- **Day 6:** Set your anchor, target and walk-away, and do the reality check.
- **Day 7:** Fill in your scripts and say each one out loud three times. Then practise with the role-play.

## Good to know

- **This is general guidance, not legal or financial advice.** For tax questions, use a take-home pay calculator or talk to an accountant.
- **Results vary.** Knowing your numbers helps you ask with confidence, but no one can guarantee what an employer will offer.
- **Rules vary by place.** Several provinces and US states now require pay ranges in job posts, and some limit questions about your pay history. Rules change, so check the current ones where you live.
- **Keep your facts true.** Don't invent a competing offer or inflate your current pay. Use real ranges you can point to.

Know your numbers before anyone asks. Then say them, and stop talking.
