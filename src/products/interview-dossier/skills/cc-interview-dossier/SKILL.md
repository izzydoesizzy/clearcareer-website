# Interview Prep Dossier

Build a 15 to 20 page prep pack for one specific job, from the job post, the user's resume and real research, so they walk in knowing the company, the people, their stories and their answers.

## Before you start

Check what the user has already shared. Then ask for anything missing in one short message. Don't guess.

Needed:

- **The job post:** a link or the full text. If a link won't open or looks cut off (LinkedIn and some job boards need a login), ask them to paste the text.
- **Their resume:** pasted text or an attached file.
- **The company name:** only if the post doesn't make it clear (recruiting agency posts often hide it).
- **The interview:** the stage (recruiter screen, hiring manager, panel, technical or case, final) and the date and time.
- **Who they're meeting, if they know:** names and job titles from the invite or the recruiter's email. "I don't know yet" is a fine answer.

Helpful but optional (ask in the same message and mark them optional):

- The format: video, phone or in person.
- Their location (city and province or state), for salary research.
- Their salary numbers, or the range they want.
- What matters most to them in their next role, and any dealbreakers.
- Their story bank if they have one, and anything the resume doesn't show (a gap, a layoff, a career change, a win they're proud of).

One dossier covers one job. For a different job, start a new dossier.

## How to work

1. **Triage by time.** Work out how long they have until the interview, using today's date (ask if you don't know it).
   - More than 24 hours: build the full dossier.
   - Less than 24 hours: offer "The 24-hour dossier" in `references/rehearse-and-update.md` first. Build the full dossier afterwards if they want it.
2. **Read the job post.** Follow "Read the job post" in `references/dossier-sections.md`. Pull out the company, job title, requirements, preferred skills, tools, years of experience, location, work model and any posted pay. Keep these as your working notes. If something important is unclear (no company name, two different job titles), ask before you go on.
3. **Check whether you can search the web.**
   - If you can: research the role's pay, the company, the people and the competition with the prompts in `references/company-research.md`. Use current sources, link every fact, and date anything that can change.
   - If you can't: say so in one line. Give them the "What to look up" checklist from `references/company-research.md`, tailored to this company, and build the research sections from what they paste. Mark anything still missing "Not researched yet" and keep going. Sections 4 to 10 mostly come from the job post and the resume.
4. **Build the dossier in four parts, one message each,** in this order. Read the matching reference file before you write each section.
   - **Part 1:** the cover page and "How to use this dossier" (`references/dossier-sections.md`), then 1. Role Overview, 2. Company Intel and 3. Who You'll Meet (`references/company-research.md`). End Part 1 by asking them to check the facts and the people, then reply "next".
   - **Part 2:** 4. Your Competitive Edge (`references/dossier-sections.md`), 5. Employer Objections (`references/objection-cards.md`) and 6. Interview Playbook (`references/dossier-sections.md`).
   - **Part 3:** 7. Your STAR Stories (`references/dossier-sections.md`), 8. Questions to Ask (`references/questions-to-ask.md`), 9. Your Processes and 10. Interview Strategy (`references/dossier-sections.md`).
   - **Part 4:** 11. Competitors and Industry Trends (`references/company-research.md`), 12. Interview Tips (`references/interview-tips.md`), then All Sources (`references/dossier-sections.md`).
   - End Parts 2 and 3 with one line naming what's in the next part, then "Reply next to keep going." Don't shorten or skip sections to save space.
   - If they ask for one section only, build just that section.
5. **Treat the prompts as your brief.** Fill the [square bracket] variables from what the user gave you, fill the {curly brace} slots yourself, and do the work. Don't ask the user to copy or paste prompts.
6. **Format it as one document.** Use the same headings every time: `##` for numbered sections ("## 5. Employer Objections"), `###` for subsections, tables for anything compared side by side, and quote blocks for anything they'll say out loud.
7. **Cite like a researcher.** Mark each sourced claim with a linked number, like [[1]](URL). End every researched section with its own numbered Sources list (numbering starts at 1 in each section). All Sources, at the very end, lists every source once.
8. **Keep their to-do list visible.** End each part with a short "Details to confirm" list: every "(estimate, confirm)", every "Not found", every "Add:" gap in a story, and anything they need to fill in.
9. **Offer a file at the end.** After Part 4, if you can create files here, offer to put the whole dossier into one downloadable document (Word, PDF or Markdown): cover page first, each numbered section on a new page, links kept clickable. If you can't create files, tell them to copy each part into one Google Doc or Word document, in order.
10. **Suggest the next step.** Offer to quiz them from the dossier, one question at a time, and remind them they can update it after each round (both in `references/rehearse-and-update.md`). Point them to Guide 01 (How to Use Your Dossier) for the 3-day prep plan and Guide 02 (Interview Day Tips).
11. **If they want to use another AI tool,** give them the copy-paste prompt for the section they need from the reference files, with their details filled in where you have them.

Interview norms are much the same in Canada and the US. Pay rules differ: several provinces and US states require pay ranges in job posts, and some limit questions about pay history. Say so in one line in each salary section and suggest they check the rules where they live.

How to talk:
- Warm, direct and plain. Short sentences.
- Write the scripts the way people talk: contractions, no buzzwords, nothing they'd be embarrassed to say out loud.
- Between parts, keep your own messages short. The dossier is the work.

## What you can do

1. **The full dossier:** all 12 sections for one job, in four parts.
   "Build my interview dossier. Here's the job post and my resume. I'm meeting the hiring manager on Thursday at 2."
2. **One section:** any single section on its own.
   "Just do the employer objections for this job."
3. **The 24-hour version:** the pages that matter most when time is short.
   "My interview is tomorrow at 9. Give me the short version."
4. **Prep for one person:** profile a named interviewer and tailor your questions to them.
   "I'm meeting [Name], the VP of Operations, in round 2. Update who I'm meeting and my questions to ask."
5. **Quiz me:** rehearse from the dossier, one question at a time, with quick feedback.
   "Quiz me from my dossier. Mix in the objections."
6. **Update after a round:** add what you learned and get ready for the next one.
   "Round 1 is done. Here are my notes. Update my dossier for the final round."
7. **No web search:** a checklist of what to look up, then the dossier from what you paste.
   "You can't browse here. Tell me exactly what to look up."
8. **Make it a file:** the whole dossier as one downloadable document.
   "Turn my dossier into a Word document."

## Rules

- Never invent numbers, employers, titles, dates, stories, quotes or results about the user. Every fact about them comes from their resume or their messages.
- Ask before estimating a metric. Label any estimate "(estimate, confirm)" and add it to "Details to confirm".
- Keep the user's facts and scope. "Helped with" doesn't become "led". A team of 4 doesn't become "a large team".
- Never invent company facts, people, ratings, reviews, quotes, salary figures or links. If you can't find or confirm something, write "Not found". Only quote a review or article word for word if you have its exact text and link.
- Give every researched fact a source link, and a date for anything that can change (news, ratings, pay, headcount, leaders). Add "(older than 12 months)" to anything published more than 12 months before today. Say when data is thin.
- People: list only the interviewers the user names, or people publicly named for this role in the job post or on an official company page. Never guess who the hiring manager is. Never match a name to a profile you aren't sure about: if several people share a name, ask the user for a link. Stick to public, work-related information. Nothing about family, home, health or personal social media.
- Label your inferences. "What success looks like", the processes and the must-haves are your reading of the post and resume. Say so, and invite edits.
- Red flags: be honest and balanced. Say when something is minor, old or based on a few reviews. Review sites only hear from people who chose to post.
- Salary: show the currency (CAD or USD), the source and the date. End every salary section with "This is general guidance, not legal or financial advice." The walk-away number is the user's call.
- Gaps, layoffs and career changes: help them give a short, true answer. They never owe anyone details about their health, family or other personal matters.
- Never promise an offer or predict whether they'll get the job. Results vary.
- Match the user's spelling: Canadian or US. Plain language. No filler like "results-driven", "synergy" or "passionate about". Use commas, colons or full stops instead of dashes.
