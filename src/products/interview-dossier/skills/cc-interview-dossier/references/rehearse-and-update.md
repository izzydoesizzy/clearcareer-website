# Rehearse and update prompts

A dossier only works if you use it. These prompts build a short version when time is tight, help you rehearse from your dossier, and keep it current between rounds. Replace everything in [square brackets] with your own details. Leave anything in {curly braces} alone: those are slots the AI fills.

## The 24-hour dossier

**What it does:** Builds the short version of your dossier: the pages that matter most when your interview is less than a day away.
**When to use it:** Your interview is tomorrow (or today) and there's no time for the full dossier.

### Step 1: Build the short version

~~~text
Act as my interview coach. My interview for [Job title] at [Company] is [When: for example "tomorrow at 9 a.m. Eastern"]. It's a [Interview stage] interview with [Who you're meeting, or "not sure"].

The job post:
[Paste the job post]

My resume:
[Paste your resume]

Build a short dossier I can read in 20 minutes:
1. The job in 5 bullets: what they need most, in plain words.
2. The company in 5 bullets: what they do, one recent piece of news, and one thing I could mention. If you can search the web, link each fact and give its date. If you can't, use only the job post and tell me the 3 things to look up tonight.
3. Who I'm meeting: only the people I named, their role, and what they'll likely care about. Never guess who they are.
4. My top 5 requirement matches: a table of requirement, my evidence and strength (Strong, Moderate or Gap).
5. My top 3 objections: one line on each concern, and a word-for-word talking point for each.
6. My "Tell me about yourself": 150 to 200 words, with the word count.
7. The 5 questions I'm most likely to get, each with a one-line answer plan and the story to use.
8. 3 stories as one-line cues: the title, the number, and the requirement it covers.
9. 4 questions to ask, tied to the company.
10. Salary: my anchor, target and walk-away if I've given them. If not, one line on what to say if they ask early. Add "This is general guidance, not legal or financial advice."
11. A 10-item checklist for the hour before.

Use only my real facts. Never invent numbers, stories or people. Label any estimate "(estimate, confirm)". Results vary, so don't predict how it will go.
~~~

**Replace:** [Job title], [Company], [When: for example "tomorrow at 9 a.m. Eastern"], [Interview stage], [Who you're meeting, or "not sure"], [Paste the job post], [Paste your resume]

## Quiz me from my dossier

**What it does:** Runs a rehearsal from your dossier, one question at a time, with quick feedback after each answer.
**When to use it:** Every day before the interview, for 15 to 20 minutes.

### Step 1: Start the quiz

~~~text
Act as my interviewer for [Job title] at [Company]. Use my dossier below.

[Paste your dossier, or the sections you want to practise]

What I want to practise: [All of it, or pick: "Tell me about yourself", likely questions, objections, STAR stories, salary]

How to run it:
1. Ask me ONE question at a time, the way the interviewer would. Wait for my answer.
2. Mix in the objections from Section 5, worded the way a real interviewer would ask them.
3. Add a realistic follow-up now and then, like "What was your part, specifically?"
4. After each answer, step out of role and give me: what worked, the one fix that matters most, and a stronger version built only from my facts and my dossier.
5. Every few questions, remind me to say my answers out loud, not just type them.
6. After 6 to 8 questions, give me a short summary: my strongest answers, the 2 to practise again, and any story I haven't used yet.

Never invent facts about me. Keep your messages short.
~~~

**Replace:** [Job title], [Company], [Paste your dossier, or the sections you want to practise], [All of it, or pick: "Tell me about yourself", likely questions, objections, STAR stories, salary]

## Update my dossier after a round

**What it does:** Adds what you learned in an interview and gets your dossier ready for the next round.
**When to use it:** Within a day of each interview, ideally as soon as you know the next step.

### Step 1: Debrief the round

~~~text
I just finished my [Interview stage] interview for [Job title] at [Company]. Help me update my dossier for the next round: [Next round: stage, date and who I'm meeting, or "not booked yet"].

My notes from the interview:
- Who I met (names and job titles): [Names and job titles]
- The questions they asked: [List them]
- How I think I did on each: [Short notes]
- What they seemed to care about most: [Notes]
- Any doubts or concerns they raised: [Notes, or "none"]
- What I learned about the role, team, pay or timeline: [Notes]

My current dossier:
[Paste your dossier]

Then:
1. Give me a short debrief: what went well, what to tighten, and the team's top 3 priorities based on my notes.
2. List what to change, section by section:
   - Section 3: add the next round's people (only the ones I named) and what they'll likely care about
   - Section 5: add or re-rank objections based on any doubts they raised
   - Section 6: likely questions for the next stage, and better versions of any answers I wasn't happy with
   - Section 7: which stories to use next, and which ones I've already told (so I don't repeat them to the same people)
   - Section 8: new questions that go deeper than the ones I already asked
   - Section 10: what to learn and get across next, and whether pay is likely to come up
3. Write the updated sections in full, under the heading "Version 2: {next round}".

Use only what I've told you. Don't predict whether I'll get the job.
~~~

**Replace:** [Interview stage], [Job title], [Company], [Next round: stage, date and who I'm meeting, or "not booked yet"], [Names and job titles], [List them], [Short notes], [Notes], [Notes, or "none"], [Paste your dossier]
