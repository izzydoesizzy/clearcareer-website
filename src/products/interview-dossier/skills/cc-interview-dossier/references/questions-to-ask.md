# Questions to ask prompts

The end of the interview is your turn. Good questions show you did your homework, help you decide whether you want the job, and give you one more chance to show how you think. These prompts build Section 8 of your dossier: 10 to 12 questions tied to what you found in your research, each with a short note on why it's worth asking. Replace everything in [square brackets] with your own details. Leave anything in {curly braces} alone: those are slots the AI fills.

A few habits that help:

- Pick 3 to 4 questions per interview. Have a couple of spares in case they answer one before you ask it.
- Skip anything the company website answers.
- Save pay, vacation and benefits for the recruiter or a later stage, unless they bring it up or it's a dealbreaker for you.
- Different people, different questions. Recruiters know the process and timeline. Hiring managers know what success looks like and where the team is stuck. Peers know the day to day. Executives think about direction and strategy.
- Don't repeat a question to the same person in a later round. Go deeper instead.

## Questions to ask, tied to your research

**What it does:** Writes 10 to 12 questions in three groups, each tied to a specific research finding, with a note on what each question tells you.
**When to use it:** After the company research. This is Section 8 of your dossier.

### Step 1: Write the questions

~~~text
Act as my interview coach. Build Section 8 of my interview dossier: Questions to Ask, for my [Interview stage] interview for [Job title] at [Company].

Who I'm meeting: [Names and job titles, or "not sure yet"]

My company research (news, products, reviews, red flags, competitors):
[Paste your Company Intel and Competitors sections, or your notes]

What I most need to know before I'd accept an offer:
[For example remote work, growth path, team stability. Or write "none"]

Write 10 to 12 questions in three groups:

## 8. Questions to Ask

### About the role and team
3 to 4 questions

### About company strategy and growth
3 to 4 questions

### About culture and development
3 to 4 questions

For each question:
- Tie it to something specific from my research (a launch, a piece of news, a theme in the reviews, a competitor's move). Name the finding in a few words.
- Add a short note in italics: *Why ask: {what this question tells me, or what it shows them about me}*

Rules:
- No questions the company website already answers.
- No questions about pay, vacation or benefits unless I've said they're a dealbreaker.
- If my research found a red flag that matters to me, include one calm, neutral question about it, worded so it doesn't sound like an accusation.
- Only use facts from my research. Never invent news or numbers to build a question around.
- After the list, mark the 3 to 4 best questions for the person I'm meeting, based on their role.
~~~

### Step 2: The closing question

~~~text
Give me one closing question to ask at the very end, worded naturally, along the lines of: "Is there anything about my background that gives you pause?"

Then give me a short plan for what to do if they name a concern: thank them, answer with evidence from my objection cards, and check whether that helped. Keep it to 3 short lines I can remember.
~~~

**Replace:** Nothing. Use this after Step 1.
