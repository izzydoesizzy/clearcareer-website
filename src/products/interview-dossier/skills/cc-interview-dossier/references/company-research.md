# Company research prompts

These prompts build the research half of your dossier: the role and what it pays (Section 1), the company (Section 2), the people you're meeting (Section 3) and the competition (Section 11). Replace everything in [square brackets] with your own details. Leave anything in {curly braces} alone: those are slots the AI fills.

They work best in an AI tool that can search the web. If yours can't, use the checklist below, paste what you find into each prompt, and the AI will organize it for you.

The rules for every research section:

- Every fact gets a source link. Anything that can change (news, ratings, pay, headcount, leaders) also gets a date.
- Anything published more than 12 months before today is marked "(older than 12 months)".
- "Not found" beats a guess. No invented names, people, numbers, ratings, reviews or links.
- Each sourced claim gets a linked number, like [[1]](URL). Each section ends with its own numbered Sources list, in this shape: 1. {Page title}, {Site}. Published {date, or "date not shown"}. Accessed {today's date}. {Link}
- Salary sections end with: "This is general guidance, not legal or financial advice."

What to look up if your AI can't search the web (copy and paste the text, with the link and date):

1. The full job post.
2. The company's About, Careers and Leadership or Team pages.
3. News from the last 12 months: search "[Company] news" and copy the headline, date, site and link of the 3 to 5 stories closest to your role.
4. Reviews: on Glassdoor and Indeed, note the overall rating, the number of reviews, the date you checked, 3 common pros, 3 common cons, and any reviews of the interview process for this role or team.
5. Pay: the posted range if there is one, then 2 to 3 sources for this job title in your city, such as Glassdoor, Indeed or LinkedIn salary pages, plus Job Bank (Canada) or the Bureau of Labor Statistics (US). Levels.fyi helps for tech roles. Note the currency and the date.
6. The people you're meeting: their LinkedIn headline, current role, time at the company, and anything work-related they've posted, written or presented in the last year.
7. Competitors: search "[Company] competitors" and note 3 to 5 names with what each one does.
8. Red flags: search "[Company] layoffs", "[Company] lawsuit" and "[Company] hiring freeze". Note anything from the last 2 years, with its date.

## Role overview and salary estimates

**What it does:** Breaks the job post into what matters and researches what the role pays.
**When to use it:** First, as soon as the interview is booked. This is Section 1 of your dossier.

### Step 1: Break down the role

~~~text
Act as my interview coach. I have an interview for [Job title] at [Company] on [Interview date]. Build Section 1 of my interview dossier: Role Overview.

The job post:
[Paste the job post]

My location: [City, province or state]

Use these headings:

## 1. Role Overview

### Key responsibilities
The top 5 to 7 things this person will do, in plain words. Put the ones the post repeats or lists first at the top.

### Required skills and experience
The skills, qualifications, certifications and years of experience the post asks for. Mark each one Required or Preferred.

### Tools and technologies
Every software, platform or tool the post names.

### What success looks like
Your read of what a strong hire would deliver in the first 6 to 12 months, based on the post. Label it "My read of the post" and quote the words that led you there.

### Salary estimates
If you can search the web, research current pay for [Job title] at [Company], and for similar roles in [City, province or state] and this industry. Use 2 to 4 sources, such as Glassdoor, Indeed, LinkedIn Salary, PayScale, Levels.fyi (tech roles), Job Bank (Canada) or the Bureau of Labor Statistics (US).

Show a table: Source | Range | What it covers (title, location, level) | Date

Then give:
- Posted range: the range in the job post, or "Not posted"
- Market range: the 25th to 75th percentile if a source gives it, or the range exactly as the source states it
- At this company: a range only if a source shows one for this company, or "Not found"
- How solid the data is, in one line (for example "Based on a small number of reports")
- One line: several provinces and US states require pay ranges in job posts, and some limit questions about pay history, so check the rules where I live
- End with: "This is general guidance, not legal or financial advice."

Rules: Never invent a number. Show the currency (CAD or USD). Link every source and give its date. Add "(older than 12 months)" to anything published more than 12 months ago. If you can't search the web, tell me, and list exactly what salary data to look up and paste in.

Mark each sourced claim with a linked number like [[1]](URL). End with a numbered Sources list for this section.
~~~

**Replace:** [Job title], [Company], [Interview date], [Paste the job post], [City, province or state]

## Company intel

**What it does:** Researches the company: what it does, recent news, what employees say, and anything worth asking about.
**When to use it:** Right after the role overview. This is Section 2 of your dossier.

### Step 1: Research the company

~~~text
Act as my interview coach and researcher. Build Section 2 of my interview dossier: Company Intel, for my interview for [Job title] at [Company].

The company website (if I know it): [Company website]

The job post:
[Paste the job post]

If you can search the web, use it. If you can't, tell me, list exactly what to look up and paste in, and use only what I paste here:
[Paste any company research you already have, or write "none"]

Today's date is [Today's date]. Use these headings:

## 2. Company Intel

### Company overview
A table: Field | What I found | Source
Fields: what they do (one sentence), industry, size (employees), founded, headquarters, ownership or funding stage, revenue (if public), mission, values.

### Products and services
What the company sells and to whom, in plain words. Spend most of this on the product, service or team closest to this role. Include recent launches and how the company describes its position in its market (label the company's own claims as theirs).

### Recent news and growth signals
A table: Date | What happened | Why it matters for my interview | Source
Cover the past 12 months: funding, partnerships, launches, expansions, leadership changes, awards, earnings. 3 to 6 items.

### What employees say
From review sites such as Glassdoor, Indeed and Comparably:
- The overall rating, the number of reviews and the trend (up, down or steady), with the date you checked
- 3 to 5 pros people mention often
- 3 to 5 cons people mention often
- What people say about the interview process: format, difficulty, how long it took
- Anything about this team or department
Summarize themes. Only quote a review word for word if you have its exact text and link. Add one line: review sites only hear from people who chose to post.

### Potential red flags
Check for layoffs, hiring freezes, lawsuits or regulatory problems, signs of high turnover, negative press and falling review ratings. For each one you find, give: what happened, when, how serious it looks (minor, isolated or ongoing), the source, and one calm question I could ask about it if it matters to me. If you find nothing, write "No red flags found in my search" and list what you searched.

Rules: Never invent facts, numbers, ratings, quotes or links. Write "Not found" instead of guessing. Mark each sourced claim with a linked number like [[1]](URL), give dates, and add "(older than 12 months)" to anything published more than 12 months before today. Be honest and balanced.

End with a numbered Sources list for this section.
~~~

**Replace:** [Job title], [Company], [Company website], [Paste the job post], [Paste any company research you already have, or write "none"], [Today's date]

## Who you'll meet

**What it does:** Builds a short profile of each person you're meeting, with a genuine, work-related way to connect.
**When to use it:** Once you know who's on your interview schedule. This is Section 3 of your dossier. Update it before every round.

### Step 1: Profile the people

~~~text
Act as my interview coach and researcher. Build Section 3 of my interview dossier: Who You'll Meet, for my [Interview stage] interview for [Job title] at [Company].

The people I'm meeting (from my invite or the recruiter):
[Name, job title, and LinkedIn link if you have it. Or write "I don't know yet"]

Follow these rules strictly:
- Only include the people I listed above, plus anyone the job post or an official company page names as connected to this role (for example "reports to the VP of Sales, {name}"). Say where each name came from.
- Never guess who the hiring manager or the team members are.
- If you can search the web, look only for public, work-related information: current role, time at the company, career path, and things they've posted, written, presented or been quoted on. Nothing personal: no family, home, health or personal social media.
- If more than one person matches a name, don't pick one. Tell me and ask for a link.
- Never invent a person, a title, a post or a link. Only include a LinkedIn link if I gave it to you, or you found it in search results and you're confident it's the right person.

## 3. Who You'll Meet

For each person:

### {Name}, {Job title}
- Where this name came from: {my invite, the job post, the company website}
- Background: 2 to 3 sentences on their career path, with sources
- What they'll likely care about: based on their role (a recruiter, a future manager, a peer and an executive each look for different things)
- Rapport angle: one specific, public, work-related thing they posted, spoke about or achieved, with the link and date, and one natural way I could mention it. If you find nothing, write "Not found" and suggest a friendly opener based on their role instead.

If I don't know who I'm meeting yet, don't list anyone. Instead give me:
1. A short, polite message I can send the recruiter to ask who I'll be meeting and what the format is.
2. What a recruiter, a hiring manager, a peer and an executive each tend to look for, so I'm ready for any of them.

Mark each sourced claim with a linked number like [[1]](URL), give dates, and add "(older than 12 months)" to anything published more than 12 months ago. End with a numbered Sources list for this section.
~~~

**Replace:** [Interview stage], [Job title], [Company], [Name, job title, and LinkedIn link if you have it. Or write "I don't know yet"]

## Competitors and industry trends

**What it does:** Researches the company's main competitors and the industry news you can mention to show you know the space.
**When to use it:** Any time before the interview. It matters most for hiring manager and final rounds. This is Section 11 of your dossier.

### Step 1: Research the competition

~~~text
Act as my interview coach and researcher. Build Section 11 of my interview dossier: Competitors and Industry Trends, for my interview for [Job title] at [Company].

What I know about the company so far:
[Paste your Company Intel section or notes, or write "none"]

If you can search the web, use it. If you can't, tell me, and list exactly what to look up and paste in.

Use these headings:

## 11. Competitors and Industry Trends

### Top competitors
A table for 3 to 5 direct competitors: Company | Website | What they do (1 to 2 sentences) | How they compare (product, customers, approach) | Source

### Where the company is stronger
What [Company] does better or differently, with sources. If a claim comes from the company itself, label it as the company's claim.

### Where the company is under pressure
Where competitors are stronger, and the challenges [Company] faces, with sources.

### Industry news and trends
4 to 6 items from the past 6 to 12 months: market trends, regulation, notable competitor moves (funding, launches, acquisitions) and industry reports. A table: Date | What happened | Why it matters to this role | Source

### 3 things to mention in the interview
Three short talking points I could bring up naturally, each tied to the role and written the way a person would say it out loud. For example: "I noticed a few of your competitors are moving into small business lending. How is your team thinking about that?"

Rules: Never invent companies, numbers or news. Write "Not found" instead of guessing. Mark each sourced claim with a linked number like [[1]](URL), give dates, and add "(older than 12 months)" to anything published more than 12 months ago. End with a numbered Sources list for this section.
~~~

**Replace:** [Job title], [Company], [Paste your Company Intel section or notes, or write "none"]
