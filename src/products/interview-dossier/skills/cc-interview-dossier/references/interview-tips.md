## 12. Interview Tips

### Before the interview
- **Find out the format.** Ask the recruiter whether it's a panel, a one-on-one, behavioural or case-based. The format tells you which stories to prepare.
- **Confirm the logistics.** The date, the time and time zone, the platform (Zoom, Teams or Google Meet) or the address, and who you're meeting. Save the link or address where you can find it fast.
- **Set up your space.** Pick a quiet, well-lit spot. Test your camera, microphone and internet the day before.
- **Dress one level up.** Match the company's dress code or go slightly above it. If you're not sure, business casual works for most interviews. Solid colours look better on camera than busy patterns.
- **Keep the job post handy.** Have it printed or open so you can point to specific requirements.

### During the interview
- **Pause before you answer.** Take 2 to 3 seconds to collect your thoughts. It feels longer to you than to them. A short pause sounds thoughtful, not awkward.
- **Slow down.** Most of us speed up when we're nervous. If you hear yourself rushing, take a breath.
- **Use STAR for behavioural questions.** When you hear "Tell me about a time when...", go Situation, Task, Action, Result. It keeps you on track and stops the rambling.
- **Aim for 60 to 90 seconds per answer.** If you pass 2 minutes, wrap up. You can always ask, "Would you like me to go deeper on any part of that?"
- **Match their energy.** If they're relaxed, you can relax too. If they're formal, stay professional.
- **Show, don't tell.** Skip "I'm a great communicator." Tell the story that proves it.
- **It's okay not to know.** Say so honestly. "I haven't used that tool, but here's how I'd get up to speed" is a strong answer.

### Video interviews
- **Camera at eye level.** Stack your laptop on books if you need to. Looking down into the camera reads as distracted.
- **Look at the lens when you talk.** That's what eye contact looks like on their screen. When they're talking, you can look at their face.
- **Close everything else.** Shut other tabs and apps, turn off notifications, and put your phone on silent, face down.
- **Keep the background simple.** A clean wall, a bookshelf or a plain virtual background.
- **Keep water nearby.** A dry throat mid-answer is no fun.
- **Wear headphones.** They cut echo and make you easier to hear. Small wired earbuds look tidier on camera than big headphones.

### Body language
- **Sit up, but stay relaxed.** Lean in a little to show you're engaged. Don't slouch or lean way back.
- **Use your hands.** Natural gestures (visible on camera) help you look confident and more like yourself.
- **Smile when it fits.** Especially at hello and when the conversation is light. A real smile builds rapport faster than any talking point.
- **Nod while you listen.** Small nods show you're following.

### After the interview
- **Send a thank-you email within 24 hours.** Mention something specific from the conversation. Keep it to 3 to 5 sentences. Don't skip this one.
- **Debrief while it's fresh.** Write down the questions they asked, how you answered, and what you wish you'd said. It makes the next round easier.
- **Follow up once.** If the date they gave you passes, send one polite check-in email. Just one.

### If your interview is by phone
- Sit up straight or stand. You'll sound more awake.
- Keep your dossier open in front of you, but don't read from it.
- Smile when you talk. It changes your tone.
- Leave a beat before you answer. They can't see you thinking, so say "Good question, let me think about that for a second" if you need longer.

### If your interview is in person
- Plan your route and aim to arrive 10 to 15 minutes early. Walk in about 5 minutes before your time.
- Bring 2 to 3 printed copies of your resume, a notebook and a pen.
- Be friendly to everyone you meet, from the front desk on.
- Greet each interviewer by name, and offer a handshake if it feels natural.
