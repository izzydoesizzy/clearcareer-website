# Employer objection prompts

Every hiring manager reads your resume with a quiet question in the back of their mind: "What could go wrong if we hire this person?" These prompts find the doubts they might have about you for this job and give you a calm, true answer to each one before you're in the room. This is Section 5 of your dossier. Replace everything in [square brackets] with your own details. Leave anything in {curly braces} alone: those are slots the AI fills.

Each objection becomes a card with five parts:

- **Severity:** High (could stop an offer if you don't address it), Medium (likely to come up, so prepare an answer) or Low (worth knowing, rarely decisive).
- **The concern:** what the hiring manager might be thinking.
- **Counter-strategy:** how to address it or reframe it, ideally before they raise it.
- **Proof point:** the evidence from your experience that answers it.
- **Talking point:** the exact words you can say if it comes up.

Here's a made-up example so you can see the shape. Sam is interviewing for a Senior Product Manager role at Northwind Bank (a fictional bank). Sam has worked in product at a retail software company and has never worked at a bank.

**No banking experience**

- **Severity:** High
- **The concern:** Banking has strict rules and slow approvals. They may worry Sam will need months to get up to speed, or will push changes through too fast.
- **Counter-strategy:** Name it before they do. Show that Sam has worked under strict rules before and learns a new field quickly, then share a plan for the first 90 days.
- **Proof point:** Sam launched a payments feature that needed sign-off from the legal and privacy teams. (In your real cards, this comes from your resume, with your real numbers.)
- **Talking point:** "I haven't worked at a bank, and I won't pretend I have. What I have done is ship a payments feature through legal and privacy review, so I know how to plan around approvals instead of fighting them. In my first 90 days, I'd sit with your compliance team before I touch the roadmap."

Notice what the talking point does: it admits the gap in one sentence, gives real proof, and ends on a plan. No apology, no spin.

## Find the employer's objections

**What it does:** Lists at least 5 doubts a hiring manager might have about you for this job, ordered by severity, each with a counter-strategy, a proof point and a word-for-word talking point.
**When to use it:** After your competitive edge (Section 4), so its Gap and Moderate rows feed straight in. This is Section 5 of your dossier.

### Step 1: Build the objection cards

~~~text
Act as a careful hiring manager for [Job title] at [Company], then switch to being my interview coach. Build Section 5 of my interview dossier: Employer Objections.

The job post:
[Paste the job post]

My resume:
[Paste your resume]

Anything they'll notice that I want help explaining:
[A gap, a layoff, a career change, short stints, being more or less senior than the role. Or write "none"]

First, think critically about where a hiring manager might hesitate:
- Skills or experience the post asks for that my resume doesn't show
- Industry or field differences
- Seniority (more senior or more junior than the role)
- Employment gaps or career changes
- Missing certifications, degrees or tools
- Short time in recent roles

Then write at least 5 objection cards, from highest to lowest severity:

## 5. Employer Objections

### {Objection title}
- **Severity:** High, Medium or Low
- **The concern:** 1 to 2 sentences on what the hiring manager might think.
- **Counter-strategy:** 2 to 3 sentences on how to address or reframe it, ideally before they raise it.
- **Proof point:** specific evidence from my resume, or from what I've told you, that answers it.
- **Talking point:** "1 to 2 sentences I can say word for word if this comes up."

Rules:
- Be honest. It's better to prepare for a tough question than to be caught off guard.
- Proof points use only my real experience. If I have no evidence for one, say so, and give me an honest way to handle it (for example, how I'd learn the missing skill, using a time I learned something similar quickly, if I've told you about one).
- Never suggest I claim experience I don't have.
- For gaps, layoffs and career changes, keep the talking point short and true. I don't owe anyone details about my health, family or other personal matters.
- Talking points should sound like a person talking: short sentences, no buzzwords, no dashes.
~~~

**Replace:** [Job title], [Company], [Paste the job post], [Paste your resume], [A gap, a layoff, a career change, short stints, being more or less senior than the role. Or write "none"]

### Step 2: Practise the top three

~~~text
Now help me practise my 3 highest-severity objections.

For each one, ask me the toughest version of the question a hiring manager might ask, worded the way a real person would ask it. Ask ONE question, then wait for my answer.

After each answer, give me:
1. What worked
2. The one fix that matters most
3. A stronger version, built only from my real facts

Then ask if I want to try again or move to the next one.
~~~

**Replace:** Nothing. Use this after Step 1.
